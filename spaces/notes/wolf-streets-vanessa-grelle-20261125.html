The Wolf Of All Streets：比特币 50 亿美元 ETF 反转背后的隐藏成本

  • Broadcast：https://x.com/i/broadcasts/2RekazAWqzzbpN
  • 时间：2026-11-25 22:00 CST 开播，全长约 40 分 54 秒（2453.6 秒）
  • 主持：The Wolf Of All Streets（Scott Melker，英文节目）
  • 嘉宾：Vanessa Grelle（RK Capital，投资加密与 AI 及广义科技）
  • 逐字稿：713 行，覆盖 [00:00:00]–[00:40:33]，末句 "Happy Thanksgiving."
  • 说明：以下均为发言者自述观点，未经核实。ASR 归一见文末。

一句话

Scott 的标题点是：11 月比特币 ETF 流出 50 亿美元、BTC 跌破 58,000 美元，但流出主要是散户在"吐"，而 13F 数据显示 2,200 多家机构在三季度还在加仓——反转的"隐藏成本"是，ETF 时代的比特币不再由散户定价，而是由机构的资金流定价，而机构此刻全线 risk-off、在场边等着。

一、开场：红色星期三

PCE 数据偏热，BTC 跌破 58,000 美元（节目中途又回到 58,000 美元上方）；通胀继续上升、关税承诺再起、AI"黑掉一切"；英伟达黄仁勋回归那波大涨已经回吐，市场随之走弱；周一 BTC 冲到约 64,000 美元的涨幅很快就没了。Scott 的玩笑：感恩节有黑色星期五，现在有了"红色星期三"。

二、ETF 流出 50 亿：散户在吐，机构在接

  • 11 月比特币 ETF 净流出 50 亿美元，BTC 跌破 60,000 美元；又一日净流出 5.7 亿美元，其中 IBIT 一天就流出 5 亿多美元，是有史以来最糟糕的一天之一。
  • 但看底层结构：流出主要是散户，而持有比特币 ETF 的是机构——按 13F 数据超过 2,200 家机构持有；三季度富国银行（Wells Fargo）的持仓上升约 106–108%，全部是注册投资顾问（RIA）代表客户加仓。
  • Vanessa 的解读：机构"迫不及待想获得更多敞口"，哪怕只是在下跌时小口吃进，或是因为这正是他们上线的时间点；而散户则彻底认亏离场。
  • 组合构建的差异：机构只配一小部分、可以上下调仓再平衡；散户如果加密占组合比重很大，熊市里就很难拿住、很难保持信念。

三、宏观：高利率 + 强美元是最差组合

  • 高利率与美元走强同时出现，可能是对加密最不利的宏观组合；BTC 跌了，但跌幅比 Vanessa 预期的要小；加密与高科技、高贝塔资产高度相关。
  • 2024 年 ETF 进来之后，交易比特币的人变了——机构管理资金流的方式与最初的散户投资者不同，相关性因此大幅上升；现在必须对"贬值交易"（debasement trade）有观点，也必须理解 BTC 波动率下降是会持续还是只是加密市场的暂时特征。
  • Vanessa 关注的是全市场的 risk-on/risk-off，而不只是加密情绪，以及 AI 交易的 unwind；英伟达的故事是触发因素之一；Anthropic IPO 表现不佳；现在人们在所有资产上都是 risk-off，在场边等着看会发生什么；加密相对 AI 交易没那么敏感。

四、PCE 与关税：数据与数据之后

  • 10 月 PCE 通胀（美联储最看重的通胀指标）升至 3.8%，为 2023 年 4 月以来第二高；符合预期；核心 PCE 比预期高 0.1%，为 3.3% 而非 3.2%；CPI、PPI、PCE 都在继续走高；12 月加息概率在上升。
  • Scott 的个人看法："我个人认为美联储现在没那么重要了。"问题是：如果美联储加息 25bp，影响是什么、何时影响通胀？并不清楚；如果概率推到 50bp，影响会更大；但大部分通胀来自油气成本上升与关税，很难看到加息的即时效果。
  • 关税：美国宣布对包括中国在内的一大批国家加征新关税——就在特朗普与习近平会面、跳着 YMCA 开心完之后；这会给通胀带来更多价格压力。

五、Anthropic IPO：看起来很像一次代币发行

  • Anthropic：发行价 83 美元，开盘 110 美元，收盘约 81 美元，盘前约 77 美元；"基本就是编好的剧本"——IPO 的机制是几乎没有流通盘，不到 1% 的股份可交易，首日卖出的只有刚买入的人；目前只有约 2% 的 Anthropic 股份解禁，一年后将是巨量解禁。
  • Scott："看起来很像一次代币发行"，这本身也在给市场施加压力；私人交易长期不上市的后果是短期上涨空间被封顶。
  • 监管方面：SEC 提出的"创新豁免"（innovation exemption）针对代币——500 万美元上限、只需一份白皮书、非合格投资者也可参与；对几万亿美元的 IPO 毫无影响，但对代币融资有意义。
  • 合格投资者法把很多人挡在有趣机会之外；代币化的 pre-IPO 股份可能打开通道；RK 投资了 Securitize，也看好 Ondo 与 Superstate 等提供 pre-IPO 股份与流动性的平台。
  • Hyperliquid 上的加密永续交易员对价格发现的判断远胜分析师：ANT 在 Hyperliquid 上交易于 90 多美元，预测市场 70–78 美元，IPO 实际开盘超过 100 美元；SpaceX 也是如此，交易量达数亿美元；大多数人其实只想要价格敞口，并不在乎有没有底层股份的权利；对冲已有持仓的用例也很有意思。

六、黑客与安全：攻击的永远是人，不是技术

  • 近期一连串事件：FinCEN 被黑（不清楚是敏感信息还是非敏感信息）、CIA 被黑（影响未分类信息）、微软 24 小时宕机；"美国财政部遭网络攻击"的标题他第一次读错了，实际并没有发生。
  • Vanessa：未来两年 AI 能力将成为大量攻击的来源，无论是加密还是常规黑客；但在加密领域，几乎永远是人为失误，不是技术能力被攻破；朝鲜用的是社会工程学的新花样、从内部进入，而不是黑掉技术、偷走密钥。
  • 怎么加固"人"这个单点故障：RK 对投资组合公司格外关注——定期电话与更新，讲 OpSec 基础流程、利用 AI 提示技术，但核心是员工培训、意识与偏执；"真正偏执的公司会成功"。
  • 风险控制：上游流程先行，再算一旦出事的最大痛苦点与最大损失；与安全公司及 "CL911"（一群帮助追回与拆解攻击的白帽）合作；币安被黑一案显示了行业的韧性——美国执法部门非常老练、介入很快，币安让投资者与客户得到全额补偿；"庆幸是币安，因为他们钱多到这只是个零头"——大多数公司填不上 64 亿美元的窟窿。
  • 币安被黑资金的去向：美国已提起诉讼，要追回朝鲜网络攻击者在币安黑客事件中窃取的超过 10 亿美元加密资产；Circle 表示可以冻结这些资产但拿它们没办法，于是干脆宣布它们"死亡"、重新发行 11 亿美元 USDC——"如果你对稳定币的中心化还有疑问，这就是答案"；美国政府将持有这些币；Tether 则对追回收取 20% 的费用（他的说法）。
  • 行业缺保险：没有大型机构愿意承保黑客风险，只有一些小型机构与部分基金合作；那些"恐龙级"或已死的小协议、账面上有几千万美元 AUM 却没人在维护，是黑客眼中的"免费钱"，这里 200 万、那里 400 万。

七、机构采用：我们得到了想要的一切，只是做的人是摩根大通

  • Scott："我们实际上得到了所有想要的东西，甚至更多……只是做的人是摩根大通。"区块链正在变成大型机构的管道系统；DeFi 与平行轨道也有进展，但主要是机构管道。
  • Vanessa 是企业以太坊联盟（Enterprise Ethereum Alliance）董事会成员；摩根大通从一开始就是该协会成员，2017 年的启动派对就在摩根大通办公室办的；大机构早就理解这项技术的力量，但当时技术不安全、没有监管框架，无法行动。
  • RK 的投资方向：利用稳定币的新银行（neobanks）、托管基础设施、收益类产品与让投资者获得传统收益类投资敞口的产品；金融服务基于加密轨道创新的 15 年窗口；《GENIUS 法案》与其他监管举措是主要瓶颈，SEC 与 CFTC 正在积极推进。
  • Securitize 当天公告：其高收益基金 HINC 上线成为 Aave Horizon 的抵押品——一只持有公司债敞口的基金，现在可以在 Aave 上作为抵押品；这可能是第一波新型资产进入 DeFi 抵押品的开始；代币化货币市场基金可以 24/7 用作抵押品、即时且便宜，这就是产品市场契合度；随着 TradFi 与 DeFi 更深度整合，采用会大幅上升。
  • Fidelity 把 Aave、Chainlink 与 Uniswap 代币带上经纪平台——现在可以在 Fidelity 买 8 种代币，1% 费用；Fidelity 在机构采用上一直走在前面（2017–2018 年就已在场）；三个都是老牌资产、普通投资者多少能从基本面理解；Paywords（口播）推出了比特币返现信用卡；Coinbase 让用户以更机构化的方式出借比特币。
  • 长尾资产的障碍：代币经济学的理解难度 vs DCF 模型；越接近股票/股权、有经济权利的代币越容易被理解，回购就是其中之一——人们理解回购，这也是 Hyperliquid 表现好的原因之一；Venice 也是如此。

八、隐私：机构采用的最大解锁

  • Vanessa 的 X 头像横幅写着"Privacy is normal"（隐私是常态）；隐私是机构采用加密的最大解锁——没有机构会公开自己的交易账本、让所有人知道它在做什么、怎么投资。
  • Zcash 是老牌项目；Zama 是 RK 投资的项目；还有一家早期公司专门为机构做隐私——把隐私嵌入钱包、稳定币、协议或交易操作中；即使看不到机构的名字，一旦有人把钱包与机构关联起来，整个交易策略就公开了。
  • 同样的问题也出现在 AI 上：人们把信息倒进封闭的 LLM，对方把信息留给自己；Venice 等项目可以屏蔽这些信息、不拿你的数据训练模型；散户为了方便一直在放弃隐私，"但这仍然值得争取"。

九、财库公司：有泡沫，但底层逻辑分得清

  • RK 的投资组合包括 SharpLink、Orange（口播，比特币财库公司）与 Forward Industries（BNB 财库）；财库公司确实有过一个小泡沫；本质上是对底层代币的杠杆敞口。
  • SharpLink 与 Forward 在他们看来做得很好，尽管股价下跌——因为底层资产也在跌；仅靠质押或参与生态系统就能比 ETH 多赚 3%，所以理论上应该比 ETH 交易得好 3%。
  • 要成功需要规模：很多小公司因流动性问题无法维持价格形成；希望一些小公司并入大机构；Orange 是巴西的比特币敞口（当地人无法在经纪账户里持有 Strategy）；Metaplanet 证明了这个模式——在日本投资 Metaplanet 比直接投资比特币更节税，当时也没有 ETF；Eric Weiss（Orange 董事）是最早向他解释这一点的人。
  • 比特币财库公司：Strategy 是无人能及的那一家；BTC 没有收益，不靠某种神奇的金融工程就无法跑赢它；现在有了不同风味的 ETF，可以获得 BTC 或质押 ETH 的额外收益。

十、四年周期：2025 年他曾完全否定它

  • Vanessa：相信四年周期，但减半的影响随时间减弱（供应减少），而且机构进来了；认为要等到 2028–29 年才会回升；与科技股、纳斯达克的相关性更强了。
  • Scott：2025 年他曾完全否定四年周期，认为"它结束了"——部分原因是 BTC 因为 ETF 提前见顶，看起来不像周期；其余加密根本没跟上，没有出现上一轮那样的山寨牛市；但他无法否认：BTC 见顶的时间与四年周期"巫师"预测的只差四天；而且见底那天恰好是 FTX 底部周年，"有人在跟我们开玩笑，我们活在模拟器里吧"。
  • Vanessa 的书：给传统投资者与 RIA 的框架，讲加密配置如何影响传统投资组合；不是鼓吹买 BTC、ETH 或 Solana，只是给一个能持续持有组合的框架；前言由 Chris John Karla（口播，按 ASR 保留，未核实姓名拼写）撰写，他也出过书。

  • 11 月比特幣 ETF 淨流出 50 億美元，BTC 跌破 60,000 美元；又一日淨流出 5.7 億美元，其中 IBIT 一天就流出 5 億多美元，是有史以來最糟糕的一天之一。

  • 高利率與美元走強同時出現，可能是對加密最不利的宏觀組合；BTC 跌了，但跌幅比 Vanessa 預期的要小；加密與高科技、高貝塔資產高度相關。
  • 2024 年 ETF 進來之後，交易比特幣的人變了——機構管理資金流的方式與最初的散戶投資者不同，相關性因此大幅上升；現在必須對「貶值交易」（debasement trade）有觀點，也必須理解 BTC 波動率下降是會持續還是只是加密市場的暫時特徵。
  • Vanessa 關注的是全市場的 risk-on/risk-off，而不只是加密情緒，以及 AI 交易的 unwind；英偉達的故事是觸發因素之一；Anthropic IPO 表現不佳；現在人們在所有資產上都是 risk-off，在場邊等著看會發生什麼；加密相對 AI 交易沒那麼敏感。

四、PCE 與關稅：資料與資料之後

  • 10 月 PCE 通膨（聯準會最看重的通膨指標）升至 3.8%，為 2023 年 4 月以來第二高；符合預期；核心 PCE 比預期高 0.1%，為 3.3% 而非 3.2%；CPI、PPI、PCE 都在繼續走高；12 月升息機率在上升。
  • Scott 的個人看法：「我個人認為聯準會現在沒那麼重要了。」問題是：如果聯準會升息 25bp，影響是什麼、何時影響通膨？並不清楚；如果機率推到 50bp，影響會更大；但大部分通膨來自油氣成本上升與關稅，很難看到升息的即時效果。
  • 關稅：美國宣布對包括中國在內的一大批國家加徵新關稅——就在川普與習近平會面、跳著 YMCA 開心完之後；這會給通膨帶來更多價格壓力。

The Wolf Of All Streets: The Hidden Cost Behind Bitcoin's $5 Billion ETF Reversal

  • Broadcast: https://x.com/i/broadcasts/2RekazAWqzzbpN
  • Time: Aired 2026-11-25 at 22:00 CST, total length about 40 min 54 sec (2453.6 seconds)
  • Host: The Wolf Of All Streets (Scott Melker, English-language program)
  • Guest: Vanessa Grelle (RK Capital, investing in crypto and AI and technology broadly)

The one-liner

Scott's headline point: Bitcoin ETFs saw $5 billion of outflows in November and BTC broke below $58,000, but the outflows were mainly retail "puking," while 13F data shows more than 2,200 institutions were still adding in Q3 — the "hidden cost" of the reversal is that in the ETF era, Bitcoin is no longer priced by retail, but by institutional flows, and institutions are currently risk-off across the board, sitting on the sidelines waiting.

1. Opening: Red Wednesday

The PCE print came in hot and BTC broke below $58,000 (it moved back above $58,000 mid-show); inflation kept rising, tariff pledges resurfaced, AI "hacked everything"; the big rally on Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia had been given back and the market weakened with it; Monday's run to about $64,000 in BTC vanished quickly. Scott's joke: Thanksgiving has Black Friday — now there is "Red Wednesday."

2. $5 billion of ETF outflows: retail puking, institutions catching

  • Bitcoin ETFs saw $5 billion of net outflows in November, and BTC broke below $60,000; another day saw $570 million of net outflows, with IBIT alone losing more than $500 million in a single day — one of the worst days ever.
  • But look at the underlying structure: the outflows were mainly retail, while the holders of Bitcoin ETFs are institutions — more than 2,200 institutions hold them per 13F data; Wells Fargo's holdings rose about 106–108% in Q3, all of it RIAs (registered investment advisors) adding on behalf of clients.
  • Vanessa's read: institutions "can't wait to get more exposure," even if only nibbling on the way down, or because this is simply when they came online; retail, by contrast, has fully capitulated and left.
  • The portfolio-construction difference: institutions allocate only a small slice and can rebalance up and down; if crypto is a large share of a retail investor's portfolio, it is very hard to hold through a bear market and keep conviction.

3. Macro: high rates + a strong dollar is the worst combination

  • High rates and a strengthening dollar occurring together may be the worst macro combination for crypto; BTC fell, but by less than Vanessa expected; crypto is highly correlated with high-tech, high-beta assets.
  • After ETFs arrived in 2024, the people trading Bitcoin changed — institutions manage flows differently from the original retail investors, so correlations rose sharply; you now have to have a view on the debasement trade, and you have to understand whether BTC's declining volatility will persist or is only a temporary feature of the crypto market.
  • Vanessa watches risk-on/risk-off across the whole market, not just crypto sentiment, as well as the unwinding of the AI trade; the Nvidia story was one of the triggers; Anthropic's IPO performed poorly; people are now risk-off across all assets, sitting on the sidelines waiting to see what happens; crypto is less sensitive than the AI trade.

4. PCE and tariffs: the data, and what came after the data

  • October PCE inflation (the Fed's preferred inflation gauge) rose to 3.8%, the second-highest since April 2023; it was in line with expectations; core PCE came in 0.1% above expectations, at 3.3% rather than 3.2%; CPI, PPI and PCE all kept moving higher; the probability of a December rate hike was rising.
  • Scott's personal view: "I personally don't think the Fed matters as much anymore." The question is: if the Fed hikes 25bp, what is the impact and when does it affect inflation? It is unclear; if the odds were pushed to 50bp, the impact would be bigger; but most of the inflation comes from rising oil and gas costs and tariffs, so it is hard to see an immediate effect from a rate hike.
  • Tariffs: the US announced new tariffs on a large group of countries including China — right after Trump met Xi Jinping and happily danced to YMCA; this will add more price pressure to inflation.

5. The Anthropic IPO: it looks a lot like a token launch

  • Anthropic: priced at $83, opened at $110, closed around $81, about $77 pre-market; "basically a scripted play" — the IPO mechanism means there is almost no float, less than 1% of shares tradable, and the only sellers on day one are people who just bought; only about 2% of Anthropic's shares are unlocked so far, and a year from now there will be a massive unlock.
  • Scott: "It looks a lot like a token launch," and that itself is putting pressure on the market; the consequence of private companies staying unlisted for a long time is that near-term upside gets capped.
  • On regulation: the SEC's proposed "innovation exemption" for tokens — a $5 million cap, only a white paper required, non-accredited investors may participate; it has no impact on multi-trillion-dollar IPOs, but it matters for token fundraising.
  • Accredited-investor law shuts many people out of interesting opportunities; tokenized pre-IPO shares could open a channel; RK has invested in Securitize, and is also bullish on platforms such as Ondo and Superstate that offer pre-IPO shares and liquidity.
  • Crypto perpetual traders on Hyperliquid judged price discovery far better than analysts: ANT traded above $90 on Hyperliquid, prediction markets had $70–78, and the IPO actually opened above $100; the same happened with SpaceX, with hundreds of millions of dollars in volume; most people really just want price exposure and don't care about the rights of underlying shares; the use case of hedging existing positions is interesting too.

6. Hackers and security: the target is always people, not technology

  • A recent string of incidents: FinCEN hacked (unclear whether sensitive or non-sensitive information), the CIA hacked (affecting unclassified information), Microsoft down for 24 hours; the "US Treasury hit by cyberattack" headline — he misread it the first time; it did not actually happen.
  • Vanessa: over the next two years AI capabilities will be the source of a large number of attacks, in crypto and in conventional hacking alike; but in crypto it is almost always human error, not technology being broken; North Korea uses new variations of social engineering, getting in from the inside, rather than hacking the technology and stealing keys.
  • How to harden the single point of failure that is "people": RK pays extra attention to portfolio companies — regular calls and updates, teaching basic OpSec processes and using AI prompting techniques, but the core is employee training, awareness and paranoia; "the really paranoid companies will succeed."
  • Risk control: upstream processes first, then work out the maximum pain point and maximum loss if something goes wrong; working with security firms and "CL911" (a group of white hats who help recover and dismantle attacks); the Binance hack showed the industry's resilience — US law enforcement is very sophisticated and moved fast, and Binance made investors and customers whole; "thank God it was Binance, because they have so much money this was just a rounding error" — most companies could not fill a $6.4 billion hole.
  • Where the Binance hack funds went: the US has filed suit to recover more than $1 billion of crypto assets stolen by North Korean cyber attackers in the Binance hack; Circle said it could freeze those assets but could do nothing with them, so it simply declared them "dead" and reissued $1.1 billion of USDC — "if you had any question about the centralization of stablecoins, this is your answer"; the US government will hold those coins; Tether, by contrast, charges a 20% fee on recoveries (his account).
  • The industry lacks insurance: no large institution is willing to underwrite hacking risk, only some small institutions working with certain funds; those "dinosaur" or dead small protocols with tens of millions of dollars of AUM on the books and nobody maintaining them are "free money" to hackers — $2 million here, $4 million there.

7. Institutional adoption: we got everything we wanted, it's just JPMorgan doing it

  • Scott: "We actually got everything we wanted, and more… it's just JPMorgan doing it." Blockchain is becoming the plumbing of large institutions; there is progress in DeFi and parallel rails too, but it is mainly institutional plumbing.
  • Vanessa is a board member of the Enterprise Ethereum Alliance; JPMorgan has been a member of the association from the very beginning, and the 2017 launch party was held at JPMorgan's offices; large institutions understood the power of this technology early on, but at the time the technology was not secure and there was no regulatory framework, so they could not act.
  • RK's investment directions: new banks (neobanks) built on stablecoins, custody infrastructure, yield products and products that give investors exposure to traditional yield-type investments; a 15-year window of financial-services innovation on crypto rails; the GENIUS Act and other regulatory moves are the main bottleneck, and the SEC and CFTC are actively pushing forward.
  • Securitize announced that day: its high-yield fund HINC went live as collateral on Aave Horizon — a fund holding corporate bond exposure can now be used as collateral on Aave; this may be the start of the first wave of new asset types entering DeFi as collateral; tokenized money market funds can be used as collateral 24/7, instantly and cheaply — that is product-market fit; as TradFi and DeFi integrate more deeply, adoption will rise substantially.
  • Fidelity brought the Aave, Chainlink and Uniswap tokens onto its brokerage platform — you can now buy 8 tokens on Fidelity for a 1% fee; Fidelity has always been ahead on institutional adoption (it was there in 2017–2018); all three are established assets that ordinary investors can more or less understand from fundamentals; Paywords (as spoken) launched a Bitcoin cashback credit card; Coinbase lets users lend out Bitcoin in a more institutional way.
  • The obstacle for long-tail assets: the difficulty of understanding tokenomics vs DCF models; tokens closer to stocks/equity, with economic rights, are easier to understand, and buybacks are one of those — people understand buybacks, which is also one of the reasons Hyperliquid has performed well; the same goes for Venice.

8. Privacy: the biggest unlock for institutional adoption

  • Vanessa's X profile banner reads "Privacy is normal"; privacy is the biggest unlock for institutional adoption of crypto — no institution will publish its trading ledger and let everyone know what it is doing and how it is investing.
  • Zcash is a long-standing project; Zama is an RK portfolio project; there is also an early-stage company doing privacy specifically for institutions — embedding privacy into wallets, stablecoins, protocols or trading operations; even if you cannot see an institution's name, once someone links a wallet to the institution, its entire trading strategy becomes public.
  • The same problem appears in AI: people pour information into closed LLMs, and the other side keeps that information for itself; projects like Venice can shield that information and not train models on your data; retail has been giving up privacy for convenience all along, "but it's still worth fighting for."

9. Treasury companies: there is a bubble, but the underlying logic is distinguishable

  • RK's portfolio includes SharpLink, Orange (as spoken, a Bitcoin treasury company) and Forward Industries (a BNB treasury); treasury companies did have a mini-bubble; in essence they are leveraged exposure to the underlying token.
  • In their view SharpLink and Forward have done very well despite their share prices falling — because the underlying assets fell too; staking or participating in the ecosystem alone can earn 3% more than ETH, so in theory they should trade 3% better than ETH.
  • Success requires scale: many small companies cannot sustain price formation because of liquidity problems; the hope is that some small companies merge into larger institutions; Orange is Brazilian Bitcoin exposure (locals cannot hold Strategy in a brokerage account); Metaplanet proved the model — in Japan, investing in Metaplanet was more tax-efficient than investing in Bitcoin directly, and there was no ETF at the time; Eric Weiss (an Orange director) was the first person to explain this to him.
  • Bitcoin treasury companies: Strategy is the one nobody can match; BTC has no yield, and without some magical financial engineering you cannot outperform it; now there are ETFs of different flavors that can earn extra yield on BTC or staked ETH.

10. The four-year cycle: in 2025 he completely dismissed it

  • Vanessa: she believes in the four-year cycle, but the impact of the halving weakens over time (supply reduction), and institutions have come in; she thinks it will not recover until 2028–29; the correlation with tech stocks and the Nasdaq has grown stronger.
  • Scott: in 2025 he completely dismissed the four-year cycle, thinking "it's over" — partly because BTC topped early due to ETFs and did not look like a cycle; the rest of crypto never followed, and there was no altcoin bull market like the last cycle; but he cannot deny it: BTC's top came within four days of the four-year-cycle "wizard" prediction, and the bottoming day happened to be the anniversary of the FTX bottom — "someone is playing a joke on us, we're living in a simulation."
  • Vanessa's book: a framework for traditional investors and RIAs on how crypto allocation affects a traditional portfolio; it does not push buying BTC, ETH or Solana, it just provides a framework for holding a portfolio sustainably; the foreword was written by Chris John Karla (as spoken, retained per ASR, spelling unverified), who has also written a book.
