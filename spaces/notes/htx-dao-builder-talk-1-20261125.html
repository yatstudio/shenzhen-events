HTX DAO Builder Talk 第一期：币圈寒冬撞上 AI 裁员潮，普通人还有出路吗？

一句话

寒冬是真的，但嘉宾们的共识是这轮寒冬和以前不一样——基建、ETF、传统资金都齐了，缺的是上涨的理由和真实的收入；AI 一边在裁员一边把门槛打下来，普通人的出路不在硬扛行情，而在把 AI 变成自己的生产力、同时攒下审美、判断力和社区信任这些 AI 拿不走的东西。

一、币圈寒冬：这轮和以前不一样

rtsky7：基建都齐了，缺的是上涨的理由。 他认为这轮寒冬和以前有比较大的不同：行业不缺基础设施、不缺资金进入的渠道（现货 ETF、越来越多交易基础设施、传统金融机构参与），但很多资产仍然缺少持续上涨的理由。他给出三个原因：

  1. 资金分流严重。 以前炒币预期有很高增长，现在回头发现 AI 也可以炒，美股、机器人都是很大的投资方向。
  2. 行业内部供应过剩。 项目越来越多、代币越来越多，但真正能创造收入、拥有真实用户的项目并没有同比例增加。
  3. 投资者对叙事脱敏。 以前写一份白皮书、讲个愿景就有人为梦想买单；现在大家只关心你到底有没有赚到钱、投资这个项目未来有什么收益。他认为这是好事，是对整个币圈商业模式的重新洗牌：以前大家考虑如何通过发币创造财富，现在考虑的是如何先创造财富、再通过代币分配财富。

他关注的方向是交易所支付、稳定币、美股代币化——至少有比较明确的真实需求。他认为 HTX DAO 最值得探索的是如何把社区参与、生态建设和真实价值形成长期正向循环。

达达：币圈正在接盘传统金融。 他转述了一组数据分析口径（未核实）：最近半年多一年，币安等交易所上线股票代币，围绕黄仁勋回归事件，币圈在股票代币市场大概接盘了 4 亿多美元，已接近整个市场约四分之一、是第二名的两倍多；SpaceX 永续单日成交 19.57 亿 USDT，已占到 SpaceX 现货股份成交的约 14.9%。他的结论是：大量币圈资金已经直接或间接参与到传统金融股价交易当中；交易所 24 小时、全年无休交易的便利性，未来可能把传统股民吸引过来。

卡尔：资金从 2025 年 9–10 月就开始往美股和 AI 流。 他认为 AI 股票像太阳、BTC 和 ETH 像月亮，资金被吸走了。2026 年 Web3 剩两个叙事：AI Agent 与 Web3 在链上形成真实收益（他称孙哥早期讲过这个叙事）、RWA 代币化。他称 7–8 月链上有几十万个 AI Agent 在跑（自述数字，未核实），是一个在增长的市场。BTC 从 12 万腰斩到 5.5 万左右，最近两天连跌；8 月 29 日 Bitcoin Asia 香港会上 CZ 还说要开启下一轮牛市，现在谁也不敢说了。美股也不正常，Anthropic 1.5–2 万亿美元的大 IPO 是人类历史上头一次；币圈现在跟资金、华尔街宏观数据、美国失业率都挂钩，不再是出个利好就涨。

他本人今年的交易：AI 是基本盘，Web3 是今年新投的仓位；买的都是比较稳的（UNI、ZEC 等小仓），不亏就算可以；核心不是看好什么，是在合适的时机出手、合适的时机退出。他认为现在反而是建仓的好时机——传统金融与 Crypto 对接是圈内人多年的梦想状态，只是对方现在也不好。

小杜：讨论的话题变了，就是寒冬的体感。 她是记者出身，去年火币节入圈约一年。她的观察是：刚入圈时大家聊的是仓位、数据、机会、BTC 走向、哪个赛道有新增长；最近更多人发给她的信息是让她辟谣——裁员、团队进展、帮孙哥辟谣，八卦成了热点。看新项目时，以前问的是"有没有空投、现在上车还有没有机会"，现在更多人持观望状态，先了解项目能不能继续生存；判断标准从看增长和想象空间，变成了看成本和生存周期。她认为寒冬不只是币价下跌，是市场更要求拿出更实际的东西。

达达总结：从"视梦率"到看真东西。 以前币圈讲"视梦率"——梦想有多大，市值就有多大；现在谁有用、谁能落地越来越清晰，这是好事。股票代币化先在中心化跑起来，链上衍生金融模式未来会起来，Crypto 的开放性、可编程性优势会慢慢凸显。他最后补了一句：现在的寒冬一定程度上不是币圈的寒冬，是经济寒冬，全球金融、全球股市都在寒冬。

二、AI 裁员潮：美国已经很悲观，中国还在暗流涌动

卡尔：2027 年是比较确定性的趋势。 他称硅谷一直比较激进悲观，微软、Google 都发邮件通知"愿意来就来、不爱来拿补偿"（他转述）；中国这边降本增效从 2023 年开始一直没停，AI 这波更严峻，但还没完全爆发，表面有说有笑、实际暗流涌动；马化腾前几天说要打硬仗（他转述）。他判断 AI 裁员潮会随着全球大势直接爆发，2027 年是比较确定性的趋势。

他还提出一个悖论：很多人没活干，但又希望什么都是无人的（无人驾驶、岗位被替代），没人拿工资就没人消费——全世界目前还没有人能解决这个问题。他称国家层面 AI 是战略核武器地位，优先发展技术、民生往后排；到 2027 年 AI 的组织方式可能发生整体变革（他引用"联鸿"的说法，未确认来源）。

设计师已经被替代，程序员、数学家排队中。 卡尔称 2024 年 Google 的 Nano Banana 模型出来后，设计师就被替代了，行业基本没了，只剩 PS、证件照等边角需求；程序员被替代也是马上（他的判断）；三年之内，数学家也被工程化——Anthropic、OpenAI 把以前认为 50–100 年解不出来的数学难题十几天、二十几天就搞定（他的说法，未核实）。他认为未来不被替代的人有两类：做生态建设、人脉资源多的人，以及天赋综合素质好的人。

三、普通人怎么办：会用 AI 和用好 AI 是两码事

德虎（文科生转开发）：先用起来，但别高估自己。 他用 AI 做客户项目和垂直行业营销工具。他的体会：写文案、做 PPT、表格、作图这些 AI 工具做起来轻而易举；不会用 AI 工具的人会被会用的人替代；公司裁员后留下的是 AI 替代不了的岗位，加上能熟练掌握 AI 工具的人（一个人做两三个人的事）。他以前在公司就推动下属用 AI，最后只留一两个就够；现在招人也要求必须掌握 AI 工具，否则排到最后甚至没有应聘机会。

他的建议：越早越好从最基础的工具用起，不用懂原理，但要会用它做事，最好懂一点原理；掌握工具后不一定非要上班，可以做自己的项目获利。但他提醒：OPC（一人公司）不是谁都适合做——很多刚毕业大学生、没 AI 经验的人去做 OPC 很不现实；全国各地很多 OPC 社区建了却没人入住，是他看到的一个奇怪现象。

卡尔：会用 AI 和用得好是两码事。 他举例：4 月 Claude Code 源码泄露后，GitHub 上很多人用 Rust 重写，觉得性能更好；但文科生对需求的理解、对软件工程的把握差很多，不知道怎么做测试，只做功能性测试、以为舒服就行，达不到产业级。真正能做 OPC 的人，本身能力已经超过半个组、在行业里已有名气，做之前就已经有人脉资源、知道需求、知道找谁。05 后比 00 后更急，休学创业非常多，一直在抓机会（他的观察）。

达达：AI 拿不走的是对需求的感受。 他认为数学家也很焦虑了，但 AI 不能替代的是人对需求的感受——了解世界、把需求说清楚、判断做出来的东西是否满足需求，这些感受性的东西 AI 不具备；观察力和判断力是未来很重要的能力。提示词工程师这个职业一定会被 AI 自己解决（人用正常语言说清楚就行）；Claude 已经在做"说清楚想要什么工具、它自己做出来装到手机/电脑上"的事，安全性等也由 AI 解决；但"需要什么、怎么说清楚、怎么判断它真的做到了"很重要。

rtsky7：拥抱 AI，效率可能是几十倍。 他是自由职业、全身心投入 Web3。他的体会：以前做自媒体要自己收集资料、写文章、做图片、剪视频，非专业根本做不出来；现在凭多个 AI，一个人能完成以前一个小团队的事（宣传海报、创意视频）。企业角度：以前 5 个人完成的工作，现在 2 个人用 AI 就能完成，剩下 3 个人的价值去哪？企业不一定裁员，但减少招聘就能改变就业市场。

他以前做 HR，认为现在大学生非常难：以前只需要比另一个大学生优秀；现在要比 AI 优秀企业才考虑用你，大学生没工作经验、社会履历不丰富，"可能还不如 AI"，就业环境非常糟糕。当每个人都有高效生产工具，效率本身不再稀缺，稀缺的是审美、判断力、对行业的认知——AI 写的文章需要你给提示、给角度，没有履历就没法用好 AI。AI 也替代不了他在币圈的经历、和社区之间的信任关系。未来知识越来越便宜，重要的是个人的判断能力和承担能力。

他的建议就一句话：积极拥抱 AI。有人排斥，觉得抢了饭碗或不够聪明而不去用；但用上之后会发现非常好用，不主动拥抱肯定被 AI 淘汰，主动拥抱效率可能是几十倍提升。他现在一天能生成十几张、几百张图片，写十几篇文章打造个人 IP，以前一天最多写一两篇文章、一张图片都做不出来；省下的时间可以跟社区互动。

四、给普通人的操作建议（达达在场内给出）

  • 没有很强交易能力就少交易，只做大仓位、大波动，逢低补仓或定投，不要上很多杠杆。
  • 熊市难熬，但熊市是最好建设的时机——大家无聊聊八卦的时候，你在偷偷用功，等牛市就有回报。

五、HTX DAO Genesis 在做什么

这是 HTX DAO Genesis 的新栏目 Builder Talk 第一期。达达介绍：Genesis 是 HTX DAO 的去中心化共建计划，任何人（包括社区成员）都可以加入；他作为生态节点之一，把认识的开发者召集起来，继续在 Web3 寻找新机会。Dexpigg 补充：Genesis 核心团队在搞"创新营"——项目方或个人开发者加入后，做出的东西可以部署在波场上或给 HTX 赋能，按贡献有对应等级，对开发者是扶持、对 HTX DAO 是代币使用场景更多，是双赢。

达达在结尾邀请更多 Builder 参与：HTX DAO 账号下有二维码，扫码填谷歌表单可加入（项目方、个人开发者都可）；下一期欢迎报名上麦分享。Builder Talk 的初衷：聊聊你想做什么、已经在做什么、看到别人在做什么；成功的经验值得分享，走过的弯路、没想明白的困惑也值得拿出来交流；抱团取暖，找到一起尝试的朋友。

一、幣圈寒冬：這輪和以前不一樣

rtsky7：基建都齊了，缺的是上漲的理由。 他認為這輪寒冬和以前有比較大的不同：行業不缺基礎設施、不缺資金進入的渠道（現貨 ETF、越來越多交易基礎設施、傳統金融機構參與），但很多資產仍然缺少持續上漲的理由。他給出三個原因：

  1. 資金分流嚴重。 以前炒幣預期有很高增長，現在回頭發現 AI 也可以炒，美股、機器人都是很大的投資方向。
  2. 行業內部供應過剩。 項目越來越多、代幣越來越多，但真正能創造收入、擁有真實使用者的項目並沒有同比例增加。
  3. 投資者對敘事脫敏。 以前寫一份白皮書、講個願景就有人為夢想買單；現在大家只關心你到底有沒有賺到錢、投資這個項目未來有什麼收益。他認為這是好事，是對整個幣圈商業模式的重新洗牌：以前大家考慮如何透過發幣創造財富，現在考慮的是如何先創造財富、再透過代幣分配財富。

他關注的方向是交易所支付、穩定幣、美股代幣化——至少有比較明確的真實需求。他認為 HTX DAO 最值得探索的是如何把社區參與、生態建設和真實價值形成長期正向循環。

達達：幣圈正在接盤傳統金融。 他轉述了一組數據分析口徑（未核實）：最近半年多一年，幣安等交易所上線股票代幣，圍繞黃仁勳回歸事件，幣圈在股票代幣市場大概接盤了 4 億多美元，已接近整個市場約四分之一、是第二名的兩倍多；SpaceX 永續單日成交 19.57 億 USDT，已佔到 SpaceX 現貨股份成交的約 14.9%。他的結論是：大量幣圈資金已經直接或間接參與到傳統金融股價交易當中；交易所 24 小時、全年無休交易的便利性，未來可能把傳統股民吸引過來。

卡爾：資金從 2025 年 9–10 月就開始往美股和 AI 流。 他認為 AI 股票像太陽、BTC 和 ETH 像月亮，資金被吸走了。2026 年 Web3 剩兩個敘事：AI Agent 與 Web3 在鏈上形成真實收益（他稱孫哥早期講過這個敘事）、RWA 代幣化。他稱 7–8 月鏈上有幾十萬個 AI Agent 在跑（自述數字，未核實），是一個在增長的市場。BTC 從 12 萬腰斬到 5.5 萬左右，最近兩天連跌；8 月 29 日 Bitcoin Asia 香港會上 CZ 還說要開啟下一輪牛市，現在誰也不敢說了。美股也不正常，Anthropic 1.5–2 萬億美元的大 IPO 是人類歷史上頭一次；幣圈現在跟資金、華爾街宏觀數據、美國失業率都掛鉤，不再是出個利好就漲。

三、普通人怎麼辦：會用 AI 和用好 AI 是兩碼事

德虎（文科生轉開發）：先用起來，但別高估自己。 他用 AI 做客戶項目和垂直行業行銷工具。他的體會：寫文案、做 PPT、表格、作圖這些 AI 工具做起來輕而易舉；不會用 AI 工具的人會被會用的人替代；公司裁員後留下的是 AI 替代不了的崗位，加上能熟練掌握 AI 工具的人（一個人做兩三個人的事）。他以前在公司就推動下屬用 AI，最後只留一兩個就夠；現在招人也要求必須掌握 AI 工具，否則排到最後甚至沒有應聘機會。

他的建議：越早越好從最基礎的工具用起，不用懂原理，但要會用它做事，最好懂一點原理；掌握工具後不一定非要上班，可以做自己的項目獲利。但他提醒：OPC（一人公司）不是誰都適合做——很多剛畢業大學生、沒 AI 經驗的人去做 OPC 很不現實；全國各地很多 OPC 社區建了卻沒人入住，是他看到的一個奇怪現象。

卡爾：會用 AI 和用得好是兩碼事。 他舉例：4 月 Claude Code 原始碼洩露後，GitHub 上很多人用 Rust 重寫，覺得效能更好；但文科生對需求的理解、對軟體工程的把握差很多，不知道怎麼做測試，只做功能性測試、以為舒服就行，達不到產業級。真正能做 OPC 的人，本身能力已經超過半個組、在行業裡已有名氣，做之前就已經有人脈資源、知道需求、知道找誰。05 後比 00 後更急，休學創業非常多，一直在抓機會（他的觀察）。

達達：AI 拿不走的是對需求的感受。 他認為數學家也很焦慮了，但 AI 不能替代的是人對需求的感受——了解世界、把需求說清楚、判斷做出來的東西是否滿足需求，這些感受性的東西 AI 不具備；觀察力和判斷力是未來很重要的能力。提示詞工程師這個職業一定會被 AI 自己解決（人用正常語言說清楚就行）；Claude 已經在做「說清楚想要什麼工具、它自己做出來裝到手機/電腦上」的事，安全性等也由 AI 解決；但「需要什麼、怎麼說清楚、怎麼判斷它真的做到了」很重要。

rtsky7：擁抱 AI，效率可能是幾十倍。 他是自由職業、全身心投入 Web3。他的體會：以前做自媒體要自己收集資料、寫文章、做圖片、剪影片，非專業根本做不出來；現在憑多個 AI，一個人能完成以前一個小團隊的事（宣傳海報、創意影片）。企業角度：以前 5 個人完成的工作，現在 2 個人用 AI 就能完成，剩下 3 個人的價值去哪？企業不一定裁員，但減少招聘就能改變就業市場。

他以前做 HR，認為現在大學生非常難：以前只需要比另一個大學生優秀；現在要比 AI 優秀企業才考慮用你，大學生沒工作經驗、社會履歷不豐富，「可能還不如 AI」，就業環境非常糟糕。當每個人都有高效生產工具，效率本身不再稀缺，稀缺的是審美、判斷力、對行業的認知——AI 寫的文章需要你給提示、給角度，沒有履歷就沒法用好 AI。AI 也替代不了他在幣圈的經歷、和社區之間的信任關係。未來知識越來越便宜，重要的是個人的判斷能力和承擔能力。

他的建議就一句話：積極擁抱 AI。有人排斥，覺得搶了飯碗或不夠聰明而不去用；但用上之後會發現非常好用，不主動擁抱肯定被 AI 淘汰，主動擁抱效率可能是幾十倍提升。他現在一天能生成十幾張、幾百張圖片，寫十幾篇文章打造個人 IP，以前一天最多寫一兩篇文章、一張圖片都做不出來；省下的時間可以跟社區互動。

1. The crypto winter: this one is different

rtsky7: The infrastructure is all there; what's missing is a reason to go up. He believes this winter differs considerably from previous ones: the industry does not lack infrastructure or channels for capital to enter (spot ETFs, ever more trading infrastructure, participation by traditional financial institutions), yet many assets still lack a reason to keep rising. He gives three reasons:

  1. Severe diversion of capital. Crypto used to promise very high growth expectations; now people look around and find AI can be traded too, and US stocks and robotics are also very large investment themes.
  2. Oversupply inside the industry. There are more and more projects and more and more tokens, but projects that genuinely create revenue and have real users have not grown in proportion.
  3. Investors have become desensitized to narratives. In the past, you could write a whitepaper, pitch a vision, and people would pay for the dream; now everyone only cares whether you are actually making money and what return investing in your project will bring in the future. He sees this as a good thing — a reshuffling of crypto's entire business model: people used to think about how to create wealth by issuing tokens; now they think about how to create wealth first and then distribute it through tokens.

The directions he watches are exchange payments, stablecoins and tokenized US stocks — areas with at least relatively clear real demand. He believes the most worthwhile thing for HTX DAO to explore is how to turn community participation, ecosystem building and real value into a long-term positive cycle.

Dada: Crypto is taking over traditional finance's flow. He relayed a set of data-analysis figures (unverified): over roughly the past year or so, exchanges such as Binance have listed stock tokens, and around the Jensen Huang return event, crypto took on roughly USD 400 million-plus of stock-token trading — already close to about a quarter of the entire market and more than twice the second-place player; SpaceX perpetuals traded 1.957 billion USDT in a single day, already about 14.9% of SpaceX's spot share trading volume. His conclusion: a large amount of crypto capital is already participating, directly or indirectly, in traditional financial stock trading; and the convenience of exchanges trading 24 hours a day, all year round, may in the future draw traditional stock investors over.

Carl: Capital started flowing into US stocks and AI back in September–October 2025. He sees AI stocks as the sun and BTC and ETH as the moon — the capital has been sucked away. In 2026, Web3 is left with two narratives: AI Agents forming real revenue on-chain with Web3 (he says Sun Ge talked about this narrative early on), and RWA tokenization. He says there were hundreds of thousands of AI Agents running on-chain in July–August (his own figures, unverified) — a market that is growing. BTC has been cut in half from 120k to around 55k and has fallen for two straight days recently; at the Bitcoin Asia conference in Hong Kong on August 29, CZ was still talking about kicking off the next bull market, and now nobody dares say that. US stocks are not normal either: Anthropic's USD 1.5–2 trillion mega-IPO is the first of its kind in human history; crypto is now tied to capital flows, Wall Street macro data and the US unemployment rate — it no longer pumps just because some bullish news comes out.

His own trading this year: AI is the core position, Web3 is a new position he opened this year; what he bought is relatively steady (small positions in UNI, ZEC and the like), and not losing money is good enough; the key is not what he is bullish on, but entering at the right time and exiting at the right time. He believes now is actually a good time to build positions — connecting traditional finance with Crypto is the dream state insiders have wanted for years; it's just that the other side is not doing well either right now.

Xiaodu: The change in conversation topics is what the winter feels like. She is a former journalist who entered crypto about a year ago, at last year's Huobi Festival. Her observation: when she first joined, everyone talked about positions, data, opportunities, where BTC was heading and which sector had new growth; recently, most of the messages people send her are asking her to debunk rumors — layoffs, team developments, debunking rumors for Sun Ge — gossip has become the hot topic. When looking at new projects, people used to ask "is there an airdrop, is there still a chance if I get in now"; now more people are in wait-and-see mode, first checking whether the project can survive; the standard of judgment has shifted from growth and imagination to costs and survival horizon. She believes the winter is not just falling coin prices — it is the market demanding something more concrete.

Dada's summary: From "dream multiple" to looking at real substance. Crypto used to talk about the "dream multiple" — the bigger the dream, the bigger the market cap; now it is becoming clearer and clearer who is useful and who can actually deliver, and that is a good thing. Stock tokenization will first run on centralized venues, on-chain derivative finance models will rise in the future, and Crypto's advantages of openness and programmability will gradually stand out. He added one more line at the end: to a certain extent, the current winter is not a crypto winter but an economic winter — global finance and global stock markets are all in winter.

2. The AI layoff wave: The US is already very pessimistic; in China it is still an undercurrent

Carl: 2027 is a relatively certain trend. He says Silicon Valley has always been aggressively pessimistic, and that Microsoft and Google have sent emails telling staff "come in if you want to; if not, take the severance" (as relayed by him); in China, cost-cutting and efficiency drives have been going nonstop since 2023, and this AI wave is more severe, but it has not fully erupted yet — smiles on the surface, undercurrents underneath; Pony Ma said a few days ago that they need to fight a tough battle (as relayed by him). His judgment: the AI layoff wave will erupt directly along with the global trend, and 2027 is a relatively certain trend.

He also raised a paradox: many people have no work to do, yet everyone wants everything to be unmanned (driverless vehicles, jobs replaced); if nobody earns a wage, nobody consumes — nobody in the world has solved this problem yet. He says that at the national level AI holds the status of a strategic nuclear weapon, so technology development comes first and livelihoods are pushed back; by 2027, the way AI is organized may change wholesale (he cited a claim by "Lianhong", source unconfirmed).

Designers have already been replaced; programmers and mathematicians are next in line. Carl says that after Google's Nano Banana model came out in 2024, designers were replaced and the industry basically disappeared, leaving only marginal needs like Photoshop work and ID photos; programmers being replaced is also imminent (his judgment); and within three years, mathematicians will be engineered too — Anthropic and OpenAI solved math problems once thought to need 50–100 years in just ten or twenty days (his claim, unverified). He believes there are two kinds of people who will not be replaced in the future: those who build ecosystems and have extensive personal networks, and those with outstanding all-round talent.

3. What can ordinary people do: Using AI and using AI well are two different things

Dehu (a liberal-arts graduate turned developer): Start using it, but don't overestimate yourself. He uses AI to deliver client projects and marketing tools for vertical industries. His experience: writing copy, making PPTs, spreadsheets and graphics is effortless for AI tools; people who can't use AI tools will be replaced by people who can; after a company lays people off, the ones who remain are those in roles AI cannot replace, plus those who have mastered AI tools (one person doing the work of two or three). At his previous company he pushed his subordinates to use AI, and in the end keeping just one or two people was enough; when hiring now he also requires candidates to have mastered AI tools, otherwise they rank last or don't even get an interview.

His advice: start as early as possible with the most basic tools; you don't need to understand the principles, but you do need to know how to get things done with them — ideally understanding a bit of the principles too; once you've mastered the tools, you don't necessarily have to work for someone else — you can make money from your own projects. But he cautions: an OPC (one-person company) is not for everyone — it is very unrealistic for many fresh graduates and people with no AI experience to do an OPC; OPC communities have been set up all over the country but sit empty, a strange phenomenon he has observed.

Carl: Using AI and using AI well are two different things. His example: after the Claude Code source code leaked in April, many people on GitHub rewrote it in Rust, thinking the performance would be better; but liberal-arts graduates have a much weaker grasp of requirements and of software engineering — they don't know how to do testing, only functional testing, thinking that if it feels comfortable it's fine, which doesn't reach industrial grade. The people who can truly run an OPC are those whose ability already exceeds half a team, who already have a reputation in the industry, and who, before they even started, already had networks, knew the demand and knew who to go to. Those born after 2005 are even more anxious than those born after 2000; very many have dropped out of school to start businesses and are constantly seizing opportunities (his observation).

Dada: What AI cannot take away is the feel for demand. He believes mathematicians are anxious too now, but what AI cannot replace is a person's feel for demand — understanding the world, articulating demand clearly, judging whether what was built actually meets the demand; these perceptual things are what AI does not have; observation and judgment will be very important abilities in the future. The profession of prompt engineer will definitely be solved by AI itself (people just need to state things clearly in normal language); Claude is already doing "state clearly what tool you want, and it builds it and installs it on your phone/computer", with security and the like also handled by AI; but "what is needed, how to state it clearly, and how to judge whether it really did it" matter a great deal.

rtsky7: Embrace AI — the efficiency gain can be dozens of times. He is a freelancer fully committed to Web3. His experience: doing self-media used to mean collecting materials, writing articles, making images and editing videos yourself — a non-professional simply couldn't do it; now, with several AIs, one person can do what used to take a small team (promotional posters, creative videos). From a company's perspective: work that used to take 5 people can now be done by 2 people with AI — where does the value of the remaining 3 people go? Companies may not necessarily lay people off, but hiring less can change the job market on its own.

In the future knowledge will get cheaper and cheaper; what matters is an individual's judgment and ability to take responsibility.
