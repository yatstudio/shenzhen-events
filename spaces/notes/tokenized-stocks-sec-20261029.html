会议纪要：代币化股票合法化——谁赢得 SEC 的五年窗口？

来源：X Space 回放录音本地转写（faster-whisper，英文逐字稿 1,132 行） 整理：中文纪要，结论先行；以下所有说法均为发言人现场自述，未经核实，不构成投资建议。

场次信息

  • 原标题：Tokenized Stocks Go Legal: Who Wins The SEC's 5-Year Window?
  • 主持人：Justin Roberty（@wenaltseason）
  • 时间：2026-10-29（Space 已结束；场内提到 10 月 28 日 DTCC 上线、临近万圣节）
  • 时长：1 小时 05 分 59 秒
  • 参与：18,237 人
  • 链接：https://x.com/i/spaces/1yxBePXvXaLJN
  • 嘉宾：Kevin（Palladium，代币化房地产）、Ari（XY Labs 首席运营官）、Matt（3Bet Media）、Crypto Walker（Anid，Solana 链上银行）、CoinTulip（听众提问）

一句话结论

SEC 给了五年试验期，但全场嘉宾的共识是：赢家大概率还是华尔街，其次是交易所，普通投资者排最后——技术层变快变便宜了，但监管结构没变；真正的悬念是五年后链上交易量能否超过链下，以及发行人有没有权否决自家股票被代币化。

核心结论

  1. SEC 五年窗口是临时豁免，不是永久修法：9 月 17 日「创新豁免」允许合规平台用区块链自动做市商（AMM）交易代币化美股，无需注册为传统交易所；试验期至 2031 年 9 月，SEC 在此期间考虑长期规则。
  2. DTCC 已下场，规模碾压加密原生市场：10 月 28 日 DTCC 代币化服务上线，首批 34 只合资格证券、约 30 万亿美元市值；摩根大通、花旗、美银、摩根士丹利、瑞银等 20 家机构已签约，跑在 Canton Network 和私有 Vesu 链上。主持人提醒：30 万亿是「可代币化市值」，不是已代币化金额。
  3. 发行人正在大规模说不：微软、Alphabet、英特尔、甲骨文、IBM 等反对自家股票以 OKX / ISE 代币化形式交易；截至 10 月 28 日已有 33 家发行人提出异议，包括摩根大通、可口可乐、戴尔——原定 62 家名单中近半已退出。嘉宾为此吵了一架：发行人到底有没有权管你怎么处置自己买的股票。
  4. 散户得到的是「更好用的传统金融」，不是主权：Ari 的判断——交易更快、可能 24 小时、费用可能更低，但你大概率还是不能像转 ETH 那样把一股代币化特斯拉直接转给朋友（KYC 挡着）；监管结构不变，只是更高配的 TradFi。
  5. 三家路数不同：Robinhood（用户基数大、年轻用户、加密+股票交叉交易）、Ondo（发行+基建，两头下注，还与 DTCC 合作）、OKX（AMM 交易场所+稳定币结算，并与纽交所传有合资）。Kevin 押 OKX，Ari 押 Robinhood。
  6. 你买到的不是股票本身，是包装：Crypto Walker 直言——底层资产由第三方持有，你拿到的是「在对方许可下使用的敞口」；发行人或托管方破产怎么办、投票权和分红归谁，细则全在小字条款里，现场没人能给出确定答案。

开场新闻梳理（主持人 Justin Roberty，00:01–00:07）

  • SEC 9 月 17 日创新豁免：合规平台五年内可交易代币化美股，无需注册为交易所；豁免是临时的，2031 年 9 月到期。
  • DTCC 10 月 28 日上线代币化服务：首批 34 只证券、约 30 万亿美元市值；20 家机构签约（摩根大通、花旗、美银、摩根士丹利、瑞银等），运行于 Canton Network 与私有 Vesu 区块链。
  • Ondo Finance 两头下注：一边与 DTCC 合作，一边扩自己的代币化股票产品。
  • Robinhood Chain 10 月 27 日消息：接入 Ripple 稳定币 RLUSD，新增 15 只代币化股票与 ETF，打通交易资产与链上支付。
  • 发行人反对：微软、Alphabet、英特尔、甲骨文、IBM 等反对 OKX / ISE 代币化形式；截至 10 月 28 日 33 家发行人已披露异议（含摩根大通、可口可乐、戴尔），62 家原定名单中约 30 家退出、近半。
  • 大卖点是 24/7 交易：稳定币结算+链上流动性池，突破华尔街交易时段；但连续交易对定价、流动性和投资者保护提出新问题。
  • 主持人定调：这五年是各方证明哪种模式行得通的窗口期；豁免「看起来很实在，同时又留了退路」。

分发言人观点

Justin Roberty（主持人）

  • 反复追问的其实是同一个问题：赢家由谁定——是最好的技术，还是最会跟传统金融打交道的人。他的判断是后者：关系正在定义赢家和输家。
  • 用自己当年帮 AccuWeather 与迪士尼合作的经历打比方：跟巨头「合作」从来不是平等合作，大多数时候是在哄巨头开心；这在某种程度上「颠覆」了加密空间的去中心化理想。
  • 对本届政府「挑选赢家」直言不舒服：钱进来了、行业被认可了，这是好事；但行政力量如此明确地选边，会成为创新的障碍。他自述「心情复杂」。
  • 举了 Robinhood 上出现 SpaceX IPO 相关代币的例子：未经公司同意就有人把它的股票代币化，「想想就觉得离谱」。
  • 提醒听众：他本人是编剧硕士出身，「不是投资建议」。

Kevin（Palladium，代币化房地产）

  • 对豁免整体乐观：一定会带来更多关注、打破壁垒；自己的项目目标是让普通人更容易进出房地产投资，这波只会帮到这个事业。
  • 买家画像：不在币圈内部，而在圈外——会把以前远离加密的人带进来。
  • 对散户的实际变化：不用再开券商账户（点名嘉信理财式账户），在交易所里就能买进头部股票；设有限额是好事，说明在「试水过渡」。
  • 预言分化：零售经纪市场会逐步瓦解，只剩机构资金（401K 等）继续用传统技术。
  • 押注 OKX 而非 Robinhood：理由是 OKX 已与纽交所成立合资企业；并提到 HYPE 当天因相关消息大涨。
  • 稳定币是必需品：普通人用稳定币计价远比用比特币或以太坊计价直观；是整个代币化股票市场的「底漆」和计价基准单位；稳定币赢家「已经基本定下来了」。
  • 全球化：门已经裂开一条缝；虽然仍有代币化比例上限、部分交易所在美国仍被地理屏蔽，但方向是向全世界开放；更多参与者进来也会带来更多波动，以及被极高净值人士公开操纵的风险。

Ari（XY Labs 首席运营官）

  • 技术层变了，主权没变：豁免让技术更高效，是对过去十年加密技术的一次「展示」；但如果你想像转 ETH 一样把一股代币化特斯拉直接转给朋友，KYC 就过不去——「主权收益」大概率落空。汽车代币同理：除非车管部门认这个 token，否则转不出去。
  • 最大赢家是交易所和想进 RWA 的创业公司；终端用户最可能被晾在一边：体验会变好（比如 24 小时交易），但监管结构不变，本质是「更高配的传统金融」，不是真正的链上股票 DeFi。
  • DTCC 的 30 万亿关键在于是不是「新钱」：如果只是把已有账户里的钱换个技术搬过来，对普通人好处有限；只有真正带来新资金、同时让交易更快更便宜，才算全赢。传统券商过去没有动力自我革新，现在是被逼着变快，否则别人会替他们变。
  • Robinhood 位置最好：用户基数大、用户年轻、已试过在 Robinhood 内交易加密；最能吃到「既想拿币又想拿股票」的交叉交易人群——比如用市值前 100 的币直接换特斯拉，反之亦然。
  • 发行人否决权问题上站在所有权一边：买车之后车企不能规定你卖给谁；买手机之后「维修权」之争也是这个逻辑。持有 10 股微软就是主权拥有，发行人凭什么规定你能不能把它包成 token？称这种自上而下的管制「在我看来是反美国的」。但同时承认：投票权、分红归谁，必须在包装条款里写清楚——比如 Meta 股票本身就有不同投票权等级，透明是前提。
  • 2031 年展望：不会是去中心化版本获胜；更可能出现三分天下——机构大额转账维持传统方式；出现一批「类区块链但中心化」的高性能系统，成为新的中心化骨干；去中心化版本只占一小部分。按交易笔数算，链上可能赢；按资金量算，未必。
  • AI 是未被讨论的变量：链上转账让 AI 交易变得极其容易、快速且便宜，未来一波 AI 交易员（机构、商业、个人）会显著推高交易量；监管会不会限制 AI 交易、限制到什么程度，将左右交易量最终流向哪里。

Matt（3Bet Media）

  • 五年「窗口长得很」：以加密的速度，五年足够发生翻天覆地的变化，尤其叠加 AI 时代；下一个方法论将与代币化资产和 AI 有关。
  • 预计会出现「代理式交易」（agentic transactions）与投资、交易结合的有趣产品和市场组合。
  • 对「五年后会不会被收回」：没人知道；但真正的加密建设者总会找到办法继续建。

Crypto Walker（Anid，Solana 链上银行，做了三年）

  • 两个世界在碰撞：一边是 Web3 原生创业者，很多人根本没去过华尔街、也不在乎华尔街，自己做自己的事；另一边是华尔街大玩家，看到列车已开走、不得不跟上。两者都会继续存在；他押注独立思考者带来的创新。
  • 政治绕不开：加密与政治、全球大事过去几年已深度绑定，当前这波推动与美国政治局势（包括中期选举）有关。
  • 最赚钱的环节：发行（issuance）会起来——明年很多玩家要发稳定币、搭平台、推新工具；交易平台也会因新用户涌入而受益；但坦言「很难准确押注」。
  • 你真正拥有什么：代币化股票是「包装」——第三方持有底层资产，给你一个使用和标记的凭证；你不是最终所有者，只是经许可使用。托管方可能倒闭、可能关门，补偿比例要看条款细则；类比银行存款保险（如欧元区 10 万欧元上限），不是全额兜底；「读小字条款」。
  • MiCA 教训：欧洲 MiCA 实行两年，纸面上很好，执行起来却让很多项目干脆不去欧洲或逃离欧洲——现金储备等要求对初创公司过高，「地下室创业者基本不可能」在欧盟合规运营。结论：没有监管不行，过度监管也不行，要找平衡点；希望美国走出更好的版本。
  • 全球化视角：欧洲用户大多仍用美元稳定币（Circle 等产品占主导），美国市场开放后欧洲用户很可能直接用美国平台；这会是全球性的事，但美国受益最大。
  • 2031 年展望：希望技术赶上、实现谈了十年的大规模采用；但不会出现「全部上链、无银行」的世界——两类体系将共存，链上交易会成为主要的去处之一。

CoinTulip（听众提问，00:45–00:48）

  • 提出全场唯一没人答得上来的问题：如果代币化股票放在链上、自己托管，被盗了怎么办？ 是像丢币一样「币就没了」，还是证券法和底层股权结构能提供真正的追回途径？
  • 主持人承认不知道现阶段监管会怎么处理；提到 Saffron 与 Nexus Mutual 的保险合作（称对方是该领域领先者、此前多次赔付，包括本月早些时候一次漏洞事件），但没有人能就代币化股票被盗的规则给出答案。
  • CoinTulip 进一步追问：如果股票、大宗商品、房地产全都要上链（提到迪拜已有代币化房地产试点），未来十年「万物代币化」之后，盗窃问题到底怎么处理？现场无人接招，话题被带过。

全场四次关键交锋

1. 发行人有没有权否决代币化？（00:32–00:41）

  • 主持人与 Kevin 先表态：发行人应该有控制权，未经公司同意就代币化其股票「很奇怪」，以后会有法律问题。
  • Ari 杀回马枪：买了股票就是你的财产，车企管不了你把车卖给谁；发行人管你能不能把股票包成 token，是自上而下的中心化管制，「在我看来是反美国的」；发行股票本身就是「交出对公司的一部分控制」。
  • 折中点：不管谁有权否决，投票权、分红、法律保护必须在条款里写清楚；否则代币持有者就是「二等持有人」。

2. 你买到的到底是什么？（00:34–00:37）

  • Crypto Walker：第三方持有底层资产，你得到的是敞口+使用许可；托管方破产、跑路都有可能，补偿看细则。
  • 主持人追问：那投票权、分红、法律保护一样吗？如果不一样，就是两种等级的持有人。
  • Ari 补充：不同股票本就权利不同（如 Meta 的多级投票权），关键是透明。

3. 谁是最大赢家？（速答环节，00:56–00:58）

  • Kevin：华尔街——因为他们有权力制定规则、让自己成为赢家。
  • Ari：华尔街第一、加密交易所第二、用户第三——「有黄金的人定规则，规则让他们得到更多黄金。」
  • Crypto Walker：华尔街在主导游戏，小鱼只能吃剩下的碎屑。

4. 到 2031 年，链上交易会超过链下吗？（00:58–01:03）

  • Kevin：零售交易上链会更多，但机构交易量是否上链「很难说」。
  • Ari：三分天下；按笔数链上可能赢，按金额未必；AI 交易员的加入会是重要变量。
  • Crypto Walker：两类体系共存，但链上会成为主要的去处之一并持续增长。

一句話結論

SEC 給了五年試驗期，但全場嘉賓的共識是：贏家大概率還是華爾街，其次是交易所，普通投資者排最後——技術層變快變便宜了，但監管結構沒變；真正的懸念是五年後鏈上交易量能否超過鏈下，以及發行人有沒有權否決自家股票被代幣化。

核心結論

  1. SEC 五年窗口是臨時豁免，不是永久修法：9 月 17 日「創新豁免」允許合規平台用區塊鏈自動做市商（AMM）交易代幣化美股，無需註冊為傳統交易所；試驗期至 2031 年 9 月，SEC 在此期間考慮長期規則。
  2. DTCC 已下場，規模碾壓加密原生市場：10 月 28 日 DTCC 代幣化服務上線，首批 34 隻合資格證券、約 30 萬億美元市值；摩根大通、花旗、美銀、摩根士丹利、瑞銀等 20 家機構已簽約，跑在 Canton Network 和私有 Vesu 鏈上。主持人提醒：30 萬億是「可代幣化市值」，不是已代幣化金額。
  3. 發行人正在大規模說不：微軟、Alphabet、英特爾、甲骨文、IBM 等反對自家股票以 OKX / ISE 代幣化形式交易；截至 10 月 28 日已有 33 家發行人提出異議，包括摩根大通、可口可樂、戴爾——原定 62 家名單中近半已退出。嘉賓為此吵了一架：發行人到底有沒有權管你怎麼處置自己買的股票。
  4. 散戶得到的是「更好用的傳統金融」，不是主權：Ari 的判斷——交易更快、可能 24 小時、費用可能更低，但你大概率還是不能像轉 ETH 那樣把一股代幣化特斯拉直接轉給朋友（KYC 擋著）；監管結構不變，只是更高配的 TradFi。

One-Sentence Conclusion

The SEC has given a five-year trial period, but the consensus among all the guests was: the winner will most likely still be Wall Street, followed by exchanges, with ordinary investors last — the technology layer has become faster and cheaper, but the regulatory structure has not changed; the real suspense is whether on-chain trading volume can exceed off-chain volume in five years, and whether issuers have the right to veto tokenization of their own shares.

Core Conclusions

  1. The SEC's five-year window is a temporary exemption, not a permanent change in the law: The September 17 "innovation exemption" allows compliant platforms to trade tokenized U.S. stocks using blockchain automated market makers (AMMs) without registering as a traditional exchange; the trial period runs until September 2031, during which the SEC will consider long-term rules.
  2. DTCC has entered the field, at a scale that dwarfs the crypto-native market: On October 28, DTCC's tokenization service went live, with an initial 34 eligible securities and roughly $30 trillion in market capitalization; 20 institutions including JPMorgan, Citigroup, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley and UBS have signed up, running on Canton Network and the private Vesu chain. The host cautioned: the $30 trillion is "tokenizable market capitalization," not the amount already tokenized.
  3. Issuers are saying no on a large scale: Microsoft, Alphabet, Intel, Oracle, IBM and others oppose their shares being traded in tokenized form via OKX / ISE; as of October 28, 33 issuers had raised objections, including JPMorgan, Coca-Cola and Dell — nearly half of the original list of 62 had withdrawn. The guests argued over this: do issuers have any right to control how you dispose of shares you bought yourself?
  4. What retail investors get is "better traditional finance," not sovereignty: Ari's judgment — trading will be faster, possibly 24 hours, and fees may be lower, but you most likely still cannot transfer a share of tokenized Tesla directly to a friend the way you transfer ETH (KYC blocks it); the regulatory structure is unchanged, it is just TradFi with higher specifications.
  5. Three different approaches: Robinhood (large user base, young users, cross-trading between crypto and stocks), Ondo (issuance + infrastructure, betting on both sides, and also partnering with DTCC), OKX (AMM trading venue + stablecoin settlement, and reportedly in a joint venture with the NYSE). Kevin backed OKX, Ari backed Robinhood.
  6. What you buy is not the stock itself, it is a wrapper: Crypto Walker said bluntly — the underlying asset is held by a third party, and what you get is "exposure that you use with their permission"; what happens if the issuer or custodian goes bankrupt, and who gets the voting rights and dividends, are all in the fine print, and no one at the session could give a definite answer.

Opening News Roundup (Host Justin Roberty, 00:01–00:07)

  • SEC innovation exemption on September 17: compliant platforms can trade tokenized U.S. stocks for five years without registering as an exchange; the exemption is temporary and expires in September 2031.
  • DTCC tokenization service launched October 28: an initial 34 securities, about $30 trillion in market capitalization; 20 institutions signed up (JPMorgan, Citigroup, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley, UBS, etc.), running on Canton Network and the private Vesu blockchain.
  • Ondo Finance is betting on both sides: partnering with DTCC on one side, and expanding its own tokenized stock products on the other.
  • Robinhood Chain news on October 27: integrating Ripple's stablecoin RLUSD, adding 15 tokenized stocks and ETFs, and connecting trading assets with on-chain payments.
  • Issuer opposition: Microsoft, Alphabet, Intel, Oracle, IBM and others oppose the OKX / ISE tokenized form; as of October 28, 33 issuers had disclosed objections (including JPMorgan, Coca-Cola, Dell), and about 30 of the original list of 62 had withdrawn — nearly half.
  • The big selling point is 24/7 trading: stablecoin settlement + on-chain liquidity pools, breaking out of Wall Street trading hours; but continuous trading raises new questions for pricing, liquidity and investor protection.
  • The host's framing: these five years are a window for all sides to prove which model works; the exemption "looks very real, while also leaving a way out."

Views by Speaker

Justin Roberty (Host)

  • What he kept asking was really the same question: who decides the winners — the best technology, or the people best at dealing with traditional finance? His judgment was the latter: relationships are defining winners and losers.
  • He drew an analogy from his own experience helping AccuWeather partner with Disney: "partnering" with a giant is never an equal partnership; most of the time you are keeping the giant happy; to some extent this "subverts" the decentralization ideal of the crypto space.
  • He said plainly that he was uncomfortable with this administration "picking winners": money has come in and the industry has been recognized, which is good; but such explicit side-taking by administrative power will become an obstacle to innovation. He described himself as having "mixed feelings."
  • He cited the example of SpaceX IPO-related tokens appearing on Robinhood: someone tokenized its stock without the company's consent — "just thinking about it feels absurd."
  • He reminded listeners: he himself has a master's degree in screenwriting, and this is "not investment advice."

Kevin (Palladium, tokenized real estate)

  • Overall optimistic about the exemption: it will definitely bring more attention and break down barriers; his own project's goal is to make it easier for ordinary people to get in and out of real estate investment, and this wave will only help that cause.
  • Buyer profile: not inside the crypto circle, but outside it — it will bring in people who previously stayed away from crypto.
  • The practical change for retail investors: no need to open a brokerage account anymore (he named Schwab-style accounts); you can buy leading stocks right inside an exchange; having limits is a good thing, it shows this is a "testing-the-waters transition."

  • Backing OKX rather than Robinhood: because OKX has already formed a joint venture with the NYSE; he also mentioned that HYPE rose sharply that day on related news.
  • Stablecoins are a necessity: for ordinary people, pricing in stablecoins is far more intuitive than pricing in Bitcoin or Ethereum; they are the "primer" and unit of account for the entire tokenized stock market; the stablecoin winners "are basically already decided."
  • Globalization: the door has cracked open; although there are still caps on tokenization ratios and some exchanges are still geo-blocked in the U.S., the direction is toward opening to the whole world; more participants coming in will also bring more volatility, and the risk of public manipulation by ultra-high-net-worth individuals.

Ari (Chief Operating Officer, XY Labs)

  • The technology layer has changed, sovereignty has not: The exemption makes the technology more efficient, a "showcase" of the past decade of crypto technology; but if you want to transfer a share of tokenized Tesla directly to a friend the way you transfer ETH, KYC will stop you — the "sovereignty gains" will most likely come to nothing. Car tokens are the same: unless the DMV recognizes the token, you cannot transfer it out.
  • The biggest winners are exchanges and startups wanting to enter RWA; end users are most likely to be left out: The experience will improve (for example, 24-hour trading), but the regulatory structure is unchanged; in essence it is "TradFi with higher specifications," not real on-chain stock DeFi.
  • The key question about DTCC's $30 trillion is whether it is "new money": If it is just moving money already in existing accounts over with a different technology, the benefit to ordinary people is limited; only if it truly brings in new funds while making trading faster and cheaper is it a complete win. Traditional brokers had no incentive to reinvent themselves in the past; now they are being forced to move faster, or others will do it for them.
  • Robinhood is best positioned: large user base, young users, and it has already tried trading crypto inside Robinhood; it is best placed to capture the cross-trading crowd that "wants both coins and stocks" — for example, swapping a top-100 coin directly for Tesla, and vice versa.
  • On the issuer veto question, he sides with ownership: After you buy a car, the carmaker cannot dictate who you sell it to; the "right to repair" fight after buying a phone follows the same logic. Holding 10 shares of Microsoft is sovereign ownership — on what grounds can the issuer dictate whether you can wrap it into a token? He called this kind of top-down control "un-American in my view." But he also acknowledged: who gets voting rights and dividends must be written clearly in the wrapper terms — for example, Meta shares themselves have different classes of voting rights; transparency is the prerequisite.
  • Outlook for 2031: It will not be the decentralized version that wins; more likely there will be a three-way split — large institutional transfers remain traditional; a batch of "blockchain-like but centralized" high-performance systems emerge as a new centralized backbone; and the decentralized version accounts for only a small share. By number of transactions, on-chain may win; by amount of money, not necessarily.
  • AI is the undiscussed variable: On-chain transfers make AI trading extremely easy, fast and cheap, and a coming wave of AI traders (institutional, commercial, personal) will significantly push up trading volume; whether regulators restrict AI trading, and to what extent, will determine where trading volume ultimately flows.

Matt (3Bet Media)

  • Five years is "a hell of a long window": at crypto speed, five years is enough for earth-shaking change, especially layered with the AI era; the next methodology will be related to tokenized assets and AI.

Crypto Walker (Anid, an on-chain bank on Solana, three years in)

  • Two worlds are colliding: On one side are Web3-native founders, many of whom have never been to Wall Street and do not care about Wall Street, doing their own thing; on the other side are the big Wall Street players, who see the train has left and have to catch up. Both will continue to exist; he bets on the innovation brought by independent thinkers.
  • Politics cannot be avoided: Crypto has become deeply bound up with politics and global events over the past few years, and the current push is related to the U.S. political situation (including the midterm elections).
  • The most profitable link: Issuance will take off — next year many players will issue stablecoins, build platforms and push new tools; trading platforms will also benefit from the influx of new users; but he admitted it is "hard to bet accurately."
  • What do you actually own: A tokenized stock is a "wrapper" — a third party holds the underlying asset and gives you a credential to use and mark it; you are not the ultimate owner, only using it with permission. The custodian may go bankrupt or shut down, and the compensation ratio depends on the fine print; he analogized it to bank deposit insurance (such as the €100,000 cap in the eurozone), which is not full coverage; "read the fine print."
  • The MiCA lesson: Europe's MiCA has been in force for two years; on paper it is very good, but in practice it has led many projects to simply not go to Europe or to flee Europe — requirements such as cash reserves are too high for startups, and "basement founders basically cannot" operate compliantly in the EU. Conclusion: no regulation does not work, and excessive regulation does not work either; a balance must be found; he hopes the U.S. produces a better version.

  • Outlook for 2031: He hopes technology catches up and achieves the mass adoption talked about for ten years; but there will not be a world of "everything on-chain, no banks" — the two systems will coexist, and on-chain trading will become one of the main destinations.

Four Key Exchanges in the Session

1. Do issuers have the right to veto tokenization? (00:32–00:41)

  • The host and Kevin spoke first: issuers should have control, and tokenizing their stock without the company's consent is "very strange" and will lead to legal problems later.
  • Ari pushed back: once you buy a stock it is your property; a carmaker cannot control who you sell your car to; an issuer controlling whether you can wrap a stock into a token is top-down centralized control, "un-American in my view"; issuing stock itself is "giving up part of the control over the company."
  • The middle ground: regardless of who has veto power, voting rights, dividends and legal protections must be written clearly in the terms; otherwise token holders are "second-class holders."

2. What exactly are you buying? (00:34–00:37)

  • Crypto Walker: a third party holds the underlying asset, and what you get is exposure + a license to use it; custodian bankruptcy or absconding are both possible, and compensation depends on the fine print.
  • The host pressed: then are voting rights, dividends and legal protections the same? If not, there are two classes of holders.
  • Ari added: different stocks inherently have different rights (such as Meta's multi-class voting rights); the key is transparency.

3. Who is the biggest winner? (Rapid-fire round, 00:56–00:58)

  • Kevin: Wall Street — because they have the power to make the rules and make themselves the winners.
  • Ari: Wall Street first, crypto exchanges second, users third — "The people with the gold make the rules, and the rules let them get more gold."
  • Crypto Walker: Wall Street is running the game, and the small fish can only eat the crumbs left over.

4. By 2031, will on-chain trading exceed off-chain? (00:58–01:03)

  • Kevin: More retail trading will go on-chain, but whether institutional trading volume goes on-chain is "hard to say."
  • Ari: A three-way split; by number of transactions on-chain may win, by amount not necessarily; the entry of AI traders will be an important variable.
  • Crypto Walker: The two systems will coexist, but on-chain will become one of the main destinations and keep growing.
