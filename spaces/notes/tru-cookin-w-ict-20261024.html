会议纪要——TRU - Cookin w ICT（ICT 交易专场）

来源：X Space 回放录音本地转写（英文逐字稿，全程 5 小时 04 分 10 秒，4,967 行），纪要为中文整理。 重要声明：以下所有观点、数字与市场判断均为发言人现场自述，未经独立核实，不构成任何投资建议。发言人归属按对话上下文推断，无法确定处标注为"某位发言者/提问者"。

一、结论先行（核心要点）

  1. 少做交易、做对少数几次就够了。Kitt 开场即点题：周四的 expansion 证明，不需要大量交易，只需要在正确的时间做对极少数交易。Pit Munch 本周只做了 4 笔原油交易（周一至周三），却是她自 2023 年开始交易以来"最好的一周"。
  2. Michael（ICT）本场的最大转向信号：从纯日内转向留"跑手"（runners）。他自述过去几年一直回避隔夜风险，但认为中期选举前后波动将放大、出现可持续数日到两周的大级别行情；计划用日内模型进场、75% 仓位日内了结、留 25% 做 1–2 周的波段，并呼吁听众每天花 10–15 分钟从 5 分钟以下图表 zoom out 到 4 小时/1 小时/周线看方向。
  3. "独角兽模型"不存在。Michael 亲自澄清："There is no unicorn model"——独角兽只是他用来形容"一切条件完美对齐、周内最大一段行情"的形容词，不是固定参数的模型；既不限于"Breaker 内嵌 FVG"，也不限于市场制造商模型的第二阶段再分配/再积累。
  4. 流动性是第一课。对新手，Michael 的建议是：不要看实时行情，先用 2–4 周每天 20–30 分钟看静态图表，研究当日高低点为何形成、价格为何去扫前一日高低、缺口与 FVG；先懂"价格为何去某个位置"，再谈入场。不要用"五分钟速成"、不要贪多，每天只练一件事。
  5. "上膛的枪"（Loaded Gun）与"去读盘，不去交易"。Pit Munch 的转变是本场最完整的学员案例：2023 年每天交易约 20 次，从 NQ 的 10 点 scalp 做到 200–300 点，再转原油 CL；现在每天上图只为 tape reading（读盘），没有"满仓信号"（多个 confluence 全部对齐）就不出手，周四、周五看到"半把枪"的机会也果断放弃。
  6. 固定百分比回报、拒绝加杠杆治过度交易。针对过度交易问题，Michael 给出"每月 6%、每周 1.5%、每笔风险 0.5%"的框架：达标即停手，逐步加目标；并明确反对"提高杠杆让赢利变大"的想法，强调复利才是解法。
  7. Michael 对后市偏空、偏防御。据其自述：纳指看向 28,815 一带（12 月合约已跌破 9 月 16 日低点 29,005），黄金关注 3,900（跌破后或看 3,600s），原油则看多、认为会再创历史新高；中期选举后波动可能加剧，他本人目前"一周只做一到两个 setup"，以防守为主。
  8. 交易 90% 是心理战。全场反复出现的主题：不要把家人、伴侣、社交媒体的期待带进交易；不要用账单钱交易；给自己"允许亏损"的许可；把交易当生意、写交易日志、把模型浓缩到"一张名片背面能写完"。

2. ICT 方法论要点（据发言人现场表述）

  • 流动性优先（Draw on Liquidity）：一切分析的起点是"价格要去哪里"——前一日高低、时段高低、相对等高/等低（relative equal highs/lows）、缺口与 FVG 都是流动性池；价格先扫流动性、再反向走。
  • AMD / Power 3：积累（Accumulation）—操纵（Manipulation）—派发（Distribution），Pit Munch 的原油交易主框架。
  • PD 数组（PD Arrays）：订单块（Order Block）、FVG（Fair Value Gap / 公允价值缺口）、成交量失衡（Volume Imbalance）、Breaker、反转 FVG（Inversion FVG）、BISI/SIBI（买方/卖方失衡）。Michael 强调：没有任何一个 PD 数组天生优先于另一个，关键是"有没有足够的空间（range）到目标"；不要只盯一个拼图碎片。
  • 订单块的定义澄清：不是每个收阴 K 线都是订单块——只有当价格预期要向上走、且该 K 线的开盘价（改变状态交付，CSD）位于现价下方、提供回踩空间时，才构成看涨订单块；反之亦然。
  • 成交量失衡（Volume Imbalance）：所有 PD 数组中最"灵活"的一个，可被反复穿越；Michael 给的过滤规则是：一旦有 K 线实体在其上方收盘，就暂时不再参考它、改找其他 PD 数组（不代表它永远失效）。
  • 折价/溢价敏感度（Discount / Premium Sensitivity）与均值阈值（Mean Threshold, 50%）：看涨时希望 K 线实体留在 PD 数组上半部分（不跌破 50%）、影线可下探；看跌时相反。缺口最好保持"部分未填补"，填补后应立即恢复原方向，否则是转弱信号。另有"未能握手（failed to glad hand）"的概念：价格没有回到应回的位置再走，是强势/速度的体现。
  • Silver Bullet（银弹）：上午 10:00（美东）出现的第一个 FVG 是重点；多个提问者用它做模型，Michael 建议新手就从"只做 10 点银弹"或"只做开盘区间缺口到中点"起步。
  • 开盘区间缺口（Opening Range Gap, ORG）：9:30 开盘价与前一日 16:14（美东）收盘价之差；最常见的情形是价格走到缺口一半（50%）处；Pit Munch 的"影子"交易即源于 10 月 2 日非农讲座中"开盘区间缺口的影子"。
  • 周度画像（Weekly Profiles）与日/周 OHLC：Michael 自述多年前通过手绘每周 OHLC 折线图发现：每周的涨跌形态只有有限几种；结合经济日历，可预判哪一天最可能出现周内高点或低点、哪一天最可能出现大级别单边行情。"画像"不是 Market Profile，而是指一周内各日如何构成周线区间的通用形态。
  • 市场制造商模型（Market Maker Models）：买方模型/卖方模型；Michael 最看重的是"第二阶段再分配/再积累"（second stage redistribution / reaccumulation），称之为"钱最多的地方"；独角兽即指这一段。
  • 独角兽（Unicorn）澄清：不是模型，只是一个形容词；用来强调"方向、位置、时间、时段全部对齐的罕见完美 setup"；Michael 说他每年都会免费给出一个"只练这一个就够了"的模型。
  • Turtle Soup（龟汤）：扫掉一侧流动性（买方或卖方）后反向扩张；不一定是大洗盘，也可能只是扫到日内小摆动低点后回落到区间一半再继续原方向；关键在高时间框架的位置，不在扫本身。Michael 明确否定 CRT（他人改名版本）："Stop learning versions of my stuff renamed."
  • 链式保管（Chain of Custody）与状态转换（Change in State of Delivery, CSD）：价格从一个 PD 数组"移交"到下一个；Neil 的系统即围绕"保管控制（custodial control）在特定时间、特定位置出现 + 状态转换确认"建立。
  • Killzone 与宏观时段（Macros）：伦敦、纽约开盘及 10:00、15:15–15:45、15:50–16:10 等宏观窗口被多次提及；Michael 建议新手把"时间"作为第一过滤器——知道什么时候该坐在图表前、什么时候不必看盘。
  • "名片背面"模型：Michael 自述 2010 年在 BabyPips 写过"Here's my card"：一个交易员的全部计划应该能写在名片背面；Pit Munch 2023 年 3 月 19 日还按此作业画过自己的模型并贴出（50 分钟流动性、卖方、市场结构转变、独角兽 Breaker+FVG），Michael 回复"My model is a clock"。
  • 结算价开关：只为寻找 volume imbalance 而切换；两种设置都标出来，看价格尊重哪一个；不要为几美分的影线长度纠结"精度"。
  • 突破缺口（Breakaway Gap）：理想状态是完全不回踩、直接远离；一旦回踩，需看到真正的沉重（heaviness）与加速远离。

2. ICT 方法論要點（據發言人現場表述）

  • 流動性優先（Draw on Liquidity）：一切分析的起點是「價格要去哪裡」——前一日高低、時段高低、相對等高/等低（relative equal highs/lows）、缺口與 FVG 都是流動性池；價格先掃流動性、再反向走。
  • AMD / Power 3：累積（Accumulation）—操縱（Manipulation）—派發（Distribution），Pit Munch 的原油交易主框架。
  • PD 陣列（PD Arrays）：訂單塊（Order Block）、FVG（Fair Value Gap / 公允價值缺口）、成交量失衡（Volume Imbalance）、Breaker、反轉 FVG（Inversion FVG）、BISI/SIBI（買方/賣方失衡）。Michael 強調：沒有任何一個 PD 陣列天生優先於另一個，關鍵是「有沒有足夠的空間（range）到目標」；不要只盯一個拼圖碎片。
  • 訂單塊的定義澄清：不是每個收陰 K 線都是訂單塊——只有當價格預期要向上走、且該 K 線的開盤價（改變狀態交付，CSD）位於現價下方、提供回踩空間時，才構成看漲訂單塊；反之亦然。
  • 成交量失衡（Volume Imbalance）：所有 PD 陣列中最「靈活」的一個，可被反覆穿越；Michael 給的過濾規則是：一旦有 K 線實體在其上方收盤，就暫時不再參考它、改找其他 PD 陣列（不代表它永遠失效）。
  • 折價/溢價敏感度（Discount / Premium Sensitivity）與均值閾值（Mean Threshold, 50%）：看漲時希望 K 線實體留在 PD 陣列上半部分（不跌破 50%）、影線可下探；看跌時相反。缺口最好保持「部分未填補」，填補後應立即恢復原方向，否則是轉弱訊號。另有「未能握手（failed to glad hand）」的概念：價格沒有回到應回的位置再走，是強勢/速度的體現。
  • Silver Bullet（銀彈）：上午 10:00（美東）出現的第一個 FVG 是重點；多個提問者用它做模型，Michael 建議新手就從「只做 10 點銀彈」或「只做開盤區間缺口到中點」起步。
  • 開盤區間缺口（Opening Range Gap, ORG）：9:30 開盤價與前一日 16:14（美東）收盤價之差；最常見的情形是價格走到缺口一半（50%）處；Pit Munch 的「影子」交易即源於 10 月 2 日非農講座中「開盤區間缺口的影子」。
  • 週度畫像（Weekly Profiles）與日/週 OHLC：Michael 自述多年前透過手繪每週 OHLC 折線圖發現：每週的漲跌形態只有有限幾種；結合經濟日曆，可預判哪一天最可能出現週內高點或低點、哪一天最可能出現大級別單邊行情。「畫像」不是 Market Profile，而是指一週內各日如何構成週線區間的通用形態。
  • 市場製造商模型（Market Maker Models）：買方模型/賣方模型；Michael 最看重的是「第二階段再分配/再累積」（second stage redistribution / reaccumulation），稱之為「錢最多的地方」；獨角獸即指這一段。
  • 獨角獸（Unicorn）澄清：不是模型，只是一個形容詞；用來強調「方向、位置、時間、時段全部對齊的罕見完美 setup」；Michael 說他每年都會免費給出一個「只練這一個就夠了」的模型。
  • Turtle Soup（龜湯）：掃掉一側流動性（買方或賣方）後反向擴張；不一定是大洗盤，也可能只是掃到日內小擺動低點後回落到區間一半再繼續原方向；關鍵在高時間框架的位置，不在掃本身。Michael 明確否定 CRT（他人改名版本）：「Stop learning versions of my stuff renamed.」
  • 鏈式保管（Chain of Custody）與狀態轉換（Change in State of Delivery, CSD）：價格從一個 PD 陣列「移交」到下一個；Neil 的系統即圍繞「保管控制（custodial control）在特定時間、特定位置出現 + 狀態轉換確認」建立。
  • Killzone 與宏觀時段（Macros）：倫敦、紐約開盤及 10:00、15:15–15:45、15:50–16:10 等宏觀窗口被多次提及；Michael 建議新手把「時間」作為第一過濾器——知道什麼時候該坐在圖表前、什麼時候不必看盤。
  • 「名片背面」模型：Michael 自述 2010 年在 BabyPips 寫過「Here's my card」：一個交易員的全部計劃應該能寫在名片背面；Pit Munch 2023 年 3 月 19 日還按此作業畫過自己的模型並貼出（50 分鐘流動性、賣方、市場結構轉變、獨角獸 Breaker+FVG），Michael 回覆「My model is a clock」。
  • 結算價開關：只為尋找 volume imbalance 而切換；兩種設置都標出來，看價格尊重哪一個；不要為幾美分的影線長度糾結「精度」。
  • 突破缺口（Breakaway Gap）：理想狀態是完全不回踩、直接遠離；一旦回踩，需看到真正的沉重（heaviness）與加速遠離。

4. 學習路徑（Michael 給新手的路線圖）

  1. 先學流動性：每天 20–30 分鐘看靜態圖，標註當日高低點如何形成、價格去了哪些前一日位置、缺口/FVG/訂單塊如何反應；持續 2–4 週。
  2. 一次只練一件事：可以是「只找 FVG」、「只做 10 點銀彈」、「只做開盤區間缺口到中點」；每天最多看 1–2 個影片，不要 6 個月刷完全部內容。
  3. 先讀盤、後交易：上圖不是為了「必須做一單」，而是 tape reading；setup 出現才做；錯過就等下一班車。
  4. 寫日誌、做復盤：記錄每天找什麼、避開了什麼、哪裡做對做錯；Michael 自述自己的全部教學都來自自己的交易日誌時間線。
  5. 固定百分比回報：月目標 6%（週 1.5%），每筆風險 0.5%，達標停手；用小資金（如 1,000 美元）先證明能穩定做到，再逐步放大目標（8.5%→10%→12.5–15%）。
  6. 不要用帳單錢、不要讓家人成為交易的「董事會成員」：交易是你自己的公司，別人的意見沒有出資、卻想當 CEO/CFO。

五、心態與交易心理（單獨成節）

本場約一半篇幅是心理與成長話題，核心脈絡如下：

  • 「必須在場」的成癮：新手覺得「必須一直在交易裡」，本質是對刺激（多巴胺）的成癮，像玩電子遊戲、像追高分；Pit Munch 自述 2023 年每天交易約 20 次、「必須交易、必須交易」，現在這種衝動「完全消失了」。
  • 交易的公車比喻：Pit Munch 轉述 Michael 的話——「Trades are like buses, let the bus go, another bus will come.」（交易像公車，錯過一班還有下一班。）
  • 「糖果店裡的孩子」：Michael 說，一旦看懂價格，到處都是機會，就像孩子進了糖果店；但什麼都吃會「肚子疼」。解決辦法是每次只專注一件事，並明確「現在我只對這個感興趣」。

2. ICT Methodology Points (as stated by the speakers live)

  • Liquidity first (Draw on Liquidity): The starting point of all analysis is "where is price going" — prior day highs/lows, session highs/lows, relative equal highs/lows, gaps, and FVGs are all liquidity pools; price sweeps liquidity first, then moves in the opposite direction.
  • AMD / Power 3: Accumulation — Manipulation — Distribution; the main framework for Pit Munch's crude oil trades.
  • PD Arrays: Order Blocks, FVGs (Fair Value Gaps), Volume Imbalances, Breakers, Inversion FVGs, BISI/SIBI (buy-side/sell-side imbalance). Michael stressed: no single PD array is inherently superior to another; the key is "whether there is enough range to the target"; don't fixate on one puzzle piece.
  • Clarifying the definition of an order block: Not every bearish candle is an order block — only when price is expected to move up, and that candle's opening price (change in state of delivery, CSD) sits below current price, providing room for a retracement, does it constitute a bullish order block; and vice versa.
  • Volume Imbalance: The most "flexible" of all PD arrays, and it can be traversed repeatedly. Michael's filter rule: once a candle body closes above it, temporarily stop referencing it and look for other PD arrays instead (that doesn't mean it is invalid forever).
  • Discount / Premium Sensitivity and the Mean Threshold (50%): When bullish, you want candle bodies to stay in the upper half of the PD array (not breaking below 50%), while wicks may probe lower; the reverse when bearish. Gaps are best kept "partially unfilled"; after being filled, price should immediately resume the original direction, otherwise it is a sign of weakening. There is also the concept of "failed to glad hand": price failing to return to where it should before moving on is a sign of strength/speed.
  • Silver Bullet: The first FVG appearing at 10:00 a.m. (US Eastern) is the key one; several questioners use it as their model, and Michael advised beginners to start with "only trading the 10-point Silver Bullet" or "only trading the opening range gap to its midpoint."
  • Opening Range Gap (ORG): The difference between the 9:30 opening price and the prior day's 16:14 (US Eastern) closing price; the most common scenario is price reaching the halfway point (50%) of the gap; Pit Munch's "shadow" trade came from the "shadows of opening range gaps" in the October 2 nonfarm lecture.
  • Weekly Profiles and daily/weekly OHLC: Michael said that years ago, by hand-drawing weekly OHLC line charts, he discovered that each week's up/down pattern comes in only a limited number of forms; combined with the economic calendar, one can anticipate which day is most likely to produce the weekly high or low and which day is most likely to see a large one-directional move. A "profile" here is not Market Profile, but the generic pattern of how the days within a week compose the weekly range.
  • Market Maker Models: Buy models / sell models; what Michael values most is the "second stage redistribution / reaccumulation," which he called "where the most money is"; the unicorn refers to this very segment.
  • Unicorn clarification: It is not a model, just an adjective, used to emphasize "a rare, perfect setup where direction, location, time, and session are all aligned"; Michael said that every year he gives away for free one model that is "enough to practice on its own."
  • Turtle Soup: After sweeping liquidity on one side (buy-side or sell-side), price expands in the opposite direction; it isn't necessarily a big stop hunt — it may simply sweep an intraday minor swing low, pull back to half the range, and then continue in the original direction; the key is the higher-timeframe location, not the sweep itself. Michael explicitly rejected CRT (the renamed version by others): "Stop learning versions of my stuff renamed."
  • Chain of Custody and Change in State of Delivery (CSD): Price being "handed over" from one PD array to the next; Neil's system is built around "custodial control appearing at a specific time and location + confirmation of a state change."
  • Killzones and Macros: The London and New York opens and macro windows such as 10:00, 15:15–15:45, and 15:50–16:10 were mentioned repeatedly; Michael advised beginners to use "time" as the first filter — know when you should be sitting in front of the charts and when you don't need to watch at all.
  • The "back of a business card" model: Michael said that in 2010 he wrote "Here's my card" on BabyPips: a trader's entire plan should fit on the back of a business card. On March 19, 2023, Pit Munch drew her own model as this assignment and posted it (50-minute liquidity, sell-side, market structure shift, unicorn Breaker+FVG), and Michael replied, "My model is a clock."
  • Settlement price toggle: Switch it only to look for volume imbalance; mark both settings and see which one price respects; don't agonize over "precision" for a few cents of wick length.
  • Breakaway Gap: Ideally there is no retest at all and price moves straight away; if it does retest, you need to see genuine heaviness and acceleration away.

4. Learning Path (Michael's Roadmap for Beginners)

  1. Learn liquidity first: spend 20–30 minutes a day looking at static charts, marking how the day's highs and lows formed, which prior-day locations price visited, and how gaps/FVGs/order blocks reacted; keep this up for 2–4 weeks.
  2. Practice only one thing at a time: it could be "only look for FVGs," "only trade the 10-point Silver Bullet," or "only trade the opening range gap to its midpoint"; watch at most 1–2 videos a day — don't binge through all the content in 6 months.
  3. Read the tape first, trade second: going to the charts is not about "having to take a trade," it is tape reading; trade only when a setup appears; if you miss it, wait for the next bus.
  4. Keep a journal and review: record each day what you looked for, what you avoided, and what you did right or wrong; Michael said all of his own teaching comes from the timeline of his own trading journal.
  5. Fixed percentage returns: a monthly target of 6% (1.5% per week), 0.5% risk per trade, and stop when you hit the target; first prove you can do it consistently with small capital (e.g., $1,000), then gradually raise the target (8.5% → 10% → 12.5–15%).
  6. Don't use bill money, and don't let family become "board members" of your trading: trading is your own company — other people's opinions come with no capital invested, yet they want to act as CEO/CFO.

5. Mindset and Trading Psychology (Dedicated Section)

Roughly half of this session was devoted to psychology and personal growth; the core threads are as follows:

  • The addiction of "having to be in the market": Beginners feel they "must always be in a trade," which is essentially an addiction to stimulation (dopamine) — like playing a video game, like chasing a high score. Pit Munch said that in 2023 she traded about 20 times a day, feeling she "had to trade, had to trade," and that this urge has now "completely disappeared."
  • The bus metaphor for trades: Pit Munch quoted Michael — "Trades are like buses, let the bus go, another bus will come." (Trades are like buses: miss one, and another will come along.)
  • "A kid in a candy store": Michael said that once you understand price, opportunities are everywhere, like a child walking into a candy store; but eating everything will give you a "stomach ache." The solution is to focus on one thing at a time and be clear that "right now, this is the only thing I'm interested in."
  • Don't bring other people's voices into your trading: Michael admitted that he too gets provoked by comments and wants to "take 15 trades in 15 minutes and make $30,000 to shove it in their face," calling this a character flaw of his and a "chip on his shoulder" (a complex: wanting to prove himself to deceased elders); he relies on the community to remind him to stay focused. Victor's case is the cautionary tale: trading to prove to his parents that "dropping out was the right choice" — the more he tried to prove it, the more he lost.
  • The view on losses: Trader in Training 60 said "a loss = I failed"; Michael responded: you must "give yourself permission to take losses"; a loss is not failure, it is "user error" — like stubbing your toe while walking: it hurts for a moment, then you keep walking. Professional traders, like businesses, expect to have losses, but they do not expect to go bankrupt.
  • Family pressure: Victor was kicked out of the house by his father, and his parents said "don't come back even if you succeed"; Michael's advice: first repair the relationship and your inner peace, keep trading separate from the people you love — "trading is 90% psychological warfare that you place on yourself"; he also suggested Victor consider returning to his studies (trading only needs 30 minutes to 1 hour a day).
  • "No shortcuts" and "no overnight success": Michael stressed repeatedly: anyone who says "you can learn to trade within a year" is lying; crash courses, signal groups, and "learn ICT in five minutes" sell a "sense of participation," not how to make money; real learning is "slow, boring, and long-term."
  • Self-encouragement and journaling: Michael said that in his early days he encouraged himself daily ("you focused well today, you avoided toxic thoughts") and measured his progress through his journal; he said the average person has about 300 negative thoughts a day, and traders must actively manage their self-talk, avoiding self-limiting statements like "I can't" or "I have no experience" (he corrected Pit Munch on the spot when she said "I can't do higher-timeframe analysis").
  • A gender perspective (the speaker's own words): Michael believes female traders are often better at "doing what needs to be done and then stopping," while men more easily fall into "size anxiety" — always feeling the profits aren't big enough and always wanting to prove themselves; this is one of his explanatory frameworks for Bobby's overtrading (his personal view, unverified).
  • The warning about "proving yourself": Michael said that at 54 he is still fighting the urge to "prove I'm stronger than everyone else"; and he reminded listeners: don't post your trades on social media seeking validation — "you don't need to prove to anyone that you can trade."
