《贤者时间》19 期：有钱了你会变坏吗？——会议纪要

  • 主持：AZ（@AZ77678），联合主持：茄哥（@qg7777）、Lacey

结论先行

这期没有统一答案，只有一个被反复确认的框架：钱本身不直接让人变坏，它做的是两件事——选择变多、约束变少。一个人"变坏"还是"变好"，取决于他原来就想做什么：原来就想多找几个伴侣的，有钱后真去找了；原来就想安静过日子的，有钱后买张机票就走了。场上最被认同的两句话是茄哥的「有钱只是让道德边界模糊了一点」和「没钱的时候不做没底线的掠夺，有钱的时候不为富不仁」。

全场实际分了两段：前两个半小时聊"有钱了会怎样"，后两个半小时滑进了爱情与亲密关系——希西老师的"爱情四分法"（浪漫、吸引、激情、长期四者互斥）是后半段最完整的理论输出。

一、开场暖场（00:00–00:34）：还没定题，先聊了一圈

  • 当天 CPI 刚出，有人做了 4000 亿市值的空单、"指引 60 个点，明天不用做了"；创作者收益被调侃"不够你亏的"，会交易的人看不上那点收益。
  • 茄哥长篇安利"每个年轻人都要有一个个人网站"：AI 时代做网站极简单，它就是你的名片；社交需求会因 AI 大面积下降，网站是"永远的私产"，别人通过它了解你的观点，而不是只看朋友圈。
  • 插曲：一位重庆的大学生说学校进了野猪攻击学生，之前还有猴子进宿舍偷水果、蛇出没；学校紧邻自然保护区。
  • 黄仁勋"出轨"八卦续集（接 11 月 9 日茄哥那场）：场上判断是他老婆"这两天必须出来"发原谅声明，董事会调查前处理掉，股价随之反弹；离婚可能性不大，"那女的是间谍"的说法概率低；英文 Space 也在讨论，但"中国人更喜闻乐见"。

二、定题：从"反脆弱"到"有钱了你会变坏吗"（00:34–00:36）

  • 先后提了几个备选：年轻人如何反脆弱、30 岁不结婚行不行、旷野派还是轨道派，最后定下「有钱了你会变坏吗」，AZ 当场改了 Space 标题。
  • 开场定义（茄哥）："坏"没有统一标准，钱带来的是选择变多、对普通人的约束不再是约束，于是"在世俗意义上就变坏了"。

三、"有钱"的划线：A9（00:44–00:51）

  • 场上划的参考线：国内 500 万美金、国外 1000 万美金现金，约等于 A9，到这个量级"可以干不少事"。
  • A9 之后最想做的事（每人一句）：
  • Beta："我要找四个男朋友"，引出整场反复出现的梗——没钱找四个会被发现后分手，有钱找四个"知道了也不会分开"；
  • 电机小子：去非洲看动物大迁徙、爬喜马拉雅（"没有缆车的山我都不爬"被群嘲）；
  • 养一支电竞战队、包一栋别墅养年轻高手打比赛；
  • 找个安静的地方远离纷扰，"A7 就够了"；
  • 开工厂、开网红店、开医院——"小一点目标开诊所，大一点目标开医院"；
  • 旅居：勾搭上谁就去哪住一年半载，"追求一种不确定性"。
  • 共识收尾：花钱本身没想象力，"有钱之后真正难的是找到让自己感兴趣的事"；越赚钱越想赚钱，"口腹之欲"一桌顶级食材加顶级酒也要看跟谁吃。

四、捐钱还是开公司：最大的慈善是什么（00:42–00:43）

  • 场上明确反对直接捐钱："捐钱是最大的贪腐，钱不好监管"，家族信托都比捐出去强。
  • 茄哥的结论被多人认同：最大的慈善是商业——开公司发工资，养活家庭和上下游产业链，"这其实就是最大的交税、最大的社会责任"。

五、全场最实在的一段：一位大学生的求助（00:53–01:05）

  • 一位在校大学生（家里唯一的大学生）上麦：父母是体力劳动者，欠了十几万债，父亲"接近崩溃"，母亲也撑不住；他两年给家里转了近十万，自己还有两年毕业，不知道能做什么。
  • 茄哥的回答（全场最干货的一段）：
  • 十几万咬牙帮还，五十万以上一毛钱都不要掏——十几万是能解决的数字，五十万以上以他刚毕业的收入两年内存不下，掏了也解决不了问题；
  • 先给父母买保险，"确保他们未来五到十年出现身体重大危机的时候"有兜底；
  • 剩下的就是"把你自己过好"，不要被拖进"乱世"里一起沉下去。
  • 其他人补充：可以卖房还债；不要找家里要生活费也别内疚，"该破产就破产"。

六、钱买不到的东西（01:25–02:19）

  • "有些东西是钱买不到的"：第一个好女人、好父母、时间与健康。
  • 一位女听众的发言是全场最动情的：家里前年被电信诈骗，今年母亲又查出癌症，"她的不愉快"让自己很想生个小孩，"在养小孩的过程中把小时候的我重养一遍"，"小孩本身生活在那儿就已经在报答养育之恩了"。茄哥回应：可以去格鲁吉亚、哈萨克斯坦冻卵，"但千万不要为了找个男人去生孩子"。
  • AZ 自述：丁克，理由是"小孩会反向套牢"——不只是钱，"很多的心情都在他身上"；但也承认"如果意外怀孕了无论怎么样我都会生"。
  • 关于"有钱之后更怕失去"：怕失去财富、怕意外，修心、找内心的平静被多次提起，有人说自己八年前目标就是"找到心理的平静"。

七、后半段：爱情四分法（03:00–03:43，希西老师）

话题从 03:00 左右彻底转向亲密关系，希西老师给出全场最成体系的一套拆分：

  • 爱情可以拆成四个互斥的维度：浪漫、吸引、激情、长期。 每人总分 200 点，四项不可能同时拉满——排他就意味着"信"（信任/忠诚）那一项打不满；想让对方拿到 400 分，就要先拆掉自己的框架。
  • 女生眼中"好男人"的标准被反复确认：拎得清、诚实、情绪稳定、底色善良；"诚实是最重要的"。
  • 一见钟情与化学反应：多人数落"一见钟情就是见色起意"，但化学反应真实存在且推不开；"爱一个人不需要一直在一起"，"爱是成全，不是占有"。
  • 抽身能力是女生最该有的能力：进入一段关系前先问自己"有没有随时能离开他的勇气"；"拿得起不是最重要的，放得下才是最重要的"。
  • 短期关系被多人明确反对："无论男的女的不要选择短则（短期关系）"；短期关系本质是"各取所需"，价格被直接报出来——包养市场"一个月一两万"，并被评价为"很伤害每一个人"。
  • 女生选择成本大于男性的讨论：生理时间线（43 岁是一道线）、同等条件下职场竞争力、"抖音小红书上人均百万"的毒化叙事。

八、收尾（03:43–04:59）

  • 利益绑定与婚前协议：多位发言者认为婚姻里的"价格"一旦和金钱符号挂钩，就变成"谁给钱多听谁的"；真正顶级的绑定不是钱，是"注意力"和"不可替代性"。
  • 长期关系的"魔鬼操作"分享：给伴侣买保险、做信托（投保人是自己、受益人是对方），"保险公司帮我稳住长期关系"；买金条存到对方名下等做法被半开玩笑地复述。
  • 投资自己与向内：反复出现的一句是"注意力向内"——评价体系向内还是向外，决定了你会不会被别人的评价绑架；"一个人能把自己修成自己的房间"。
  • 04:58，AZ 收尾："圆满了我们就怀着各自的爱意睡觉"，众人互道晚安，Space 结束。

結論先行

這期沒有統一答案，只有一個被反覆確認的框架：錢本身不直接讓人變壞，它做的是兩件事——選擇變多、約束變少。一個人"變壞"還是"變好"，取決於他原來就想做什麼：原來就想多找幾個伴侶的，有錢後真去找了；原來就想安靜過日子的，有錢後買張機票就走了。場上最被認同的兩句話是茄哥的「有錢只是讓道德邊界模糊了一點」和「沒錢的時候不做沒底線的掠奪，有錢的時候不為富不仁」。

全場實際分了兩段：前兩個半小時聊"有錢了會怎樣"，後兩個半小時滑進了愛情與親密關係——希西老師的"愛情四分法"（浪漫、吸引、激情、長期四者互斥）是後半段最完整的理論輸出。

Sage Time Episode 19: If You Got Rich, Would You Turn Bad? — Meeting Notes

  • Time: 2026-11-10 22:30 – 2026-11-11 03:29 (about 4 hours 59 minutes)

Key Takeaways First

This episode had no single answer, only one framework confirmed again and again: money itself does not directly make a person bad; it does two things — more choices, and fewer constraints. Whether someone "turns bad" or "turns good" depends on what they wanted to do in the first place: someone who always wanted multiple partners actually goes and finds them once rich; someone who always wanted a quiet life buys a plane ticket and leaves once rich. The two lines most endorsed on the floor were Qie Ge's "money only blurs your moral boundaries a little" and "when you have no money, don't engage in bottomless plunder; when you have money, don't be rich and heartless."

The session actually split into two parts: the first two and a half hours discussed "what happens when you get rich," and the last two and a half hours slid into love and intimate relationships — Teacher Xixi's "four-part division of love" (romance, attraction, passion, and long-term, mutually exclusive) was the most complete theoretical output of the second half.

1. Opening Warm-Up (00:00–00:34): No Topic Yet, Chatting Around First

  • CPI had just come out that day; someone had taken a short position on a 400-billion market cap — "guidance of 60 points, no need to work tomorrow"; creator earnings were mocked as "not enough to cover your losses," and people who can trade look down on that bit of income.
  • Qie Ge gave a long pitch that "every young person should have a personal website": in the AI era a website is extremely simple to make, and it is your business card; social needs will drop massively because of AI, a website is "permanent private property," and others learn your views through it rather than only seeing your Moments.
  • Interlude: a university student from Chongqing said a wild boar had entered the school and attacked students; before that monkeys had entered dorms to steal fruit, and snakes had appeared; the school is right next to a nature reserve.
  • Sequel to the Jensen Huang "affair" gossip (continuing from Qie Ge's session on November 9): the floor's judgment was that his wife "must come out in the next two days" with a forgiveness statement, to settle it before a board investigation, after which the share price would rebound; divorce was unlikely, the claim that "that woman is a spy" had low probability; English-language Spaces were also discussing it, but "Chinese people enjoy it more."

2. Setting the Topic: From "Antifragility" to "If You Got Rich, Would You Turn Bad?" (00:34–00:36)

  • Several alternatives were proposed in turn: how young people can be antifragile, whether it's OK not to marry by 30, wilderness school vs. track school — and finally they settled on "If you got rich, would you turn bad?", and AZ changed the Space title on the spot.
  • Opening definition (Qie Ge): there is no unified standard for "bad"; what money brings is more choices, and constraints that bind ordinary people are no longer constraints, so "in the worldly sense, you have turned bad."

3. Drawing the Line for "Rich": A9 (00:44–00:51)

  • The reference line drawn on the floor: 5 million US dollars in cash domestically, 10 million US dollars abroad, roughly equal to A9 — at that level "you can do quite a lot."
  • The first thing each person most wanted to do after A9 (one line each):
  • Beta: "I want to find four boyfriends," launching the running joke of the whole session — with no money, finding four would end in breakup once discovered; with money, finding four means "even if they know, they won't leave";
  • Dianji Kid: go to Africa to see the great animal migration, climb the Himalayas (mocked by the group for "I won't climb a mountain without a cable car");
  • Raise an esports team, rent a whole villa to house young pros to play matches;
  • Find a quiet place away from the noise, "A7 is enough";
  • Open a factory, open an influencer shop, open a hospital — "a smaller goal is a clinic, a bigger goal is a hospital";
  • Slow travel living: whoever you hook up with, go live where they are for a year or so, "pursuing a kind of uncertainty."
  • Closing consensus: spending money itself takes no imagination; "what is truly hard after getting rich is finding something that interests you"; the more money you make, the more you want to make, and even for "appetites of the palate," a table of top ingredients and top wine depends on who you eat with.

4. Donate Money or Start a Company: What Is the Greatest Charity? (00:42–00:43)

  • The floor explicitly opposed donating money directly: "donating money is the greatest corruption; money is hard to supervise," and even a family trust is better than donating it away.
  • Qie Ge's conclusion was endorsed by many: the greatest charity is business — start a company and pay wages, supporting families and the upstream and downstream industrial chain; "this is actually the greatest tax payment, the greatest social responsibility."

5. The Most Grounded Segment of the Night: A University Student Asks for Help (00:53–01:05)

  • A university student (the only university student in his family) came on mic: his parents are manual laborers, in debt by more than a hundred thousand, his father "close to breaking down," his mother also unable to hold on; in two years he had transferred nearly a hundred thousand home, he still had two years until graduation, and he didn't know what he could do.
  • Qie Ge's answer (the most practical segment of the night):
  • Grit your teeth and help repay a debt of a hundred-odd thousand, but don't pay a single cent toward anything over five hundred thousand — a hundred-odd thousand is a solvable number; over five hundred thousand, with his income right after graduation, he couldn't save it in two years, and paying wouldn't solve the problem anyway;
  • First buy insurance for his parents, "to ensure they have a safety net when a major health crisis hits in the next five to ten years";
  • The rest is "live your own life well," and don't get dragged down into the "chaos" together.
  • Others added: the family could sell the house to repay debts; don't ask family for living expenses and don't feel guilty, "if bankruptcy is due, go bankrupt."

6. Things Money Can't Buy (01:25–02:19)

  • "Some things money can't buy": the first good woman, good parents, time and health.
  • One female listener's remarks were the most moving of the night: her family had been hit by telecom fraud the year before last, and this year her mother was diagnosed with cancer; "her unhappiness" made her really want to have a child, "to raise my childhood self again in the process of raising a child," "the child, simply by living there, is already repaying the kindness of being raised." Qie Ge responded: you can freeze eggs in Georgia or Kazakhstan, "but never have a child just to find a man."
  • AZ's self-account: childfree by choice, because "a child reverse-mortgages you" — not just money, "so much of your emotional state is tied up in them"; but she also admitted, "if I got pregnant unexpectedly, I would give birth no matter what."
  • On "fearing loss more after getting rich": fear of losing wealth, fear of accidents; cultivating the mind and finding inner peace were mentioned many times, and someone said their goal eight years ago was "to find psychological peace."

7. Second Half: The Four-Part Division of Love (03:00–03:43, Teacher Xixi)

Around 03:00 the topic shifted entirely to intimate relationships, and Teacher Xixi gave the most systematic breakdown of the night:

  • Love can be split into four mutually exclusive dimensions: romance, attraction, passion, and long-term. Each person has a total of 200 points, and the four cannot all be maxed out at once — exclusivity means the "trust" (trust/loyalty) item cannot be filled; if you want the other person to score 400, you must first dismantle your own framework.
  • The standard for a "good man" in women's eyes was confirmed again and again: clear-headed, honest, emotionally stable, and kind at the core; "honesty is the most important."
  • Love at first sight and chemistry: many people dismissed "love at first sight is just lust at first sight," but chemistry really exists and cannot be pushed away; "loving someone doesn't require being together all the time," "love is fulfillment, not possession."
  • The ability to withdraw is the ability women most need: before entering a relationship, ask yourself "do I have the courage to leave him at any time?"; "being able to pick it up is not the most important thing; being able to put it down is."
  • Short-term relationships were explicitly opposed by many: "whether male or female, don't choose short-term (short-term relationships)"; a short-term relationship is essentially "each taking what they need," and the price was quoted outright — the kept-partner market is "ten to twenty thousand a month," judged as "hurting everyone involved."
  • Discussion of women's higher choice costs than men's: the biological timeline (43 is a line), workplace competitiveness under equal conditions, and the toxic narrative on Douyin and Xiaohongshu that "everyone is a millionaire."

8. Closing (03:43–04:59)

  • Interest binding and prenuptial agreements: several speakers held that once the "price" in a marriage is tied to money symbols, it becomes "whoever pays more gets obeyed"; the truly top-level binding is not money, but "attention" and "irreplaceability."
  • Sharing of "devilish moves" for long-term relationships: buying insurance for a partner, setting up a trust (yourself as policyholder, the partner as beneficiary) — "the insurance company helps me stabilize the long-term relationship"; buying gold bars and storing them in the partner's name, and similar moves, were repeated half-jokingly.
  • Investing in yourself and turning inward: a line that kept recurring was "attention inward" — whether your evaluation system faces inward or outward determines whether you will be held hostage by others' evaluations; "a person can cultivate themselves into their own room."
  • At 04:58, AZ closed: "It's complete — let's sleep holding our respective loves," everyone said goodnight to each other, and the Space ended.
