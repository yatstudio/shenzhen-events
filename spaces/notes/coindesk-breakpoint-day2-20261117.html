一句话

这期 75 分钟基本是 Breakpoint Day 2 的“落地篇”：球迷经济、稳定币支付、机构入场、代币化利用率、汇款、机器人数据、美国合规永续——七个话题指向同一个判断，链上金融正在从交易投机转向真实使用，而 Solana 想当那个默认结算层。

核心内容

1. Peter Moore：球迷是最被低估的链上用户（前 Sega 总裁、Xbox、EA、利物浦 CEO）

  • 履历：Reebok 出身，1998 年 44 岁转行进游戏业；Sega 总裁任内推出 Dreamcast（首台联网主机）；后为比尔·盖茨操盘 Xbox 与 Xbox Live、Xbox 360 上市；EA Sports 总裁、EA 首席运营官；最后回英国任利物浦足球俱乐部 CEO，任内与主帅克洛普共事。现为波兰历史俱乐部维斯瓦·克拉科夫（Wisła Kraków）部分所有者，并为 Wrexham（瑞安·雷诺兹与罗伯·麦克尔亨尼所有）提供建议。本场主旨演讲题为《球迷的价值》。
  • 利物浦自称约有 3 亿球迷，绝大多数一辈子到不了安菲尔德（球场仅容纳 61,000 人），球迷互动只能靠内容与票务完成。
  • 数字化落地的真实成本：利物浦花了两年时间教球迷用手机出示二维码/动态码进场，包括为习惯纸票的老年球迷设线下培训点。他的结论是“技术永远跑在消费者理解能力前面”，必须按最不懂技术的球迷来设计。
  • 区块链的用法：追踪与奖励球迷、治理票务欺诈。英国法律禁止门票高于面值转售，倒票背后有组织犯罪。他在台上讲的方案叫 Lucky Fan Index（幸运球迷指数）：看你去过哪些比赛、买过哪位球员的球衣、球队赢还是输——赢球指数上升、输球下降，指数越高忠诚积分越多，整套行为记录上链。
  • 细节：他 4 岁（1959 年）第一次去安菲尔德，父亲往闸机塞了 2 先令 6 便士。EA 经典配音“It's in the game”出自 Andrew Anthony，此人住在他家附近的圣巴巴拉，每年仍收一笔不菲的费用，EA 多年不换这个声音。

2. Ahmed Zifzaf：Ramp——稳定币是“24 小时营业的支付轨道”

  • Ramp 是企业财务运营平台，稳定币是其较新的业务线。过去几个月已有约 2,500–3,000 家企业在用稳定币支付；70% 的交易量发生在银行下班之后——企业不睡觉，支付也不该睡觉。
  • 客户从 AI 实验室、建筑公司到农场都有。稳定币原生 CFO 看中的是法币与稳定币支付统一在一个平台，不用多处拼账。
  • Ramp 已支持 Solana 上的 USDG 与 OUSD（OpenUSD，背后是 Visa、Stripe、Coinbase、Mastercard）。选币标准首先是客户需求，其次是团队可信度；一位新入职工程师两周内就完成了 USDG on Solana 的接入。
  • Ramp 内部工作流高度 AI 化（自研 AI 平台 Glass），入职培训基本靠 AI 文档平台完成。
  • 区域稳定币：目前约 95% 的稳定币交易仍以美元稳定币计价；Ramp 希望推动区域稳定币，因为能优化企业财资后端与运营资金。

3. Nameel Dalal：Y Combinator——机构终于走到前面来了

  • Dalal 是 YC 访问合伙人，主攻金融科技与加密；曾在 Coinbase 七年，主导 USDC；2012 年 YC 创业者，同批的布莱恩·阿姆斯特朗后来创办 Coinbase。
  • YC 至今资助 6,000 多家创业公司，其中加密项目 100 多家。他的判断：Solana 是“互联网资本市场”的中心，股票、债券等一切资产正在上链。
  • 稳定币圆桌的核心结论有二：一是信任仍是第一要素；二是收益分成正在成为选型关键——USDC 早期余额净息差约 1%，如今是 4%–5%，对零售商这类大余额持有者来说，这笔钱开始重要了。x402（Agent 支付协议）值得关注：稳定币是“Agent 的钱”，因为有 API、全球可用、易于调用，Circle 已在重注 Agent 叙事。
  • YC 上月发布的加密创业征集（RFS）首次出现机构申请者多于消费者的情况。未来一两年的重点：股票代币化、稳定币普及、Agent 可用的基础设施，以及 AI 融资——他称 AI 融资是“人类历史上最大规模的融资事件”，堪比铁路与电气化，加密能为其解锁全球流动性。
  • 机构接棒的原因有三：产品从测试版走向机构可信任；监管明朗（美国《GENIUS 法案》让稳定币成为生态的一等公民，各国立法也在跟进）；用例终于从投机交易扩展到支付与真实对冲。
  • 一个数字：YC 的单人创业者占比两年内从 3% 跃升至 20%，原因是 AI 工具让一个人也能做出好产品；而且买家越来越多是 AI Agent——Alchemy 的 CTO 告诉他，该公司最大的用户现在就是 Agent。开发工具的买家正在从开发者变成 Agent。

4. Kashi Danda：Jupiter——代币化的 99% 利用率缺口

  • Danda 是 Jupiter 首席运营官，自称头衔其实是“Cat Herder”（管猫的人）。Jupiter 从 Solana 最大交易聚合器长成了链上金融超级应用，自我定位是“金融界的谷歌”：交易、借贷、预测市场、抵押、组合管理都在一个屋檐下。
  • 八天内连发七个产品：上周四个面向用户（研究中心、链上 ETF“篮子”、聚合收益机会的金库产品——与 Bitwise、RE7、K3、HiveMind 合作精选、聚合各协议的组合工具）；昨天主舞台三个，围绕“代币化之后”：代币化资产名义规模 4,600 亿美元，真正进入 DeFi 的只有约 35 亿美元，99% 的利用率缺口才是问题。
  • 补缺口的第一步是 Jupiter Global Passport：用零知识技术做的 KYC 体系——默认匿名，需要时才提供信息；按司法辖区模块化叠加问题（美国发行人问合格投资者资质，香港发行人问另一套）；稳定币消费卡（带返现）是诱导用户完成 KYC 的“特洛伊木马”。
  • 与机构交易所 Bullish 合作推出发行人担保市场：真正的代币化股票，持币即持股、名字上股东名册，需 KYC。
  • 数据：10 月是 Jupiter 代币化股票交易最好的一月，环比 9 月增长约 50%；今年迄今全产品交易量约 5,000 亿美元，其中代币化股票约 80–90 亿美元，占比仍小；用户以零售与国际用户为主（本地开券商账户不便的人），不少交易量是纽交所与链上之间的套利。Jupiter 现为 Solana 上最大的借贷市场。

5. Malcolm Clark：Western Union——汇款终于等到了链

  • Clark 是 Western Union 数字资产全球负责人。公司在 Solana 上发行了自有稳定币 USDPT（PT 即 payment token，支付代币），配套稳定卡已在 40 个国家上线，目标年底超过 60 个（他本人打了折扣：可能到不了 60）。
  • 卡的用法：收款人不用再去门店取现金，钱直接进卡，在 Visa 网络消费，或在西联网点提现。重点营销市场是墨西哥、哥伦比亚、阿根廷，以及玻利维亚或秘鲁之一。
  • 最关键的变化是关系链的延长：过去收款人进店取现走人，关系就结束了；现在卡上有持续的消费数据，稳定币可编程——可以给持币收益、可以做忠诚计划（“在这家店花，1 美元值 1.05 美元”）。
  • 主持人的点评：汇款是 2016–2017 年加密圈最常讲的用例，当时钱包与出入金通道都不成熟；现在真正的难点是现金进出（cash in / cash out），而这正是西联的强项。菲律宾市场即将上线（一位主持人住在菲律宾）。

6. Jonathan Victor：BitRobot——机器人缺的不是模型，是数据

  • Victor 是 BitRobot 总裁。核心论点：大语言模型有互联网文本可训，机器人没有“手臂如何移动、抓握用多大力”的数据集，这是机器人远比 AI 落后的主因。
  • 产品 RoboCap 是一顶可穿戴采集帽：侧面与正面多摄像头，能看到佩戴者看到的一切甚至更多（手在画面外时侧摄仍能捕捉），记录叠衣服、做饭、组装宜家家具等真实任务，用来训练机器人策略。
  • 上周开源了 10,000 小时真实工作录像，覆盖 11 个国家、21 个行业；对比之下，一两年前机器人领域所谓“大规模数据”不过 100 小时量级。
  • 为什么不用 Meta 眼镜这类现成设备：单摄像头无深度信息、做不了手部追踪；电池只能录约一分钟；帽子约 270 克、可换电池、能连续戴数小时。
  • 商业模式：平价出售帽子，配套 App 按数据被实验室采纳的情况向采集者支付加密货币；迄今约 100,000 名贡献者、覆盖 100 多个国家，全部结算走 Solana——理由是溯源（实验室要知道数据来源）与全球支付成本（传统数据支付中介收 5% 手续费，即时提现再加 10%–15%，稳定币几乎零成本）。
  • 数据处理：上传后做人脸模糊与去标识化，不合规内容会反馈拒绝。真实世界的“脏数据”恰恰是价值所在：有人叠衣服时一只鸡跑进来、幼儿打翻篮子——仓库里搭一万个叠衣台也复现不了这种混乱。

7. Armani Ferrante：Backpack——美国第一家从零拿到 CFTC 清算所+交易所的加密原生平台

  • Ferrante 是 Backpack CEO。头条消息（前一晚公布）：Backpack 成为第一家在美国从零获得 CFTC 监管的清算所与交易所双牌照的加密原生交易所，永续合约（无到期日的期货）与加密抵押品、24 小时交易即将合规进入美国。
  • 历时三四年，团队包括前 CFTC 专员及代理主席 Mark Wetjen（奥巴马时期任职）与前 CME 首席风险官 Sasha Rosenberg。系统已实盘运行数年，从设计之初就按合规来做——他强调没有任何一天、任何一笔存款或订单能让纽约用户靠 VPN 偷偷交易永续合约。
  • 竞争格局：永续合约每天在岸外交易数千亿美元，是整个加密的价格发现所在；钱包与消费应用都在接去中心化交易所，但都在岸外。Backpack 的打法不是只做自己的消费产品，而是把合规基础设施做成通用层，让整个生态能触达全球最大的资本市场。
  • 为什么选 Solana 做代币化股票：速度与可扩展性已解决、开发者生态强、还有一群彼此竞争的创业公司，他称之为“金融界的硅谷”，且遍布全球而非一国；DTCC 本周宣布将其代币化股票平台带入 Solana 生态就是佐证。

8. 收尾：主持人各自的 Top 3

  • 共同提名：鲍里斯·约翰逊（把 Satoshi Nakamoto 念错，已成 meme 素材）；西联汇款那场对话。
  • 一位主持人另提 Day 1 回忆：NEAR 的 Ilya（AI 与区块链的一站式）、Austin Federa 讲 DoubleZero（聚合多家 ISP、降延迟提带宽）；另一位提名营销人才正在进入加密：Ian Rogers（曾任 LVMH 数字负责人、创办 Apple Music、参与 Beats Music、做过 Beastie Boys 第一个网站，现主导 Ledger 的用户体验）与 Solana 基金会的 Rachel Conlon（前币安 CMO）。
  • 全场反复出现的口号：token supercycle（代币超级周期）、always-on capital markets（永不打烊的资本市场）——“市场不需要开盘，如果它从不收盘”。

值得注意的点

  • 与 Day One 的关系：这是 CoinDesk 在 Breakpoint 做的第二天演播室节目（约 75 分钟），与 11 月 16 日那场 4.5 小时的官方主舞台直播是两个不同的节目；嘉宾与议题基本不重叠，互补大于重复。
  • 共识主线：六位嘉宾说的其实是同一件事的不同切面——球迷、汇款、机器人数据、机构资金都在找“真实使用”，而 Solana 的叙事已从“最快的链”转向“默认的金融结算层”。
  • 两个最具体的数字：Jupiter 的 99% 利用率缺口（4,600 亿 vs 35 亿）与 Ramp 的 70% 盘后交易量，都是“链上金融正在变成日常基础设施”的实证口径。
  • 合规是 Day 2 的暗线：Backpack 的 CFTC 双牌照、Jupiter 的 ZK-KYC、西联的持牌稳定币，三件事同天出现，说明机构入场的瓶颈正在从技术转向牌照与合规设计。

2. Ahmed Zifzaf：Ramp——穩定幣是「24 小時營業的支付軌道」

  • Ramp 是企業財務營運平台，穩定幣是其較新的業務線。過去幾個月已有約 2,500–3,000 家企業在用穩定幣支付；70% 的交易量發生在銀行下班之後——企業不睡覺，支付也不該睡覺。
  • 客戶從 AI 實驗室、建築公司到農場都有。穩定幣原生 CFO 看中的是法幣與穩定幣支付統一在一個平台，不用多處拼帳。
  • Ramp 已支援 Solana 上的 USDG 與 OUSD（OpenUSD，背後是 Visa、Stripe、Coinbase、Mastercard）。選幣標準首先是客戶需求，其次是團隊可信度；一位新入職工程師兩週內就完成了 USDG on Solana 的接入。
  • Ramp 內部工作流高度 AI 化（自研 AI 平台 Glass），入職培訓基本靠 AI 文件平台完成。
  • 區域穩定幣：目前約 95% 的穩定幣交易仍以美元穩定幣計價；Ramp 希望推動區域穩定幣，因為能優化企業財資後端與營運資金。

3. Nameel Dalal：Y Combinator——機構終於走到前面來了

  • Dalal 是 YC 訪問合夥人，主攻金融科技與加密；曾在 Coinbase 七年，主導 USDC；2012 年 YC 創業者，同批的布萊恩·阿姆斯特朗後來創辦 Coinbase。
  • YC 至今資助 6,000 多家新創公司，其中加密項目 100 多家。他的判斷：Solana 是「網際網路資本市場」的中心，股票、債券等一切資產正在上鏈。
  • 穩定幣圓桌的核心結論有二：一是信任仍是第一要素；二是收益分成正在成為選型關鍵——USDC 早期餘額淨息差約 1%，如今是 4%–5%，對零售商這類大餘額持有者來說，這筆錢開始重要了。x402（Agent 支付協議）值得關注：穩定幣是「Agent 的錢」，因為有 API、全球可用、易於調用，Circle 已在重注 Agent 敘事。
  • YC 上月發布的加密創業徵集（RFS）首次出現機構申請者多於消費者的情況。未來一兩年的重點：股票代幣化、穩定幣普及、Agent 可用的基礎設施，以及 AI 融資——他稱 AI 融資是「人類歷史上最大規模的融資事件」，堪比鐵路與電氣化，加密能為其解鎖全球流動性。
  • 機構接棒的原因有三：產品從測試版走向機構可信任；監管明朗（美國《GENIUS 法案》讓穩定幣成為生態的一等公民，各國立法也在跟進）；用例終於從投機交易擴展到支付與真實對沖。
  • 一個數字：YC 的單人創業者佔比兩年內從 3% 躍升至 20%，原因是 AI 工具讓一個人也能做出好產品；而且買家越來越多是 AI Agent——Alchemy 的 CTO 告訴他，該公司最大的用戶現在就是 Agent。開發工具的買家正在從開發者變成 Agent。

4. Kashi Danda：Jupiter——代幣化的 99% 利用率缺口

  • Danda 是 Jupiter 首席營運官，自稱頭銜其實是「Cat Herder」（管貓的人）。Jupiter 從 Solana 最大交易聚合器長成了鏈上金融超級應用，自我定位是「金融界的谷歌」：交易、借貸、預測市場、抵押、組合管理都在一個屋簷下。
  • 八天內連發七個產品：上週四個面向用戶（研究中心、鏈上 ETF「籃子」、聚合收益機會的金庫產品——與 Bitwise、RE7、K3、HiveMind 合作精選、聚合各協議的組合工具）；昨天主舞台三個，圍繞「代幣化之後」：代幣化資產名義規模 4,600 億美元，真正進入 DeFi 的只有約 35 億美元，99% 的利用率缺口才是問題。
  • 補缺口的第一步是 Jupiter Global Passport：用零知識技術做的 KYC 體系——默認匿名，需要時才提供資訊；按司法轄區模組化疊加問題（美國發行人問合格投資者資質，香港發行人問另一套）；穩定幣消費卡（帶返現）是誘導用戶完成 KYC 的「特洛伊木馬」。
  • 與機構交易所 Bullish 合作推出發行人擔保市場：真正的代幣化股票，持幣即持股、名字上股東名冊，需 KYC。
  • 數據：10 月是 Jupiter 代幣化股票交易最好的一月，較 9 月成長約 50%；今年迄今全產品交易量約 5,000 億美元，其中代幣化股票約 80–90 億美元，佔比仍小；用戶以零售與國際用戶為主（本地開券商帳戶不便的人），不少交易量是紐交所與鏈上之間的套利。Jupiter 現為 Solana 上最大的借貸市場。

5. Malcolm Clark：Western Union——匯款終於等到了鏈

  • Clark 是 Western Union 數位資產全球負責人。公司在 Solana 上發行了自有穩定幣 USDPT（PT 即 payment token，支付代幣），配套穩定卡已在 40 個國家上線，目標年底超過 60 個（他本人打了折扣：可能到不了 60）。
  • 卡的用法：收款人不用再去門市取現金，錢直接進卡，在 Visa 網路消費，或在西聯網點提現。重點行銷市場是墨西哥、哥倫比亞、阿根廷，以及玻利維亞或秘魯之一。
  • 最關鍵的變化是關係鏈的延長：過去收款人進店取現走人，關係就結束了；現在卡上有持續的消費數據，穩定幣可程式化——可以給持幣收益、可以做忠誠計畫（「在這家店花，1 美元值 1.05 美元」）。
  • 主持人的點評：匯款是 2016–2017 年加密圈最常講的用例，當時錢包與出入金通道都不成熟；現在真正的難點是現金進出（cash in / cash out），而這正是西聯的強項。菲律賓市場即將上線（一位主持人住在菲律賓）。

6. Jonathan Victor：BitRobot——機器人缺的不是模型，是數據

  • Victor 是 BitRobot 總裁。核心論點：大語言模型有網際網路文本可訓，機器人沒有「手臂如何移動、抓握用多大力」的數據集，這是機器人遠比 AI 落後的主因。
  • 產品 RoboCap 是一頂可穿戴採集帽：側面與正面多攝影機，能看到佩戴者看到的一切甚至更多（手在畫面外時側攝仍能捕捉），記錄疊衣服、做飯、組裝宜家家具等真實任務，用來訓練機器人策略。
  • 上週開源了 10,000 小時真實工作錄影，覆蓋 11 個國家、21 個行業；對比之下，一兩年前機器人領域所謂「大規模數據」不過 100 小時量級。
  • 為什麼不用 Meta 眼鏡這類現成設備：單攝影機無深度資訊、做不了手部追蹤；電池只能錄約一分鐘；帽子約 270 克、可換電池、能連續戴數小時。
  • 商業模式：平價出售帽子，配套 App 按數據被實驗室採納的情況向採集者支付加密貨幣；迄今約 100,000 名貢獻者、覆蓋 100 多個國家，全部結算走 Solana——理由是溯源（實驗室要知道數據來源）與全球支付成本（傳統數據支付仲介收 5% 手續費，即時提現再加 10%–15%，穩定幣幾乎零成本）。
  • 數據處理：上傳後做人臉模糊與去識別化，不合規內容會回饋拒絕。真實世界的「髒數據」恰恰是價值所在：有人疊衣服時一隻雞跑進來、幼兒打翻籃子——倉庫裡搭一萬個疊衣台也復現不了這種混亂。

In one sentence

These 75 minutes are essentially the "real-world deployment" installment of Breakpoint Day 2: fan economies, stablecoin payments, institutional entry, tokenization utilization, remittances, robotics data, and compliant perpetuals in the US — seven topics pointing to a single judgment, namely that on-chain finance is shifting from trading speculation to real usage, and Solana wants to be the default settlement layer for it.

Key content

1. Peter Moore: Fans are the most underrated on-chain users (former Sega president, Xbox, EA, Liverpool CEO)

  • Background: Started at Reebok; switched into the games industry in 1998 at age 44. As president of Sega he launched the Dreamcast (the first internet-connected console); he later ran Xbox and Xbox Live for Bill Gates and launched the Xbox 360; he was president of EA Sports and COO of EA; finally he returned to the UK as CEO of Liverpool Football Club, working alongside manager Jürgen Klopp. He is now a part-owner of the historic Polish club Wisła Kraków and advises Wrexham (owned by Ryan Reynolds and Rob McElhenney). His keynote at the event was titled "The Value of a Fan."
  • Liverpool claims roughly 300 million fans, the vast majority of whom will never in their lives get to Anfield (the stadium holds only 61,000 people), so fan engagement has to happen through content and ticketing.
  • The real cost of going digital: Liverpool spent two years teaching fans to enter with QR codes / dynamic codes on their phones, including setting up in-person training points for older fans used to paper tickets. His conclusion: "technology always runs ahead of consumers' ability to understand it," and you have to design for the least tech-savvy fan.
  • What blockchain is for: tracking and rewarding fans, and governing ticket fraud. UK law prohibits reselling tickets above face value, and organized crime sits behind ticket touting. The scheme he described on stage is called the Lucky Fan Index: it looks at which matches you have attended, which player's shirt you have bought, and whether the team won or lost — the index rises when the team wins and falls when it loses; the higher the index, the more loyalty points, with the whole record of behavior put on-chain.
  • Details: He first went to Anfield at age 4 (in 1959), when his father slipped 2 shillings and 6 pence into the turnstile. EA's classic voice line "It's in the game" was recorded by Andrew Anthony, who lives near his home in Santa Barbara and still receives a substantial fee every year; EA has kept the same voice for years.

2. Ahmed Zifzaf: Ramp — stablecoins are "payment rails that are open 24 hours"

  • Ramp is a corporate finance operations platform, and stablecoins are a newer business line for it. Over the past few months, some 2,500–3,000 companies have already been paying with stablecoins; 70% of the transaction volume happens after banks close — businesses don't sleep, and payments shouldn't either.
  • Customers range from AI labs and construction companies to farms. What stablecoin-native CFOs value is having fiat and stablecoin payments unified on one platform, instead of stitching accounts together in multiple places.
  • Ramp now supports USDG and OUSD (OpenUSD, backed by Visa, Stripe, Coinbase, and Mastercard) on Solana. Its coin selection criteria are, first, customer demand, and second, team credibility; a newly hired engineer completed the USDG-on-Solana integration within two weeks.
  • Ramp's internal workflows are heavily AI-driven (its in-house AI platform is called Glass), and onboarding training is done largely through its AI documentation platform.
  • Regional stablecoins: About 95% of stablecoin transactions are still denominated in dollar stablecoins; Ramp wants to promote regional stablecoins because they can optimize corporate treasury back ends and working capital.

3. Nameel Dalal: Y Combinator — institutions have finally moved to the front

  • Dalal is a YC visiting partner focused on fintech and crypto; he spent seven years at Coinbase, where he led USDC; he was a YC founder in 2012, in the same batch as Brian Armstrong, who later founded Coinbase.
  • YC has funded more than 6,000 startups to date, over 100 of them crypto projects. His judgment: Solana is the center of the "internet capital markets," and every asset — stocks, bonds, and more — is moving on-chain.
  • The stablecoin roundtable reached two core conclusions: first, trust remains the number-one factor; second, revenue sharing is becoming a key selection criterion — in USDC's early days the net interest margin on balances was about 1%, whereas today it is 4%–5%, and for large balance holders such as retailers, that money is starting to matter. x402 (the agent payments protocol) is worth watching: stablecoins are "the agent's money," because they have APIs, are globally available, and are easy to call, and Circle is already betting heavily on the agent narrative.
  • In the crypto Request for Startups (RFS) YC released last month, institutional applicants outnumbered consumer applicants for the first time. The priorities for the next year or two: stock tokenization, stablecoin adoption, infrastructure usable by agents, and AI financing — which he called "the largest financing event in human history," comparable to the railroads and electrification, with crypto able to unlock global liquidity for it.
  • There are three reasons institutions are taking over: products have moved from beta to something institutions can trust; regulation has become clearer (the US GENIUS Act makes stablecoins first-class citizens of the ecosystem, and legislation in other countries is following); and use cases have finally expanded beyond speculative trading to payments and real hedging.
  • One figure: YC's share of solo founders has jumped from 3% to 20% within two years, because AI tools let one person build a good product; moreover, the buyers are increasingly AI agents — Alchemy's CTO told him that the company's biggest user is now an agent. The buyer of developer tools is shifting from developers to agents.

4. Kashi Danda: Jupiter — the 99% utilization gap in tokenization

  • Danda is Jupiter's COO, though he says his title is really "Cat Herder." Jupiter has grown from Solana's largest trading aggregator into an on-chain finance super app, positioning itself as "the Google of finance": trading, lending, prediction markets, collateral, and portfolio management all under one roof.
  • Seven products launched in eight days: four user-facing ones last week (a research center, on-chain ETF "baskets," a vault product aggregating yield opportunities — curated in partnership with Bitwise, RE7, K3, and HiveMind — and a portfolio tool aggregating positions across protocols); and three on the main stage yesterday, centered on "after tokenization": tokenized assets total $460 billion in notional size, but only about $3.5 billion has actually entered DeFi — the 99% utilization gap is the real problem.
  • The first step to closing the gap is Jupiter Global Passport: a KYC system built with zero-knowledge technology — anonymous by default, providing information only when needed; modular add-on questions by jurisdiction (US issuers ask about accredited-investor status, Hong Kong issuers ask a different set); and a stablecoin spending card (with cashback) serves as the "Trojan horse" to induce users to complete KYC.
  • In partnership with the institutional exchange Bullish, it is launching an issuer-backed market: genuine tokenized stocks, where holding the token means holding the shares, with names on the shareholder register; KYC required.
  • Data: October was Jupiter's best month ever for tokenized stock trading, up about 50% from September; year-to-date trading volume across all products is about $500 billion, of which tokenized stocks account for roughly $8–9 billion, still a small share; users are mainly retail and international users (people for whom opening a local brokerage account is inconvenient), and a good deal of the volume is arbitrage between the NYSE and on-chain venues. Jupiter is now the largest lending market on Solana.

5. Malcolm Clark: Western Union — remittances finally got their chain

  • Clark is Western Union's global head of digital assets. The company has issued its own stablecoin, USDPT (PT stands for payment token), on Solana, and its companion stablecoin card is already live in 40 countries, with a target of over 60 by year-end (he himself hedged: it may not reach 60).
  • How the card works: recipients no longer have to go to a store to pick up cash; the money goes straight onto the card, to be spent on the Visa network or withdrawn in cash at Western Union locations. Key marketing markets are Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, and one of Bolivia or Peru.
  • The most important change is the lengthening of the relationship chain: previously, a recipient would walk into a store, collect cash, and leave, and the relationship ended there; now the card generates ongoing spending data, and stablecoins are programmable — they can pay yield to holders and support loyalty programs ("spend at this store and $1 is worth $1.05").
  • The hosts' commentary: remittances were the most talked-about use case in crypto in 2016–2017, when wallets and on/off-ramps were not yet mature; the real difficulty now is cash in / cash out, which happens to be Western Union's strength. The Philippines market is launching soon (one of the hosts lives in the Philippines).

6. Jonathan Victor: BitRobot — what robots lack is not models, it's data

  • Victor is the president of BitRobot. His core argument: large language models have the text of the internet to train on, but robots have no dataset for "how an arm should move, or how much force a grip should use" — the main reason robots lag so far behind AI.
  • The product RoboCap is a wearable data-collection cap: multiple cameras on the side and front can see everything the wearer sees and more (the side cameras can still capture the hands when they are out of the front frame), recording real tasks such as folding clothes, cooking, and assembling IKEA furniture, to train robot policies.
  • Last week it open-sourced 10,000 hours of real work footage, covering 11 countries and 21 industries; by contrast, what the robotics field called "large-scale data" a year or two ago was on the order of only 100 hours.
  • Why not use an off-the-shelf device like Meta glasses: a single camera provides no depth information and cannot do hand tracking; the battery records only about a minute; the cap weighs about 270 grams, has swappable batteries, and can be worn continuously for hours.
  • Business model: the caps are sold at an affordable price, and the companion app pays contributors cryptocurrency based on whether their data is adopted by labs; so far there are about 100,000 contributors across more than 100 countries, with all settlement on Solana — the reasons being provenance (labs need to know where the data came from) and the cost of global payments (traditional data-payment intermediaries charge a 5% fee, plus another 10%–15% for instant withdrawal, while stablecoins cost almost nothing).
  • Data processing: after upload, faces are blurred and data is de-identified, and non-compliant content is rejected with feedback. The "dirty data" of the real world is precisely where the value lies: a chicken wanders in while someone is folding clothes, a toddler knocks over a basket — you could set up ten thousand folding stations in a warehouse and never reproduce that kind of chaos.

7. Armani Ferrante: Backpack — the first crypto-native platform to obtain a CFTC clearinghouse + exchange from scratch in the US

  • Ferrante is the CEO of Backpack. The headline news (announced the previous evening): Backpack has become the first crypto-native exchange to obtain, from scratch, dual CFTC-regulated clearinghouse and exchange licenses in the United States, and perpetual futures (futures with no expiry date) with crypto collateral and 24-hour trading are about to enter the US compliantly.
  • The process took three to four years; the team includes former CFTC commissioner and acting chairman Mark Wetjen (who served under Obama) and former CME chief risk officer Sasha Rosenberg. The system has been running live for years and was built for compliance from the very design stage — he stressed that there was never a single day, deposit, or order that would let a New York user secretly trade perpetuals via a VPN.
  • The competitive landscape: perpetuals trade hundreds of billions of dollars offshore every day and are where price discovery for all of crypto happens; wallets and consumer apps are all integrating decentralized exchanges, but all offshore. Backpack's play is not to build only its own consumer product, but to turn its compliant infrastructure into a general-purpose layer so the whole ecosystem can reach the world's largest capital market.
  • Why Solana for tokenized stocks: speed and scalability are solved problems, the developer ecosystem is strong, and there is a cluster of startups competing with one another, which he called "the Silicon Valley of finance," spread across the globe rather than in one country; DTCC's announcement this week that it is bringing its tokenized stock platform into the Solana ecosystem is evidence of exactly that.
