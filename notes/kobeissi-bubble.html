一句话：Kobeissi 的答案是——没有泡沫破裂。这 45 天是 AI 交易的一次正常回调：市场上能想到的坏消息，大半已经写进价格里了；高质量科技股的风险回报反而在变好。但他把话说得很稳：这不是见底断言，只是「悲观定价得比基本面还狠」。

45 天里发生了什么

标普 500 在 10 月 6 日见历史高点之后一路向下：财报季开场、10 月 22 日 AI 股开始抛售、美联储会议、11 月 3 日中期选举、11 月 12 日黄仁勋辞职风波，再叠上 Anthropic 的历史级 IPO 在最近几个交易日抽走市场流动性。45 天里标普蒸发约 6 万亿美元，同期黄金蒸发约 13 万亿、比特币约 5,500 亿（均为原文统计口径）。他的复盘方法是先问一句：什么真的变了，什么已经定价了？

担忧一：CapEx 与自由现金流——是真的，但已定价

这轮抛售的源头是市场开始算账：亚马逊、Alphabet、Meta、微软四家三季度资本开支合计创下 2,019 亿美元的纪录，自由现金流却转成 -264 亿美元，三年累计 CapEx 已达 1.6 万亿。Kobeissi 不否认担忧成立，但指出另一面：标普 500 三季度 EPS 同比增长 31.9%，是至少四年最强，且已连续 13 个季度超预期——「很少有市场同时这么恐惧、又增长这么强」。他的判断是：这部分担忧大半已经定价，真正的答案（投入资本回报率）要等到 2027 年一、二季度才会明朗。

担忧二：「Trump Put」终结

他把最近科技股急跌的一个关键催化剂归于中期选举：民主党横扫之后，所谓「Trump Put」——市场跌狠了特朗普总会出手托底的预期——就此终结；11 月 4 日已有民主党议员公开主张暂停先进 AI 研发、禁止超级智能，监管恐慌被市场提前定价。Kobeissi 认为这部分明显夸大：美国真禁 AI 等于把全球 AI 竞赛让给中国，不会发生；监管和新税会来，但市场定价的是比最终落地更极端的剧本。

担忧三：美联储还在加息

伊朗战争打到近 300 天，美国通胀已连续 67 个月高于 2% 目标，美联储主席 Warsh 的口径是通胀不回 2% 没得商量，于是 AI 基建的钱只会更贵；11 月美国投资级公司债发行预计达 1,750 亿美元、创历年 11 月纪录，等于大家都在抢贵的钱。但他也留了转机：油价本月已跌约 22% 到 82.5 美元附近，战争接近尾声，到 2027 年年中美联储政策转松的概率在上升。

芯片股被当成「出事了」在定价

英伟达两周内跌约 40%，AMD 被一份做空报告指控向中国走私芯片（原文亦注明未证实）。板块前瞻市盈率已跌到 2020 年 3 月以来最低，英伟达前瞻市盈率只剩 9 倍——他的读法是：市场不是在给增长定价，是根本不信那个「E」：哪怕英伟达给出约 7,500 亿美元的 FY28 收入指引，市场也按「黄仁勋回不来、指引全作废」在卖。这种定价方式本身，就留出了超预期上行的空间。

情绪已经到危机级别

VIX 从 10 月低点翻倍、11 月 5 日创下 2018 年 2 月以来最大单日涨幅并站上 30；Anthropic 的 IPO 认购总额约 5,000 亿美元、约 7.8 倍超额，等于一次性从市场抽走巨额流动性；对冲基金过去 6 周（截至 11 月 13 日）的科技股净流出创十年纪录。他的结论是：价格的暴跌是情绪突变叠加流动性抽紧打出来的，等流动性回来，市场会自己稳住。

结论，以及怎么打折看这篇

他的落点：这不是熊市开端，是正常回调——哪怕标普再跌 10%，也还在健康回调的范围内；对「还要再跌 30%+」的流行叙事要保持警惕。2027 年之前，市场定价的是高利率、强监管、AI 投入回报不及预期、龙头公司长期不确定性这一整套组合，任何一项落空都是正向惊喜。买跌资金会像 2025 年 4 月那样快速轮回高质量科技股。需要打折的地方也要说清：这是一篇付费通讯的引流长文，文末照例晒了自家策略 2020 年以来 516% 对标普 112% 的战绩——立场天然偏多，结论当一家之言看。

对你的落点

这篇正好和这周 Space 里听到的声音构成对照：普达特喊「全面崩盘无处可逃」，贤者时间里切哥他们在等 BTC 跌到 5 万以下再抄。Kobeissi 这篇是美股版的第三种声音——不喊底，只算「什么已经定价了」。对你最有用的其实是这个方法而不是结论本身：你的仓位在加密不在美股科技，但恐慌期的动作纪律是通用的——先分清「基本面变了」和「情绪与流动性在杀人」，再决定动不动。你 11 月 2 日给自己定的调子（稳健、控风险、也抓机会）和这篇的落点其实合拍：不跟标题喊泡沫，也不急着证明自己敢抄底，等流动性回来，只看能把投入变成利润的那批公司。

In one line: Kobeissi's answer is — no bubble has burst. These 45 days are a normal correction in the AI trade: most of the bad news anyone can think of is already written into prices, and the risk-reward on high-quality tech is actually improving. But he phrases it carefully: this is not a bottom call, just "pessimism priced in beyond what the fundamentals warrant."

What happened in 45 days

After the S&P 500 topped on October 6, it slid the whole way: earnings season opened, AI stocks started selling off on October 22, then the Fed meeting, the November 3 midterms, the November 12 Jensen Huang resignation turmoil, and on top of it all Anthropic's historic IPO draining market liquidity over the most recent sessions. In 45 days the S&P shed roughly $6 trillion; over the same stretch gold shed about $13 trillion and Bitcoin about $550 billion (all figures as stated in the original). His review method starts with one question: what actually changed, and what is already priced in?

Worry #1: CapEx and free cash flow — real, but priced in

The selloff began when the market started doing the math: Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft together spent a record $201.9 billion on capex in Q3, while free cash flow swung to -$26.4 billion; cumulative capex over three years has reached $1.6 trillion. Kobeissi doesn't deny the concern, but points to the other side: S&P 500 Q3 EPS grew 31.9% year over year — the strongest in at least four years — with beats for 13 straight quarters. "Few markets are simultaneously this fearful and growing this strongly." His judgment: most of this worry is already priced in, and the real answer (return on invested capital) won't be clear until Q1–Q2 2027.

Worry #2: The end of the "Trump Put"

He ties a key catalyst for the recent tech plunge to the midterms: after the Democratic sweep, the so-called "Trump Put" — the expectation that Trump would step in to support the market if it fell hard — is over. On November 4 a Democratic lawmaker publicly called for pausing advanced AI development and banning superintelligence, and regulatory panic got priced in early. Kobeissi thinks this is clearly overdone: a real US AI ban would hand the global AI race to China and won't happen; regulation and new taxes will come, but the market is pricing a script more extreme than what will actually land.

Worry #3: The Fed is still hiking

The Iran war is nearing day 300, US inflation has now run above the 2% target for 67 straight months, and Fed Chair Warsh's line is that inflation comes back to 2%, no exceptions — so money for AI buildout only gets more expensive. November investment-grade corporate bond issuance is expected to hit $175 billion, a record for any November: everyone is competing for expensive money. But he leaves room for a turn: oil is down about 22% this month to around $82.50, the war is nearing its end, and the odds of Fed policy loosening by mid-2027 are rising.

Chip stocks are priced as if something broke

Nvidia fell about 40% in two weeks; AMD was accused by a short report of smuggling chips to China (unproven, as the original itself notes). The sector's forward P/E has dropped to its lowest since March 2020, and Nvidia's forward P/E is down to 9x. His reading: the market is not pricing growth — it simply doesn't believe the "E." Even with Nvidia guiding to roughly $750 billion of FY28 revenue, the market is selling as if "Huang isn't coming back and the guidance is void." Pricing like that is exactly what leaves room for upside surprises.

Sentiment is at crisis levels

The VIX has doubled from its October low, posted its biggest one-day gain since February 2018 on November 5, and broken above 30. Anthropic's IPO drew roughly $500 billion in orders — about 7.8x oversubscribed — a one-off drain of enormous liquidity. Hedge funds' net outflows from tech over the past six weeks (through November 13) hit a ten-year record. His conclusion: the price collapse was produced by a sentiment snap compounded by a liquidity squeeze; once liquidity returns, the market will stabilize on its own.

His conclusion — and how to discount this piece

His landing point: this is not the start of a bear market but a normal correction — even another 10% down on the S&P would still sit inside a healthy pullback — and readers should stay wary of the popular "another 30%+ to fall" narrative. Heading into 2027, the market is pricing a whole bundle: higher rates, aggressive regulation, disappointing AI returns, and long-term uncertainty around key tech leaders; any one of them failing to materialize is a positive surprise. Dip-buying will rotate back into high-quality tech quickly, like the April 2025 setup. The discount to apply: this is a paid newsletter's free teaser, and it closes by advertising the house strategy's 516% return since 2020 versus the S&P's 112% — structurally long-biased, so treat the conclusion as one house's view.

The takeaway for you

This piece forms a neat contrast with the voices in this week's Spaces: Predator was calling a "total collapse with nowhere to hide," while in Xianzhe Time, Qiege and others were waiting for BTC under $50K to buy. Kobeissi is the US-equity version of a third voice — no bottom call, just the accounting of "what is already priced in." For you, the method is worth more than the conclusion: your positions are in crypto, not US tech, but panic-period discipline transfers — first separate "the fundamentals changed" from "sentiment and liquidity are doing the killing," then decide whether to move. The stance you set for yourself on November 2 (steady, risk-controlled, but ready to take opportunities) actually matches this piece's landing point: don't shout "bubble" along with the headlines, don't rush to prove you dare to buy the dip — wait for liquidity to return, and only look at the companies that turn spending into profit.
