ActivityPub 是 Fediverse 的心脏：对话协议共同作者 Evan Prodromou

节目：The Fediverse Files（WordPress.com）· 嘉宾：Evan Prodromou（ActivityPub 共同作者、现任标准编辑）· 发布：2024-08-05 · 时长：11 分钟 说明：本纪要据 YouTube 英文字幕整理。文中观点为发言人现场口径。

一句话

ActivityPub 之于联邦宇宙（Fediverse），就像 RSS 之于播客：它是让 Threads、Mastodon、WordPress、Ghost 等不同服务在后台互通的开放协议，目标是让用户能带着自己的内容和关注关系在服务之间自由迁徙，把社交关系的控制权从大公司手里拿回来。

什么是 Fediverse

  • 定义：一组通过开放协议（主要是 ActivityPub）自由连接的网站与应用的集合。已接入的包括 Threads、Flipboard、WordPress、Ghost、Mastodon、Pleroma、Pixelfed 等。
  • 「联邦」的含义：没有哪一个组织说了算。光 Mastodon 服务器就有数万台，加上无数 WordPress 博客，每个节点都有自己的管理员与审核者——社交关系由一大群人分布地维护，而不是一家公司掌控。这是一种更分布式、更民主的社交网络形态。
  • 类比 RSS 与播客：大多数播客用户从不去想「RSS」这个词，只知道任何播客 App 都能订阅任何播客源。ActivityPub 想为开放社交网络做同样的事——成为用户无需感知的基础管道。互联网本就是这样设计的：电子邮件、万维网都靠开放标准让任意系统互通。

迁徙自由：像携号转网之前的电话号码

  • 在 Fediverse 里，如果你不满意当前的 Mastodon 服务器，可以搬到另一台，粉丝能跟着你走，内容理论上也能迁移（Prodromou 承认这还不完美，但方向如此）。这会形成一个服务之间的自由市场，逼平台把服务做好。
  • 主持人提到他常用的类比：早年的电话公司控制一切，换运营商就丢掉电话号码——对做了招牌推广号码的商家是灾难；这正像今天想离开 Twitter 或在平台间搬家。是否需要靠国会立法来解决？Prodromou 不自称政策专家，但强调：用户现在就已经有选择——博客、Threads、Mastodon 都很容易开通；想要那种有选择权的社交体验，就得真的去行使选择。

真正革命性的部分：把控制权拿回来

  • 很多人都有这种经历：在一个平台上投入大量心血建社区、攒粉丝、写内容，某天早上醒来看到一篇「感谢陪伴，我们将于月底关停」的博客。平台把用户的连接与关系当作商品，不再相关时就随手丢弃。
  • Fediverse 的革命性在于：用户重新掌控自己的社交连接、图片、文字与视频——不会因为某家公司被收购、某个产品被关停就凭空消失。这是对用户与大平台之间权力关系的一次再平衡。
  • 关于体验层面的诉求（算法控制权、无广告、更好的审核、数据自主）：这些不是协议层要解决的，而是平台层的事；但 ActivityPub 让这类平台更容易被建出来、更容易互联，用户因此能找到无广告、无算法、审核良好的社区。「选择」是内建的。反过来协议也不禁止广告模式——想要更大上传量、愿意用广告换体验的人，同样可以自己选客户端和服务器加入。

ActivityPub Is the Heart of the Fediverse: A Conversation with Protocol Co-author Evan Prodromou

Program: The Fediverse Files (WordPress.com) · Guest: Evan Prodromou (co-author of ActivityPub, current standards editor) · Published: 2024-08-05 · Duration: 11 minutes Note: These notes were compiled from the YouTube English subtitles. The views expressed are those stated by the speaker.

In One Sentence

ActivityPub is to the Fediverse what RSS is to podcasts: it is the open protocol that lets different services such as Threads, Mastodon, WordPress, and Ghost interoperate behind the scenes, with the goal of letting users move freely between services while taking their content and follow relationships with them, and taking control of social relationships back from large companies.

What Is the Fediverse

  • Definition: a collection of websites and applications freely connected through open protocols (primarily ActivityPub). Services already connected include Threads, Flipboard, WordPress, Ghost, Mastodon, Pleroma, Pixelfed, and others.
  • The meaning of "federated": no single organization is in charge. Mastodon alone has tens of thousands of servers, plus countless WordPress blogs, and each node has its own administrators and moderators — social relationships are maintained in a distributed way by a large group of people, rather than controlled by a single company. This is a more distributed, more democratic form of social networking.
  • The RSS and podcast analogy: most podcast users never think about the word "RSS"; they just know that any podcast app can subscribe to any podcast feed. ActivityPub aims to do the same for open social networking — to become the underlying plumbing that users do not need to be aware of. The internet was designed this way from the start: email and the World Wide Web both rely on open standards to let any systems interoperate.

Freedom to Migrate: Like Phone Numbers Before Number Portability

  • In the Fediverse, if you are dissatisfied with your current Mastodon server, you can move to another one, your followers can come with you, and your content can in theory migrate as well (Prodromou acknowledges this is not yet perfect, but that is the direction). This creates a free market among services, forcing platforms to do a better job.
  • The host mentioned an analogy he often uses: in the early days, phone companies controlled everything, and switching carriers meant losing your phone number — a disaster for businesses that had promoted their number on their signage; this is exactly like wanting to leave Twitter today or move between platforms. Does solving this require legislation from Congress? Prodromou does not claim to be a policy expert, but emphasizes: users already have choices right now — blogs, Threads, and Mastodon are all easy to start; if you want a social experience where you have that choice, you have to actually exercise it.

The Truly Revolutionary Part: Taking Control Back

  • Many people have had this experience: you invest a great deal of effort in a platform building a community, gathering followers, and writing content, then one morning you wake up to a blog post saying "Thank you for being with us; we will shut down at the end of the month." Platforms treat users' connections and relationships as commodities, discarding them when they are no longer relevant.
  • What is revolutionary about the Fediverse is that users regain control over their own social connections, images, text, and video — they will not vanish into thin air just because a company is acquired or a product is shut down. This is a rebalancing of the power relationship between users and large platforms.
  • On experience-level demands (control over algorithms, no ads, better moderation, data autonomy): these are not things the protocol layer is meant to solve, but rather matters for the platform layer; however, ActivityPub makes such platforms easier to build and easier to interconnect, so users can find communities with no ads, no algorithms, and good moderation. "Choice" is built in. Conversely, the protocol does not prohibit advertising models either — people who want larger upload allowances and are willing to trade ads for the experience can likewise choose their own client and server to join.
