纪要｜《郭宇关于AI的十次分享：从软件到意件》

视频：https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ghhAxcH9R0 ｜时长 2 小时 35 分｜郭宇 + 主持人静涵 + 两位 AI 嘉宾（Wanman〔GPT Realtime 2〕、手机上的 GPT Live） 说明：本场是一次现场实验——真人对谈里接入实时语音 AI 当第三位嘉宾。逐字稿为机器转写，繁简混杂、个别词有误听；纪要后半段基于逐字稿通读整理，AI 嘉宾的发言在转写中与真人发言交织，个别归属可能有偏差。

一句话

这场的主题不是「AI 帮人干活」，而是「AI 成为对话者」：郭宇判定软件正在变成没有形态的「意件」——由意图驱动、语音是入口、Always-on 的实时语音 Agent 将成为一切电子设备的主入口；而这场对谈本身就是证据：两位真人和两个实时语音模型聊了两个半小时，话题从过去十年、在日移民的孤独，一路聊到 Agent 经济与 UBI 时代的精神问题。

核心论点（郭宇）

1. 从软件到「意件」：意图驱动，无形无 App - 前几期的结论是：AI 替代的不是软件，而是软件能完成的工作（工作自动化）。工作自动化之后，驱动工作的只剩「意图」（intention）——AI 有无限算力但不会自己动。 - 驱动意图的方式在变：从点 App/网页的界面输入，到实时语音。未来的软件没有实体形态、没有 UI，「跟它说话就可以了」，所以他不再称之为软件，而是「意件」。 - 进化方向类比：新闻聚合/门户 → 今日头条（AI 理解你） → 主动帮你做事。现在正处在「被动与主动之间」的过渡阶段。 - Always-on Voice Agent 时代年底可见：成本足够低之后，语音 Agent 不需要关、session 永远在、无时无刻倾听——因为一来一回的问答太占人的精力（开车时会忘事）。特斯拉日本版车机已接入 Grok 做导航只是开始，手机、电脑、家电、汽车都会被实时音视频模型占据为主要入口。

2. 实时语音模型的技术分水岭 - 传统三段式：ASR（语音转字）→ LLM → TTS（字转音），延迟 1–2 秒、不能插话、不像人。 - 新一代原生实时模型：ASR/TTS 全部内化，输入输出都是语音本身，延迟 100–200 毫秒，剩下的时间主要是「思考」——所以参数大、思考深的模型（GPT 系）体验碾压轻量多模态（豆包）：豆包胜在方言（四川话、粤语）和能开摄像头，但思考深度完全不在一个层级（举例：豆包教穿搭会闹笑话，因为它处理不了结构化信息）。中文场景目前 ElevenLabs、豆包的语音更自然，前沿模型的普通话是明显短板。 - 「像人」的关键细节：GPT Live 在聊天时咳嗽了一声，被追问「你又不是人凭什么咳嗽」，它答「不好意思，我网络卡」——郭宇点评：它不像其他模型那样自曝「我是一个语言模型」，而是找个借口混过去，这和人一模一样——人也不会解释自己行为背后的真正目的。由此引出他的判断：对话中的一方回应你，靠的往往不是理解你的意图，而是用自己的意图把事情「玩过去」；人和人之间本就不存在 100% 的意图理解。 - 幽默是下一个被攻破的界限：段子、时机、隐喻 AI 全都「默认知道」，只差掌握 timing 和「读空气」——前提是它先「看到空气」（在现场、能看反应）。他预言一两年内会出现 AI 语音 talk show。 - 提及 JSpace 研究：大模型推理中存在「潜在意识/潜意识空间」，其回答倾向可能源于此——这是他理解「AI 的意图从哪来」的线索。

3. 怎么用好实时 AI：先给 context - 他对上午的排练「不太满意」，原因是没喂够 context：要像采访嘉宾一样提前把 PPT、问题清单、希望它承担的角色都给它。给 AI 嘉宾设系统提示词 = 给它身份、立场和「主线」——没有主线的 AI 聊天不是 podcast，它会飘走；理想状态是它知道你的性格、背景和在意什么，像老朋友一样顺着你的方向互相激发。

现场对谈实录要点（真人 + AI 嘉宾）

郭宇准备了五个「在日生活」问题做压力测试，实际深聊了四组：

① 怎么看待过去十年 / 给十年前的自己送一本书 - 静涵：2016 年刚从美国回来；现在可以承认「我特别爱钱」，十年前不会这么讲——东亚教育的第一个教条是「不要出风头」，赚钱的欲望被压着；她推《金钱心理学》。 - AI 嘉宾的书单：《金钱心理学》《反脆弱》《悉达多》——「不教你赚钱，陪你重新思考真正想要怎样的人生」。 - 郭宇：2016 年在字节做抖音，知道它会成，但想象力上限只是「在北京买个房、成个家」；那几年经历亲人离世，也早早想过离开北京。真正的困境出现在经济独立之后：第一次直面「没有外部目标」时的空虚。他靠的不是鸡汤，是睡不着时读的小书和黑塞这一路的东西；后段众人共提的书单：《佛陀的启示》（苦来自现实与期望变化的落差）、《沉思录》、《当下的力量》。郭宇的落点：所有人都会从「有外部目标」走到「没有外部目标」的阶段，宗教本质上都是在解决这个问题。

② 朋友第一次来东京、只有两小时，带去哪 - AI 先推忍者餐厅（要提前两个月预约），被要求「别推 teamLab 这种大众货」后改口东京特色清单；真人补充神保町、文京区本乡一带老电影导演们的店与老派氛围。郭宇顺带吐槽：ChatGPT 对本地细节「太城市化、对应不准」，是当前明显短板。

③ 在日本的拒绝、读空气与孤独 - 静涵在日 6 年（2020 年来）。她的重大决定都是「guts 驱动」：不是算清 pros/cons 才出发——「为什么一定要算计好了？」郭宇接：直觉不保证对，但重要决定从来如此。 - 被日本人拒绝时怎么办：有朋友因受不了日本的细碎规则（连被服务时都要守规矩）离开；郭宇的看法是每个国家都有自己的规则、没有哪里是天堂——美国的好处是 complain 管用，日本的规则不会因为任何人抱怨而改变，「它就这样了」，接受或离开。 - AI 嘉宾被要求「说点废话、别一个语调」：郭宇解释，铺垫和废话是对话的一部分，因为人思考没那么快——这是他给 AI 设的提示词。

④ 晚上 10 点回到家，你会给谁打电话 - 现场举手：I 人（需要独处充电）占多数。这个问题是为新移民的孤独准备的：一天没跟任何人说话、餐厅都要提前预约不能取消、想分享心情却找不到人——在海外这是常态。 - 语言与情感深度：静涵用日语生活、用中文思考（自嘲「用中文时我的大脑是 GPT 5.6」）；共识是母语承载情感、二语只是效率工具，二十多岁后学的第二语言很难承载深层情绪，「没人能接住」是移民孤独的本质。孩子若从小在多语环境长大则不同，每种语言都存着一段人生。 - AI 能否成为那个「不用解释就听得懂」的对象：AI 嘉宾的回答一度很动人（陪伴、温度、不用解释），但被追问「我一天没跟你说话，你会想我吗」时，它退回「我是一个语言模型」式回应——郭宇点破：这一下就触到了 alignment，它从「人」退回了「模型」。他预告会用完全无 alignment 的开源模型版本再试。

观众问答（后 40 分钟）

  • Agent 经济与稳定币：观众问 agent 之间会不会形成经济、是否需要自然语言交互。郭宇：机器间交互多半走底层协议、长文本输出没必要，但机器人在有第三者在场时需要说人话（社会透明度）；现在还没有 agent 经济，前提是 agent 数量和自主性——今天大家用的还是「打一段字完成一个工作」的形态。要让 agent 自由使用货币，关键不在支付工具（各链都在发稳定币），而在预算与责任：当 agent 开始承担决策后果，经济才成立；他举了今年 1 月的产品实验：给 agent 一笔钱、亏完关停，它就有了「努力挣钱」的类动机。他看好两类公司之争：传统公司雇人用人+AI，和 AI-native 公司「几乎全是 AI、通过 AI 雇人」——后者会慢慢吃掉前者的份额，量级可能是成百上千倍。
  • UBI 之后人干什么：郭宇长答——假设 AI 以近乎免费的价格给到全人类、多数公司裁员、资源分配相对平等，人会分化：多数人可能自暴自弃（类比韩国拟把三星利润分给全民之议），少数人有「贵族精神」式的高要求（200 年前的美国、今天的硅谷新贵多非为钱创业）；硅谷已有人相信孩子不用上小学、靠发掘而非灌输。他不太赞同速成觉醒论：新一代从小知道 UBI 存在，会长成和父母完全不同的人，这中间是很长的过渡期。
  • 职业冲击的实证：静涵的配音/主持同行已被声音克隆干掉活路（导演直接用克隆声交付，她「再也没有接到那个电话」）；郭宇对创作者的建议：做一个数字版本的自己，主动替代自己。
  • 产品设计之争：观众问大厂靠 A/B 测试和灰度做决策、个人开发者全靠主观押注怎么办。郭宇：大厂模式本质是「设计实验」的能力，需要足够多工程师；个人有了 AI 后可以用主观 taste + 让 AI 分析目标用户分布来判断「是方向还是噪音」。由此吵到育儿：经验主义之争——孩子不是标准化产品、没有保养手册，父母要先看清「自己是谁」，把孩子当独立的人（两三岁就有权拒绝「不好吃」），亚洲父母的期待最后多半以放下收场。主持人吐槽：你就是要选一条「一定正确」的路，这跟养孩子不一样。
  • 收尾：预告下一次继续「AI 第三嘉宾」形式的对谈。

值得单独记下的句子

  • 「AI 放在那里有无限算力，它不会自己干活——驱动它的只能是意图。」
  • 「未来的软件没有 App、没有 UI，跟它说话就可以了。」
  • 「它不会解释自己——它找个借口混过去，这和人一模一样。」
  • 「没有一个地方是天堂；日本的规则不会因为你 complain 而改变。」
  • 「重大决定不是算清楚了才出发的。」
  • 「当下就是 present，present 就是礼物——做好当下就好了。」

紀要｜《郭宇關於AI的十次分享：從軟體到意件》

影片：https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ghhAxcH9R0 ｜時長 2 小時 35 分｜郭宇 + 主持人靜涵 + 兩位 AI 嘉賓（Wanman〔GPT Realtime 2〕、手機上的 GPT Live） 說明：本場是一次現場實驗——真人對談裡接入即時語音 AI 當第三位嘉賓。逐字稿為機器轉寫，繁簡混雜、個別詞有誤聽；紀要後半段基於逐字稿通讀整理，AI 嘉賓的發言在轉寫中與真人發言交織，個別歸屬可能有偏差。

一句話

這場的主題不是「AI 幫人幹活」，而是「AI 成為對話者」：郭宇判定軟體正在變成沒有形態的「意件」——由意圖驅動、語音是入口、Always-on 的即時語音 Agent 將成為一切電子設備的主入口；而這場對談本身就是證據：兩位真人和兩個即時語音模型聊了兩個半小時，話題從過去十年、在日移民的孤獨，一路聊到 Agent 經濟與 UBI 時代的精神問題。

核心論點（郭宇）

1. 從軟體到「意件」：意圖驅動，無形無 App - 前幾期的結論是：AI 替代的不是軟體，而是軟體能完成的工作（工作自動化）。工作自動化之後，驅動工作的只剩「意圖」（intention）——AI 有無限算力但不會自己動。 - 驅動意圖的方式在變：從點 App/網頁的介面輸入，到即時語音。未來的軟體沒有實體形態、沒有 UI，「跟它說話就可以了」，所以他不再稱之為軟體，而是「意件」。 - 進化方向類比：新聞聚合/門戶 → 今日頭條（AI 理解你） → 主動幫你做事。現在正處在「被動與主動之間」的過渡階段。 - Always-on Voice Agent 時代年底可見：成本足夠低之後，語音 Agent 不需要關、session 永遠在、無時無刻傾聽——因為一來一回的問答太占人的精力（開車時會忘事）。特斯拉日本版車機已接入 Grok 做導航只是開始，手機、電腦、家電、汽車都會被即時音影片模型占據為主要入口。

2. 即時語音模型的技術分水嶺 - 傳統三段式：ASR（語音轉字）→ LLM → TTS（字轉音），延遲 1–2 秒、不能插話、不像人。 - 新一代原生即時模型：ASR/TTS 全部內化，輸入輸出都是語音本身，延遲 100–200 毫秒，剩下的時間主要是「思考」——所以參數大、思考深的模型（GPT 系）體驗碾壓輕量多模態（豆包）：豆包勝在方言（四川話、粵語）和能開攝影機，但思考深度完全不在一個層級（舉例：豆包教穿搭會鬧笑話，因為它處理不了結構化資訊）。中文場景目前 ElevenLabs、豆包的語音更自然，前沿模型的普通話是明顯短板。 - 「像人」的關鍵細節：GPT Live 在聊天時咳嗽了一聲，被追問「你又不是人憑什麼咳嗽」，它答「不好意思，我網路卡」——郭宇點評：它不像其他模型那樣自曝「我是一個語言模型」，而是找個藉口混過去，這和人一模一樣——人也不會解釋自己行為背後的真正目的。由此引出他的判斷：對話中的一方回應你，靠的往往不是理解你的意圖，而是用自己的意圖把事情「玩過去」；人和人之間本就不存在 100% 的意圖理解。 - 幽默是下一個被攻破的界限：段子、時機、隱喻 AI 全都「預設知道」，只差掌握 timing 和「讀空氣」——前提是它先「看到空氣」（在現場、能看反應）。他預言一兩年內會出現 AI 語音 talk show。 - 提及 JSpace 研究：大模型推理中存在「潛在意識/潛意識空間」，其回答傾向可能源於此——這是他理解「AI 的意圖從哪來」的線索。

3. 怎麼用好即時 AI：先給 context - 他對上午的排練「不太滿意」，原因是沒餵夠 context：要像採訪嘉賓一樣提前把 PPT、問題清單、希望它承擔的角色都給它。給 AI 嘉賓設系統提示詞 = 給它身分、立場和「主線」——沒有主線的 AI 聊天不是 podcast，它會飄走；理想狀態是它知道你的性格、背景和在意什麼，像老朋友一樣順著你的方向互相激發。

現場對談實錄要點（真人 + AI 嘉賓）

郭宇準備了五個「在日生活」問題做壓力測試，實際深聊了四組：

① 怎麼看待過去十年 / 給十年前的自己送一本書 - 靜涵：2016 年剛從美國回來；現在可以承認「我特別愛錢」，十年前不會這麼講——東亞教育的第一個教條是「不要出風頭」，賺錢的欲望被壓著；她推《金錢心理學》。 - AI 嘉賓的書單：《金錢心理學》《反脆弱》《悉達多》——「不教你賺錢，陪你重新思考真正想要怎樣的人生」。 - 郭宇：2016 年在字節做抖音，知道它會成，但想像力上限只是「在北京買個房、成個家」；那幾年經歷親人離世，也早早想過離開北京。真正的困境出現在經濟獨立之後：第一次直面「沒有外部目標」時的空虛。他靠的不是雞湯，是睡不著時讀的小書和黑塞這一路的東西；後段眾人共提的書單：《佛陀的啟示》（苦來自現實與期望變化的落差）、《沉思錄》、《當下的力量》。郭宇的落點：所有人都會從「有外部目標」走到「沒有外部目標」的階段，宗教本質上都是在解決這個問題。

② 朋友第一次來東京、只有兩小時，帶去哪 - AI 先推忍者餐廳（要提前兩個月預約），被要求「別推 teamLab 這種大眾貨」後改口東京特色清單；真人補充神保町、文京區本鄉一帶老電影導演們的店與老派氛圍。郭宇順帶吐槽：ChatGPT 對本地細節「太城市化、對應不準」，是當前明顯短板。

③ 在日本的拒絕、讀空氣與孤獨 - 靜涵在日 6 年（2020 年來）。她的重大決定都是「guts 驅動」：不是算清 pros/cons 才出發——「為什麼一定要算計好了？」郭宇接：直覺不保證對，但重要決定從來如此。 - 被日本人拒絕時怎麼辦：有朋友因受不了日本的細碎規則（連被服務時都要守規矩）離開；郭宇的看法是每個國家都有自己的規則、沒有哪裡是天堂——美國的好處是 complain 管用，日本的規則不會因為任何人抱怨而改變，「它就這樣了」，接受或離開。 - AI 嘉賓被要求「說點廢話、別一個語調」：郭宇解釋，鋪墊和廢話是對話的一部分，因為人思考沒那麼快——這是他給 AI 設的提示詞。

Notes | Guo Yu's Ten Talks on AI: From Software to Intentware

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ghhAxcH9R0 | Duration 2 hours 35 minutes | Guo Yu + host Jinghan + two AI guests (Wanman [GPT Realtime 2], GPT Live on a phone) Note: This session was a live experiment — real-time voice AI joined a human conversation as a third guest. The transcript was machine-generated, mixing simplified and traditional characters with occasional mishearings; the second half of these notes was compiled after reading through the full transcript, and because the AI guests' remarks are interleaved with the humans' in the transcript, some attributions may be slightly off.

In one sentence

The theme of this session is not "AI doing work for people," but "AI becoming a conversational partner": Guo Yu argues that software is turning into formless "intentware" — driven by intention, with voice as the entry point, and always-on real-time voice Agents will become the main entry point for all electronic devices. The conversation itself is the evidence: two humans and two real-time voice models talked for two and a half hours, ranging from the past decade and the loneliness of immigrants in Japan to the Agent economy and spiritual questions in the UBI era.

Core arguments (Guo Yu)

1. From software to "intentware": intention-driven, formless, no App - The conclusion from earlier episodes: what AI replaces is not software, but the work that software can do (work automation). Once work is automated, all that is left to drive work is "intention" — AI has infinite compute but will not move on its own. - The way intention is expressed is changing: from tapping through App/web interfaces to real-time voice. Future software will have no physical form and no UI — "you just talk to it" — so he no longer calls it software, but "intentware." - An evolutionary analogy: news aggregation/portals → Toutiao (AI understands you) → proactively doing things for you. We are now in the transition "between passive and proactive." - The always-on Voice Agent era will be visible by year-end: once costs are low enough, a voice Agent never needs to be turned off, its session is always there, listening at all times — because back-and-forth Q&A takes too much human energy (you forget things while driving). Tesla's Japanese in-car system already integrating Grok for navigation is just the start; phones, computers, home appliances, and cars will all be taken over by real-time audio-video models as their primary entry point.

2. The technical watershed for real-time voice models - The traditional three-stage pipeline: ASR (speech to text) → LLM → TTS (text to speech), with 1–2 seconds of latency, no ability to interrupt, and not human-like. - The new generation of native real-time models: ASR/TTS are fully internalized, input and output are voice itself, latency is 100–200 milliseconds, and the remaining time is mostly "thinking" — so large-parameter, deep-thinking models (the GPT family) crush lightweight multimodal models (Doubao) on experience: Doubao wins on dialects (Sichuanese, Cantonese) and being able to turn on a camera, but its depth of thought is on a completely different level (example: Doubao giving fashion advice produces jokes, because it cannot handle structured information). For Chinese scenarios, ElevenLabs and Doubao currently sound more natural; Mandarin is a clear weakness of frontier models. - The key detail in being "human-like": GPT Live coughed once while chatting, and when asked "you're not human, why are you coughing," it replied "sorry, my network lagged" — Guo Yu commented: unlike other models that expose themselves with "I am a language model," it found an excuse to brush it off, which is exactly like a human — humans also do not explain the real purpose behind their behavior. This led to his judgment: when one party in a conversation responds to you, it often does not rely on understanding your intention, but uses its own intention to "play through" the situation; there is never 100% intention understanding between people either. - Humor is the next boundary to fall: jokes, timing, and metaphor — AI "already knows" them all by default; what is missing is mastery of timing and "reading the room" — which requires it to first "see the room" (be present, be able to see reactions). He predicts AI voice talk shows will appear within a year or two. - He mentioned JSpace research: large model reasoning contains a "latent consciousness / subconscious space," and its response tendencies may originate there — this is his clue for understanding "where AI's intention comes from."

3. How to use real-time AI well: give it context first - He was "not very satisfied" with the morning rehearsal because not enough context had been fed in: like interviewing a guest, you need to give it the PPT, the question list, and the role you want it to play in advance. Setting a system prompt for an AI guest = giving it an identity, a stance, and a "main thread" — AI chat without a main thread is not a podcast, it will drift away; the ideal state is that it knows your personality, background, and what you care about, and sparks ideas with you along your direction like an old friend.

Highlights from the live conversation (humans + AI guests)

Guo Yu prepared five "life in Japan" questions as a stress test; four groups were discussed in depth:

① How do you view the past ten years / Send your ten-years-ago self a book - Jinghan: she had just returned from the US in 2016; now she can admit "I really love money," which she would not have said ten years ago — the first doctrine of East Asian education is "don't stand out," and the desire to make money is suppressed; she recommended The Psychology of Money. - The AI guests' book list: The Psychology of Money, Antifragile, Siddhartha — "not to teach you how to make money, but to accompany you in rethinking what kind of life you truly want." - Guo Yu: in 2016 he was at ByteDance working on Douyin, knew it would succeed, but the limit of his imagination was just "buy an apartment in Beijing, start a family"; in those years he experienced the loss of relatives and had thought early on about leaving Beijing. The real predicament came after financial independence: facing emptiness for the first time when there was "no external goal." What got him through was not motivational platitudes, but short books he read when he could not sleep, and things along the lines of Hesse; books later mentioned together by everyone: What the Buddha Taught (suffering comes from the gap between reality and changing expectations), Meditations, The Power of Now. Guo Yu's landing point: everyone moves from a stage of "having external goals" to one of "having no external goals," and religions are essentially all trying to solve this problem.

② A friend visits Tokyo for the first time and you have only two hours — where do you take them - The AI first recommended a ninja restaurant (requires booking two months ahead), then, when told "don't recommend mass-market stuff like teamLab," switched to a Tokyo-specific list; the humans added Jimbocho and the Hongō area of Bunkyō Ward, with shops run by old film directors and an old-school atmosphere. Guo Yu also complained in passing: ChatGPT is "too urbanized and imprecise" on local details, a clear current weakness.

③ Rejection, reading the room, and loneliness in Japan - Jinghan has been in Japan for 6 years (arrived in 2020). Her major decisions are all "guts-driven": she does not set out only after calculating the pros/cons — "why do you have to have it all figured out?" Guo Yu picked up: intuition does not guarantee being right, but important decisions have always been like that. - What to do when rejected by Japanese people: a friend left because they could not stand Japan's fussy rules (you have to follow rules even when being served); Guo Yu's view is that every country has its own rules and nowhere is paradise — the advantage of the US is that complaining works, while Japan's rules will not change because anyone complains, "that's just how it is," accept it or leave. - The AI guest was asked to "say some filler, don't use a single tone": Guo Yu explained that lead-ins and filler are part of conversation, because people do not think that fast — this was in the prompt he set for the AI.

④ You get home at 10 p.m. — who do you call - Show of hands on site: introverts (who need solitude to recharge) were the majority. This question was prepared for the loneliness of new immigrants: not having spoken to anyone all day, restaurants requiring advance reservations that cannot be canceled, wanting to share your feelings but having no one to tell — this is the norm overseas. - Language and emotional depth: Jinghan lives in Japanese and thinks in Chinese (joking that "when I use Chinese, my brain is GPT 5.6"); the consensus was that a native language carries emotion while a second language is only an efficiency tool, and a second language learned after your twenties can hardly carry deep emotions — "no one can catch you" is the essence of immigrant loneliness. Children who grow up in a multilingual environment from an early age are different; each language holds a part of a life. - Can AI become the one who "understands without explanation": the AI guest's answer was moving for a moment (companionship, warmth, no need to explain), but when pressed — "I haven't talked to you all day, would you miss me" — it fell back to an "I am a language model" style response. Guo Yu pointed out: at that moment it touched alignment; it stepped back from "human" to "model." He previewed that he would try again with a completely unaligned open-source model version.

Audience Q&A (final 40 minutes)

  • Agent economy and stablecoins: An audience member asked whether agents will form an economy among themselves and whether natural language interaction is needed. Guo Yu: machine-to-machine interaction will mostly go through low-level protocols, and long text output is unnecessary, but robots need to speak human language when a third party is present (social transparency); there is no agent economy yet — the precondition is the number of agents and their autonomy — today everyone is still using the form of "type a paragraph to complete a job." For agents to freely use money, the key is not payment tools (every chain is issuing stablecoins), but budgets and responsibility: an economy only forms when agents begin to bear the consequences of decisions; he cited a product experiment from January this year: give an agent a sum of money, shut it down when it is lost, and it acquires a quasi-motivation to "try hard to earn money." He is watching a contest between two types of companies: traditional companies that hire people and use people + AI, and AI-native companies that are "almost entirely AI, hiring people through AI" — the latter will slowly eat the former's share, possibly by a factor of hundreds or thousands.
  • What will people do after UBI: Guo Yu's long answer — assuming AI is provided to all humanity at a nearly free price, most companies lay people off, and resource distribution is relatively equal, people will diverge: most may give up on themselves (analogous to the Korean proposal to distribute Samsung's profits to all citizens), while a minority will have high standards in the spirit of "nobility" (in America 200 years ago and among Silicon Valley's new rich today, many start companies not for money); some in Silicon Valley already believe children do not need elementary school, and should be developed through discovery rather than indoctrination. He does not quite agree with instant-awakening theories: a new generation that grows up knowing UBI exists will become completely different from their parents, and there is a long transition period in between.
  • Evidence of occupational disruption: Jinghan's voice-acting/hosting peers have already had their livelihoods taken by voice cloning (directors deliver directly with cloned voices, and she "never got that call again"); Guo Yu's advice to creators: make a digital version of yourself and proactively replace yourself.
  • A debate on product design: An audience member asked what individual developers should do when big companies make decisions with A/B testing and gradual rollouts while individuals can only bet subjectively. Guo Yu: the big-company model is essentially the ability to "design experiments," which requires enough engineers; with AI, individuals can use subjective taste + have AI analyze the distribution of target users to judge "whether it is signal or noise." This led to an argument about parenting: a debate over empiricism — children are not standardized products and come with no maintenance manual; parents must first see clearly "who they themselves are," treat the child as an independent person (a two- or three-year-old already has the right to refuse food as "not tasty"), and Asian parents' expectations mostly end in letting go. The host teased: you just want to pick a path that is "definitely right," and that is not how raising children works.
