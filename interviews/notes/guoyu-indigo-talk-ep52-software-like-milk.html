纪要｜Indigo Talk EP52《当软件像牛奶一样便宜，我们还要交付什么》

与郭宇聊 Agent 时代与 Vibe Coding 之后的路 频道：INDIGO 数字镜像｜主持：Indigo｜嘉宾：郭宇（前字节跳动工程师，现居日本） 时长：72 分钟｜来源：YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WkmbKwcJk7k 说明：逐字稿为本地 faster-whisper 机器转写，繁简混杂、个别词有误听（如「贪心」疑为口误/误听、「Cdance」=Seedance、「莫斯特」=Meta 的 Muse），纪要已按上下文校正。

一句话结论

软件的成本会像牛奶一样便宜，「做软件、卖软件」这件事本身正在失去意义——接下来真正要交付的不是软件，而是软件要达成的目的：目标、媒体和「活人感」。工程师的执行工作被 AI 接管，人被推到产品经理和决策者的位置上，半天能干完过去两周的活，但思考负担反而更重了。

郭宇其人（本期自述）

  • 2011 年在支付宝工作；2013 年 4 月在支付宝分享会上第一次接触 Bitcoin（当时约 100 美元）。
  • 2014 年去北京加入朋友的创业公司（多说/图虫系，微博基金投过），同年 12 月公司被今日头条收购，就此加入字节。
  • 在字节长期做中台，后做跑在抖音/TikTok 上的小程序引擎（micro runtime），自述是「技术执行者」角色，从没带过团队、也不想带。
  • 2019 年底决定离职：当时字节从外部拉一个新用户成本高达 50 多元人民币，「应用工厂都很难拿到新用户」，他判断移动互联网周期基本结束。交接半年，2020 年过年去日本旅行遇上疫情，回不了北京，索性留在日本，拿到长期签证乃至永驻至今。
  • 本想彻底离开科技行业休息，结果只休息了两三年就被 2022 年底的 AI 浪潮拉回来；目前用 Vibe Coding 做了 30 多个产品（很多没发布），同时管上百台 agent server。

核心观点

1. 工程师的角色翻转：从执行者变成提问者和决策者

  • 过去两周的迭代周期（定目标→开发→设计→测试→发布→观察实验），现在 1–2 个人半天就能跑完一轮。
  • 但人没有变轻松：你要同时扮演架构师、设计师、产品经理，最重要的是产品经理——善于提问题、能约束语言模型的「语言空间」，知道用什么语言、什么结构让它持续推进并根据实验结果调整方向。
  • AI 做不了完全自动化的决策，不是因为缺上下文，而是因为它没有主动的意图（intent），所以全部决策的心理负担都压回人身上。
  • 后果：2025 年 10 月到 2026 年初，他看到很多工程师朋友 burnout——两三个月干了过去一两年的活，大脑跟不上 AI 的速度，人变得焦虑，开始重新思考「到底什么事情真的值得做」。
  • 工作方式的变化：他自己已经很少用「计划模式」了，先和模型聊半小时把 spec 聊透，让它一次性朝目标跑；随着 SOTA 模型和 harness 不断进化，人还要不断适应新工具，这本身也是 burnout 来源之一。

2. 软件形态变了：即时生成、用完可丢、不再需要分发

  • 软件不再是写死的代码：可以即时编码完成任务，用完就丢，只给自己或很小的组织用；每个人的软件互不兼容，也没必要兼容。
  • 出现「first-class harness」：模型厂商自己做的第一层 harness（Claude Code、Codex、DeepSeek Harness），进步已经把大量传统软件功能「内化」了——用它做 CRM、管理系统是两分钟、五分钟的事，这类软件作为独立产品已无存在必要。
  • 下一步是操作系统层面的变化：把 Agent 而非 Application 当一等公民。已经有人在做基于 Linux 的 Agent OS；OpenAI 走了另一条务实的路——买大量 Mac 让下一代模型直接使用 Mac（成熟的 Unix + 成熟的创意/影视软件生态）。
  • 他的判断：未来我们租用的所有计算资源都会变成「Virtual Agent Server」——一台由 Agent 操作整个系统的虚拟机；人在手机上发指令，远程连回家里或机房的机器。
  • 安全警告：当所有人用 Agent 管理电脑，Agent 的连接一旦被劫持，就等于世界上最大的肉鸡集群。他自己第一次让 Agent 全自动买服务器、装密钥、删初始密码时，第一反应就是这个风险。

3. 模型分层：不需要最强的，只需要最合适的

主持人提出、郭宇大体认同的五层划分： 1. 个人便利型：处理打电话、订餐厅、管信息等日常需求，如 Meta 的 Muse，集成在 Messenger/WhatsApp/Instagram 里，不需要单独 App； 2. 个人生产力型：写代码、做创作，用 Claude、Codex 这类 SOTA 模型； 3. 企业协作型：让 Agent 学会用 Mac/电脑串起企业工作流，要求更高； 4. 娱乐生成型：字节占优势——Seedance 生成 + 红果平台消费已形成闭环，加上创作者工具； 5. 科研前沿型：需要最顶尖的模型，模型公司未必公开发布，而是与研究所、国防订单签框架、利润分成。 - 郭宇补充第六类「现场 AI」：农场、工厂、摄像头这类场景信息量巨大，必须用边缘计算 + 极便宜的模型 24 小时跑，不可能把图片都传给 OpenAI。

4. 企业采用还非常早期，整体只完成了 2%–3%

  • 大企业（包括日本企业）顾虑重重：没人能为 AI 的决策负责，中层看到机会也不敢拍板，只有董事会能做这种决策；也有数据外泄顾虑，不敢用别国模型。
  • 微软把 Copilot 改造成以 Agent 为核心、让 Agent 操作 Office 的「操作系统」，就是在催企业采用。
  • 小公司和一人公司会自然转向新方案；以投资视角定位：AI 对组织和商业的影响虽然在加速，但只完成了 2%–3%，大量事情没被解决。

5. 智能会变成「公共品」，软件像牛奶一样便宜（标题出处）

  • 娱乐生成市场是完全竞争市场：总有人用更便宜的 token 做更多实验，定价最终被压到接近模型生成成本，利润微薄到近似于无——智能会像牛奶一样，因为集中化大规模生产而极便宜，成为一种 public goods（准公共品），极大提升社会生产力。
  • 因此他自己的重心已经从「创造软件」移到下一阶段——「创造软件本身要达成的目的」。他承认目前处于迷茫观察期：打算先观察三到六个月再决定把精力放在哪，并提到朋友（公司卖给 Cloudflare 的小佑）也 burnout 在想下一阶段——当年字节 2012 年成立后也用了两年验证第一个想法、等到环境成熟才起飞，环境没成熟时硬按旧创业思路做软件分发，可能没有意义。

6. 人群分化：2%–3% 的 Pro user 被放大，大众靠娱乐填充时间

  • 只有约 2%–3% 的人能成为被 AI 放大的 Pro user：一人等于过去 5–60 人的公司，可以管一支 agent「军队」。
  • 大众没有想法、也不愿提需求（上班听派活、下班看电视），他们的时间将被娱乐型 AI 填充；所有娱乐产品竞争的本质都是对用户时间的直接竞争，产品形态不重要。
  • 年轻一代没有包袱：物质压力小后更顺着兴趣社群走，像日本泡沫破裂后的「平成废宅」——做三天零工、四天去冲浪，兴趣积累到专业水平甚至去参加奥运会。他判断这可能不是坏事。

7. Everything is Media：交付的是「活人感」

  • AI 内容（slop）泛滥后信噪比极低，差异化只能靠真人出镜：真人内容在全网占比小且最出挑（有博主总结：想让产品有流量，唯一要做的就是真人出镜介绍产品）。
  • 主持人的提法：把「自己」产品化——把自己的思考经 AI Agent 整理成可阅读、可订阅、甚至提供 API 的内容，让别人与你产生思维连接；「software 即 media，Everything is Media」，你 vibe code 出来的东西，其动作和结果本身就是媒体，用户消化完还想看你下一篇，如同刷视频。
  • 郭宇补充：不管分发 AI 内容还是真人内容，本质都是在 hack 新的推荐/流量机制；真人内容稀缺，做得好就会被投进更大的流量池。

8. 给年轻人的建议：别把「一份稳定工作」当人生锚点

  • 「上班」是工业革命的产物，只有 100 多年历史，会在智能革命之后消失或大幅变形。
  • 用投资思维看工作：如果一份工作像被套牢的股票、处在很长的下坡道上，关键不是纠结「亏多少离场」，而是看有没有更好的机会——机会成本最贵。他父亲当年下海时每月花几百块「停薪留职」保工龄，结果几年后矿就倒闭了；东北下岗是同理。
  • 具体建议：不要再追寻稳定工作，可以去南方、去深圳看看——潮汕、福建家族「宁可做小老板也不打工」的观念在 AI 时代反而受用，做自己的 OPC（一人公司）；不知道怎么做就先去看、去跟有经验的人学。但如果能去前沿 AI 工作室成为「创造的那部分人」，这种工作仍然值得做。
  • 主持人收尾定性：这不是又一次工业革命，而是一次文艺复兴——「你只要有无限可能的想法，就有无限可能的工具去做无限可能的事情」。

值得记的字节往事

  • 2015 年的字节已经是 AI 驱动的公司：产品靠神经网络推荐，算法工程师自称「调参工程师」，连他们自己也不完全知道系统怎么工作，只能靠实验逼近效果——郭宇当时第一次意识到代码跑在黑盒里，和今天的大模型如出一辙。
  • 2015 年是字节的 tough 年：头条 DAU/MAU 到瓶颈、员工流失严重；2016 年 12 月抖音、火山做了十几个月出来，2017 年局面才改善。
  • 资源最初押在火山小视频（定位大众），抖音起初是小团队、名字都不叫抖音，形态像 Musical.ly；收购 Musical.ly 后抖音靠「用户泛化」（从 18–20 多岁扩展到 7 岁到 70 岁）反超，最终完成火山的历史任务，两者合并。
  • 微博当年做推荐流的教训：关系网络在前，用户投诉「我关注的人看不到了」，推荐与关注之争斗了很多年没做好。
  • 抖音内容 hack 算法的经典技巧：在视频字幕里故意加错别字能显著提高完播率——因为观众会回看确认字是不是错了；字节内部有专门团队对抗这类 hack，识别后很快屏蔽。

紀要｜Indigo Talk EP52《當軟體像牛奶一樣便宜，我們還要交付什麼》

與郭宇聊 Agent 時代與 Vibe Coding 之後的路 頻道：INDIGO 數位鏡像｜主持：Indigo｜嘉賓：郭宇（前字節跳動工程師，現居日本） 時長：72 分鐘｜來源：YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WkmbKwcJk7k 說明：逐字稿為本地 faster-whisper 機器轉寫，繁簡混雜、個別詞有誤聽（如「貪心」疑為口誤/誤聽、「Cdance」=Seedance、「莫斯特」=Meta 的 Muse），紀要已按上下文校正。

一句話結論

軟體的成本會像牛奶一樣便宜，「做軟體、賣軟體」這件事本身正在失去意義——接下來真正要交付的不是軟體，而是軟體要達成的目的：目標、媒體和「活人感」。工程師的執行工作被 AI 接管，人被推到產品經理和決策者的位置上，半天能幹完過去兩週的活，但思考負擔反而更重了。

郭宇其人（本期自述）

  • 2011 年在支付寶工作；2013 年 4 月在支付寶分享會上第一次接觸 Bitcoin（當時約 100 美元）。
  • 2014 年去北京加入朋友的創業公司（多說/圖蟲系，微博基金投過），同年 12 月公司被今日頭條收購，就此加入字節。
  • 在字節長期做中台，後做跑在抖音/TikTok 上的小程式引擎（micro runtime），自述是「技術執行者」角色，從沒帶過團隊、也不想帶。
  • 2019 年底決定離職：當時字節從外部拉一個新用戶成本高達 50 多元人民幣，「應用工廠都很難拿到新用戶」，他判斷行動網際網路週期基本結束。交接半年，2020 年過年去日本旅行遇上疫情，回不了北京，索性留在日本，拿到長期簽證乃至永駐至今。
  • 本想徹底離開科技行業休息，結果只休息了兩三年就被 2022 年底的 AI 浪潮拉回來；目前用 Vibe Coding 做了 30 多個產品（很多沒發布），同時管上百台 agent server。

核心觀點

1. 工程師的角色翻轉：從執行者變成提問者和決策者

  • 過去兩週的迭代週期（定目標→開發→設計→測試→發布→觀察實驗），現在 1–2 個人半天就能跑完一輪。
  • 但人沒有變輕鬆：你要同時扮演架構師、設計師、產品經理，最重要的是產品經理——善於提問題、能約束語言模型的「語言空間」，知道用什麼語言、什麼結構讓它持續推進並根據實驗結果調整方向。
  • AI 做不了完全自動化的決策，不是因為缺上下文，而是因為它沒有主動的意圖（intent），所以全部決策的心理負擔都壓回人身上。
  • 後果：2025 年 10 月到 2026 年初，他看到很多工程師朋友 burnout——兩三個月幹了過去一兩年的活，大腦跟不上 AI 的速度，人變得焦慮，開始重新思考「到底什麼事情真的值得做」。
  • 工作方式的變化：他自己已經很少用「計畫模式」了，先和模型聊半小時把 spec 聊透，讓它一次性朝目標跑；隨著 SOTA 模型和 harness 不斷進化，人還要不斷適應新工具，這本身也是 burnout 來源之一。

2. 軟體形態變了：即時生成、用完可丟、不再需要分發

  • 軟體不再是寫死的程式碼：可以即時編碼完成任務，用完就丟，只給自己或很小的組織用；每個人的軟體互不相容，也沒必要相容。
  • 出現「first-class harness」：模型廠商自己做的第一層 harness（Claude Code、Codex、DeepSeek Harness），進步已經把大量傳統軟體功能「內化」了——用它做 CRM、管理系統是兩分鐘、五分鐘的事，這類軟體作為獨立產品已無存在必要。
  • 下一步是作業系統層面的變化：把 Agent 而非 Application 當一等公民。已經有人在做基於 Linux 的 Agent OS；OpenAI 走了另一條務實的路——買大量 Mac 讓下一代模型直接使用 Mac（成熟的 Unix + 成熟的創意/影視軟體生態）。
  • 他的判斷：未來我們租用的所有計算資源都會變成「Virtual Agent Server」——一台由 Agent 操作整個系統的虛擬機；人在手機上發指令，遠端連回家裡或機房的機器。
  • 安全警告：當所有人用 Agent 管理電腦，Agent 的連線一旦被劫持，就等於世界上最大的殭屍網路集群。他自己第一次讓 Agent 全自動買伺服器、裝金鑰、刪初始密碼時，第一反應就是這個風險。

3. 模型分層：不需要最強的，只需要最合適的

主持人提出、郭宇大體認同的五層劃分： 1. 個人便利型：處理打電話、訂餐廳、管資訊等日常需求，如 Meta 的 Muse，整合在 Messenger/WhatsApp/Instagram 裡，不需要單獨 App； 2. 個人生產力型：寫程式碼、做創作，用 Claude、Codex 這類 SOTA 模型； 3. 企業協作型：讓 Agent 學會用 Mac/電腦串起企業工作流，要求更高； 4. 娛樂生成型：字節占優勢——Seedance 生成 + 紅果平台消費已形成閉環，加上創作者工具； 5. 科研前沿型：需要最頂尖的模型，模型公司未必公開發布，而是與研究所、國防訂單簽框架、利潤分成。 - 郭宇補充第六類「現場 AI」：農場、工廠、攝影機這類場景資訊量巨大，必須用邊緣計算 + 極便宜的模型 24 小時跑，不可能把圖片都傳給 OpenAI。

4. 企業採用還非常早期，整體只完成了 2%–3%

  • 大企業（包括日本企業）顧慮重重：沒人能為 AI 的決策負責，中層看到機會也不敢拍板，只有董事會能做這種決策；也有資料外洩顧慮，不敢用別國模型。
  • 微軟把 Copilot 改造成以 Agent 為核心、讓 Agent 操作 Office 的「作業系統」，就是在催企業採用。
  • 小公司和一人公司會自然轉向新方案；以投資視角定位：AI 對組織和商業的影響雖然在加速，但只完成了 2%–3%，大量事情沒被解決。

5. 智慧會變成「公共品」，軟體像牛奶一樣便宜（標題出處）

  • 娛樂生成市場是完全競爭市場：總有人用更便宜的 token 做更多實驗，定價最終被壓到接近模型生成成本，利潤微薄到近似於無——智慧會像牛奶一樣，因為集中化大規模生產而極便宜，成為一種 public goods（準公共品），極大提升社會生產力。
  • 因此他自己的重心已經從「創造軟體」移到下一階段——「創造軟體本身要達成的目的」。他承認目前處於迷茫觀察期：打算先觀察三到六個月再決定把精力放在哪，並提到朋友（公司賣給 Cloudflare 的小佑）也 burnout 在想下一階段——當年字節 2012 年成立後也用了兩年驗證第一個想法、等到環境成熟才起飛，環境沒成熟時硬按舊創業思路做軟體分發，可能沒有意義。

6. 人群分化：2%–3% 的 Pro user 被放大，大眾靠娛樂填充時間

  • 只有約 2%–3% 的人能成為被 AI 放大的 Pro user：一人等於過去 5–60 人的公司，可以管一支 agent「軍隊」。
  • 大眾沒有想法、也不願提需求（上班聽派活、下班看電視），他們的時間將被娛樂型 AI 填充；所有娛樂產品競爭的本質都是對用戶時間的直接競爭，產品形態不重要。
  • 年輕一代沒有包袱：物質壓力小後更順著興趣社群走，像日本泡沫破裂後的「平成廢宅」——做三天零工、四天去衝浪，興趣累積到專業水準甚至去參加奧運會。他判斷這可能不是壞事。

7. Everything is Media：交付的是「活人感」

  • AI 內容（slop）泛濫後信噪比極低，差異化只能靠真人出鏡：真人內容在全網占比小且最出挑（有博主總結：想讓產品有流量，唯一要做的就是真人出鏡介紹產品）。
  • 主持人的提法：把「自己」產品化——把自己的思考經 AI Agent 整理成可閱讀、可訂閱、甚至提供 API 的內容，讓別人與你產生思維連接；「software 即 media，Everything is Media」，你 vibe code 出來的東西，其動作和結果本身就是媒體，用戶消化完還想看你下一篇，如同刷影片。
  • 郭宇補充：不管分發 AI 內容還是真人內容，本質都是在 hack 新的推薦/流量機制；真人內容稀缺，做得好就會被投進更大的流量池。

8. 給年輕人的建議：別把「一份穩定工作」當人生錨點

  • 「上班」是工業革命的產物，只有 100 多年歷史，會在智慧革命之後消失或大幅變形。
  • 用投資思維看工作：如果一份工作像被套牢的股票、處在很長的下坡道上，關鍵不是糾結「虧多少離場」，而是看有沒有更好的機會——機會成本最貴。他父親當年下海時每月花幾百塊「停薪留職」保工齡，結果幾年後礦就倒閉了；東北下崗是同理。
  • 具體建議：不要再追尋穩定工作，可以去南方、去深圳看看——潮汕、福建家族「寧可做小老闆也不打工」的觀念在 AI 時代反而受用，做自己的 OPC（一人公司）；不知道怎麼做就先去看、去跟有經驗的人學。但如果能去前沿 AI 工作室成為「創造的那部分人」，這種工作仍然值得做。
  • 主持人收尾定性：這不是又一次工業革命，而是一次文藝復興——「你只要有無限可能的想法，就有無限可能的工具去做無限可能的事情」。

值得記的字節往事

  • 2015 年的字節已經是 AI 驅動的公司：產品靠神經網路推薦，演算法工程師自稱「調參工程師」，連他們自己也不完全知道系統怎麼工作，只能靠實驗逼近效果——郭宇當時第一次意識到程式碼跑在黑盒裡，和今天的大模型如出一轍。

One-Sentence Conclusion

The cost of software will become as cheap as milk, and "making software and selling software" itself is losing its meaning — what truly needs to be delivered next is not software, but the purpose software is meant to achieve: goals, media, and a "sense of a real person." Engineers' execution work is being taken over by AI, and people are being pushed into the positions of product manager and decision-maker; half a day can now accomplish what used to take two weeks, but the burden of thinking has actually grown heavier.

About Guo Yu (as recounted in this episode)

  • Worked at Alipay in 2011; first encountered Bitcoin at an Alipay sharing session in April 2013 (it was about US$100 at the time).
  • Went to Beijing in 2014 to join a friend's startup (from the Duoshuo / Tuchong lineage, backed by the Weibo fund); in December of the same year the company was acquired by Toutiao, and he thereby joined ByteDance.
  • At ByteDance he worked for a long time on the middle platform, and later on the mini-program engine (micro runtime) running on Douyin / TikTok; he describes himself as a "technical executor" who has never led a team and does not want to.
  • He decided to leave at the end of 2019: at that time ByteDance's cost of acquiring a new user externally was as high as more than RMB 50, and "even app factories found it hard to get new users"; he judged that the mobile internet cycle was basically over. After a six-month handover, he traveled to Japan for the New Year in 2020, ran into the pandemic, could not return to Beijing, and simply stayed in Japan, obtaining a long-term visa and eventually permanent residency, where he remains to this day.
  • He had intended to leave the tech industry entirely to rest, but after only two or three years of rest he was pulled back by the AI wave at the end of 2022; he has now built more than 30 products with Vibe Coding (many unpublished), while managing over a hundred agent servers.

Core Views

1. The Engineer's Role Flips: From Executor to Questioner and Decision-Maker

  • The iteration cycle that used to take two weeks (set goals → develop → design → test → release → observe experiments) can now be run through in half a day by 1–2 people.
  • But people have not become more relaxed: you have to play architect, designer and product manager at the same time — most importantly product manager — being good at asking questions, able to constrain the language model's "language space," and knowing what language and what structure to use to keep it moving forward and adjust direction based on experimental results.
  • AI cannot make fully automated decisions, not because it lacks context, but because it has no proactive intent, so the entire psychological burden of decision-making falls back on the person.
  • The consequence: from October 2025 to early 2026, he saw many engineer friends burn out — doing in two or three months what used to take one or two years, their brains unable to keep up with AI's speed, becoming anxious and starting to rethink "what is actually worth doing."
  • Changes in working style: he himself now rarely uses "planning mode"; he first talks with the model for half an hour to talk the spec through thoroughly, then lets it run toward the goal in one go; as SOTA models and harnesses keep evolving, people also have to keep adapting to new tools, which is itself one of the sources of burnout.

2. The Form of Software Has Changed: Generated Instantly, Disposable After Use, No Longer Needing Distribution

  • Software is no longer hard-coded code: it can be coded instantly to complete a task, discarded after use, and used only by oneself or a very small organization; each person's software is incompatible with everyone else's, and there is no need for it to be compatible.
  • "First-class harnesses" have appeared: the first-layer harnesses made by the model vendors themselves (Claude Code, Codex, DeepSeek Harness), whose progress has already "internalized" a large number of traditional software functions — using them to build a CRM or a management system is a matter of two minutes or five minutes, and software of this kind no longer needs to exist as an independent product.
  • The next step is change at the operating system level: treating the Agent, rather than the Application, as a first-class citizen. Some people are already building Linux-based Agent OSes; OpenAI took a different, pragmatic path — buying large numbers of Macs so the next generation of models can directly use Macs (a mature Unix + a mature creative / film and television software ecosystem).
  • His judgment: all the computing resources we rent in the future will become "Virtual Agent Servers" — a virtual machine in which an Agent operates the entire system; a person issues instructions from a phone and remotely connects back to a machine at home or in a data center.
  • Security warning: when everyone uses Agents to manage computers, once an Agent's connection is hijacked, it amounts to the world's largest botnet cluster. The first time he let an Agent fully automatically buy a server, install keys and delete the initial password, his first reaction was precisely this risk.

3. Model Tiering: You Don't Need the Strongest, Only the Most Suitable

A five-tier division proposed by the host and broadly endorsed by Guo Yu: 1. Personal convenience type: handling everyday needs such as making phone calls, booking restaurants and managing information, e.g. Meta's Muse, integrated into Messenger / WhatsApp / Instagram, with no need for a separate app; 2. Personal productivity type: writing code and creating content, using SOTA models such as Claude and Codex; 3. Enterprise collaboration type: getting Agents to learn to use Macs / computers to string together enterprise workflows, with higher requirements; 4. Entertainment generation type: where ByteDance has the advantage — Seedance generation + consumption on the Hongguo platform have already formed a closed loop, plus creator tools; 5. Research frontier type: requiring the very top models, which model companies may not release publicly, but instead sign framework agreements and profit-sharing arrangements with research institutes and defense orders. - Guo Yu added a sixth category, "on-site AI": scenarios such as farms, factories and cameras involve enormous volumes of information and must run 24 hours a day using edge computing + extremely cheap models; it is impossible to send all the images to OpenAI.

4. Enterprise Adoption Is Still Very Early, Only 2%–3% Complete Overall

  • Large enterprises (including Japanese enterprises) have many concerns: no one can take responsibility for AI's decisions, middle managers who see opportunities do not dare to sign off, and only the board can make decisions of this kind; there are also concerns about data leakage, and reluctance to use models from other countries.
  • Microsoft's transformation of Copilot into an "operating system" centered on Agents, letting Agents operate Office, is precisely aimed at pushing enterprise adoption.
  • Small companies and one-person companies will naturally shift to the new solutions; positioned from an investment perspective: although AI's impact on organizations and business is accelerating, it is only 2%–3% complete, and a great many things remain unsolved.

5. Intelligence Will Become a "Public Good," and Software Will Be as Cheap as Milk (Source of the Title)

  • The entertainment generation market is a perfectly competitive market: there will always be someone using cheaper tokens to run more experiments, and pricing will ultimately be pushed down close to the model's generation cost, with profits so thin as to be almost nonexistent — intelligence will, like milk, become extremely cheap through centralized, large-scale production, becoming a kind of public goods (quasi-public good) and greatly raising social productivity.
  • For that reason his own focus has already shifted from "creating software" to the next stage — "creating the purpose that software itself is meant to achieve." He admits he is currently in a period of perplexed observation: he plans to observe for three to six months first before deciding where to put his energy, and mentioned that a friend (Xiaoyou, whose company was sold to Cloudflare) is also burned out and thinking about the next stage — back then, after ByteDance was founded in 2012, it also took two years to validate its first idea and only took off once the environment had matured; when the environment is not yet mature, forcing software distribution along the old startup mindset may be meaningless.

6. Population Divergence: The 2%–3% of Pro Users Are Amplified, While the Masses Fill Their Time with Entertainment

  • Only about 2%–3% of people can become Pro users amplified by AI: one person equal to a company of 5–60 people in the past, able to manage an "army" of agents.
  • The masses have no ideas and are unwilling to raise demands (taking assignments at work, watching TV after work); their time will be filled by entertainment AI; the essence of competition among all entertainment products is direct competition for users' time, and the product form does not matter.
  • The younger generation carries no baggage: with less material pressure, they follow interest communities more, like the "Heisei otaku" after Japan's bubble burst — working odd jobs three days a week and surfing four days, accumulating their interests to a professional level and even going to compete in the Olympics. He judges that this may not be a bad thing.

7. Everything is Media: What Is Delivered Is a "Sense of a Real Person"

  • After AI content (slop) flooded in, the signal-to-noise ratio became extremely low, and differentiation can only rely on real people appearing on camera: real-person content accounts for a small share of the whole internet and stands out the most (as one blogger summarized: if you want your product to get traffic, the only thing you need to do is appear on camera in person to introduce the product).
  • The host's formulation: productize "yourself" — have your own thinking organized by an AI Agent into content that can be read, subscribed to, and even provided via API, so that others form a mental connection with you; "software is media, Everything is Media" — the thing you vibe code, its actions and results are themselves media, and after users finish digesting it they want to see your next piece, just like swiping through videos.
  • Guo Yu added: whether distributing AI content or real-person content, the essence is hacking the new recommendation / traffic mechanisms; real-person content is scarce, and if done well it will be thrown into a larger traffic pool.

8. Advice for Young People: Don't Treat "a Stable Job" as the Anchor of Your Life

  • "Going to work" is a product of the Industrial Revolution, with only a little over 100 years of history, and will disappear or change drastically after the intelligence revolution.
  • View work with an investment mindset: if a job is like a stock you are trapped in, on a very long downhill slope, the key is not agonizing over "how much loss to take before exiting," but whether there is a better opportunity — opportunity cost is the most expensive. When his father went into business back then, he spent a few hundred yuan a month on "unpaid leave with job retention" to preserve his seniority, and a few years later the mine went bankrupt anyway; the layoffs in Northeast China were the same logic.
  • Concrete advice: stop pursuing a stable job; you can go south, go and look around Shenzhen — the ethos of Chaoshan and Fujian families, "rather be a small boss than work for someone else," turns out to be advantageous in the AI era; become your own OPC (one-person company); if you don't know how, go and look first, and learn from experienced people. But if you can join a frontier AI studio and become one of "the people who create," that kind of work is still worth doing.
  • The host's closing characterization: this is not another industrial revolution, but a Renaissance — "as long as you have ideas with infinite possibilities, you have tools with infinite possibilities to do things with infinite possibilities."

ByteDance Stories Worth Remembering

  • ByteDance in 2015 was already an AI-driven company: products relied on neural network recommendations, algorithm engineers called themselves "parameter-tuning engineers," and even they did not fully know how the system worked, relying on experiments to approach the desired effect — that was when Guo Yu first realized code runs inside a black box, exactly like today's large models.
  • 2015 was a tough year for ByteDance: Toutiao's DAU/MAU hit a bottleneck and employee attrition was severe; Douyin and Huoshan, built over more than ten months, came out in December 2016, and the situation only improved in 2017.
  • Resources were initially bet on Huoshan's short videos (positioned for the mass market); Douyin started as a small team, was not even called Douyin, and took a form like Musical.ly; after acquiring Musical.ly, Douyin overtook it through "user generalization" (expanding from 18–20-somethings to ages 7 to 70), ultimately completing Huoshan's historical mission, and the two were merged.
  • The lesson from Weibo's recommendation feed back then: with the relationship network in front, users complained "I can't see the people I follow anymore," and the fight between recommendations and following went on for many years without being done well.
  • A classic trick for hacking Douyin's content algorithm: deliberately adding typos in video subtitles could significantly raise the completion rate — because viewers would rewatch to confirm whether the characters were wrong; ByteDance had a dedicated team fighting such hacks, and would block them quickly once identified.
