  • 承诺（全场最硬的一句表态）：公司会按自己的节奏推进 AI 系统的训练与部署，花多长时间都可以，只要能确信系统安全且始终处于人类控制之下；不会向商业压力或政治压力低头。上市让公司财务更强，只会强化这个决心。

  • 收尾：机器智能的降临是人类历史上决定性的时刻之一，人类有难得的机会把它塑造成更好的样子。

Note: This video is the full Nasdaq opening bell ceremony on Anthropic's first day of listing, with three speeches plus the bell ringing; no trading data such as the issue price, opening price or gains were announced at the ceremony. The following are summaries of the speakers' live remarks, not verbatim translations.

In one sentence

Anthropic officially listed on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT; at the bell-ringing ceremony, Nasdaq Chair Adena Friedman and the Amodei siblings gave speeches that addressed a single theme throughout: how a company that has written "safety and public benefit" into its charter proves to public markets that mission and commercial success can coexist.

1. Opening by Jack Castle (00:00–01:01)

Jack Castle, Nasdaq's head of listings for the western US, hosted the ceremony. He said Anthropic has from the start been guided by a clear mission and committed to building advanced AI responsibly, which is precisely the significance of today's listing celebration; he then introduced Nasdaq Chair and CEO Adena Friedman.

2. Speech by Adena Friedman (01:01–03:21)

  • She welcomed co-founder and CEO Dario Amodei, co-founder and President Daniela Amodei, the whole Anthropic team, investors and guests to Nasdaq.
  • Framing: Artificial intelligence may be the most defining technology of our era, and Anthropic has from day one confronted both its promise and its risks head-on.
  • Company background: Anthropic was founded in 2021 as a public benefit corporation, with a mission to responsibly develop and maintain advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity; its result is Claude, now used by scientists, developers, businesses and consumers worldwide to do meaningful work.
  • Listing means a new kind of responsibility: public markets require a company to be transparent, accountable, and to earn investor trust every day; for a company built on trust, that discipline is not a burden but an extension of its mission.
  • Nasdaq calls itself the "home of the innovation economy" and said it is proud to stand alongside Anthropic in facing the biggest questions this generation of technology raises.

3. Speech by Daniela Amodei (03:27–08:32)

  • Opening framing: Anthropic is an "unusual, even a bit weird" company, and deliberately "built backwards" — it had an AI safety team before launching its first product; after Claude was released, it refused the advertising model that sustains most of the internet; when a new model was found to be particularly good at finding security vulnerabilities, they held it back first and gave priority access to defenders of critical systems.
  • Public benefit corporation status means: legally, public benefit must be weighed alongside shareholder returns; they care equally about people who do not use Claude.
  • Customers: More than half of the Fortune 500 use Claude (drug discovery, underwriting, preventing financial fraud, etc.).
  • Three short stories:
  • An elementary school principal in São Paulo used to manually register student grades in a 40-page booklet, and analysing what each child needed took 30 days; this month she photographed the pages and gave them to Claude, which finished in a few minutes — for a child who is falling behind, that is one month less of waiting to be noticed.
  • Blackington in Massachusetts (founded in 1852, hand-making police and fire badges) used to take 6–10 weeks to deliver orders; production planning manager Edgar used Claude to build new tools for the company's 40-year-old legacy system, cutting the delivery cycle in half.
  • Open Evidence (a medical research tool powered by Claude): US clinicians alone used it 42 million times in August this year; it is working with Anthropic to expand free access to clinicians in about 100 countries, including Haiti and Mongolia.
  • Other customers named: Walmart, Bristol Myers Squibb (researchers found in 5 minutes answers that used to take nearly two months to find), and a community restaurant.
  • One reason for listing: to let more people "buy in" and take part in building AI.
  • To people uneasy about AI: she said what this technology needs is your voices — not trust, not use, but speaking up; the ending of the story is not yet written, intelligence is now something humans can build, and what to build with it is decided by everyone together.

4. Speech by Dario Amodei (08:41–13:38)

  • Personal motivation: His and Daniela's father died of a disease that "became curable a few years later"; his career began in biology, wanting future therapies to arrive faster. In the lab he saw that tools able to cut through the complexity of disease — gene editing, gene synthesis, optogenetic control of brain cells — all began as pure ideas, from thought, from intelligence.
  • Judgement: Sufficiently powerful AI working with humans could compress a century of scientific and medical progress into a decade; but he stressed that so far there are only early results, and experiments and clinical trials still take time.
  • Where Claude is applied: protein folding, drug design, cancer, genetics, clinical trial management; as well as economic growth, poverty alleviation, protecting democracy and free societies, and even making everyday services such as the DMV work better.
  • He was blunt about risks: misuse by individuals or nation-state actors, economic shocks, humans losing control of AI systems. He said he talks about these risks not because they are inevitable, but because they are avoidable — depending on the choices of the builders; "negligence on safety" and "obsession with safety" will lead to very different AI futures.
  • Safety practices: safeguards against biological and cyber risks, alignment training and testing, "looking inside the model" like an X-ray (interpretability) — methods largely pioneered by Anthropic.
