纪要｜《郭宇｜关于AI一人公司，大家最想知道的都在这里》

视频：https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=diN1fiE52aI ｜时长 63 分钟｜郭宇一人公司/Wanman 主题分享后的集中问答 说明：机器转写，繁简混杂、个别专名有误听（Wanman 转写为「万万/弯慢/One慢」，OpenClaw 转写为「OpenCloud」，SOTA 转写为「sortar」等）；纪要按上下文校正。产品数据为讲述者现场自述，未经核实。本片由若干段问答剪辑而成，开场的太空数据中心话题在片尾又出现了一次，纪要只记一遍。

一句话

这一场是「一人公司」主题的问答合集，核心信息只有三条：①用 AI 的心法是「不要把 AI 当工具，把它当人」——信任靠把你的审美写成文档 + 花时间长谈来建立；②Wanman 的真实用法曝光：它最大的价值不是从零创业，而是「接管模式」——接管你已有的代码仓库、自动修 bug、补测试，24 小时不停，这是他做这个产品 80% 的动机；③成本结构：不用 API、用订阅制，Codex Max 每月 200 美元跑满额度，API 贵 10–12 倍。

信任问题：怎么敢把活全交给 AI

观众最集中的焦虑（尤其是程序员）：代码我能 review，商业计划书、财务模型我看不懂，怎么知道它不是幻觉？

他的回答分三层： - 写下来：如果你有强烈的主观意愿和审美，就把它写成文档、写成 Skill 文件，AI 才可能稳定复现你的风格；你想控制每个细节，前提是先把细节说清楚。 - 用时间换信任：「你有没有和 AI 彻夜长谈三个小时？」没有的话，它不理解你要控制的距离和细节，你也不理解它。本质和人与人建立信任一样。 - 让市场裁定：AI 做的财务计划会联网检索、交叉对比并附参考文档；至于计划本身合不合理，他没有专业能力评判——拿去银行谈融资，银行经理认可，它就是合理。隐私政策、用户条款他全部交给 Codex/Claude 写，自己从没逐条看过。他的底层立场：对 SOTA 模型有基本信任（做不到 99 分，60–70 分是有的），两三年前还要频繁做事实确认，现在顶尖模型（他举例 GPT-5.5、Opus 4.7）已经不会再编造一个打开是 404 的论文链接。

他明确不做的事：省 token 的记忆压缩、各种成本优化技巧——「那是模型公司该干的事」。他做的只有「调度」：让 agent ①懂各行各业怎么做事，②有协调能力（发短信、发邮件、打电话），两点齐了产生广泛可组合性，就是超级个体。算力成本问题留给模型进化解决。

Wanman 现状（自述）

  • 发布 3 天，沙箱被打满；东京一台 128 核服务器，用户约 4,000–5,000 人（他没看 DAU，估算）。瓶颈不在模型，在虚拟化技术——支撑上万人、百万级在线的沙箱基础设施还没有。
  • 从今年 1 月做到现在，自评 80 分：整个产品像乐高，每个小方块（包括沙箱基础设施）都是他自己搭的。
  • 免费、部分开源，不挣钱：「还没到足够高的品质。」他把这归为开源文化：早期阶段先把东西做得足够好、变成公共领域的知识，收费和持续运营是后面的事（对比日本社会更倾向先申请专利）。

两个模式

从零模式——苹果农场案例：一个真实任务，用户只给了一句话：「运营一个观光苹果农场，帮我做好对应的 IT 系统和所有基础设施（并制定持续盈利方案）。」AI 员工团分工：CEO 不写代码、只复核每轮任务是否完成；开发做实施计划与选型；财务出 36 个月财务预测（种多少树、多久收获、回本前投多少、门票定价、靠什么产品赚钱）；市场只产出一份增长策略报告。给了预算（例：5,000 万日元封顶）它会自己找地、查去哪买树、查 JA 贷款渠道。40 多分钟出完整计划。人类阻断点是刻意设计的：需要人出面时它停下来，把合同等文件全部备齐，甚至可以用他的翻译电话产品（车点 AI）自动打电话预约时间，然后明确告诉你「这一步你不做完，我没法继续」。

接管模式——他自称这是做 Wanman 80% 的原因：接管任意已有代码仓库，给目标即可，例如「把测试覆盖率提到 100%，不到不许结束」，它自动跑到达标。他名下约 15 个上线项目，所有 bug 走 webhook 进 Wanman 由 AI 员工自动修、自动跑；没有 bug 就暂停，有新 bug 再启动。他早上起来常看到 100 来个分支、几十个 PR，自己看不过来，就交给本地的 Claude Code / Codex 审阅合并，也可以新开一个 Story 让 Wanman 自己审 PR、自动合并。

另一个接管案例（日本蓝莓农场）：为农场主做自动灌溉/施肥系统的完整调研——传感器选型（土壤水分、pH、树龄与生育阶段）、300 棵树 10 个区域的物料清单、数据表设计、现地调查报告模板。他的判断：这本质上是咨询公司干的活，日本中小企业极度依赖咨询公司把想法变成文件再找 IT 团队，Wanman 把这条链压缩了。

成本：订阅制而非 API——Codex 在订阅 plan 里跑到 5 小时额度/周额度为止，他的 Codex Max 和 Claude 都是 200 美元/月；接 API 约贵 10–12 倍。他提到 Claude 在他的 Wanman 接入里试过、很快被封号，所以主力用 Codex。（此为他个人经历自述。）

新手与组织

  • 完全不会编程的人从哪开始：从你去年的工作开始、从小事开始——先让它做采访提纲，再到剪辑、素材搜索，在建立信任的过程里逐步给它宏大目标。他现场建议：打开 wanman.ai，注册 Codex 账户授权后，把自己的 YouTube 频道链接发给它，说「帮我安排接下来 17 个采访计划」，它会自己去查、自己安排。对宏大目标（比如「做到百万粉丝」），AI 给出的方案会有很多你不认同的东西，但也会有意想不到的思路，有助于重新思考自己想做的事。
  • 本地派 vs 云端派：OpenClaw 式 local-first 在你电脑里跑，能拿到你全部数据（包括隐私、信用卡），高效但危险；云端派算力更多、连接性更广，但上下文要靠上传补齐。Wanman 是云端派。他预告文件夹上传功能很容易补上。
  • 大公司现状：非常混乱，第一反应普遍是裁员替换；硅谷有些公司把 AI 装进每个员工的电脑进行监控，让它在员工的工作里自我进化。他认为转型期人的位置更像观察者。
  • 股票盯盘需求：定投 DCA 券商（如长桥）默认支持，不必自己造；真要自动化，用 OpenClaw/Codex 操作已登录的券商网页即可，但强烈建议放在一台单独设备上，安全得多——它对你电脑里的事无所不知。
  • 怎么让别人（比如女友）用起来：没有技巧。先让他亲身感受它有多厉害，动力只能从内心涌现，逼不出来；很多人还没紧迫到要考虑生计。七八岁的小孩和成年人的差距，很多就在这件事上。
  • 付费建议：公司不报销、纯业余兴趣的人，没有必要强花这笔钱；但想用 AI 武装自己的人，从今天就该开始。有些东西 AI 替代不了：金牌销售的资源、体力劳动。

收尾的两个大话题

退休后要「足够放松」：有观众问，如果还在字节、会不会为竞争力焦虑。他说自己从来不是好员工，只在乎事情好不好玩。混乱中才会建立新秩序，时代一成不变，普通人就没有机会——现在没钱，反而有机会分一杯羹。太执着于目标肌肉会紧绷，事情做不好；所有目标都需要你足够放松。

权力终局（开场与结尾同一段论述）：放松的前提是看清本质——谁拥有能源与计算能力。马斯克要把数据中心建到太空、美国要在月球建永久基地，先建通信和太空推理数据中心，有持续且绝对安全的能源后，超级 AI 可以全自动运行一个文明（比如月球），制造在月球、推理回德州——这种权力集中度是人类没遇到过的。他的判语：「我个人觉得所有的科技主义者都是反人类主义者，他们想把人变成神、变成万能的东西」；这条路从工业革命起就埋在所有人心里、越走越快，不会停。结论落在生活层面：安稳的小日子会越来越难、也越来越值得珍惜。

另有一段现场演示：他让 Wanman 跑「成为百万粉丝 YouTuber」的任务，AI 先算海外中文用户规模、对标频道、内容定位（不是做中文新闻台，而是帮海外华人解决身份、关系、家庭问题），排了每周两条长视频的更新表，并做了成本-收入模型——结论是 20 万到 50 万粉之前都是亏的，所以千万别请人，全用 AI 做。

以及关于记录：他从小写日记，现在愿意把生活公开发出来——在 AI 时代这格外有价值：你发到互联网上的一切，最终都会成为 AI 模型的一部分，是你留给世界的 legacy。

從零模式——蘋果農場案例：一個真實任務，用戶只給了一句話：「運營一個觀光蘋果農場，幫我做好對應的 IT 系統和所有基礎設施（並制定持續盈利方案）。」AI 員工團分工：CEO 不寫程式碼、只覆核每輪任務是否完成；開發做實施計劃與選型；財務出 36 個月財務預測（種多少樹、多久收穫、回本前投多少、門票定價、靠什麼產品賺錢）；市場只產出一份增長策略報告。給了預算（例：5,000 萬日元封頂）它會自己找地、查去哪買樹、查 JA 貸款渠道。40 多分鐘出完整計劃。人類阻斷點是刻意設計的：需要人出面時它停下來，把合約等檔案全部備齊，甚至可以用他的翻譯電話產品（車點 AI）自動打電話預約時間，然後明確告訴你「這一步你不做完，我沒法繼續」。

In One Sentence

This session is a compilation of Q&A on the "one-person company" theme, and its core information comes down to just three points: (1) The mindset for using AI is "don't treat AI as a tool, treat it as a person" — trust is built by writing your taste into documents + spending time in long conversations; (2) Wanman's real use is revealed: its greatest value is not starting a business from zero, but "takeover mode" — taking over your existing code repositories, automatically fixing bugs and adding tests, running 24 hours without stopping, which accounts for 80% of his motivation for building the product; (3) The cost structure: no API, subscription only — Codex Max at $200 per month used to its full quota, while the API costs 10–12 times more.

The Trust Question: How Do You Dare Hand All the Work to AI?

The audience's most concentrated anxiety (especially among programmers): I can review code, but I can't read business plans or financial models — how do I know it isn't hallucinating?

His answer has three layers: - Write it down: If you have strong subjective preferences and taste, write them into documents, into Skill files, and only then can AI stably reproduce your style; if you want to control every detail, the prerequisite is to spell the details out clearly first. - Trade time for trust: "Have you ever had a three-hour all-night conversation with AI?" If not, it doesn't understand the distance and details you want to control, and you don't understand it either. In essence it is the same as building trust between people. - Let the market decide: The financial plans AI makes will search online, cross-check and attach reference documents; as for whether the plan itself is reasonable, he has no professional ability to judge — take it to a bank to discuss financing, and if the bank manager approves it, it is reasonable. He hands his privacy policy and user terms entirely to Codex/Claude to write and has never read them line by line himself. His underlying stance: he has basic trust in SOTA models (they may not score 99, but 60–70 is there); two or three years ago frequent fact-checking was still needed, but today's top models (he cites GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.7 as examples) no longer fabricate a paper link that opens to a 404.

What he explicitly does not do: memory compression to save tokens, and various cost-optimization tricks — "that is the model companies' job." What he does is only "orchestration": making agents (1) understand how work is done in different industries, and (2) have coordination ability (sending texts, sending emails, making phone calls); when both are in place, broad composability emerges, and that is the super individual. The compute cost problem is left for model evolution to solve.

Wanman's Current State (His Account)

  • Three days after release, the sandbox was maxed out; one 128-core server in Tokyo, with about 4,000–5,000 users (he hasn't checked DAU; this is an estimate). The bottleneck is not the model but virtualization technology — sandbox infrastructure supporting tens of thousands of users and millions online does not yet exist.
  • Built from January this year to now, he rates it 80 points: the whole product is like Lego, and every small block (including the sandbox infrastructure) was built by himself.
  • Free, partially open source, not making money: "It hasn't reached a high enough quality yet." He attributes this to open-source culture: in the early stage, first make the thing good enough and turn it into public-domain knowledge; charging and sustained operation come later (in contrast, Japanese society tends more toward filing patents first).

Two Modes

From-zero mode — the apple farm case: A real task where the user gave only one sentence: "Run a sightseeing apple farm, and build the corresponding IT system and all the infrastructure for me (and develop a plan for sustained profitability)." The AI employee team divided the work: the CEO writes no code and only checks whether each round of tasks is complete; development handles implementation plans and technology selection; finance produced a 36-month financial forecast (how many trees to plant, how long until harvest, how much to invest before breaking even, ticket pricing, and which products make money); marketing produced only a growth strategy report. Given a budget (e.g., capped at 50 million yen), it finds land itself, checks where to buy trees, and checks JA loan channels. A complete plan in just over 40 minutes. Human blocking points are deliberately designed: when a person needs to step in, it stops, prepares all the documents such as contracts, and can even use his translation phone product (Chedian AI) to automatically call and book an appointment time, then tells you clearly, "If you don't finish this step, I can't continue."

Takeover mode — he says this is 80% of the reason he built Wanman: Take over any existing code repository and just give it a goal, for example "raise test coverage to 100%, and don't stop until you get there," and it runs automatically until the target is met. He has about 15 live projects under his name; all bugs flow into Wanman via webhook and are automatically fixed and run by AI employees; when there are no bugs it pauses, and when new bugs appear it starts again. In the morning he often wakes up to see over 100 branches and dozens of PRs, more than he can review himself, so he hands them to local Claude Code / Codex to review and merge, or he can open a new Story and let Wanman review and auto-merge PRs itself.

Another takeover case (a Japanese blueberry farm): a complete research project for the farm owner on an automatic irrigation/fertilization system — sensor selection (soil moisture, pH, tree age and growth stage), a bill of materials for 300 trees across 10 zones, data table design, and an on-site survey report template. His judgment: this is essentially the work consulting firms do; Japanese SMEs rely heavily on consulting firms to turn ideas into documents and then find IT teams, and Wanman compresses that chain.

Cost: Subscription rather than API — Codex runs within the subscription plan until the 5-hour quota / weekly quota is used up; his Codex Max and Claude are both $200/month; using the API costs about 10–12 times more. He mentioned that he tried connecting Claude into his Wanman setup and the account was banned very quickly, so Codex is the main tool. (This is his personal experience as recounted by him.)

Beginners and Organizations

  • Where should someone who can't program at all start: Start from your work last year, start from small things — first have it make interview outlines, then move to editing and material search, and gradually give it grander goals in the process of building trust. His on-site suggestion: open wanman.ai, register and authorize a Codex account, then send it the link to your own YouTube channel and say "help me arrange my next 17 interview plans," and it will research and arrange them itself. For grand goals (such as "reach a million followers"), the plans AI gives will contain many things you disagree with, but also unexpected ideas that help you rethink what you want to do.
  • Local camp vs. cloud camp: OpenClaw-style local-first runs inside your computer and can access all your data (including privacy and credit cards) — efficient but dangerous; the cloud camp has more compute and broader connectivity, but context has to be filled in through uploads. Wanman is in the cloud camp. He previewed that a folder upload feature would be very easy to add.
  • The state of large companies: Very chaotic, with the first reaction generally being layoffs and replacement; some Silicon Valley companies install AI on every employee's computer for monitoring, letting it self-evolve within employees' work. He believes that in the transition period, people's position is more like that of observers.
  • Stock-watching needs: Brokers supporting DCA regular investment (such as Longbridge) support it by default, so there is no need to build it yourself; if you really want automation, just use OpenClaw/Codex to operate the already-logged-in broker webpage, but it is strongly recommended to put it on a separate device, which is much safer — it knows everything about what is on your computer.
  • How to get someone else (e.g., a girlfriend) to start using it: There is no trick. First let them experience firsthand how powerful it is; motivation can only emerge from within and cannot be forced; many people are not yet pressed enough to think about their livelihood. Much of the gap between a seven- or eight-year-old child and an adult lies in exactly this.
  • Spending advice: For people whose company won't reimburse it and who are purely hobbyists, there is no need to force themselves to spend this money; but people who want to arm themselves with AI should start today. Some things AI cannot replace: a top salesperson's resources, and physical labor.

Two Big Topics at the Close

After retirement, you need to be "relaxed enough": An audience member asked whether, if he were still at ByteDance, he would feel anxious about competitiveness. He said he was never a good employee and only cares whether something is fun. New order is only built amid chaos; if the era never changes, ordinary people have no opportunity — having no money now actually gives you a chance to get a share. Being too fixated on a goal tenses your muscles and things go badly; every goal requires you to be relaxed enough.

The endgame of power (the same argument at the opening and the close): The prerequisite for relaxation is seeing the essence clearly — who owns energy and computing power. Musk wants to build data centers in space, the US wants to build a permanent base on the Moon, first building communications and space inference data centers; once there is a continuous and absolutely secure energy supply, a super AI can fully automatically run a civilization (for example on the Moon), with manufacturing on the Moon and inference back in Texas — this degree of power concentration is something humanity has never encountered. His verdict: "Personally, I think all technologists are anti-humanists; they want to turn people into gods, into omnipotent beings"; this path has been buried in everyone's heart since the Industrial Revolution, moving faster and faster, and it will not stop.
