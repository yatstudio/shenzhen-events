纪要｜《郭宇关于AI的十次分享：AI做影片》

视频：https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcMssIgsvdI ｜时长 2 小时 23 分｜郭宇 + 孙静涵主持，现场分享 + 实操演示 + 问答 说明：机器转写，繁简混杂、个别专名有误听（如 C-Dance=Seedance、即梦转写为「寂梦」、Visio Studio 音近「VCO/Wesley」）；纪要按上下文校正。文中行业数字与传闻均为讲述者现场说法，未经核实。

一句话

AI 视频已经越过「猫猫狗狗娱乐短片」阶段，进入严肃影视生产：中国创作者用 Seedance 等工具做出的 3–5 分钟短片已达流媒体级质量，真人短剧行业大批从业者没活干、转做 AI 漫剧。郭宇的判断是：这相当于短视频史上的 2016 年——门槛被拉平之后，AI 原生内容会是 PGC+UGC 的百倍千倍；而人的位置只剩一个：做那个定故事、定世界观、在无限生成结果里做选择的人。

行业现状（他刚从国内回来的一手观察）

  • 行业实况（他的现场观察）：真人短剧剧组大批接不到生意、从业者纷纷转向「AI 漫剧」（AI 做的真人剧/动漫剧）；字节的红果短剧按流量分成，推着创作者用 AI 工具。
  • 质量跃升点在今年 5 月：相当多「可上流媒体」的严肃短片出现，几乎都出自中国创作者，在 X、Instagram 上几千万播放。现场放了三部：音乐 MV 出身的创作者做的废土风短片（对标 Netflix《爱死机》单集 100–300 万美元成本，AI 版是几千分之一）；署名「牛豆」的作品——一个人包办导演、剪辑、音乐、美术；贾樟柯与字节合作的 Seedance 短片（贾樟柯与自己的 AI 数字人对谈，全片生成）。
  • 成本账：一小时严肃 AI 视频，用 Seedance + 音乐生成、按 50% 容错（抽两条留一条）算，约 2,000 美元。对工作室不算钱，对个人创作者必须能回收；明后年还会大幅降。
  • Netflix 的动向（转述）：他小姨在 Netflix 工作，据称其团队在做一个新平台——影视生产的框架全保留，但 90% 内容交给 AI，人类只留编剧角色负责讲故事，小团队负责用 AI 把故事呈现出来。郭宇称未来会看到「全部 Powered by AI」的流媒体平台。
  • 版权收紧反而是好事：Seedance 2.0 后，迪士尼、美国作家协会发函，含版权角色（米老鼠等）和真人权限只开放给特定 API——普通人被迫不依赖现成 IP、创造自己的角色和故事。他预言一两个月内会出现第一批受大众喜欢的 AI 原生 IP，00 后、05 后的童年将伴随 AI 动画长大。
  • 为什么是中国创作者领先：中国科技公司把推理算力大量投给视频模型做消费娱乐，字节 Seedance 目前全球略微领先——赢在人物一致性、镜头语言和出错率低。等 OpenAI/Anthropic 赶上，会在全球范围爆发。

方法论：AI 做片的实际工作流（现场演示）

整条流水线已经不需要专业软件，一个 Codex / Claude Code 就能跑：

  1. 故事与分镜：把想法告诉 Codex，它给创意选项，生成分镜脚本——先定世界观、人物性格与情感基调（先定「四个点」：世界观、人物、情感、冲突），再逐镜头拆解。
  2. 分镜图：GPT Image 2 生成分镜图片（15–20 秒一张），人物设定卡/三视图保证一致性。
  3. 生成视频：分镜图 + 氛围/音乐描述丢给 Seedance（或经字节 API 到即梦），10–15 秒一段；失败率约一半，用参考图/参考视频（比如指定「只拿一个行李箱」）能大幅降低失败率。
  4. 自动质检迭代：让 Codex 对照分镜检查生成结果（哪里不符合、为什么），再重生成——「就像拍电影不断 retake」；可以给它一笔预算（「今天把这 500 美元花完」）让它无人值守地跑。
  5. 声音：旁白/配音跨集保持一致；音乐用 Suno（它想做音乐界的平台/Spotify）。环境音、氛围都要在提示词里写明。

提示词的颗粒度是核心手艺：不能只写「依依不舍地道别」——要写清谁先入镜、镜头怎么摇、握手还是拥抱、表情定几个、几秒、谁说话、说什么、停顿在哪、什么口音。抽象情绪词对 AI 没意义，必须翻译成可见的画面指令。

他自己的两个实证： - 给翻译电话产品 Tua 做广告：全程没找网红，两个视频广告 10 分钟做完挂上官网（有 bug 也懒得修，反而让人直观感到是 AI 生成）。 - Visio Studio（今年 1 月起做）：起因是小姨（硅谷工程师、写了十几本小说，包括讲 2000 年 dot-com 泡沫华人故事的《The Spring of Ice Age》和恐怖小说系列）的小说影视化版权卖给腾讯等平台后做得不满意。产品把长篇小说自动拆解：7 万字里分析出全部角色、生成设定与三视图、拆分镜、统一配音——本质是把编剧的活产品化。

观点交锋

  • 「匠人精神/古法手搓」之争：静涵带着偏见来（她自认），郭宇的回应：工具而已——从编辑器到 IDE 到 AI agent，他一直享受的是创造本身；先摘掉偏见、用起来，想法和视角自然会变。但他承认圈内人的创作惯性很难 think out of the box，这波领跑的恰恰多是行业外的人。
  • 风格问题：观众问「我不想要贾樟柯 style，我想要我自己的风格怎么办」——答：风格 = 你到底想表达什么，没说出口的东西 AI 理解不了；你要先做「导演」：把世界观、情感、表达全部定下来，让 AI 当伙伴而不是替你决定。和 AI 协作的好处是更快意识到自己做得好或不好。
  • 「无限解空间」的虚无：实拍有「上天给的礼物」（夕阳余晖那种不可复制的瞬间）；AI 生成则什么都能得到，于是问题反转——当解无限多，如何在无限解里找最优解、如何不麻木。这不止是做片，也是人生规划问题：明天可以做任何事的第一反应是触碰虚无。他把这留作今年后续专场的话题：IntentWare——人类如何产生意图。
  • Vlog 的真实性：他的点子是拍照片、中间用 AI 填充视频，解决「拍摄侵蚀生活」的博主困境；静涵质疑填充的不是真实发生的事。他回应：照片与足迹是真实记录，而人的记忆本来就脆弱、会被重塑。
  • 小众语言的机会：聊到《给阿嬷的情书》时他想到——为什么没有客家语电影？因为以前拍不起。AI 让任何语言（哪怕只剩极少使用者的语言）都能做成影视和音乐，这是只有 AI 能办到的事。
  • 教育：AI 不会「学导演」，但会让所有人变成导演；想学任何东西直接问 AI 即可，电影学院式学校的存在理由只剩「环境塑造性格」；前提始终是动机——AI 原生代的孩子里会出现拿学院奖的导演。

问答精选

  • 怎么保持兴趣广泛又专注（他做了很多不同软件）：兴趣驱动的前提是财富自由——「不然我也不会放弃本科专业去写代码」；没钱时人被房租和家庭推着走，很难直面意图。对世界的建议是：不要抱偏见、不要有上帝视角，固执地觉得「这事不该发生」只会让你在很多年后被动接受。
  • 跟 AI 沟通的本质：一位观众苦恼于「我说的它听不懂」。郭宇：AI 没体验过真实世界，光从哪个角度打过来都要你告诉它；但人与人之间也一样——语言靠的是极小的「公约数交集」和大量隐喻填充，听不懂常常不是 AI 的问题，而是表达方式的问题。解法：把意图约束到具体的语言空间里，不加隐喻、非常具体；同时把 AI 当无所不知的伙伴而不是工具，更多地从它那里学。
  • 工程师的短板：对大陆工程师来说，与 AI 协作最吃亏的是母语表达——文理分科让很多工程师不擅把想法说清楚，这成了使用 AI 的第一瓶颈。
  • 「先卖不存在的产品」创业法（回应一位想创业的观众）：用 GPT Image 生成逼真产品图、Seedance 做宣传视频，先上架预售——有人下单（现场案例 300–400 单）再联系中国的 3D 打印/工厂生产发货，没有订单就零成本放弃。「先把想法变成真实的销售意向」，是近乎无风险的创业验证。他顺带批评：华人社区「太缺乏想象力」，现在靠 AI 赚钱最显眼的是卖课的，但真正的机会在把想象力变成产品。
  • 小白焦虑：什么都学、什么都跟不上怎么办——主动性/意图是另一个大话题（预告专场）；外部约束（比如豆瓣小组的叫醒电话）有用，但根本是内心有没有强大的推动力。别把焦虑当行动。
  • 在日本做产品、职人不信任 AI 怎么办：信任的建立不在「产品与人」这个层面，而在人与人之间——日本人做生意讲信任感，过年过节送什么、平时怎么相处，比产品里是不是 AI 更重要；何况很多日本本土程序员做出来的东西也很粗糙，成品够好时多数人其实看不出来。
  • 收尾的工具建议：别再造轮子——先把第一层 harness 用好：Codex / Claude Code 能控制你的电脑、服务器上 24 小时跑着也不会停；买 $200 档、算力随便用就够了。他自嘲：之前做的 Wanman（实时语音助手）现在意义不大了，因为优秀模型厂商把这些能力都内置了，简单一个 API 请求就能调动。

值得记下的句子

  • 「AI 不会取代导演，它会让所有人都变成导演。」
  • 「故事如何设计、怎么把拼接起来的东西变得有趣，意图很难让 AI 自己表达——你必须做那个 lead。」
  • 「当你什么都能得到的时候，你会问：为什么我什么都做不出来？」
  • 「先把想法变成真实的销售意向——这是毫无风险的创业。」
  • 「我们永远没有办法成为上帝，尤其在 AI 的当下更是这样。」

影片：https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcMssIgsvdI ｜時長 2 小時 23 分｜郭宇 + 孫靜涵主持，現場分享 + 實操演示 + 問答 說明：機器轉寫，繁簡混雜、個別專名有誤聽（如 C-Dance=Seedance、即夢轉寫為「寂夢」、Visio Studio 音近「VCO/Wesley」）；紀要按上下文校正。文中行業數字與傳聞均為講述者現場說法，未經核實。

行業現狀（他剛從國內回來的一手觀察）

  • 行業實況（他的現場觀察）：真人短劇劇組大批接不到生意、從業者紛紛轉向「AI 漫劇」（AI 做的真人劇/動漫劇）；字節的紅果短劇按流量分成，推著創作者用 AI 工具。
  • 品質躍升點在今年 5 月：相當多「可上串流媒體」的嚴肅短片出現，幾乎都出自中國創作者，在 X、Instagram 上幾千萬播放。現場放了三部：音樂 MV 出身的創作者做的廢土風短片（對標 Netflix《愛死機》單集 100–300 萬美元成本，AI 版是幾千分之一）；署名「牛豆」的作品——一個人包辦導演、剪輯、音樂、美術；賈樟柯與字節合作的 Seedance 短片（賈樟柯與自己的 AI 數位人對談，全片生成）。
  • 成本帳：一小時嚴肅 AI 影片，用 Seedance + 音樂生成、按 50% 容錯（抽兩條留一條）算，約 2,000 美元。對工作室不算錢，對個人創作者必須能回收；明後年還會大幅降。

  • 版權收緊反而是好事：Seedance 2.0 後，迪士尼、美國作家協會發函，含版權角色（米老鼠等）和真人權限只開放給特定 API——普通人被迫不依賴現成 IP、創造自己的角色和故事。他預言一兩個月內會出現第一批受大眾喜歡的 AI 原生 IP，00 後、05 後的童年將伴隨 AI 動畫長大。
  • 為什麼是中國創作者領先：中國科技公司把推理算力大量投給影片模型做消費娛樂，字節 Seedance 目前全球略微領先——贏在人物一致性、鏡頭語言和出錯率低。等 OpenAI/Anthropic 趕上，會在全球範圍爆發。

方法論：AI 做片的實際工作流（現場演示）

整條流水線已經不需要專業軟體，一個 Codex / Claude Code 就能跑：

  1. 故事與分鏡：把想法告訴 Codex，它給創意選項，生成分鏡腳本——先定世界觀、人物性格與情感基調（先定「四個點」：世界觀、人物、情感、衝突），再逐鏡頭拆解。
  2. 分鏡圖：GPT Image 2 生成分鏡圖片（15–20 秒一張），人物設定卡/三視圖保證一致性。
  3. 生成影片：分鏡圖 + 氛圍/音樂描述丟給 Seedance（或經字節 API 到即夢），10–15 秒一段；失敗率約一半，用參考圖/參考影片（比如指定「只拿一個行李箱」）能大幅降低失敗率。
  4. 自動質檢迭代：讓 Codex 對照分鏡檢查生成結果（哪裡不符合、為什麼），再重生成——「就像拍電影不斷 retake」；可以給它一筆預算（「今天把這 500 美元花完」）讓它無人值守地跑。
  5. 聲音：旁白/配音跨集保持一致；音樂用 Suno（它想做音樂界的平台/Spotify）。環境音、氛圍都要在提示詞裡寫明。

提示詞的顆粒度是核心手藝：不能只寫「依依不捨地道別」——要寫清誰先入鏡、鏡頭怎麼搖、握手還是擁抱、表情定幾個、幾秒、誰說話、說什麼、停頓在哪、什麼口音。抽象情緒詞對 AI 沒意義，必須翻譯成可見的畫面指令。

他自己的兩個實證： - 給翻譯電話產品 Tua 做廣告：全程沒找網紅，兩個影片廣告 10 分鐘做完掛上官網（有 bug 也懶得修，反而讓人直觀感到是 AI 生成）。 - Visio Studio（今年 1 月起做）：起因是小姨（矽谷工程師、寫了十幾本小說，包括講 2000 年 dot-com 泡沫華人故事的《The Spring of Ice Age》和恐怖小說系列）的小說影視化版權賣給騰訊等平台後做得不滿意。產品把長篇小說自動拆解：7 萬字裡分析出全部角色、生成設定與三視圖、拆分鏡、統一配音——本質是把編劇的活產品化。

觀點交鋒

  • 「匠人精神/古法手搓」之爭：靜涵帶著偏見來（她自認），郭宇的回應：工具而已——從編輯器到 IDE 到 AI agent，他一直享受的是創造本身；先摘掉偏見、用起來，想法和視角自然會變。但他承認圈內人的創作慣性很難 think out of the box，這波領跑的恰恰多是行業外的人。
  • 風格問題：觀眾問「我不想要賈樟柯 style，我想要我自己的風格怎麼辦」——答：風格 = 你到底想表達什麼，沒說出口的東西 AI 理解不了；你要先做「導演」：把世界觀、情感、表達全部定下來，讓 AI 當夥伴而不是替你決定。和 AI 協作的好處是更快意識到自己做得好或不好。
  • 「無限解空間」的虛無：實拍有「上天給的禮物」（夕陽餘暉那種不可複製的瞬間）；AI 生成則什麼都能得到，於是問題反轉——當解無限多，如何在無限解裡找最優解、如何不麻木。這不止是做片，也是人生規劃問題：明天可以做任何事的第一反應是觸碰虛無。他把這留作今年後續專場的話題：IntentWare——人類如何產生意圖。
  • Vlog 的真實性：他的點子是拍照片、中間用 AI 填充影片，解決「拍攝侵蝕生活」的博主困境；靜涵質疑填充的不是真實發生的事。他回應：照片與足跡是真實記錄，而人的記憶本來就脆弱、會被重塑。

Notes | Guo Yu's Ten Talks on AI: Making Films with AI

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcMssIgsvdI | Duration 2 hours 23 minutes | Guo Yu + hosted by Sun Jinghan, live talk + hands-on demo + Q&A Note: Machine transcript, with mixed simplified/traditional characters and some proper names misheard (e.g. C-Dance = Seedance, Jimeng transcribed as "Jimeng" variants, Visio Studio sounding like "VCO/Wesley"); the notes have been corrected in context. Industry figures and rumours in the text are as stated live by the speakers and have not been verified.

In one sentence

AI video has moved past the "cute cats and dogs entertainment clips" stage and into serious film and TV production: 3–5 minute shorts made by Chinese creators with tools such as Seedance have reached streaming-level quality, while large numbers of people in the live-action short-drama industry have no work and are switching to AI comic dramas. Guo Yu's judgement is that this is the equivalent of 2016 in short-video history — once the barrier is levelled, AI-native content will be a hundred or a thousand times PGC+UGC; and only one role remains for humans: the person who sets the story and the worldview and makes choices among infinite generated results.

State of the industry (first-hand observations from his recent return to China)

  • On-the-ground reality (his live observations): Many live-action short-drama crews cannot get business, and practitioners are switching to "AI comic dramas" (AI-made live-action / animated dramas); ByteDance's Hongguo short-drama platform shares revenue by traffic, pushing creators to use AI tools.
  • The quality leap came in May this year: A considerable number of serious shorts "good enough for streaming" appeared, almost all by Chinese creators, with tens of millions of views on X and Instagram. Three were shown live: a post-apocalyptic short by a creator from a music-video background (benchmarked against Netflix's Love, Death & Robots at US$1–3 million per episode, with the AI version costing a few thousandths of that); a work credited to "Niu Dou" — one person handling directing, editing, music and art; and a Seedance short by Jia Zhangke in collaboration with ByteDance (Jia Zhangke in conversation with his own AI digital human, fully generated).
  • The cost maths: One hour of serious AI video, using Seedance + music generation and allowing 50% tolerance (generate two, keep one), costs about US$2,000. That is nothing for a studio, but an individual creator must be able to recoup it; costs will fall sharply further in the next year or two.
  • Netflix's move (as relayed): His aunt works at Netflix, and according to him her team is building a new platform — the framework of film and TV production is fully retained, but 90% of content is handed to AI, humans keep only the screenwriter role of telling the story, and a small team uses AI to present the story. Guo Yu said we will in future see streaming platforms that are "entirely Powered by AI".
  • Tighter copyright is actually a good thing: After Seedance 2.0, Disney and the Writers Guild of America sent letters, and copyrighted characters (Mickey Mouse, etc.) and real-person likenesses were opened only to specific APIs — ordinary people are forced not to rely on existing IP and to create their own characters and stories. He predicted that within a month or two the first AI-native IPs loved by the general public will appear, and children born after 2000 and 2005 will grow up accompanied by AI animation.
  • Why Chinese creators are ahead: Chinese tech companies have poured large amounts of inference compute into video models for consumer entertainment, and ByteDance's Seedance is currently slightly ahead globally — winning on character consistency, camera language and low error rates. Once OpenAI/Anthropic catch up, it will explode worldwide.

Methodology: The practical AI filmmaking workflow (live demo)

The whole pipeline no longer needs professional software; a single Codex / Claude Code can run it:

  1. Story and storyboard: Tell Codex the idea, it offers creative options and generates a storyboard script — first set the worldview, character personalities and emotional tone (first set the "four points": worldview, characters, emotion, conflict), then break it down shot by shot.
  2. Storyboard images: GPT Image 2 generates storyboard images (15–20 seconds per image), with character setting cards / three-view sheets ensuring consistency.
  3. Generate video: Feed the storyboard image + atmosphere/music description to Seedance (or via the ByteDance API to Jimeng), 10–15 seconds per clip; the failure rate is about half, and using reference images / reference video (for example specifying "carry only one suitcase") can greatly reduce the failure rate.
  4. Automatic quality-check iteration: Have Codex check the generated result against the storyboard (what does not match, and why), then regenerate — "like constantly retaking on a film set"; you can give it a budget ("spend this US$500 today") and let it run unattended.
  5. Sound: Narration / voice acting stays consistent across episodes; music uses Suno (which wants to be the platform / Spotify of music). Ambient sound and atmosphere must all be spelled out in the prompt.

The granularity of the prompt is the core craft: You cannot just write "say a reluctant farewell" — you must specify who enters the frame first, how the camera pans, handshake or hug, how many facial expressions and for how many seconds, who speaks, what they say, where the pauses are, and what accent. Abstract emotion words mean nothing to AI; they must be translated into visible visual instructions.

His own two proofs: - An ad for the translation-call product Tua: He never hired an influencer at all; two video ads were finished in 10 minutes and put on the official website (he could not be bothered to fix the bugs, which actually made it intuitively clear they were AI-generated). - Visio Studio (built since January this year): It began because his aunt (a Silicon Valley engineer who has written more than a dozen novels, including The Spring of Ice Age about Chinese people in the 2000 dot-com bubble and a horror novel series) was dissatisfied with how platforms such as Tencent adapted her novels after buying the film/TV rights. The product automatically breaks down a long novel: from 70,000 characters it analyses all characters, generates settings and three-view sheets, splits storyboards, and unifies voice acting — in essence productising the screenwriter's work.

Clash of views

  • The "craftsman spirit / old-school handcrafting" debate: Jinghan came with a bias (by her own admission). Guo Yu's response: It is just a tool — from editors to IDEs to AI agents, what he has always enjoyed is creation itself; drop the bias first and use it, and your ideas and perspective will naturally change. But he acknowledged that insiders' creative inertia makes it hard to think out of the box, and those leading this wave are often outsiders.
  • The style question: An audience member asked, "I don't want Jia Zhangke style, I want my own style, what should I do?" — Answer: Style = what you actually want to express; AI cannot understand what you have not said. You must first be the "director": set the worldview, emotion and expression completely, and let AI be a partner rather than deciding for you. The benefit of collaborating with AI is becoming aware faster of whether you are doing well or badly.
  • The nihilism of the "infinite solution space": Live shooting has "gifts from heaven" (irreproducible moments like the afterglow of a sunset); with AI generation you can get anything, so the problem reverses — when there are infinitely many solutions, how do you find the optimal one among them, and how do you avoid going numb? This is not only about filmmaking, but also about life planning: the first reaction to being able to do anything tomorrow is to touch nihilism. He left this as a topic for a later session this year: IntentWare — how humans generate intent.
  • The authenticity of vlogs: His idea is to shoot photos and use AI to fill in the video in between, solving the creator's dilemma that "filming erodes life"; Jinghan questioned that what is filled in is not what really happened. He responded: Photos and traces are real records, and human memory is inherently fragile and can be reshaped.
  • The opportunity for minority languages: Talking about Dear You (a letter to Grandma) he thought — why is there no Hakka-language film? Because it used to be unaffordable to shoot. AI allows any language (even one with only very few remaining speakers) to be made into film/TV and music; this is something only AI can do.
  • Education: AI will not "learn directing", but it will turn everyone into a director; if you want to learn anything just ask AI, and the only remaining reason for film-school-type schools to exist is "environment shapes character"; the prerequisite is always motivation — among AI-native children there will be directors who win Academy Awards.

Selected Q&A

  • How to keep interests broad yet stay focused (he has made many different pieces of software): The prerequisite for interest-driven work is financial freedom — "otherwise I would not have given up my undergraduate major to write code"; when you have no money, rent and family push you along and it is hard to face your intent directly. His advice to the world is: do not hold biases, do not take a God's-eye view; stubbornly thinking "this should not happen" will only make you passively accept it many years later.
  • The essence of communicating with AI: An audience member was troubled that "it doesn't understand what I say". Guo Yu: AI has not experienced the real world; even the angle light comes from must be told by you. But it is the same between people — language relies on a tiny "common-divisor intersection" and a lot of metaphor to fill in; not being understood is often not AI's problem, but a problem of expression. The solution: constrain intent into a concrete language space, without metaphor, very specifically; at the same time treat AI as an omniscient partner rather than a tool, and learn more from it.
  • Engineers' weakness: For mainland engineers, the biggest disadvantage in collaborating with AI is expression in their mother tongue — the science/humanities streaming in education leaves many engineers not good at articulating ideas clearly, which has become the first bottleneck in using AI.
  • The start-up method of "selling a product that doesn't exist yet" (in response to an audience member who wanted to start a business): Use GPT Image to generate realistic product images, Seedance to make a promotional video, list it for pre-sale first — if someone orders (a live case had 300–400 orders) then contact 3D printing / factories in China to produce and ship; if there are no orders, abandon it at zero cost. "First turn an idea into real sales intent" is a nearly risk-free start-up validation. He also criticised in passing: the Chinese community "lacks imagination far too much"; the most conspicuous people making money from AI right now are those selling courses, but the real opportunity is in turning imagination into products.
  • Beginner anxiety: Learning everything and keeping up with nothing — what to do? Initiative / intent is another big topic (a dedicated session was trailed); external constraints (such as wake-up calls from a Douban group) help, but fundamentally it is whether you have a strong inner drive. Do not mistake anxiety for action.
  • Making products in Japan, what if craftspeople don't trust AI?: Trust is not built at the "product and person" level, but between people — Japanese business is about a sense of trust; what you give at New Year and festivals and how you get along day to day matter more than whether there is AI in the product. Besides, many things made by local Japanese programmers are also quite rough; when the finished product is good enough, most people actually cannot tell.
  • Closing tool advice: Stop reinventing the wheel — first make good use of the first-layer harness: Codex / Claude Code can control your computer and will keep running 24 hours on a server without stopping; buying the $200 tier and using compute freely is enough. He joked about himself: Wanman (a real-time voice assistant) he made before is not very meaningful now, because the excellent model vendors have built all these capabilities in, and a simple API request can invoke them.
