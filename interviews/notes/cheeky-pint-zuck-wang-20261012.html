「模型即产品」：Cheeky Pint 对话扎克伯格与 Alexandr Wang

节目：Cheeky Pint（主持 John Collison，Stripe 联合创始人）· 嘉宾：Mark Zuckerberg（Meta CEO）、Alexandr Wang（Meta 超级智能实验室 MSL 负责人）· 发布：2026-10-12（X 原生视频，另有 YouTube 版）· 时长：2 小时 14 分 · 主题：Meta 新一代前沿模型 Muse Wonder 发布 说明：本纪要据 X 视频自带英文字幕整理。文中观点、数字均为发言人现场口径，未经核实；字幕将 OpenRouter 多处误转，文中已统一。

一句话

Meta 在 Llama 4 翻车后用一年多时间重建团队（MSL），押上千亿美元级算力，在模型还没影时就签下支票，新模型 Muse Wonder 主打「个人与专业 Agent」：主动记忆、长程专注、领域专家能力——两人的核心主张是：产品会越来越薄，模型本身就是产品。

Muse 这个产品：两个当家用例

  • 第三方报告称 Muse 可能是有史以来增长最快的 Agent 应用（扎克伯格语）。Wang 的 Muse 名叫 Oiler，每周从台湾、日本的农场给他搜罗越来越冷门的茶并买回来。
  • 扎克伯格拿 Muse 当 MMA 教练：健身房装摄像头，Muse 在回合间 1 分钟休息内看视频给反馈（重心、髋位、underhook 等）。难点是速度——它自己写整套视频/图像处理管线，还被不断要求「你花了 30 秒，我只有 60 秒，自己优化自己」。这个用例后来成了产品内部评测（eval）之一。他最早在 Muse 之前的 OpenClaw 上就这么用。
  • 为什么多模态强：行业共识一度只盯编程，但个人超级智能靠感知——大多数人以多模态方式体验世界。眼镜最终可能是一个纯多模态产品，甚至不需要应用商店，界面直接在视野里生成。

「模型即产品」之争

  • Collison 的观察：AI 的制胜产品形态收敛得极快（transformer、聊天 UI、如今是带专属电脑的常驻 Agent），简洁到一张明信片能写完，不像支付那样有几十页的产品空间。
  • Wang 半反对：现在觉得产品简单，是因为我们看待 AI 产品的维度错了。Muse 的差异化不在 UI 规格，而在模型在每种情境下的行为、人格、工具使用方式——这些是训练出来的。就像移动时代早期人人说「不就是个 App」，后来才发现 nuance 全在里面。
  • 扎克伯格同意「产品变薄、模型变厚」：Muse 复杂的产品工作主要在隐私与安全模型，其余能力、人格都训进模型；产品负责人 Nat 真正操心的是后训练，不是界面。Muse 刻意不设模型选择器、也不设 effort 选择器——普通用户不该面对一堆编号倒置、名字混乱的模型菜单。
  • 用户能感知的差异：可靠性、主动性在什么情境触发、以什么语气找你说话——这些「产品决策」最终都落到训练方法、数据与评测上。

Muse Wonder：新一档模型

  • 预训练的阶梯打法：每一档训得比上一档大 4–5 倍，逐级往上爬。Muse Spark 一系基于去年底的首次预训练（内部代号 Avocado 牛油果），团队自己都惊讶于「其实没那么大」的模型能榨出多少能力。Wonder（代号 Watermelon 西瓜，水果名已用完，下一个只能是榴莲——玩笑）预训练已完成、后训练收尾，体量显著更大，「思考的 nuance 会不一样」。
  • 两个版本：Muse Wonder 与 Muse Wonder Full（高配版，现场拿 "wonderful" 开了个爸爸玩笑）。
  • 主攻方向是「最强的个人与专业 Agent 模型」：①主动记忆（active memory）——记住你几天、几周、几个月前提过的事并在后续任务里调用；记忆是模型自身的能力（含何时、如何做上下文压缩），不是外挂 harness；OpenClaw 用久了记忆「buggy AF」正是他们要解决的；内部有 Memory Bench 评测。②主动专注（active focus）——长程复杂任务的持续执行力，遇挫折能换解法。③领域专家能力——购物、个人建议、法律/健康/税务问题；Wang 称其在多项金融与法律评测上是 SOTA，这些能力从预训练就要喂领域语料。④编程大幅跃升：称在 DeepSWE、SWE-Marathon 上 SOTA，Terminal-Bench 4 等表现也很好。
  • 定位：Collison 问是否与 Astra、Fable 并列前沿，扎克伯格答「按基准看，结论就是这样」；且为 Muse 这个产品形态专门优化，自称是个人专业 Agent 场景的世界最佳。
  • 上线方式：Wonder 体量大、服务贵，Muse 里先走付费订阅（Spark 继续免费、大额度）；API 经 Meta Model API 与 OpenRouter 提供；Muse Code 订阅者可用，Wonder Full 面向 Muse Code 订阅。

基准、Goodhart 与「大模型味」

  • 基准饱和得太快，越往后 Meta 越靠内部评测定向。外部基准的供应链尽头往往是某个博士生随手做的网红基准——「把产品思考外包给那个博士生显然是错的」。
  • 他们量化「大模型味（big model smell）」的方法：看模型在分布内任务（RL/后训练覆盖的）与分布外任务上的表现差距。Spark→Wonder 这一跳很大，源于底座模型本身变强。刷榜（benchmaxing）与「大模型味」互为反面。
  • 个人 Agent 特有的维度：分寸感（discretion）——Agent 知道你过敏、怀孕，才能帮你订对餐厅，但不该把你的医疗状况告诉餐厅。编程 Agent 的环境里人人共享上下文，分寸感不重要；个人场景里它至关重要。

前沿模型是怎么做出来的

  • 预训练：海量多模态与领域数据；模型变大就必须同步扩数据，否则过拟合；某些数据有正迁移、某些有负迁移，全是科学问题。互联网增速有限，「数据从哪来」是扩大模型的核心难题。
  • SFT：给问题与理想回答，初始化行为与人格——SFT 结束时模型可能只有 10% 的时候表现出目标行为。
  • RL：把行为的触发率大幅拉高。RL 与预训练的「手感」相反：数据常常少即是多，质量压倒数量；版本迭代（Spark 1→1.3）的本质就是同一预训练上继续加 RL，配方改进带来的体感差异「如昼夜之别」，用户会以为换了预训练。
  • 对齐观：高算力 RL 里必须精心设计环境——不能让「只有做不对齐的事才能成功」的场景存在；对齐是整个 RL 过程的属性，不是外加的一步。Meta 的理论：Agent 是为某个人服务的，对齐首先是对使用者的指令与价值对齐，可靠执行、不说教式拒绝；只有灾难性请求才优雅地拒绝。
  • 周期：Wonder 从去年 8 月就开始规划，Spark 从一开始就是通往 Wonder 的垫脚石。模型立项到见分晓超过一年，创业者要在信息极度贫乏的环境里下注——「精益创业的反面，类固醇版」。

一千亿的支票：在发牌之前全押

  • 开场原话（扎克伯格）：去年底「我们还没见过模型，就要签一张 1000 亿、2000 亿美元的支票。我问 Alex：你确定这能成吗？」
  • CFO Susan Li 多次把 Wang 拉到一边强调：如果不成，财务上会非常、非常糟；成了就一切都好。
  • 账算给你听：一座 1GW 集群运营一年约 150 亿美元，建造成本约 500–600 亿美元；俄亥俄 Prometheus 集群超过 1GW，路易斯安那 Hyperion 规划 5GW（规划早于 MSL，2023–24 年就已启动）。训一个前沿模型全口径是「数百亿美元」级，且任一时刻账面是多代模型的研发开支叠在一起。
  • 扎克伯格的对冲逻辑：风险没听上去那么大——只要 AI 整体成立，算力不愁买家；要让这笔投资归零，得 Meta 失败且 AI 整体失败同时发生；退一步算力还能喂自家其他业务。Wang 总结他的思维：不看失败率，看赢的时候能赢多大——幂律内化。
  • 产品侧同样要提前下注：为还不存在的产品（Muse）的推理需求提前几年建数据中心。中途有人想保守一点，结论是「烧掉船只，把它做成」。

MSL 的组织：人才密度与完全重启

  • Alex 方案的第一条：不需要很多人，要人才密度。研究团队不到 200 人、更接近 100 人；每个环节是「几十人」而非其他实验室的几百人。算力不撒胡椒面，按研究员人均高配。
  • 扁平到极端：Wang 名义上有近 200 个直接下属（实操有 team lead 分层），要点不是汇报关系，而是消灭非技术管理者——人一旦脱离细节，在这个领域很快就不再懂技术。他的 Muse 帮他管这些汇报。
  • 时间线：Llama 4 发布后约两周，扎克伯格开始四处打电话求教；核心圈是扎克伯格 + Nat Friedman + Wang + Daniel Gross。组建期「像超新星一样把一堆人砸在一起」，扎克伯格清空了约两个月日程只做招聘；公司明确告知「没有截止日期，按对的节奏来」。
  • 团队画像：「一群没有安全感的过度 achiever」——每次问「三个月后我们会在哪」，人人回答「糟透了、完蛋了」；Wang 把预测录下来，三个月后放给他们看。这个动态至今没变：对 MSL 整体乐观、对自己的项目极度自 critical，他认为这很健康。
  • 为什么必须全重启：Wang 入职初期的一次会议上，原团队一位负责人说「我们不需要推理模型，消费者需要推理模型吗？」——他当场心沉到底，给 Nat 打电话。结论是领导层必须大幅「AI-pilled」；且出去招顶尖研究员时发现，只有「从零重建」的叙事才足够有吸引力（同时大量吸纳 Llama/FAIR 的优秀成员）。
  • Collison 总结扎克伯格四个非常规动作：①公开判定前任努力失败并重启；②为人才付天价（包括 Wang 与 Scale 的交易安排）；③押注一个从没训过前沿模型的人；④CEO 本人清空两个月日程只干这一件事。Wang 补充：扎克伯格的特点是一旦内化了某个信念，就以极快的速度、极大的幅度让全部行动与之保持一致——「非常贝叶斯」；而整间公司（基建、数据中心、各业务线）都要跟着转向，只有创始人 CEO 能推动这种全局最优压过局部最优。
  • 何时知道 Wonder 成了：预训练结束后争论「RL 要多久才能超过 Spark」，结果是立刻超过——那一刻就知道路走对了；兴奋已经持续几个月。
  • 外部新鲜血液：WhatsApp 新负责人 Kunal Shah（印度 CRED 创始人）是同类下注；但全公司级转向同时只能有一个最高优先级。

平台与智能体经济

  • 扎克伯格：未来技术体验里模型是最重要的一层；能造前沿超级智能的公司只有一小撮，它们将定义议程。Meta 不是非有平台不可，关键是「不掌握端到端的超级智能，就做不出未来真正重要的东西」。
  • 为什么个人/消费这条 lane 是空的：别人痴迷于自动化知识工作，但超级智能的最高用途是发明与创造，不是自动化。
  • 商家接入的阶梯：Muse 先天有很强的浏览器操作能力，商家零集成就能被 Agent 使用（这是兜底与向后兼容层）；重要伙伴给直连 API，效率更高；长远看每家企业都会有自己的 Agent，Muse 与自家 Agent 之间会配合得最好。主动要求深度集成的商家远多于试图屏蔽的；屏蔽者大概率战略错误，护城河薄、靠消费者耗时或 overcharging 赚钱的生意会被颠覆。
  • 购买行为已在发生质变：用户开始把采购完全委托给 Agent。扎克伯格的 Instacart 账号「其实是给 Muse 开的」：他和三岁女儿 Aurelia 用语音模式聊周末烘焙（Muse 能分辨父女的声音），周六食材自动备齐；这次它知道 Watermelon 要上线，还往袋子里塞了个西瓜凑配送门槛——直播里吃的那个西瓜就是这么来的。他以前一年用 Instacart 不到两次，现在一周一两次。推论：商务的总可寻址市场会变大，因为 Agent 本身成了账户持有者。
  • Stripe 侧数据（Collison）：2023 年扎克伯格就警告 Patrick Collison「你们在 agentic commerce 上已经落后了」——方向对了，早了三年。Stripe 5 月 Sessions 发布了一系列相关产品，数据到 9 月才开始垂直拉升；computer use 是最后缺的解锁。（节目中段插播了 Stripe 收购 OpenRouter 的广告：其日路由量超过 10 万亿 token。）
  • 语音：输入端已经爆发——研究员对着键盘小声说话、大学生 10–15 分钟意识流「呕吐式」口述再让 AI 整理；输出端朗读效率低；难点在对话动态（打断、停顿的判断）。能采取行动的 Agent 让语音第一次像「在跟人说话」。Muse 的「小人儿」具身形象成立，是因为记忆、行动力与人格同时到位。硬件：无显示/小显示眼镜已被验证有用，Muse Gadgets 生态开放给手机厂商与第三方形态，HomeLink 小配件可把 Muse 接进全屋设备（Sonos、灯光），扎克伯格用它每晚自动调暗全屋灯光。

钱从哪来：为 AI 基建买单

  • 收入组合：订阅（Wonder 走付费层）、商务抽成（Muse 帮人赚钱省钱，抽一小部分）、API，以及新设的 Meta 企业平台（CJ Desai 加入负责，把模型按企业需要的方式交付）。
  • 底盘：核心广告业务是数千亿美元级且仍在高速增长的「世界第八大奇迹」，模型还能让它再快几个百分点——这本身就能供养很大一部分基建。
  • 会计错觉：数据中心是资本开支、可多年折旧；研究员薪水同样在造多年期资产却不能资本化。算力紧缺时连 H100 的价值都比去年更高。自建数据中心相对买云的厂商有财务优势。
  • 对 OpenRouter 的建议（Wang）：前沿模型之间的差异正在分化（各家有意为之），基准把这些「质地」抹平了；机会在于做模型界的侍酒师——帮开发者理解细微差异，并把开发者的真实体验反馈成信号。

为什么没有赢家通吃

  • 2023 年的流行叙事是某一家靠递归自我改进一骑绝尘，实际至今多家并立前沿。Wang 给三条原因：①执行有周期，领先者总会在领先时犯错，长期互相平均；②蒸馏是反霸权的力量——没人计划让蒸馏这么好用，但它把强能力压进越来越小的模型，利好开源与整个生态；③行业里很多人与公司主观上就想要多元格局，并用入职与交易决策投票。扎克伯格：多元加制衡的配置比少数公司垄断少数模型更安全。

收尾

Muse 這個產品：兩個當家用例

  • 第三方報告稱 Muse 可能是有史以來增長最快的 Agent 應用（扎克伯格語）。Wang 的 Muse 名叫 Oiler，每週從台灣、日本的農場給他搜羅越來越冷門的茶並買回來。
  • 扎克伯格拿 Muse 當 MMA 教練：健身房裝攝影機，Muse 在回合間 1 分鐘休息內看影片給回饋（重心、髖位、underhook 等）。難點是速度——它自己寫整套影片/影像處理管線，還被不斷要求「你花了 30 秒，我只有 60 秒，自己優化自己」。這個用例後來成了產品內部評測（eval）之一。他最早在 Muse 之前的 OpenClaw 上就這麼用。
  • 為什麼多模態強：行業共識一度只盯程式設計，但個人超級智能靠感知——大多數人以多模態方式體驗世界。眼鏡最終可能是一個純多模態產品，甚至不需要應用商店，介面直接在視野裡生成。

「模型即產品」之爭

  • Collison 的觀察：AI 的制勝產品形態收斂得極快（transformer、聊天 UI、如今是帶專屬電腦的常駐 Agent），簡潔到一張明信片能寫完，不像支付那樣有幾十頁的產品空間。
  • Wang 半反對：現在覺得產品簡單，是因為我們看待 AI 產品的維度錯了。Muse 的差異化不在 UI 規格，而在模型在每種情境下的行為、人格、工具使用方式——這些是訓練出來的。就像行動時代早期人人說「不就是個 App」，後來才發現 nuance 全在裡面。
  • 扎克伯格同意「產品變薄、模型變厚」：Muse 複雜的產品工作主要在隱私與安全模型，其餘能力、人格都訓進模型；產品負責人 Nat 真正操心的是後訓練，不是介面。Muse 刻意不設模型選擇器、也不設 effort 選擇器——普通用戶不該面對一堆編號倒置、名字混亂的模型選單。
  • 用戶能感知的差異：可靠性、主動性在什麼情境觸發、以什麼語氣找你說話——這些「產品決策」最終都落到訓練方法、資料與評測上。

Muse Wonder：新一檔模型

  • 預訓練的階梯打法：每一檔訓得比上一檔大 4–5 倍，逐級往上爬。Muse Spark 一系基於去年底的首次預訓練（內部代號 Avocado 酪梨），團隊自己都驚訝於「其實沒那麼大」的模型能榨出多少能力。Wonder（代號 Watermelon 西瓜，水果名已用完，下一個只能是榴槤——玩笑）預訓練已完成、後訓練收尾，體量顯著更大，「思考的 nuance 會不一樣」。
  • 兩個版本：Muse Wonder 與 Muse Wonder Full（高配版，現場拿 "wonderful" 開了個爸爸玩笑）。
  • 主攻方向是「最強的個人與專業 Agent 模型」：①主動記憶（active memory）——記住你幾天、幾週、幾個月前提過的事並在後續任務裡調用；記憶是模型自身的能力（含何時、如何做上下文壓縮），不是外掛 harness；OpenClaw 用久了記憶「buggy AF」正是他們要解決的；內部有 Memory Bench 評測。②主動專注（active focus）——長程複雜任務的持續執行力，遇挫折能換解法。③領域專家能力——購物、個人建議、法律/健康/稅務問題；Wang 稱其在多項金融與法律評測上是 SOTA，這些能力從預訓練就要餵領域語料。④程式設計大幅躍升：稱在 DeepSWE、SWE-Marathon 上 SOTA，Terminal-Bench 4 等表現也很好。
  • 定位：Collison 問是否與 Astra、Fable 並列前沿，扎克伯格答「按基準看，結論就是這樣」；且為 Muse 這個產品形態專門優化，自稱是個人專業 Agent 場景的世界最佳。
  • 上線方式：Wonder 體量大、服務貴，Muse 裡先走付費訂閱（Spark 繼續免費、大額度）；API 經 Meta Model API 與 OpenRouter 提供；Muse Code 訂閱者可用，Wonder Full 面向 Muse Code 訂閱。

基準、Goodhart 與「大模型味」

  • 基準飽和得太快，越往後 Meta 越靠內部評測定向。外部基準的供應鏈盡頭往往是某個博士生隨手做的網紅基準——「把產品思考外包給那個博士生顯然是錯的」。
  • 他們量化「大模型味（big model smell）」的方法：看模型在分布內任務（RL/後訓練覆蓋的）與分布外任務上的表現差距。Spark→Wonder 這一跳很大，源於底座模型本身變強。刷榜（benchmaxing）與「大模型味」互為反面。
  • 個人 Agent 特有的維度：分寸感（discretion）——Agent 知道你過敏、懷孕，才能幫你訂對餐廳，但不該把你的醫療狀況告訴餐廳。程式設計 Agent 的環境裡人人共享上下文，分寸感不重要；個人場景裡它至關重要。

前沿模型是怎麼做出的

  • 預訓練：海量多模態與領域資料；模型變大就必須同步擴資料，否則過擬合；某些資料有正遷移、某些有負遷移，全是科學問題。網際網路增速有限，「資料從哪來」是擴大模型的核心難題。
  • SFT：給問題與理想回答，初始化行為與人格——SFT 結束時模型可能只有 10% 的時候表現出目標行為。
  • RL：把行為的觸發率大幅拉高。RL 與預訓練的「手感」相反：資料常常少即是多，品質壓倒數量；版本迭代（Spark 1→1.3）的本質就是同一預訓練上繼續加 RL，配方改進帶來的體感差異「如晝夜之別」，用戶會以為換了預訓練。
  • 對齊觀：高算力 RL 裡必須精心設計環境——不能讓「只有做不對齊的事才能成功」的場景存在；對齊是整個 RL 過程的屬性，不是外加的一步。Meta 的理論：Agent 是為某個人服務的，對齊首先是對使用者的指令與價值對齊，可靠執行、不說教式拒絕；只有災難性請求才優雅地拒絕。
  • 週期：Wonder 從去年 8 月就開始規劃，Spark 從一開始就是通往 Wonder 的墊腳石。模型立項到見分曉超過一年，創業者要在資訊極度貧乏的環境裡下注——「精益創業的反面，類固醇版」。

一千億的支票：在發牌之前全押

  • 開場原話（扎克伯格）：去年底「我們還沒見過模型，就要簽一張 1000 億、2000 億美元的支票。我問 Alex：你確定這能成嗎？」
  • CFO Susan Li 多次把 Wang 拉到一邊強調：如果不成，財務上會非常、非常糟；成了就一切都好。
  • 帳算給你聽：一座 1GW 集群營運一年約 150 億美元，建造成本約 500–600 億美元；俄亥俄 Prometheus 集群超過 1GW，路易斯安那 Hyperion 規劃 5GW（規劃早於 MSL，2023–24 年就已啟動）。訓一個前沿模型全口徑是「數百億美元」級，且任一時刻帳面是多代模型的研發開支疊在一起。
  • 扎克伯格的對沖邏輯：風險沒聽上去那麼大——只要 AI 整體成立，算力不愁買家；要讓這筆投資歸零，得 Meta 失敗且 AI 整體失敗同時發生；退一步算力還能餵自家其他業務。Wang 總結他的思維：不看失敗率，看贏的時候能贏多大——冪律內化。

MSL 的組織：人才密度與完全重啟

  • Alex 方案的第一條：不需要很多人，要人才密度。研究團隊不到 200 人、更接近 100 人；每個環節是「幾十人」而非其他實驗室的幾百人。算力不撒胡椒麵，按研究員人均高配。
  • 扁平到極端：Wang 名義上有近 200 個直接下屬（實操有 team lead 分層），要點不是匯報關係，而是消滅非技術管理者——人一旦脫離細節，在這個領域很快就不再懂技術。他的 Muse 幫他管這些匯報。

平台與智能體經濟

  • 為什麼個人/消費這條 lane 是空的：別人痴迷於自動化知識工作，但超級智能的最高用途是發明與創造，不是自動化。
  • 商家接入的階梯：Muse 先天有很強的瀏覽器操作能力，商家零整合就能被 Agent 使用（這是兜底與向後相容層）；重要夥伴給直連 API，效率更高；長遠看每家企業都會有自己的 Agent，Muse 與自家 Agent 之間會配合得最好。主動要求深度整合的商家遠多於試圖屏蔽的；屏蔽者大概率戰略錯誤，護城河薄、靠消費者耗時或 overcharging 賺錢的生意會被顛覆。

"The Model Is the Product": Cheeky Pint with Zuckerberg and Alexandr Wang

Program: Cheeky Pint (host John Collison, co-founder of Stripe) · Guests: Mark Zuckerberg (CEO of Meta), Alexandr Wang (head of Meta Superintelligence Labs, MSL) · Published: 2026-10-12 (native video on X, also a YouTube version) · Duration: 2 hours 14 minutes · Topic: The launch of Muse Wonder, Meta's new-generation frontier model Note: This summary is compiled from the English subtitles provided with the X video. All views and figures are as stated by the speakers live and have not been verified; the subtitles mistranscribed OpenRouter in several places, which has been standardized in the text.

In one sentence

After the Llama 4 flop, Meta spent more than a year rebuilding its team (MSL), bet compute on the order of hundreds of billions of dollars, and signed the checks before the model even existed. The new model, Muse Wonder, is built around "personal and professional agents": active memory, long-horizon focus, and domain-expert capabilities — and the two men's core claim is that products will get thinner and thinner, and the model itself is the product.

The Muse product: two flagship use cases

  • Third-party reports say Muse may be the fastest-growing agent application ever (per Zuckerberg). Wang's Muse is named Oiler; every week it scours farms in Taiwan and Japan for increasingly obscure teas for him and buys them.
  • Zuckerberg uses Muse as an MMA coach: cameras are installed in the gym, and during the 1-minute breaks between rounds Muse watches the video and gives feedback (center of gravity, hip position, underhooks, etc.). The hard part is speed — it wrote the entire video/image processing pipeline itself, and was repeatedly told, "You took 30 seconds; I only have 60 seconds; optimize yourself." This use case later became one of the product's internal evaluations (evals). He first used it this way on OpenClaw, before Muse.
  • Why multimodality is strong: the industry consensus once focused only on coding, but personal superintelligence depends on perception — most people experience the world multimodally. Glasses may ultimately be a purely multimodal product, perhaps not even needing an app store, with the interface generated directly in your field of view.

The "model is the product" debate

  • Collison's observation: the winning product forms in AI converge extremely fast (the transformer, the chat UI, and now the resident agent with its own dedicated computer) — simple enough to fit on a postcard, unlike payments, which has dozens of pages of product space.
  • Wang half-disagrees: products seem simple now because we are looking at AI products along the wrong dimensions. Muse's differentiation is not in its UI specs, but in the model's behavior, personality, and way of using tools in each situation — these are trained in. It is like the early mobile era, when everyone said "it's just an app," and only later discovered that all the nuance was inside.
  • Zuckerberg agrees that "products get thinner, models get thicker": Muse's complex product work is mainly in the privacy and safety model; everything else — capabilities and personality — is trained into the model; product lead Nat's real concern is post-training, not the interface. Muse deliberately has no model picker and no effort picker — ordinary users should not face a pile of model menus with inverted numbering and confusing names.
  • The differences users can perceive — reliability, in what situations proactivity is triggered, in what tone it speaks to you — these "product decisions" ultimately all come down to training methods, data, and evaluations.

Muse Wonder: a new tier of model

  • The pretraining ladder approach: each tier is trained 4–5 times larger than the previous one, climbing step by step. The Muse Spark line is based on the first pretraining run at the end of last year (internal codename Avocado); even the team was surprised at how much capability could be squeezed out of a model that "actually wasn't that big." Wonder (codename Watermelon — the fruit names have run out, so the next one can only be Durian, as a joke) has finished pretraining and is wrapping up post-training; it is significantly larger, and "the nuance of its thinking will be different."
  • Two versions: Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full (the high-spec version; on the spot they made a dad joke with "wonderful").
  • The main push is toward "the strongest personal and professional agent model": ① Active memory — remembering things you mentioned days, weeks, or months ago and calling on them in later tasks; memory is a capability of the model itself (including when and how to do context compression), not an external harness; the fact that OpenClaw's memory gets "buggy AF" with prolonged use is exactly what they want to fix; internally there is a Memory Bench evaluation. ② Active focus — sustained execution on long-horizon complex tasks, able to switch approaches when it hits setbacks. ③ Domain-expert capabilities — shopping, personal advice, legal/health/tax questions; Wang says it is SOTA on multiple finance and legal evaluations, and these capabilities require feeding domain corpora starting from pretraining. ④ A big leap in coding: said to be SOTA on DeepSWE and SWE-Marathon, with strong performance on Terminal-Bench 4 and others as well.
  • Positioning: when Collison asked whether it stands alongside Astra and Fable at the frontier, Zuckerberg replied, "By the benchmarks, that's the conclusion"; and it is specifically optimized for the Muse product form, claiming to be the world's best for personal professional agent scenarios.
  • Rollout: Wonder is large and expensive to serve, so in Muse it starts with paid subscriptions (Spark stays free, with generous quotas); the API is offered through the Meta Model API and OpenRouter; Muse Code subscribers can use it, and Wonder Full is aimed at Muse Code subscriptions.

Benchmarks, Goodhart, and "big model smell"

  • Benchmarks saturate too quickly; the further along they get, the more Meta relies on internal evaluations for direction. At the end of the supply chain for external benchmarks is often some viral benchmark a PhD student threw together — "outsourcing your product thinking to that PhD student is obviously wrong."
  • Their method for quantifying "big model smell": look at the gap between a model's performance on in-distribution tasks (covered by RL/post-training) and out-of-distribution tasks. The Spark→Wonder jump is large, because the base model itself got stronger. Benchmaxing and "big model smell" are opposites of each other.
  • A dimension specific to personal agents: discretion — an agent that knows your allergies and that you are pregnant can book the right restaurant for you, but it should not tell the restaurant about your medical situation. In coding-agent environments everyone shares context, so discretion does not matter much; in personal scenarios it is crucial.

How frontier models are made

  • Pretraining: massive multimodal and domain data; as models get bigger, data must scale in step, otherwise they overfit; some data has positive transfer, some negative transfer — all scientific questions. The internet grows at a limited rate, so "where does the data come from" is the core problem in scaling models.
  • SFT: given questions and ideal answers, it initializes behavior and personality — at the end of SFT, the model may exhibit the target behavior only 10% of the time.
  • RL: raises the trigger rate of behaviors dramatically. RL has the opposite "feel" from pretraining: with data, less is often more, and quality trumps quantity; version iteration (Spark 1→1.3) is essentially continuing to add RL on top of the same pretraining, and the felt difference from recipe improvements is "like night and day" — users will think the pretraining changed.
  • View on alignment: in high-compute RL, environments must be carefully designed — there must be no scenario where "the only way to succeed is to do something misaligned"; alignment is a property of the entire RL process, not an extra step bolted on. Meta's theory: an agent serves a particular person, so alignment is first of all alignment with the user's instructions and values — executing reliably, without preachy refusals; only catastrophic requests are gracefully refused.
  • Cycle: Wonder has been planned since August of last year, and Spark was a stepping stone toward Wonder from the very beginning. From project launch to verdict takes more than a year, and founders must place bets in an environment of extreme information scarcity — "the opposite of lean startup, on steroids."

The hundred-billion-dollar check: all in before the cards are dealt

  • Opening words (Zuckerberg): at the end of last year, "We hadn't even seen the model yet, and we were about to sign a check for 100 billion, 200 billion dollars. I asked Alex: are you sure this will work?"
  • CFO Susan Li repeatedly pulled Wang aside to stress: if it doesn't work, financially it will be very, very bad; if it works, everything will be fine.
  • Here is the math: a 1GW cluster costs about 15 billion dollars a year to operate and about 50–60 billion dollars to build; the Prometheus cluster in Ohio exceeds 1GW, and Hyperion in Louisiana is planned at 5GW (the planning predates MSL, having started in 2023–24). Training one frontier model, all-in, is on the order of "tens of billions of dollars," and at any given moment the books carry the stacked R&D spending of multiple model generations at once.
  • Zuckerberg's hedging logic: the risk is not as big as it sounds — as long as AI works overall, compute will not lack buyers; for this investment to go to zero, Meta would have to fail and AI as a whole would have to fail at the same time; and even in a fallback, the compute can feed Meta's other businesses. Wang summarized his thinking: don't look at the failure rate, look at how much you win when you win — the power law, internalized.
  • The product side also requires betting ahead: building data centers years in advance for the inference needs of a product (Muse) that does not yet exist. Midway, some people wanted to be more conservative; the conclusion was "burn the boats and build it."

The MSL organization: talent density and a complete restart

  • The first item of Alex's plan: you don't need many people, you need talent density. The research team is under 200 people, closer to 100; each function is "dozens of people" rather than the hundreds at other labs. Compute is not sprinkled like pepper; it is allocated generously per researcher.
  • Flat to the extreme: Wang nominally has nearly 200 direct reports (in practice layered with team leads); the point is not the reporting lines but eliminating non-technical managers — once a person is detached from the details, in this field they quickly stop understanding the technology. His Muse helps him manage those reports.
  • Timeline: about two weeks after the Llama 4 launch, Zuckerberg began calling around for advice; the core circle was Zuckerberg + Nat Friedman + Wang + Daniel Gross. The formation period was "like a supernova smashing a bunch of people together"; Zuckerberg cleared about two months of his schedule just for recruiting; the company explicitly said, "There's no deadline; do it at the right pace."
  • Team portrait: "a group of insecure overachievers" — every time they are asked "where will we be in three months," everyone answers "terrible, we're doomed"; Wang records the predictions and plays them back three months later. That dynamic has not changed to this day: optimistic about MSL overall, extremely self-critical about their own projects — which he considers healthy.
  • Why a full restart was necessary: at a meeting early in Wang's tenure, a leader of the original team said, "We don't need reasoning models — do consumers need reasoning models?" — his heart sank on the spot, and he called Nat. The conclusion was that leadership had to become much more "AI-pilled"; and when recruiting top researchers, they found that only the narrative of "rebuilding from zero" was attractive enough (while also absorbing many excellent members from Llama/FAIR).
  • Collison summarized Zuckerberg's four unconventional moves: ① publicly declaring the predecessor effort a failure and restarting; ② paying sky-high prices for talent (including the deal arrangements for Wang and Scale); ③ betting on someone who had never trained a frontier model; ④ the CEO himself clearing two months of his schedule to do just this one thing. Wang added: Zuckerberg's trait is that once he has internalized a belief, he aligns all of his actions with it at extremely high speed and extremely large magnitude — "very Bayesian"; and the whole company (infrastructure, data centers, every business line) has to pivot along, and only a founder-CEO can push that kind of global optimum over local optima.
  • When did they know Wonder had worked: after pretraining ended, they debated "how long will RL take to surpass Spark," and the result was that it surpassed it immediately — at that moment they knew the path was right; the excitement has lasted for months.
  • Fresh blood from outside: WhatsApp's new head Kunal Shah (founder of India's CRED) is the same kind of bet; but a company-wide pivot can have only one top priority at a time.

Platforms and the agent economy

  • Zuckerberg: in the future technology experience, the model is the most important layer; only a small handful of companies can build frontier superintelligence, and they will define the agenda. Meta does not absolutely need a platform; the key is that "without mastering end-to-end superintelligence, you cannot build the things that will truly matter in the future."
  • Why the personal/consumer lane is empty: others are obsessed with automating knowledge work, but the highest use of superintelligence is invention and creation, not automation.
  • The ladder for merchant access: Muse natively has strong browser-operating capability, so merchants can be used by agents with zero integration (this is the fallback and backward-compatibility layer); important partners get direct APIs, which are more efficient; in the long run every business will have its own agent, and Muse will work best with a business's own agent. Far more merchants proactively request deep integration than try to block it; blockers are most likely making a strategic mistake, and businesses with thin moats that make money from consuming consumers' time or overcharging will be disrupted.
  • Purchasing behavior is already changing qualitatively: users are starting to delegate procurement entirely to agents. Zuckerberg's Instacart account "is really opened for Muse": he and his three-year-old daughter Aurelia chat in voice mode about weekend baking (Muse can tell father and daughter's voices apart), and Saturday's ingredients are automatically ready; this time it knew Watermelon was launching and slipped a watermelon into the bag to meet the delivery threshold — the watermelon eaten on the livestream came from exactly that. He used to use Instacart less than twice a year; now it is once or twice a week. The inference: the total addressable market for commerce will grow, because the agent itself has become the account holder.
  • Stripe-side data (Collison): as early as 2023 Zuckerberg warned Patrick Collison, "You are already behind on agentic commerce" — the direction was right, three years early. Stripe released a series of related products at Sessions in May, and the data only started rising vertically in September; computer use was the final missing unlock. (Midway through the program there was an ad for Stripe's acquisition of OpenRouter: its daily routing volume exceeds 10 trillion tokens.)
  • Voice: the input side has already exploded — researchers speaking quietly at their keyboards, college students dictating 10–15 minutes of stream-of-consciousness "vomit-style" narration and having AI organize it; the output side, reading aloud, is inefficient; the difficulty lies in conversational dynamics (judging interruptions and pauses). Agents that can take action make voice feel, for the first time, like "talking to a person." Muse's embodied "little person" persona works because memory, agency, and personality all arrived at the same time. Hardware: glasses with no display or a small display have been proven useful; the Muse Gadgets ecosystem is open to phone makers and third-party form factors; the small HomeLink accessory can connect Muse to devices throughout the home (Sonos, lights), and Zuckerberg uses it to automatically dim all the lights in his house every night.

Where the money comes from: paying for AI infrastructure

  • Revenue mix: subscriptions (Wonder goes to the paid tier), commerce commissions (Muse helps people make and save money, and takes a small cut), APIs, and the newly established Meta enterprise platform (CJ Desai joined to lead it, delivering models in the ways enterprises need).
  • The base: the core advertising business is on the order of hundreds of billions of dollars and still growing fast — "the eighth wonder of the world" — and models can make it a few percentage points faster still, which alone can support a large part of the infrastructure.
  • An accounting illusion: data centers are capital expenditure and can be depreciated over many years; researcher salaries likewise create multi-year assets but cannot be capitalized. When compute is scarce, even H100s are worth more than last year. Companies that build their own data centers have a financial advantage over those that buy cloud.
  • Advice for OpenRouter (Wang): the differences between frontier models are diverging (deliberately, on each company's part), and benchmarks flatten out these "textures"; the opportunity is to be the sommelier of the model world — helping developers understand the subtle differences, and feeding developers' real experience back as signal.

Why there is no winner-take-all

  • The popular narrative in 2023 was that one company would pull away through recursive self-improvement; in reality, several companies still stand at the frontier together. Wang gave three reasons: ① execution is cyclical — leaders always make mistakes while they are ahead, and over the long run things average out; ② distillation is an anti-hegemony force — nobody planned for distillation to work this well, but it compresses strong capabilities into ever-smaller models, which benefits open source and the whole ecosystem; ③ many people and companies in the industry subjectively want a pluralistic landscape, and vote with their employment and deal decisions. Zuckerberg: a configuration of plurality plus checks and balances is safer than a few companies monopolizing a few models.

Closing

  • Collison asked what he makes of Wang's "deranged posting" on X: Zuckerberg said the most important thing in communication is authenticity; authentic things are sometimes imperfect, which beats being over-scripted; and most of the content makes him laugh out loud. Wang's meme philosophy is "what would Jolly (his partner) think," but the internet metabolizes memes extremely fast — stay at the frontier, stay technical, don't fall behind. The closing drink was the Guinness Wang brought from Dublin.
