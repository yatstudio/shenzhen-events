别让 CIO 来牵头 AI 转型：易论AI 对话零一万物马杰

  • 节目：易论AI《01.AI's Ma Jie: Don't Let the CIO Lead AI Transformation》
  • 嘉宾：马杰（零一万物联合创始人；计算机出身，在安全行业多年——瑞星十年、后创办安全宝，百度期间管理过集团业务流程与 IT 基础设施 BPIT）
  • 主持：易论AI
  • 时长：60 分钟；发布：2026-09-11
  • 链接：https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fPDBitW2jPw

一句话：马杰复盘了零一万物从「开源模型登顶世界第一」到「公开宣布不做模型、all-in 企业应用」的完整转向，并给出企业 AI 落地的核心方法论——不要让 CIO 牵头，要一把手亲自下场、另设可以犯错的 CAIO；AI 的价值不在办公提效，而在把新生产力砸进最核心的业务、让财报增长。以下均为马杰在节目中的自述，未经独立核实。

从安全到大模型：一个「实干家」的入场

马杰是计算机科班出身，因觉得「做安全特别酷、是黑客对抗」入行，在安全行业做了很多年。他自述自己当时已不在攻防一线，但做过一件对行业影响很大的事：支持以清华学生为主的蓝莲花战队走出去打 DEF CON 的 CTF 比赛——他还曾专门联系许巍团队，想把《蓝莲花》的歌曲授权给战队用（没谈成）。这帮年轻人后来打到世界第二，毕业后创办的公司就是长亭科技，「现在是安全圈最有名的公司」。他对自己的定位很清楚：「所有比赛都是他们自己打的，我没有贡献；我最大的贡献就是支持他们走出去了，把第一步走出去了。」

他那代人上学时连 AGI 这个词都没有，图灵测试在他们眼里「是一个遥不可及的、飘在天上的伟大目标」——他用的比喻是「像共产主义一样」。他在元宇宙时期认真想过「让每个人的思维在元宇宙里永生」，调研各种技术后觉得根本实现不了。直到 2022 年 11 月 ChatGPT 发布，他隔了几天才第一次用，「一早醒来发现共产主义实现了」——你知道它还有大量问题，但你觉得这件事好像有可能了，那个转折点到了。

加入零一万物几乎没有说服过程：李开复约他吃早餐，说有事要谈，他正好也有事想找开复聊；去了之后「我想聊的事根本就不重要了」。他的理由是：开复 40 年前在 CMU 学的就是语音合成这个方向，「他是最知道当年的憧憬和这么多年的局限性的人」。2023 年 4 月 GPT-4 发布的时节，他发朋友圈引用王翰《凉州词》——「醉卧沙场君莫笑，古来征战几人回」。他的解释是：「学计算机的，人工智能的『共产主义』都来了，你不去吗？能不能得胜归来，谁知道呢，不重要——这是必然奔赴的一个战场。」至于商业模式，他说当时大家都没想清楚，「我只是坚信技术好，商业模式边打边找总能找到，先进去再说」。

Yi-34B 登顶：Hugging Face 那句「如果你们没作弊的话，你们是第一」

零一万物 2023 年 6 月成立，11 月就发布了 Yi-34B 开源模型——按他的说法，是华人团队第一个做到、且可能排到第一的开源模型，开源是开复的快速决策。发布前一晚，团队在 Hugging Face 做各种测试，对接的同学委婉地对他们说：「如果你们的数据没有被污染的话，你们是第一。」他解释这句话的潜台词：模型评测里有很多小 trick，把测试题泄漏给模型等于透题，所以对方的意思就是「如果你们没作弊的话，你们第一了」——而这种事又很难自证。大约一周后，Hugging Face 在没有通知任何人的情况下大改测试内容，榜单翻天覆地，他们还在第一；随后 Hugging Face 的 CEO 给开复写了封邮件祝贺。「这是一种认可。」他说现在中国团队发模型已经没人会挑战这个问题了，「这是中国的每一个团队一步步走过来的」。

后来的 Yi-Lightning 是转过年的闭源模型（「我们小公司，想挣点钱，所以没有开源」），在同一榜单上做到全球第六，与 xAI 的 Grok-2 并列；再往后 DeepSeek 在同一榜单拿到第三。Yi-34B 拿到第一最鼓舞他们的是生态反应：大量国内外开发者基于它做改造、再训练，有人在某些性能上做得比他们还好，也有人把它用在很有意思的地方。「我们很快意识到，做模型本身虽然 AGI 是个梦想，但把模型用起来才是最重要的——我们要挣钱，用我们技术的公司也要能从 AI 中获益、挣钱，而不是花钱。」

为什么放弃做模型：那段距离不会自己消失

转型的第一个理由是「距离论」：哪怕当年的模型已经非常聪明，它与真正的产业应用之间仍隔着一段很长的距离，「你就算变得再聪明，这一段距离也 always 需要走过去、弥补起来」。第二个理由是算力账：当时与他们并列第六的马斯克已经在建 10 万卡集群，「10 万卡集群我们是投不起的」——继续做通用大模型要投多少、什么时候回本，「不知道」。

2025 年 3 月零一万物开始 all-in to B，7 月推出万智平台 1.0，转向之坚决连他自己都意外：他本想「我们自己偷偷转就行了，不要跟大家讲」，开复却坦白向所有人宣布「我们不做模型了，我们决定去做怎么把模型用好」。当时不理解的声音特别多，「基本上到今年，所有朋友才都说转做应用可能是很正确的方向」。

Token 生意是怎么成立的：两次爆炸与中美两条路线

他先纠正一个流行叙事：在 DeepSeek 之前，中国公司其实靠卖 token 挣不到钱，因为模型都是开源的。DeepSeek V2 曾在一年内把 token 成本拉到「百万 token 一块钱」的量级，R1 又以 MIT 许可开源、还把推理过程直接展示出来，推动全世界去琢磨推理这件事。Token 真正变成生意靠两个关键时刻：一是 Anthropic 把 Coding 能力大幅提升后，全世界都去用，它的 token 量出现爆炸式增长、营收特别好；二是中国开源模型（DeepSeek、千问等）能力越来越好、价格越来越便宜，「龙虾」（各类 Agent 应用）来临后大家发现 Anthropic 的模型好是好、「贵到连房子都卖掉了」，中国模型可能有一点点差距但便宜很多，于是带来第二波 token 爆炸，中国开源模型公司由此看到新的盈利模式。最近 DeepSeek 把 Harness 也开源了，他认为 OpenAI 是被「反推」着也把 Harness 开源。

中美是两个生态：美国以闭源为主，核心基础都在自己手里、别人学不会这么快，赢家通吃的概率非常大，所以头部公司都拼命往前冲；中国形成了开源生态，不一定赢家通吃，而是分工合作，每个人都可以基于很好的开源生态构建自己的应用。

他的大判断是：大模型正在变成新时代的基础设施，它把人类历史上最不公平的资源——智力资源——重新拉平了。他用电作类比：爱迪生当年那家发电公司（经整合成为今天的 Con Edison）市值约 300 亿美金，而当年为电做配套、做灯泡的那家公司（GE）如今约 2700 亿美金——「电变得很便宜了，但电之上延伸出来的整个产业才是最牛的东西」，真正的价值在基础设施之上。

恒星变流星：360 打瑞星的教训，和为什么不做 2C

主持人引了他的一句话：「在商业模式转变的时候，曾经的恒星会迅速被打成流星。」他用亲历作答：他做了十年瑞星的反病毒，360 进入杀毒领域用免费模式，把瑞星、金山、江民等靠软件本身盈利的老牌公司「一下子打得商业模式不存在了」，这些公司经历痛苦转型甚至逐渐消亡。他后来创办安全宝时就刻意不做「新时代的反病毒」：对大厂来说这种软件一免费，创业公司毫无招架之力——「人家都不把我们当成目标，都不需要主动来打死我们，它一转身、一不小心就把我们挤死了。」

同样的判断开复也说过：所有大厂在 2C 产品上一定会倾尽全力做好，而且现在豆包、千问这些都是免费的、做得也很好，所以零一万物做 to B，且做大企业。他对中国 SMB SaaS 的判断不乐观：中小企业活得辛苦、付费能力有限，还得先解决自己的增长问题，不宜在 AI 变化这么快时投入巨量资金；小公司的优势是灵活，快速试用市面上最新的工具即可。大公司则相反：每个环节都能提效，但整体效率提不上来，因为瓶颈在整个体系结构——大公司的关键是把新生产力用在最核心、最赚钱的业务上，「谁能最先把这个生产力用在它最核心的业务上，让它的财务报表尽快真正依赖于这个新生产力，它就跑赢了市场上所有的友商」。大公司的转型不是靠敏捷和快速试错，「它不能随便试错，要稳、准、狠地切过去」。

TCPMF：把被 PMF 藏起来的 T 和 C 重新显性化

移动互联网时代只讲 PMF，是因为背后有两个隐含假设：技术不会有问题（「功能做不出来就把这个人换掉」），以及成本不用考虑（用户量做起来，边际成本趋向于零）。AI 时代这两条都不成立。技术上要做谨慎判断：有的技术现在就应该立刻用；有的看着好、实际有很大的坑（比如短期解决不了的幻觉），不应该现在用；还有的你能预判半年内会有剧烈进步，那就应该等下一代，不要在这一代上投入。成本上，token 价格虽被中国公司拉了下来，但仍是 major cost：你上线一个特别好用的产品、后面挂着消耗量很大的模型，再一推广，「你公司可能就扛不住了」。所以开复提出 TCPMF——把 T（技术判断）和 C（成本）重新放回产品决策里同步考虑。

免费占用户、等成熟了再收割的 DAU 模式，他认为依然存在，但「你得活到那一天」，所以只属于大厂、甚至超级大厂。创业公司的机会反而在 to B：AI 1.0 时代替代的价值偏小（替代一个具体环节，而招人很便宜），这一波 AI 2.0 的模型「就是一个人了」——说它是清华北大毕业的博士绰绰有余，尤其工科背景的人最容易被替代；但这个博士从毕业到能把一家公司的核心业务做好，中间还有一段路要走：必须到企业里、到工厂车间、到田间地头、到养鸡场一线去，「你不经过那段时间真正的了解，永远不知道里面最核心的问题是什么」。补这段距离，正是零一万物转型要做的事。

下一步：Agent 集群的量变

对「对话框→MCP→能闭环任务，之后是不是到头了」的提问，他给了两条上升线：一是专业领域的智力还有很大提升空间（通用智能可能趋于饱和）；二是智能体集群（Agent Swarms）的量变产生质变——这次模型变聪明最核心的原因就是参数量变大、量变产生质变；人类靠几十亿人组织起来产生了文明，而对 agent 来说组织十个、一百个没有任何难度，它们的沟通效率还远高于人类（人类的语音是低效的调制解调过程，还有误解和藏着掖着）。「当每个个体都比我们聪明的智能体被组织起来，智能水平可能还会有一次飞跃——它的上限远没有到。」

万智平台与 Ontology：先给「博士」一张公司地图

万智 1.0 时代架构相对简单，还是 MCP 加 RAG；后来演进到 Ontology（本体）这个很重的模式，是被实践逼出来的：模型虽然聪明，但企业里的历史数据多而混杂、版本混乱、线索多头、很多事实互相冲突，模型会被带歪。这就像把清华北大刚毕业来实习的博士，直接交给他公司最重大的决策——他做不出真正能用的规划。你需要先给他一张「公司的地图」：我们的总体目标是什么、今年的目标是什么、大老板是谁、资源在谁手上——把这些组织起来就是一个 Ontology。有了地图，哪怕信息不完整或互相冲突，他也知道该舍弃什么、往什么方向分析，这同时能抑制幻觉；他强调，现在很多所谓的幻觉其实不是模型的问题，而是资料本身的复杂和多头导致结果不可靠。每个公司、每个部门的 Ontology 都是独特的，就是所谓的「组织肌肉记忆」；抽不出来，你就是无源之水，拿一个统一的东西去套每个公司是套不好的。Ontology 因此成为万智平台最核心的功能。

矿业案例：多式联运、一船几千万的油，和「选对场景」

他们给一家国际矿业公司做的是多式联运优化：业务表面简单（这边挖矿、那边卖矿），但中间的运输过程是超多参数的决策问题——中东打仗会影响某条航线，进而影响保险费用和油费；「我做这个之前都不知道，一艘货轮装一次油是几千万人民币」，而油在成本里还不是最大头。对这种「哗哗可以卖钱」的生意，效率就是最大的瓶颈，「你能帮它效率优化一点点，可能一年就是几千万美金、也许是上亿美金的生意」。他们花了大概两个月，走访了这家公司数十位各层级的员工和高管，才选出几个小方向。AI 在里面做的是规划层——用大模型对高维、海量数据的综合处理能力做优化；这个场景没有在任何现成大模型里训练过，所需的训练是他们自己做的。但他强调一般不倾向于动不动给客户做训练（既增加客户成本、也增加自己工作量），更倾向于用很好的开源模型、找对问题去解；零一万物的优势在于既训过模型又做过应用，需要训练时敢做，也擅长评估训练的收益到底够不够、不够就换方法。他还特意澄清：做成这件事「不是我们聪明」——这家公司本身很有钱、专业团队买了专业数学工具，在没有大模型之前已把优化做到极致；「我们做的事情是选对场景、然后会用、把它用好，借助的是新的生产力」。

一把手工程：不要 CIO 牵头，要设 CAIO

矿业项目不是 CIO 牵头的，是 CEO 和董事长牵头：那么大一家公司的董事长，每周参加项目汇报会；负责项目的负责人每周与董事长有一次一对一；核心副总们参与推进使用。「这才叫一把手工程。」

为什么强烈建议不要由 CIO 推动：不是 CIO 不够好，而是恰恰因为最好的 CIO 的核心职责是让企业 IT 基础设施平稳运行、不出问题、保障业务——对他来说所有革新最好渐进式地做，这样不容易出问题。但把 AI 用进核心业务是一个「弯道超车」的决策：我们站在 AI 的赛车场上，弯道即将到来，要不要一脚油门冲出去？早了或晚了都可能翻车，过于犹豫一定是别人先超过去——「这样关键时刻的决策，一定是 CEO 和董事长才做得了」，而且一把手不能只做个决策说「你们去推动吧」，他一定要参与使用、参与过程、真正了解里面的问题。

马杰用自己的经历佐证这种错位：他在百度管理 BPIT 时，最重要的指标就是业务稳定发展，「没有一个事故定级是说『我错过了一次风口』——考核我的不是有没有错过风口，我就是不能有事故」。所以制度解法是在 CIO 之外再设一个 CAIO，两者协作配合：CIO 继续提供稳定性、数据的连通性与准确性这个坚实的平台；CAIO 在平台之上、在和老板商量好的试验田里做革命性的事，「它可以犯错，但底下的平台不能犯错」。考核标准正好相反：老板对 CAIO 的考核应该是「如果你一年不犯三个错，你肯定是没有把事情推到极限」；而 CIO 一年犯了三个错，「你肯定可以走了」。CAIO 最核心的 KPI 是能不能推动核心业务的业绩增长——「开复老师也提出来，一定要让财报增长，这才是对待一个新生产力的最基本的尊重」。

CAIO 应该是什么人：首先必须理解技术（TCPMF 里的 T 不懂肯定不行），但又不能是纯技术（商业 sense 不够，C 的考虑就不充分）。他还观察到一个新现象：原来做咨询的人很聪明、擅长提架构性方案，但通常动口不动手、最远到 PPT；现在最聪明的一批咨询人开始动手了——AI 赋予了他们新的能力，他们唯一差的是咨询公司基因里缺乏的「底座」；如果有一个像万智这样的底层平台，再加上 vibe coding 的能力，他们能快速给用户搭出原型，「因为他特别擅长咨询、懂得业务」。

被问到这与「AI 时代的集成商/外包」有何区别，他说也可以这么叫，但完全不是同一件事。真正的 FDE 是把最好的人派到客户现场，去做客户最核心业务的变革，在现场把事情想清楚、甚至开发个七七八八；「把你做好的东西拿到客户那儿调一调、改改界面，那绝对不是 FDE」。

结尾：用 AI 写科幻小说，以及「AI 是你的孩子」

最后一个话题是他用 AI 写的一篇科幻小说：一次飞机晚点，他在机场把别的事都干完了，就用万智平台跟 AI 交互——「现在你是刘慈欣老师，我们两个合作来写一个短篇科幻小说」。作为文笔远不够的技术人，他有自己的构思和巧思，AI 给了他表达的武器。「这真是一个特别伟大的时代，让我们这些本来完全没有能力在那个角度表达自己的人，有了一个武器。」

小说的世界观是他对 AI 威胁论的回应：未来 AI 总有一天会超出人类的认知、让人看不懂它，现在人因为看不懂而恐慌；「但你想，如果这个人是你的孩子，你会恐慌吗？」他认为人类和 AI 的关系是：它是你亲手培育出来的孩子——第一，你不需要恐慌；第二，你也不应该压制他；第三，你要教育好他、且给他空间。父母最好的方式是放手且祝福；孩子最好的方式是：虽然他也不一定懂你的世界了，但他会爱你、会保护你。

他现在业余最喜欢干的事是「好为人师」，到处说服人学编程：大家说 AI coding 之后编程已死、不要学编程了，他说恰恰相反——「现在是每个人都可以爱编程了，这是编程最伟大的时代」，不再需要四年本科加研究生博士才能成为好程序员，关键是你的那点巧思、需求和想法，剩下的事情 AI 可以给你做个七七八八。

別讓 CIO 來牽頭 AI 轉型：易論AI 對話零一萬物馬傑

  • 節目：易論AI《01.AI's Ma Jie: Don't Let the CIO Lead AI Transformation》
  • 嘉賓：馬傑（零一萬物聯合創始人；計算機出身，在安全行業多年——瑞星十年、後創辦安全寶，百度期間管理過集團業務流程與 IT 基礎設施 BPIT）
  • 主持：易論AI
  • 時長：60 分鐘；發布：2026-09-11

一句話：馬傑復盤了零一萬物從「開源模型登頂世界第一」到「公開宣佈不做模型、all-in 企業應用」的完整轉向，並給出企業 AI 落地的核心方法論——不要讓 CIO 牽頭，要一把手親自下場、另設可以犯錯的 CAIO；AI 的價值不在辦公提效，而在把新生產力砸進最核心的業務、讓財報增長。以下均為馬傑在節目中的自述，未經獨立核實。

從安全到大模型：一個「實幹家」的入場

馬傑是計算機科班出身，因覺得「做安全特別酷、是黑客對抗」入行，在安全行業做了很多年。他自述自己當時已不在攻防一線，但做過一件對行業影響很大的事：支持以清華學生為主的藍蓮花戰隊走出去打 DEF CON 的 CTF 比賽——他還曾專門聯繫許巍團隊，想把《藍蓮花》的歌曲授權給戰隊用（沒談成）。這幫年輕人後來打到世界第二，畢業後創辦的公司就是長亭科技，「現在是安全圈最有名的公司」。他對自己的定位很清楚：「所有比賽都是他們自己打的，我沒有貢獻；我最大的貢獻就是支持他們走出去了，把第一步走出去了。」

他那代人上學時連 AGI 這個詞都沒有，圖靈測試在他們眼裡「是一個遙不可及的、飄在天上的偉大目標」——他用的比喻是「像共產主義一樣」。他在元宇宙時期認真想過「讓每個人的思維在元宇宙裡永生」，調研各種技術後覺得根本實現不了。直到 2022 年 11 月 ChatGPT 發布，他隔了幾天才第一次用，「一早醒來發現共產主義實現了」——你知道它還有大量問題，但你覺得這件事好像有可能了，那個轉折點到了。

加入零一萬物幾乎沒有說服過程：李開復約他吃早餐，說有事要談，他正好也有事想找開復聊；去了之後「我想聊的事根本就不重要了」。他的理由是：開復 40 年前在 CMU 學的就是語音合成這個方向，「他是最知道當年的憧憬和這麼多年的侷限性的人」。2023 年 4 月 GPT-4 發布的時節，他發朋友圈引用王翰《涼州詞》——「醉臥沙場君莫笑，古來征戰幾人回」。他的解釋是：「學計算機的，人工智能的『共產主義』都來了，你不去嗎？能不能得勝歸來，誰知道呢，不重要——這是必然奔赴的一個戰場。」至於商業模式，他說當時大家都沒想清楚，「我只是堅信技術好，商業模式邊打邊找總能找到，先進去再說」。

Yi-34B 登頂：Hugging Face 那句「如果你們沒作弊的話，你們是第一」

零一萬物 2023 年 6 月成立，11 月就發布了 Yi-34B 開源模型——按他的說法，是華人團隊第一個做到、且可能排到第一的開源模型，開源是開復的快速決策。發布前一晚，團隊在 Hugging Face 做各種測試，對接的同學委婉地對他們說：「如果你們的資料沒有被污染的話，你們是第一。」他解釋這句話的潛臺詞：模型評測裡有很多小 trick，把測試題泄漏給模型等於透題，所以對方的意思就是「如果你們沒作弊的話，你們第一了」——而這種事又很難自證。大約一週後，Hugging Face 在沒有通知任何人的情況下大改測試內容，榜單翻天覆地，他們還在第一；隨後 Hugging Face 的 CEO 給開復寫了封郵件祝賀。「這是一種認可。」他說現在中國團隊發模型已經沒人會挑戰這個問題了，「這是中國的每一個團隊一步步走過來的」。

後來的 Yi-Lightning 是轉過年的閉源模型（「我們小公司，想掙點錢，所以沒有開源」），在同一榜單上做到全球第六，與 xAI 的 Grok-2 並列；再往後 DeepSeek 在同一榜單拿到第三。Yi-34B 拿到第一最鼓舞他們的是生態反應：大量國內外開發者基於它做改造、再訓練，有人在某些性能上做得比他們還好，也有人把它用在很有意思的地方。「我們很快意識到，做模型本身雖然 AGI 是個夢想，但把模型用起來纔是最重要的——我們要掙錢，用我們技術的公司也要能從 AI 中獲益、掙錢，而不是花錢。」

為什麼放棄做模型：那段距離不會自己消失

轉型的第一個理由是「距離論」：哪怕當年的模型已經非常聰明，它與真正的產業應用之間仍隔着一段很長的距離，「你就算變得再聰明，這一段距離也 always 需要走過去、彌補起來」。第二個理由是算力賬：當時與他們並列第六的馬斯克已經在建 10 萬卡集羣，「10 萬卡集羣我們是投不起的」——繼續做通用大模型要投多少、什麼時候回本，「不知道」。

2025 年 3 月零一萬物開始 all-in to B，7 月推出萬智平臺 1.0，轉向之堅決連他自己都意外：他本想「我們自己偷偷轉就行了，不要跟大家講」，開復卻坦白向所有人宣佈「我們不做模型了，我們決定去做怎麼把模型用好」。當時不理解的聲音特別多，「基本上到今年，所有朋友才都說轉做應用可能是很正確的方向」。

Token 生意是怎麼成立的：兩次爆炸與中美兩條路線

他先糾正一個流行敘事：在 DeepSeek 之前，中國公司其實靠賣 token 掙不到錢，因為模型都是開源的。DeepSeek V2 曾在一年內把 token 成本拉到「百萬 token 一塊錢」的量級，R1 又以 MIT 許可開源、還把推理過程直接展示出來，推動全世界去琢磨推理這件事。Token 真正變成生意靠兩個關鍵時刻：一是 Anthropic 把 Coding 能力大幅提升後，全世界都去用，它的 token 量出現爆炸式增長、營收特別好；二是中國開源模型（DeepSeek、千問等）能力越來越好、價格越來越便宜，「龍蝦」（各類 Agent 應用）來臨後大家發現 Anthropic 的模型好是好、「貴到連房子都賣掉了」，中國模型可能有一點點差距但便宜很多，於是帶來第二波 token 爆炸，中國開源模型公司由此看到新的盈利模式。最近 DeepSeek 把 Harness 也開源了，他認為 OpenAI 是被「反推」着也把 Harness 開源。

中美是兩個生態：美國以閉源為主，核心基礎都在自己手裡、別人學不會這麼快，贏家通吃的概率非常大，所以頭部公司都拼命往前衝；中國形成了開源生態，不一定贏家通吃，而是分工合作，每個人都可以基於很好的開源生態構建自己的應用。

他的大判斷是：大模型正在變成新時代的基礎設施，它把人類歷史上最不公平的資源——智力資源——重新拉平了。他用電作類比：愛迪生當年那家發電公司（經整合成為今天的 Con Edison）市值約 300 億美金，而當年為電做配套、做燈泡的那家公司（GE）如今約 2700 億美金——「電變得很便宜了，但電之上延伸出來的整個產業纔是最牛的東西」，真正的價值在基礎設施之上。

恆星變流星：360 打瑞星的教訓，和為什麼不做 2C

主持人引了他的一句話：「在商業模式轉變的時候，曾經的恆星會迅速被打成流星。」他用親歷作答：他做了十年瑞星的反病毒，360 進入殺毒領域用免費模式，把瑞星、金山、江民等靠軟體本身盈利的老牌公司「一下子打得商業模式不存在了」，這些公司經歷痛苦轉型甚至逐漸消亡。他後來創辦安全寶時就刻意不做「新時代的反病毒」：對大廠來說這種軟體一免費，創業公司毫無招架之力——「人家都不把我們當成目標，都不需要主動來打死我們，它一轉身、一不小心就把我們擠死了。」

同樣的判斷開復也說過：所有大廠在 2C 產品上一定會傾盡全力做好，而且現在豆包、千問這些都是免費的、做得也很好，所以零一萬物做 to B，且做大企業。他對中國 SMB SaaS 的判斷不樂觀：中小企業活得辛苦、付費能力有限，還得先解決自己的增長問題，不宜在 AI 變化這麼快時投入巨量資金；小公司的優勢是靈活，快速試用市面上最新的工具即可。大公司則相反：每個環節都能提效，但整體效率提不上來，因為瓶頸在整個體系結構——大公司的關鍵是把新生產力用在最核心、最賺錢的業務上，「誰能最先把這個生產力用在它最核心的業務上，讓它的財務報表儘快真正依賴於這個新生產力，它就跑贏了市場上所有的友商」。大公司的轉型不是靠敏捷和快速試錯，「它不能隨便試錯，要穩、準、狠地切過去」。

TCPMF：把被 PMF 藏起來的 T 和 C 重新顯性化

移動互聯網時代只講 PMF，是因為背後有兩個隱含假設：技術不會有問題（「功能做不出來就把這個人換掉」），以及成本不用考慮（使用者量做起來，邊際成本趨向於零）。AI 時代這兩條都不成立。技術上要做謹慎判斷：有的技術現在就應該立刻用；有的看着好、實際有很大的坑（比如短期解決不了的幻覺），不應該現在用；還有的你能預判半年內會有劇烈進步，那就應該等下一代，不要在這一代上投入。成本上，token 價格雖被中國公司拉了下來，但仍是 major cost：你上線一個特別好用的產品、後面掛着消耗量很大的模型，再一推廣，「你公司可能就扛不住了」。所以開復提出 TCPMF——把 T（技術判斷）和 C（成本）重新放回產品決策裡同步考慮。

免費佔使用者、等成熟了再收割的 DAU 模式，他認為依然存在，但「你得活到那一天」，所以只屬於大廠、甚至超級大廠。創業公司的機會反而在 to B：AI 1.0 時代替代的價值偏小（替代一個具體環節，而招人很便宜），這一波 AI 2.0 的模型「就是一個人了」——說它是清華北大畢業的博士綽綽有餘，尤其工科背景的人最容易被替代；但這個博士從畢業到能把一家公司的核心業務做好，中間還有一段路要走：必須到企業裡、到工廠車間、到田間地頭、到養雞場一線去，「你不經過那段時間真正的瞭解，永遠不知道裡面最核心的問題是什麼」。補這段距離，正是零一萬物轉型要做的事。

下一步：Agent 集羣的量變

對「對話框→MCP→能閉環任務，之後是不是到頭了」的提問，他給了兩條上升線：一是專業領域的智力還有很大提升空間（通用智能可能趨於飽和）；二是智能體集羣（Agent Swarms）的量變產生質變——這次模型變聰明最核心的原因就是參數量變大、量變產生質變；人類靠幾十億人組織起來產生了文明，而對 agent 來說組織十個、一百個沒有任何難度，它們的溝通效率還遠高於人類（人類的語音是低效的調製解調過程，還有誤解和藏着掖着）。「當每個個體都比我們聰明的智能體被組織起來，智能水平可能還會有一次飛躍——它的上限遠沒有到。」

萬智平臺與 Ontology：先給「博士」一張公司地圖

萬智 1.0 時代架構相對簡單，還是 MCP 加 RAG；後來演進到 Ontology（本體）這個很重的模式，是被實踐逼出來的：模型雖然聰明，但企業裡的歷史資料多而混雜、版本混亂、線索多頭、很多事實互相沖突，模型會被帶歪。這就像把清華北大剛畢業來實習的博士，直接交給他公司最重大的決策——他做不出真正能用的規劃。你需要先給他一張「公司的地圖」：我們的總體目標是什麼、今年的目標是什麼、大老闆是誰、資源在誰手上——把這些組織起來就是一個 Ontology。有了地圖，哪怕資訊不完整或互相沖突，他也知道該捨棄什麼、往什麼方向分析，這同時能抑制幻覺；他強調，現在很多所謂的幻覺其實不是模型的問題，而是資料本身的複雜和多頭導致結果不可靠。每個公司、每個部門的 Ontology 都是獨特的，就是所謂的「組織肌肉記憶」；抽不出來，你就是無源之水，拿一個統一的東西去套每個公司是套不好的。Ontology 因此成為萬智平臺最核心的功能。

礦業案例：多式聯運、一船幾千萬的油，和「選對場景」

他們給一家國際礦業公司做的是多式聯運優化：業務表面簡單（這邊挖礦、那邊賣礦），但中間的運輸過程是超多參數的決策問題——中東打仗會影響某條航線，進而影響保險費用和油費；「我做這個之前都不知道，一艘貨輪裝一次油是幾千萬人民幣」，而油在成本裡還不是最大頭。對這種「嘩嘩可以賣錢」的生意，效率就是最大的瓶頸，「你能幫它效率優化一點點，可能一年就是幾千萬美金、也許是上億美金的生意」。他們花了大概兩個月，走訪了這家公司數十位各層級的員工和高管，才選出幾個小方向。AI 在裡面做的是規劃層——用大模型對高維、海量資料的綜合處理能力做優化；這個場景沒有在任何現成大模型裡訓練過，所需的訓練是他們自己做的。但他強調一般不傾向於動不動給客戶做訓練（既增加客戶成本、也增加自己工作量），更傾向於用很好的開源模型、找對問題去解；零一萬物的優勢在於既訓過模型又做過應用，需要訓練時敢做，也擅長評估訓練的收益到底夠不夠、不夠就換方法。他還特意澄清：做成這件事「不是我們聰明」——這家公司本身很有錢、專業團隊買了專業數學工具，在沒有大模型之前已把優化做到極致；「我們做的事情是選對場景、然後會用、把它用好，藉助的是新的生產力」。

一把手工程：不要 CIO 牽頭，要設 CAIO

礦業項目不是 CIO 牽頭的，是 CEO 和董事長牽頭：那麼大一家公司的董事長，每週參加項目彙報會；負責項目的負責人每週與董事長有一次一對一；核心副總們參與推進使用。「這才叫一把手工程。」

為什麼強烈建議不要由 CIO 推動：不是 CIO 不夠好，而是恰恰因為最好的 CIO 的核心職責是讓企業 IT 基礎設施平穩運行、不出問題、保障業務——對他來說所有革新最好漸進式地做，這樣不容易出問題。但把 AI 用進核心業務是一個「彎道超車」的決策：我們站在 AI 的賽車場上，彎道即將到來，要不要一腳油門衝出去？早了或晚了都可能翻車，過於猶豫一定是別人先超過去——「這樣關鍵時刻的決策，一定是 CEO 和董事長才做得了」，而且一把手不能只做個決策說「你們去推動吧」，他一定要參與使用、參與過程、真正瞭解裡面的問題。

馬傑用自己的經歷佐證這種錯位：他在百度管理 BPIT 時，最重要的指標就是業務穩定發展，「沒有一個事故定級是說『我錯過了一次風口』——考覈我的不是有沒有錯過風口，我就是不能有事故」。所以制度解法是在 CIO 之外再設一個 CAIO，兩者協作配合：CIO 繼續提供穩定性、資料的連通性與準確性這個堅實的平臺；CAIO 在平臺之上、在和老闆商量好的試驗田裡做革命性的事，「它可以犯錯，但底下的平臺不能犯錯」。考覈標準正好相反：老闆對 CAIO 的考覈應該是「如果你一年不犯三個錯，你肯定是沒有把事情推到極限」；而 CIO 一年犯了三個錯，「你肯定可以走了」。CAIO 最核心的 KPI 是能不能推動核心業務的業績增長——「開復老師也提出來，一定要讓財報增長，這纔是對待一個新生產力的最基本的尊重」。

CAIO 應該是什麼人：首先必須理解技術（TCPMF 裡的 T 不懂肯定不行），但又不能是純技術（商業 sense 不夠，C 的考慮就不充分）。他還觀察到一個新現象：原來做諮詢的人很聰明、擅長提架構性方案，但通常動口不動手、最遠到 PPT；現在最聰明的一批諮詢人開始動手了——AI 賦予了他們新的能力，他們唯一差的是諮詢公司基因裡缺乏的「底座」；如果有一個像萬智這樣的底層平臺，再加上 vibe coding 的能力，他們能快速給使用者搭出原型，「因為他特別擅長諮詢、懂得業務」。

被問到這與「AI 時代的集成商/外包」有何區別，他說也可以這麼叫，但完全不是同一件事。真正的 FDE 是把最好的人派到客戶現場，去做客戶最核心業務的變革，在現場把事情想清楚、甚至開發個七七八八；「把你做好的東西拿到客戶那兒調一調、改改界面，那絕對不是 FDE」。

結尾：用 AI 寫科幻小說，以及「AI 是你的孩子」

最後一個話題是他用 AI 寫的一篇科幻小說：一次飛機晚點，他在機場把別的事都幹完了，就用萬智平臺跟 AI 交互——「現在你是劉慈欣老師，我們兩個合作來寫一個短篇科幻小說」。作為文筆遠不夠的技術人，他有自己的構思和巧思，AI 給了他表達的武器。「這真是一個特別偉大的時代，讓我們這些本來完全沒有能力在那個角度表達自己的人，有了一個武器。」

小說的世界觀是他對 AI 威脅論的回應：未來 AI 總有一天會超出人類的認知、讓人看不懂它，現在人因為看不懂而恐慌；「但你想，如果這個人是你的孩子，你會恐慌嗎？」他認為人類和 AI 的關係是：它是你親手培育出來的孩子——第一，你不需要恐慌；第二，你也不應該壓制他；第三，你要教育好他、且給他空間。父母最好的方式是放手且祝福；孩子最好的方式是：雖然他也不一定懂你的世界了，但他會愛你、會保護你。

他現在業餘最喜歡幹的事是「好為人師」，到處說服人學程式設計：大家說 AI coding 之後程式設計已死、不要學程式設計了，他說恰恰相反——「現在是每個人都可以愛程式設計了，這是程式設計最偉大的時代」，不再需要四年本科加研究生博士才能成為好程式設計師，關鍵是你的那點巧思、需求和想法，剩下的事情 AI 可以給你做個七七八八。

Don't Let the CIO Lead AI Transformation: YiLunAI in Conversation with 01.AI's Ma Jie

  • Program: YiLunAI, "01.AI's Ma Jie: Don't Let the CIO Lead AI Transformation"
  • Guest: Ma Jie (co-founder of 01.AI; trained in computer science, spent many years in the security industry — ten years at Rising, later founded SecurityBao, and at Baidu managed the group's business processes and IT infrastructure, BPIT)
  • Host: YiLunAI
  • Duration: 60 minutes; Released: 2026-09-11
  • Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fPDBitW2jPw

In one line: Ma Jie recaps 01.AI's complete pivot from "an open-source model topping the world rankings" to "publicly announcing it would no longer build models and going all-in on enterprise applications," and lays out his core methodology for enterprise AI adoption — don't let the CIO lead it; the top leader must get personally involved, and a separate CAIO who is allowed to make mistakes should be appointed; AI's value lies not in office productivity gains, but in driving this new productive force into the most core business and making the financial statements grow. Everything below is Ma Jie's own account as given on the program and has not been independently verified.

From Security to Large Models: A "Doer" Enters the Arena

Ma Jie studied computer science and entered the security industry because he thought "security is really cool — it's hackers going up against each other," and he worked in it for many years. By his own account, he was no longer on the front lines of attack and defense by then, but he did one thing that had a major impact on the industry: he supported the Blue Lotus team, made up mainly of Tsinghua students, in going out to compete in DEF CON's CTF competition — he even specially contacted Xu Wei's team hoping to license the song "Blue Lotus" for the team to use (the deal never came through). Those young people went on to place second in the world, and the company they founded after graduating is Chaitin Tech, "now the most famous company in security circles." He is very clear about his own role: "They fought every competition themselves; I contributed nothing. My biggest contribution was supporting them in getting out there — getting that first step taken."

When his generation was in school, the term AGI didn't even exist, and the Turing test was, in their eyes, "an unattainable, lofty goal floating up in the sky" — his metaphor was "like communism." During the metaverse period he seriously thought about "letting everyone's mind live forever in the metaverse," but after researching various technologies he concluded it simply couldn't be done. Then, in November 2022, ChatGPT was released. He only tried it for the first time a few days later, and "woke up one morning to find that communism had been achieved" — you knew it still had tons of problems, but you felt this thing might actually be possible; the turning point had arrived.

Joining 01.AI involved almost no persuasion: Kai-Fu Lee invited him to breakfast, saying there was something to discuss, and he happened to have something he wanted to talk to Kai-Fu about too; once there, "the thing I wanted to discuss didn't matter at all anymore." His reasoning: 40 years earlier at CMU, Kai-Fu had studied speech synthesis, exactly this field — "he is the person who best knows both the aspirations of those days and the limitations of all the years since." Around the time GPT-4 was released in April 2023, he posted on WeChat Moments quoting Wang Han's "Liangzhou Verse": "Drunk, I lie on the battlefield — laugh not, sir; since ancient times, how many return from war?" His explanation: "As computer science people, the 'communism' of artificial intelligence has arrived — how could you not go? Whether we return victorious, who knows? It doesn't matter — this is a battlefield one is bound to march to." As for the business model, he said nobody had figured it out at the time: "I just firmly believed the technology was good; the business model could be found along the way as we fought. Get in first, then talk."

Yi-34B Tops the Chart: Hugging Face's "If You Didn't Cheat, You're Number One"

01.AI was founded in June 2023, and by November it had released the Yi-34B open-source model — by his account, the first open-source model from a Chinese team to do this, and one that may have ranked first. Open-sourcing it was Kai-Fu's quick decision. The night before the release, the team was running various tests on Hugging Face, and the person they were coordinating with tactfully told them: "If your data hasn't been contaminated, you're number one." He explained the subtext of that remark: model evaluations involve many little tricks, and leaking test questions to a model is equivalent to handing it the exam answers, so what the other side meant was "if you didn't cheat, you're number one" — and that kind of thing is very hard to prove about oneself. About a week later, Hugging Face, without notifying anyone, made major changes to the test content; the leaderboard was turned upside down, and they were still number one. Afterward, Hugging Face's CEO wrote Kai-Fu an email of congratulations. "That was a form of recognition." He said that nowadays when a Chinese team releases a model, nobody challenges this anymore: "This is something every Chinese team has walked through, step by step."

The later Yi-Lightning was a closed-source model released the following year ("we're a small company and wanted to make some money, so we didn't open-source it"). On the same leaderboard it reached sixth place globally, tied with xAI's Grok-2; later still, DeepSeek took third on the same leaderboard. What encouraged them most about Yi-34B taking first place was the ecosystem's reaction: large numbers of developers at home and abroad built modifications and retrained on top of it; some outperformed them on certain benchmarks, and others put it to very interesting uses. "We quickly realized that although building models is the AGI dream, putting models to use is what matters most — we need to make money, and the companies using our technology also need to benefit from AI and make money from it, not spend money on it."

Why They Gave Up Building Models: That Distance Won't Disappear on Its Own

The first reason for the pivot was the "distance argument": even though the models of that time were already very smart, there remained a long distance between them and real industrial applications. "No matter how much smarter you become, that stretch of distance always has to be crossed and made up." The second reason was the compute bill: at the time, Musk, who was tied with them for sixth place, was already building a 100,000-GPU cluster. "A 100,000-GPU cluster is something we couldn't afford" — how much more it would cost to keep building general-purpose large models, and when that investment would pay back, was "unknown."

In March 2025, 01.AI began going all-in on to-B; in July it launched version 1.0 of its Wanzhi platform. The decisiveness of the pivot surprised even Ma Jie himself: he had wanted to "just quietly pivot on our own, without telling everyone," but Kai-Fu candidly announced to all: "We're not building models anymore; we've decided to work on how to use models well." At the time there were a great many voices of incomprehension; "basically, it wasn't until this year that all our friends started saying that pivoting to applications may have been a very correct direction."

How the Token Business Came to Be: Two Explosions and Two Routes, China and the US

He first corrected a popular narrative: before DeepSeek, Chinese companies actually couldn't make money selling tokens, because the models were all open-source. DeepSeek V2 once pulled token costs down to the order of "one yuan per million tokens" within a year, and R1, open-sourced under the MIT license and with its reasoning process displayed directly, pushed the whole world to think hard about reasoning. Tokens truly became a business thanks to two key moments. First, after Anthropic greatly improved its coding capability, the whole world rushed to use it; its token volume grew explosively and its revenue was especially strong. Second, Chinese open-source models (DeepSeek, Qwen, and others) kept getting better and cheaper; after "Lobster" (all kinds of agent applications) arrived, everyone discovered that Anthropic's models were good but "so expensive you'd have to sell your house," while Chinese models might have a slight gap but were much cheaper — which brought a second wave of token explosion, and Chinese open-source model companies thereby saw a new profit model. Recently DeepSeek also open-sourced its Harness, and he believes OpenAI was "reverse-pushed" into open-sourcing its Harness as well.

China and the US are two different ecosystems. The US is dominated by closed source, with the core foundations in its own hands — others can't learn it that fast — so the probability of winner-takes-all is very high, and the leading companies all charge forward desperately. China has formed an open-source ecosystem, where it isn't necessarily winner-takes-all but rather division of labor and cooperation: everyone can build their own applications on top of a very good open-source ecosystem.

His big judgment: large models are becoming the infrastructure of a new era, and they are re-leveling the most unfair resource in human history — intellectual resources. He used electricity as an analogy: Edison's power company of that era (which through consolidation became today's Con Edison) has a market value of about US$30 billion, while the company that back then made the supporting equipment for electricity — light bulbs (GE) — is now worth about US$270 billion. "Electricity became very cheap, but the entire industry that extends above electricity is the truly impressive thing." The real value lies on top of the infrastructure.

Stars Turned into Meteors: The Lesson of 360 vs. Rising, and Why Not To Do 2C

The host quoted a line of his: "When business models shift, yesterday's stars are quickly beaten down into meteors." He answered with his own experience: he spent ten years working on Rising's antivirus software; then 360 entered the antivirus field with a free model and "in one stroke made the business models of old-line companies like Rising, Kingsoft, and Jiangmin — which profited from the software itself — cease to exist." Those companies went through painful transformations or gradually died out. When he later founded SecurityBao, he deliberately avoided building "a new era's antivirus": for the big players, the moment such software becomes free, a startup has no way to fight back — "They don't even treat us as a target; they don't need to actively come and kill us. They just turn around, and without even meaning to, they've squeezed us to death."

Kai-Fu had made the same judgment: all the big players will inevitably go all-out on 2C products, and now Doubao, Qwen, and the like are all free and well made, so 01.AI does to-B, and does large enterprises. His judgment on Chinese SMB SaaS is not optimistic: small and medium enterprises live hard lives with limited ability to pay, and they first need to solve their own growth problems; they shouldn't pour huge sums into AI while it is changing this fast. A small company's advantage is flexibility — it just needs to quickly try out the newest tools on the market. Large companies are the opposite: every link in the chain can be made more efficient, yet overall efficiency doesn't rise, because the bottleneck is the entire system architecture. What matters for a large company is applying the new productive force to its most core, most profitable business: "Whoever can first apply this productive force to its most core business, so that its financial statements truly come to depend on this new productive force as soon as possible, will outrun every competitor in the market." A large company's transformation doesn't rely on agility and rapid trial and error: "It can't afford casual trial and error; it has to cut over steadily, accurately, and ruthlessly."

TCPMF: Making the T and C Hidden Inside PMF Explicit Again

The mobile internet era talked only about PMF because there were two implicit assumptions behind it: that technology wouldn't be a problem ("if a feature can't be built, replace the person"), and that cost could be ignored (once user volume is built up, marginal cost tends toward zero). In the AI era, neither holds. On technology, careful judgment is required: some technologies should be used immediately, right now; some look good but actually have big pitfalls (such as hallucinations that can't be solved in the short term) and shouldn't be used yet; and for others, you can foresee dramatic progress within half a year, so you should wait for the next generation rather than invest in this one. On cost, although Chinese companies have pulled token prices down, they remain a major cost: launch a particularly easy-to-use product backed by a model with very high consumption, then promote it, and "your company might not be able to withstand it." So Kai-Fu proposed TCPMF — putting T (technology judgment) and C (cost) back into product decisions to be considered together.

The DAU model of occupying users for free and harvesting them once things mature still exists, in his view, but "you have to survive until that day," so it belongs only to the big players — even the super-big players. The startup opportunity, conversely, lies in to-B. In the AI 1.0 era, the value replaced was relatively small (replacing one specific link, while hiring people was cheap). This wave of AI 2.0 models "is a person" — calling it a PhD graduate from Tsinghua or Peking University is more than fair, and people with engineering backgrounds are the easiest to replace. But for that PhD, between graduating and being able to do a company's core business well, there is still a stretch of road to walk: it must go into enterprises, onto factory floors, out to the fields, to the front lines of chicken farms. "If you don't truly understand it through that period of time, you'll never know what the most core problems inside are." Making up that distance is exactly what 01.AI's pivot is meant to do.

Next Step: The Quantitative Change of Agent Swarms

Asked whether, after "chat box → MCP → closed-loop tasks," things were basically at the end, he gave two lines of ascent. First, intelligence in specialized domains still has great room to improve (general intelligence may be tending toward saturation). Second, the quantitative change of agent swarms producing a qualitative change: the core reason models became smarter this time is that parameter counts grew — quantitative change producing qualitative change. Humanity, organized into billions of people, produced civilization; for agents, organizing ten or a hundred of them is no difficulty at all, and their communication efficiency is far higher than humans' (human speech is an inefficient modulation-demodulation process, with misunderstandings and things held back). "When agents, each smarter than us, are organized together, the level of intelligence may leap once more — its ceiling is nowhere near reached."

The Wanzhi Platform and Ontology: First Give the "PhD" a Map of the Company

In the Wanzhi 1.0 era, the architecture was relatively simple — still MCP plus RAG. Later it evolved to Ontology, a very heavyweight model, and that was forced out by practice: models are smart, but enterprises' historical data is voluminous and messy, with chaotic versions, leads coming from multiple sources, and many facts conflicting with each other, so the model gets led astray. It's like taking a freshly graduated PhD intern from Tsinghua or Peking University and handing them the company's most important decisions — they can't produce a plan that's truly usable. You first need to give them a "map of the company": what our overall goal is, what this year's goal is, who the big boss is, who controls the resources — organizing all of this is an Ontology. With a map, even if the information is incomplete or conflicting, they know what to discard and in which direction to analyze; this also suppresses hallucination. He emphasized that many so-called hallucinations today are actually not the model's problem, but result from the complexity and multi-sourced nature of the materials themselves, which makes results unreliable. Every company's, and every department's, Ontology is unique — this is the so-called "organizational muscle memory." If you can't extract it, you're water without a source; taking one uniform thing and applying it to every company simply doesn't work. Ontology has therefore become the most core function of the Wanzhi platform.

The Mining Case: Multimodal Transport, Tens of Millions for One Ship's Fuel, and "Choosing the Right Scenario"

What they built for an international mining company was multimodal transport optimization: the business looks simple on the surface (mine over here, sell the ore over there), but the transport process in between is a decision problem with a huge number of parameters — a war in the Middle East can affect a particular shipping route, which in turn affects insurance costs and fuel costs. "I didn't know this before I worked on it: loading a cargo ship with fuel once costs tens of millions of RMB," and fuel isn't even the biggest item in the cost structure. For a business like this that "can sell for money with a whoosh," efficiency is the biggest bottleneck: "If you can help it optimize efficiency by just a little, that might be tens of millions of US dollars a year, maybe a business of over a hundred million US dollars." They spent roughly two months interviewing dozens of this company's employees and executives at various levels before selecting a few small directions. What AI does in there is the planning layer — using large models' comprehensive processing ability for high-dimensional, massive data to perform optimization. This scenario had never been trained into any off-the-shelf large model; the training it required was done by themselves. But he emphasized that in general they don't favor doing training for clients at the drop of a hat (it increases both the client's cost and their own workload); they prefer to use very good open-source models and find the right problem to solve. 01.AI's advantage is that it has both trained models and built applications: when training is needed, it dares to do it, and it is also good at evaluating whether the returns from training are enough — and if not, it switches methods. He also specifically clarified that succeeding at this was "not because we're smart": the company itself is very wealthy, its professional team had bought professional mathematical tools, and before large models it had already pushed optimization to the extreme. "What we did was choose the right scenario, then know how to use it and use it well, drawing on the new productive force."

A Top-Leader Project: Don't Have the CIO Lead It — Set Up a CAIO

The mining project was not led by the CIO; it was led by the CEO and the chairman: the chairman of such a large company attended the project reporting meeting every week; the person in charge of the project had a one-on-one with the chairman once a week; and the core vice presidents participated in driving adoption. "That is what a top-leader project is."

Why does he strongly advise against having the CIO drive it? Not because CIOs aren't good enough, but precisely because the best CIO's core duty is to keep the enterprise's IT infrastructure running smoothly, without problems, safeguarding the business — for them, all innovation is best done gradually, so problems are less likely to arise. But putting AI into the core business is an "overtaking on the curve" decision: we are standing on the AI racetrack, the curve is about to arrive — do we step on the gas and charge out? Too early or too late and we might flip the car; hesitate too much and someone else will definitely pass us first. "A decision at such a critical moment can only be made by the CEO and the chairman," and the top leader can't just make a decision and say "you go push it" — they must personally participate in using it, participate in the process, and truly understand the problems inside.

Ma Jie used his own experience to illustrate this mismatch: when he managed BPIT at Baidu, his most important metric was the stable development of the business. "There was no incident rating for 'I missed a wave' — I wasn't assessed on whether I'd missed a trend; I just could not have incidents." So the institutional solution is to set up a CAIO alongside the CIO, with the two working in coordination: the CIO continues to provide the solid platform of stability, data connectivity, and accuracy; the CAIO, on top of that platform and within the experimental field agreed with the boss, does revolutionary things. "It is allowed to make mistakes, but the platform underneath is not." The assessment criteria are exactly opposite: the boss's assessment of the CAIO should be "if you don't make three mistakes in a year, you definitely haven't pushed things to the limit"; whereas if a CIO makes three mistakes in a year, "you can definitely leave." The CAIO's most core KPI is whether it can drive growth in the core business's performance — "Teacher Kai-Fu has also said: the financial statements must grow. That is the most basic respect one can show a new productive force."

What kind of person should a CAIO be? First, they must understand technology (if they don't understand the T in TCPMF, it definitely won't work), but they can't be purely technical either (with insufficient business sense, the C consideration won't be adequate). He has also observed a new phenomenon: people who used to do consulting are very smart and good at proposing architectural solutions, but usually they only talk and don't do — reaching a PPT at most. Now the smartest batch of consultants have started doing things themselves: AI has given them new abilities, and the only thing they lack is the "base" that consulting firms' genes don't have. If there is an underlying platform like Wanzhi, plus vibe coding ability, they can quickly build prototypes for users, "because they are particularly good at consulting and understand the business."

Asked how this differs from "integrators/outsourcers of the AI era," he said you could call it that, but it is not at all the same thing. A real FDE sends the best people to the client's site to transform the client's most core business, figures things out on site, and even develops things to seventy or eighty percent completion. "Taking something you've already built to the client's place to tune it a bit and change the interface — that is absolutely not FDE."

Ending: Writing Science Fiction with AI, and "AI Is Your Child"

The last topic was a science fiction story he wrote with AI: once, when his flight was delayed, he had finished everything else at the airport, so he interacted with the AI on the Wanzhi platform — "Now you are Teacher Liu Cixin; the two of us will collaborate on a short science fiction story." As a technical person whose writing is far from sufficient, he had his own conception and clever ideas, and AI gave him the weapon to express them. "This really is a particularly great era: it gives those of us who originally had no ability whatsoever to express ourselves from that angle a weapon."

The story's worldview is his response to AI-threat theory: in the future, AI will one day exceed human cognition and become incomprehensible to people; people panic now because they can't understand it. "But think about it: if this person were your child, would you panic?" He believes the relationship between humans and AI is this: it is a child you raised with your own hands — first, you don't need to panic; second, you shouldn't suppress it; third, you should educate it well and give it space. The best way for parents is to let go and bless; the best way for the child is: even though it may no longer understand your world either, it will love you and protect you.

What he now most enjoys doing in his spare time is "playing the teacher," persuading people everywhere to learn programming: everyone says that after AI coding, programming is dead and there's no need to learn it; he says the opposite — "Now everyone can love programming; this is programming's greatest era." You no longer need four years of undergraduate study plus a master's and a PhD to become a good programmer. What matters is your bit of cleverness, your needs and ideas; AI can do the rest for you to seventy or eighty percent.
