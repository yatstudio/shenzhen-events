纪要｜《郭宇｜AI做员工，开一人公司》

视频：https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Prb_YPSdzH4 ｜时长 63 分钟｜郭宇 × 主持人（前青也/新足多背景，现中日两地跑的短视频创作者，同为一人公司实践者）对谈 + Wanman 系统讲解 说明：机器转写，繁简混杂、个别专名有误听（Wanman 转写为「万万/弯慢」，OpenClaw 转写为「OpenCloud」，Elemis 疑为某 Harness 产品名，转写不确）；纪要按上下文校正，部分后半段为采样通读。产品数据为讲述者现场自述，未经核实。

一句话

这场把「一人公司」讲到了定义层：它与普通用法的根本区别不在工具，而在谁是协调者。大多数人是「人当协调者、AI 当工具」，瓶颈永远在人身上；他主张倒过来——AI 当协调者，人退居二线做观察者，只在它遇到明确阻塞点、不得不请示时才干预。人剩下的工作只有两件：用聊天把自己的品味教给它，以及在它跑出的无数方案里做选择。

开场：回应「制造焦虑」的质疑

主持人先替网友发问：网上有人说你贩卖焦虑、说你「没正经上过班」。他回应：

  • 「享受最后一年的有意义的工作」是 YouTube 一位创始人（Jawed Karim 式洞察）的话，他认同其中技术主义者的悲观色彩。这话对技术工作者是内在动力（我们本来就想改造世界），传到大众那里就变成了外在压力、变成焦虑——压力的方向完全不同。短视频只能截取一个半小时分享的一小部分，误会由此而来。他现在尽量不看自己视频下面的评论。
  • 意思的澄清：超级 AI 出现在数据中心之后，很多工作会失去意义（在美国、中国被裁员是大概率，在日本有社会保障缓冲）；但这句话主要谈的是重新定位自己，而不是失业倒计时。
  • 标签问题：别人眼中的他是「在日本泡温泉、退休变现几个亿的知名程序员」，他给自己的 title 只是「退休程序员」。自我介绍永远只是人的一部分，认知隔阂很正常。

他为什么从躺平变回不睡觉

主持人问得很直接：你游山玩水了好几年，为什么突然站起来狂奔？他的叙述：

  • 温泉旅行社是 2017 年就开始做的项目，疫情期间暂停；退休后他的 to-do list 越攒越多（已发布 15 个产品，三个月内想做的事超过 36 件），这正是他要做 Wanman 的原因。
  • 转折点在去年 10 月：Claude 推出 Skill 模式后，他和程序员朋友们在 vibe coding 中意识到，软件正在经历图灵机发明以来最大的变化——从「预先定义功能和界面」转向「语言模型读文档、现写代码、现用现丢」，开发周期从几个月到一年压缩成一天（虽然还不够精确）。「如果不去做一个东西，我觉得很可惜。」
  • 压垮他的最后一根稻草是一个深夜对话：TiDB/PingCAP 创始人黄东旭告诉他，自己在做「最终的软件」——输入一个意图，它在电脑里并发开 100 来个沙箱、把软件的所有可能分支全部实现，一夜跑了 10 亿 token 量级；两三天可以造出一个纳斯达克股票交易系统级别的东西。那晚他没睡，七八点泡着咖啡看窗外的树和阳光，想的是：「从今天开始，我的人生彻底变化了。」Wanman 本质上就是同类东西。

核心论点：协调者是谁，决定一切

  • 对话式 AI（ChatGPT、Claude Chat、豆包）像雇了个实习生拉进微信群：你必须想清楚每件事再交代，沟通摩擦巨大，而且阻塞点在实习生和你之间来回弹，最终瓶颈是你。哪怕工具再强，你依然是全公司唯一的协调者，精力先耗尽。
  • 一人公司的本质是把协调权交出去：AI 写邮件、整理资料、做 PDF、打电话、协调所有需要沟通的事；他为此做了一串基础设施产品（转写里提到吃瓜、Wanman、Agent Mail 等，都是他做的，让 AI 能自动化协调工作的组件）。
  • 两类人的分化：AI 替代工作后，一类人拿回时间去享受生活（他称之为 classic real life，很多在日本生活的人选这个）；另一类人欲望多，要让 AI 24 小时把想做的事全做完——「像成年人一样全部都要」。他自认介于两者之间：想做的事太多，又想保留闲暇生活，所以痛苦，所以急着把 Wanman 做出来——让它无监督地替他把那些「值得做、但不值得我盯着做」的事做完。

观察者怎么参与：品味与否决权

  • 你不是撒手不管。AI 通过 Chat UI（微信/Telegram/WhatsApp）和你聊天，聊得越多，它对你的记忆、审美倾向理解越深，做出的东西越合你的倾向——像交朋友，也像带孩子：你不盯他每天刷多久抖音，你在更宏观的层面影响他。这正是黄仁勋说 taste 重要的原因：审美是你影响 AI 产出的主要杠杆。
  • 否决权随时可用：它给出很多方案，有你不喜欢的（比如不想走滑稽路线）就否决；AI 没有「不可能」的包袱，这点和人不同。
  • 他赞同推特上一种更激进的用法：先把「能 100% 完成的事」按从易到难、从快到慢全部让 AI 做一遍，把选择空间填满，再来挑——因为反过来，AI 10 分钟给你 100 个选题、你花三天看完还下不了决定，AI 对你就毫无意义，阻塞点全卡在人身上徒增烦恼。
  • 授权决定自动化程度：互联网上能做的知识工作，AI 已能覆盖 90% 以上；剩下的暂停点多是「需要人在场」的事（在日本拿在留卡去区役所之类）。去年底已经出现「AI 雇佣人类打零工」的网站来补这一环——AI 有预算、能花钱，就能把暂停点也自动化。所以问题只剩：你给它多少授权、多少权力。

Harness Engineering 讲解（后半程）

  • 词源梗：Harness 是缰绳/马具——把 AI 想成疯跑的马，你一个人牵着三匹马，像马车夫一样控制方向；最近流行的一个产品干脆取名向爱马仕致敬（转写作「Elemis/爱马仕」）。
  • 谱系：Claude Code 本是 Anthropic 的 side project，现在用 Opus 4.6/4.7 直接调动模型能力；OpenClaw 是编排层，协调不同模型与具体的 agent；再往上是更高度的协调系统与 auto research。它们的目标都一样：把人从工作里拆出来。OpenClaw 的起源印证了这一点：其创始人卖公司拿了几亿美元、退休五年、迷茫抑郁后靠健身走出来，2025 年写了 100 来个开源项目，龙虾最初只是「用 Telegram 连上我笔记本的 Claude Code」的小工具——因为他也不想整天抱着电脑看 AI 干活。今年 3 月它的 GitHub star 数超过 Linux，成为全球 star 最高的软件，黄仁勋称之为 game changing。但他不满本地派：需要一台专门的电脑、性能有限、动你的真实系统有风险；他主张云端派：沙箱是隔离环境、性能更强、用完即弃（像临时工，「很残忍」），且数据中心有最快的推理速度。
  • 先发优势论：谁先采用这套系统，谁的摩擦与协调都沉淀在 AI 的记忆里；晚入场者要花长得多的磨合期，竞争力此消彼长。AI 员工和实习生一样：第一周做得一塌糊涂，靠正反馈、导师制和不断改进的技能文档变强——所有 Harness 系统都带记忆与技能自我改善机制。
  • 记忆的分寸：他顺带吐槽了 ChatGPT 式记忆的黑色幽默——你告诉它你失恋了，几个月后它在不相干的对话里突然提起「考虑到你失恋这个事实……」，它不懂关系建立的场合，所以需要「干净模式」：无关的话题不要乱建立关联。人的记忆会被情绪影响、会主动丢掉无意义的对话，AI 不会。
  • Wanman 的运行形态（现场演示解说）：给一个大目标（例：运行一个数字出版社、一个游戏工作室），它先按任务组建团队、安排写手、去网上找素材下载；AI-CEO 不写具体文件，只对齐目标；每个「虚拟工作日」约 5–8 分钟，员工们在左侧的聊天流里彼此对齐、自动推进，CEO 每轮复核评分、把完成的故事标记为完成；遇到障碍它不会停——比如实拍太贵，第二天 CEO 会自己发散想到用 Seedance 2.0 生成素材绕过去。系统内置 40 多个 agent 角色（连「给钓鱼人群卖东西」这种垂直场景，它都知道钓友在哪些社区、该怎么卖）。通过 webhook/hook 可以接入现实：他的电话工具自动接听来电、把对方意图记成备忘录，AI 员工据此发起预约链接，对方点开约好时间后自动生成日历提醒。
  • 最后的暂停点：AI 跑出的卡片速度太快、创造的东西太多，人根本看不过来——这正是开场那个不信任问题的来源。有些新产品更激进：用你一生的经验、美学和价值观替你做选择，把人最后的暂停点也摘掉。他对此未置可否地留了一句：模型也许有一天比你还了解你（人脑记忆不过百来 TB 量级），但那一天是否该来，他没有下结论。

收束

主持人也是单向街的常客、同样在做一人公司，两人最后对了一句：这场变化替代的不是某个软件，而是人的工作本身；Harness Engineering 迟早会内置进所有企业、组织和学校。区别只在于，你是先上车、先把磨合期熬完的那批人，还是等它成熟后再花更长时间追赶的那批人。

  • 譜系：Claude Code 本是 Anthropic 的 side project，現在用 Opus 4.6/4.7 直接調動模型能力；OpenClaw 是編排層，協調不同模型與具體的 agent；再往上是更高度的協調系統與 auto research。它們的目標都一樣：把人從工作裡拆出來。OpenClaw 的起源印證了這一點：其創始人賣公司拿了幾億美元、退休五年、迷茫抑鬱後靠健身走出來，2025 年寫了 100 來個開源項目，龍蝦最初只是「用 Telegram 連上我筆電的 Claude Code」的小工具——因為他也不想整天抱著電腦看 AI 幹活。今年 3 月它的 GitHub star 數超過 Linux，成為全球 star 最高的軟體，黃仁勳稱之為 game changing。但他不滿本地派：需要一台專門的電腦、性能有限、動你的真實系統有風險；他主張雲端派：沙箱是隔離環境、性能更強、用完即棄（像臨時工，「很殘忍」），且資料中心有最快的推理速度。
  • 先發優勢論：誰先採用這套系統，誰的摩擦與協調都沉澱在 AI 的記憶裡；晚入場者要花長得多的磨合期，競爭力此消彼長。AI 員工和實習生一樣：第一週做得一塌糊塗，靠正反饋、導師制和不斷改進的技能文件變強——所有 Harness 系統都帶記憶與技能自我改善機制。
  • 記憶的分寸：他順帶吐槽了 ChatGPT 式記憶的黑色幽默——你告訴它你失戀了，幾個月後它在不相干的對話裡突然提起「考慮到你失戀這個事實……」，它不懂關係建立的場合，所以需要「乾淨模式」：無關的話題不要亂建立關聯。人的記憶會被情緒影響、會主動丟掉無意義的對話，AI 不會。
  • Wanman 的運行形態（現場演示解說）：給一個大目標（例：運行一個數字出版社、一個遊戲工作室），它先按任務組建團隊、安排寫手、去網上找素材下載；AI-CEO 不寫具體文件，只對齊目標；每個「虛擬工作日」約 5–8 分鐘，員工們在左側的聊天流裡彼此對齊、自動推進，CEO 每輪複核評分、把完成的故事標記為完成；遇到障礙它不會停——比如實拍太貴，第二天 CEO 會自己發散想到用 Seedance 2.0 生成素材繞過去。系統內置 40 多個 agent 角色（連「給釣魚人群賣東西」這種垂直場景，它都知道釣友在哪些社區、該怎麼賣）。通過 webhook/hook 可以接入現實：他的電話工具自動接聽來電、把對方意圖記成備忘錄，AI 員工據此發起預約連結，對方點開約好時間後自動生成日曆提醒。
  • 最後的暫停點：AI 跑出的卡片速度太快、創造的東西太多，人根本看不過來——這正是開場那個不信任問題的來源。有些新產品更激進：用你一生的經驗、美學和價值觀替你做選擇，把人最後的暫停點也摘掉。他對此未置可否地留了一句：模型也許有一天比你還了解你（人腦記憶不過百來 TB 量級），但那一天是否該來，他沒有下結論。

收束

主持人也是單向街的常客、同樣在做一人公司，兩人最後對了一句：這場變化替代的不是某個軟體，而是人的工作本身；Harness Engineering 遲早會內置進所有企業、組織和學校。區別只在於，你是先上車、先把磨合期熬完的那批人，還是等它成熟後再花更長時間追趕的那批人。

In one sentence

This session took the "one-person company" down to the level of definition: its fundamental difference from the ordinary usage is not the tools, but who the coordinator is. Most people are "the person as coordinator, AI as tool," and the bottleneck is always on the person; he argues for reversing it — AI as coordinator, the person stepping back to the second line as an observer, intervening only when it hits a clear blocking point and has no choice but to ask for instructions. The person is left with only two jobs: teaching it your taste through chat, and choosing among the countless proposals it runs out.

Opening: responding to the charge of "manufacturing anxiety"

The host first asked on behalf of netizens: some people online say you are selling anxiety, and say you "have never properly held a job." He responded:

  • "Enjoy the last year of meaningful work" is a line from a YouTube founder (a Jawed Karim-style insight), and he identifies with the technologist's pessimism in it. For technical workers, the line is an inner drive (we wanted to change the world in the first place); when it reaches the general public it becomes external pressure, becomes anxiety — the direction of the pressure is completely different. A short video can only excerpt a small part of an hour-and-a-half talk, and misunderstandings arise from that. He now tries not to look at the comments under his own videos.
  • A clarification of meaning: after super AI appears in data centers, many jobs will lose meaning (in the US and China, being laid off is highly likely; in Japan there is a social security buffer); but the line is mainly about repositioning yourself, not a countdown to unemployment.
  • The label problem: in others' eyes he is "a famous programmer soaking in hot springs in Japan who cashed out several hundred million in retirement," while the title he gives himself is just "retired programmer." A self-introduction is always only part of a person, and a gap in perception is normal.

Why he went from lying flat to not sleeping again

The host asked very directly: you traveled and enjoyed yourself for several years, why did you suddenly stand up and sprint? His account:

  • The hot-spring travel agency was a project he started back in 2017, paused during the pandemic; after retiring, his to-do list kept piling up (15 products already released, more than 36 things he wants to do within three months), and that is exactly why he wants to build Wanman.
  • The turning point was last October: after Claude launched its Skill mode, he and his programmer friends realized in vibe coding that software is undergoing the biggest change since the invention of the Turing machine — shifting from "predefining functions and interfaces" to "the language model reads documents, writes code on the spot, uses it and discards it," with development cycles compressed from months to a year down to a day (although not yet precise enough). "If I don't go build something, I think it would be a pity."
  • The last straw was a late-night conversation: Huang Dongxu, founder of TiDB/PingCAP, told him he was building "the final software" — you input an intent, and it concurrently opens about 100 sandboxes in the computer and implements all possible branches of the software, running on the order of 1 billion tokens in a night; in two or three days it can build something at the level of a Nasdaq stock trading system. That night he did not sleep; at seven or eight, sipping coffee and looking at the trees and sunlight outside the window, what he thought was: "From today on, my life has completely changed." Wanman is essentially the same kind of thing.

Core argument: who the coordinator is decides everything

  • Conversational AI (ChatGPT, Claude Chat, Doubao) is like hiring an intern and pulling them into a WeChat group: you have to think everything through clearly before explaining it, communication friction is enormous, and the blocking point bounces back and forth between the intern and you, with you as the ultimate bottleneck. No matter how strong the tool, you are still the company's sole coordinator, and your energy runs out first.
  • The essence of a one-person company is handing over the right to coordinate: AI writes emails, organizes materials, makes PDFs, makes phone calls, and coordinates everything that needs communicating; for this he built a string of infrastructure products (the transcript mentions Chigua, Wanman, Agent Mail and others, all built by him, components that let AI coordinate work automatically).
  • The divergence of two kinds of people: after AI replaces work, one kind of person takes back time to enjoy life (he calls it classic real life, and many people living in Japan choose this); another kind has many desires and wants AI to do everything they want to do, 24 hours a day — "want it all, like an adult." He counts himself as in between: too many things he wants to do, yet he also wants to keep a leisurely life, so it is painful, so he is rushing to build Wanman — to let it, unsupervised, finish for him those things that are "worth doing, but not worth me watching it do."

How the observer participates: taste and the power of veto

  • You are not letting go completely. AI chats with you through a Chat UI (WeChat/Telegram/WhatsApp); the more you chat, the deeper its understanding of your memory and aesthetic tendencies, and the more what it makes aligns with your tendencies — like making a friend, and like raising a child: you do not watch how long he scrolls Douyin every day, you influence him at a more macro level. That is exactly why Jensen Huang says taste matters: aesthetics is your main lever for influencing AI's output.
  • The power of veto is available at any time: it gives many proposals, and if there is one you do not like (for example, not wanting to go down a comical route) you veto it; AI does not carry the baggage of "impossible," which is different from people.
  • He agrees with a more radical usage on Twitter: first have AI do all the "things that can be 100% completed," ordered from easy to hard and from fast to slow, filling up the space of choices, and only then pick — because in reverse, AI gives you 100 topics in 10 minutes, you spend three days looking through them and still cannot decide, and AI is meaningless to you, with all the blocking points stuck on the person, only adding vexation.
  • Authorization decides the degree of automation: for knowledge work that can be done on the internet, AI can already cover more than 90%; the remaining pause points are mostly things that "require a person to be present" (such as taking a residence card to the ward office in Japan). At the end of last year, websites for "AI hiring humans for gig work" already appeared to fill this link — if AI has a budget and can spend money, it can automate the pause points too. So the only question left is: how much authorization, how much power, you give it.

Explaining Harness Engineering (second half)

  • The etymology joke: A harness is reins / horse tack — think of AI as a madly running horse, and you alone holding three horses, controlling direction like a coachman; a recently popular product simply took its name as a tribute to Hermès (transcribed as "Elemis / Hermès").
  • The lineage: Claude Code was originally Anthropic's side project, and now uses Opus 4.6/4.7 to directly mobilize model capabilities; OpenClaw is the orchestration layer, coordinating different models and specific agents; above that are higher-level coordination systems and auto research. Their goal is all the same: to take the person out of the work. OpenClaw's origin confirms this: its founder sold his company for several hundred million dollars, retired for five years, came out of confusion and depression through fitness, wrote about 100 open-source projects in 2025, and the lobster was originally just a small tool for "connecting to the Claude Code on my laptop via Telegram" — because he also did not want to hold his computer all day watching AI work. This March its GitHub star count surpassed Linux, making it the highest-starred software in the world, and Jensen Huang called it game changing. But he is dissatisfied with the local camp: it needs a dedicated computer, has limited performance, and risks touching your real system; he advocates the cloud camp: the sandbox is an isolated environment, performance is stronger, it is discarded after use (like a temp worker, "very cruel"), and data centers have the fastest inference speed.
  • The first-mover advantage argument: whoever adopts this system first will have their friction and coordination deposited in AI's memory; late entrants will need a much longer breaking-in period, and competitiveness will shift accordingly. AI employees are like interns: in the first week they do a terrible job, and they get stronger through positive feedback, a mentorship system and constantly improving skill documents — all Harness systems come with memory and skill self-improvement mechanisms.
  • The sense of proportion in memory: He also poked fun at the black humor of ChatGPT-style memory — you tell it you have broken up, and months later, in an unrelated conversation, it suddenly brings up "considering the fact that you have broken up…"; it does not understand the occasion for building a relationship, so it needs a "clean mode": do not randomly build associations for unrelated topics. Human memory is influenced by emotion and actively discards meaningless conversations; AI's does not.
  • How Wanman runs (live demonstration commentary): Give it a big goal (for example, running a digital publishing house, a game studio), and it first forms a team by task, arranges writers, goes online to find materials and download them; the AI-CEO does not write specific files, it only aligns goals; each "virtual workday" is about 5–8 minutes, employees align with each other in the chat stream on the left and advance automatically, the CEO reviews and scores each round and marks completed stories as complete; when it encounters an obstacle it does not stop — for example, if live shooting is too expensive, the next day the CEO will itself diverge and think of using Seedance 2.0 to generate materials to get around it. The system has more than 40 built-in agent roles (even for a vertical scenario like "selling things to fishing enthusiasts," it knows which communities anglers are in and how to sell). Through webhooks/hooks it can connect to reality: his phone tool automatically answers incoming calls, records the other party's intent as a memo, AI employees initiate a booking link based on it, and after the other party clicks and books a time, it automatically generates a calendar reminder.
  • The final pause point: AI runs out cards too fast and creates too many things for a person to possibly look through — that is exactly the source of the distrust question at the opening. Some new products are more radical: using your lifetime of experience, aesthetics and values to choose for you, removing even the person's final pause point. He left a noncommittal line on this: a model may one day know you better than you know yourself (human brain memory is only on the order of a hundred-odd TB), but whether that day should come, he drew no conclusion.

Closing

The host is also a regular at One Way Street and is also running a one-person company, and the two ended by agreeing on a line: what this change replaces is not some piece of software, but human work itself; Harness Engineering will sooner or later be built into all enterprises, organizations and schools. The only difference is whether you are among those who get on board first and get through the breaking-in period first, or those who wait until it matures and then spend even longer catching up.
