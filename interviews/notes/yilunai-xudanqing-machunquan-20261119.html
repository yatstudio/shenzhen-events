纪要：易论AI《AI 应用到底是不是一门好生意？》（许丹青 × 马春荃）

  • 时间：2026-11-19（YouTube 发布；X 推文标题《AI 应用到底是不是一门好生意？》，YouTube 标题《2027年AI应用将迎来爆发》）
  • 主持：雅婷（易论AI）
  • 嘉宾：许丹青（财搭子创始人）、马春荃（合思创始人）
  • 时长：2 小时 22 分
  • 来源：YouTube 频道易论AI，视频 aKkliD-5KWo；无官方字幕，本稿据音频机器转写整理（3153 行），个别专有名词以节目与公开信息为准

本纪要为中文整理。营收、成本等数字均为嘉宾现场自述口径，未独立核实。

一句话

两位一线创始人对账 AI 应用的生意经：现在中美能赚到钱的 AI 应用，几乎全是 2B/2P 里「价值算得明白」的效率生意，2C 还没跑出明确赚钱的，但两人都判 2027 年是拐点。这一期最硬的共识有三条：token 账单不会随模型降价而下降（任务变复杂了）、ARR 是正在失效的虚荣指标、应用公司真正的壁垒是用户个性化数据加未成文的 know-how——以及马春荃那句暴论：与其建壁垒防着被大模型杀死，不如先杀掉自己、重新投胎。

一、两家先自报家门

  • 财搭子（许丹青）：2024 年 6 月创立，为普通散户做 AI 投资助手。团队 12 人，自称非常 AI 原生；收入规模几百万元人民币，在盈亏平衡边缘——不算人力成本是盈利的，加上人力还不够。许丹青本人计算机出身，硕士期间用强化学习预测金融市场获奖，后在通联数据、罗伯投研做投研产品（主持人介绍其产品巅峰日活达百万级）。
  • 合思（马春荃）：2014 年成立（前身易快报），从费控报销起家，延展到企业因公消费与财务收支管理，现做智能财务与智能商旅两条线。团队 400 人，营收三个多亿，今年终于走出历史亏损周期。马春荃在用友十多年；主持人点出合思是老牌软件厂商里「革自己命最彻底的」——他曾拉一个 Harness 小组封闭开发，目标就是用 AI 干掉合思自己。

二、用户到底愿意为什么付钱

  • 财搭子最受欢迎的功能换过一茬：2025 年下半年到 2026 年初是研报（基于行业框架生成个股/行业研报，并做动态更新，追踪边际变化）；模型公司把 Deep Research 做成免费通用能力后，这个功能的转化明显下降。今年 3 月推出的托管功能成为新主力：用户上传资产截图或做账户授权，系统全托管地跟进其账户相关信息并主动推送，付费转化率非常高，许丹青称其在国内 2C 商业化里算领先。他讲的差异点：通用模型（豆包/GPT）是单次问答式的「泛而全」深度分析，而投资真正要的是边际信息的变化对你这个资产的当前影响，再叠加基于你投资画像的个性化处理与推送——「更懂你」。
  • 合思客户最愿意买单的是能算清 ROI 的产品，典型是智能审核：发票合规性、企业内部制度（消费标准、三单匹配）全都要审。案例：给一家 2000 家门店的上市服装连锁做「改衣费」单场景审核，省了 35 个人；该企业一年改衣费 6000 多万元，至少再省 5% 的费用。马春荃说，过去卖数字化工具，企业总怀疑「你真给我省钱了吗」；AI 进去之后是肉眼可见的——「这 35 个人就没这活了」。

三、为什么不能把报销制度丢给 ChatGPT 自己审

  • 马春荃讲了个段子：让大模型比较清华和蓝翔技校哪个好，它说清华好；操作者说「对不起，我是蓝翔毕业的」，它立刻改口找出一堆蓝翔好的理由。大模型有讨好型人格，会顺着用户的态度走；而企业财务要的是一致性、准确性、没有二义性——财务不能弄错账，有就是有、没有就是没有。更关键的是验证：通用模型不提供准确率与一致性率的持续跟踪，合思的做法像流水线质检，跑完要按抽样率复核验证。他顺带开炮：程序员是「掌握着最高科技、却用最 low 的方式工作的一群人」——作坊式生产，每个人用 Codex 产出的 500 行代码质量都不一样；AI 应该像工厂流水线一样开机自动作业、自动验证，而不是模仿程序员。他甚至跟豆包团队当面说过：给员工充值的模式是过渡产品，token 应该充给 Agent，不应该充给员工——就像锅炉出现之后，煤是分配给锅炉的，不是分配给锅炉工的，锅炉工效率高低不影响锅炉吞吐。

四、谁在赚钱：中美两张图

  • 许丹青点名：Claude Code、Codex、Manus 算真正赚到钱的；他有朋友给海外商户做直播智能体（数字人直播），收入规模到了千万美金级。共识判断：现在能赚钱的中美应用都集中在偏 2B/2P、有明确场景、能算明白价值的效率类；2C 还没有明确能赚钱的，但 2027 年 2C 会迎来真正赚钱的机会。
  • 马春荃谈中美分化：AI 之前中美软件上市公司市值差 15 倍，AI 之后差了 37 倍——中国软件公司在走下坡，美国的翻倍了。他在硅谷听李飞飞的学生讲数字人导购项目（试穿、问库存尺码），中国客户问「这种工具在国内没人买单，用户觉得跟它对话有点傻，你怎么卖出去的」；对方的回答很本质：你在旧金山的体育商店被店员冷落过 10 分钟以上吗？不是 AI 更先进，是美国的服务底座更低——替代一个岗位价值 10 万美元，花 3 万、5 万、8 万都划算。另一类赚钱的是美图这种：原有庞大用户基数，加 AI 多收钱，毛利 70%，海外增长很快；海外优质服务极贵、还要付 18% 小费，机械化一点的 AI 服务用户已经觉得 OK。
  • 国内则是另一面：他上午刚拜访的一家上市公司，不计代价做 Agent，单个客户年费收几百万上千万，很可能还是亏损的——他们不敢把 token 成本算进去，一算进去销售就不敢卖了。

五、北极星指标：从 ARR 到任务完成率与续费

  • 财搭子每天面板上第一个蹦出来的指标是任务完成率：产品是 Personal Agent 架构，后台基于用户投资画像拆出要盯的任务，看每个任务的进展与是否到达可交付状态；第二个是用户反馈清单——App 内任意界面点右下角即可截图反馈，后台有 Agent 管线判断用户对哪里不满，直接进多维表格。
  • 合思全公司对齐两个指标：损益和续费率。新签靠竞争招标、卷价格，不亏就算不错，利润全部来自老客户续费，第二年不续费这个单子就是纯亏。AI 之后续费要求更高，因为 AI 的收入形态变了：从一年发生一次的 license，变成每天发生的用量计费。合思的 AI 审核软件本身不收费，按审核一张单 10 元收，前提是判别准确率大于 90% 才收费；收入增长靠扩场景（差旅单→招待费→应付付款→整个收支管理）和扩品类渗透，就像当年卖机票的把酒店、打车也托管过来。
  • ARR 为什么没人讲了：许丹青说 ARR 本质是「乐观情况下的现金外推」，首购或首订乘以 12，对早期 AI 应用不 make sense，是虚荣指标；他引一组数据称 AI 产品年留存率只有约 21%，远低于优秀 SaaS（NDR 超 100%、续费率 70% 以上）。马春荃补了 ARR 的来历：它是资本市场为 SaaS 收入确定性设计的平衡工具，但销售卖多年期合同会把它扭曲（提成先落袋，明年我走了谁管你）；而 AI 是时刻发生的用量，有高峰有低谷（双十一短信量暴涨），电信运营商早就用「用量值」而不是 ARR 核定——AI 公司也应该回到用量口径，月收入乘 12 顶多叫 forecast ARR，不科学。

六、token 账：单价在降，账单在涨

  • 财搭子的 token 成本约占总成本 30%（含人力），不含人力则占 70%–80%，且占比同比是上升的。许丹青拆解：底层是复杂模型路由，主力模型 DeepSeek 约占一半，性价比高；但模型单价并没有传说中的十倍下降，而账单涨在三处——用户变多、单用户任务变多、单任务为追求完成率消耗变多（现在平均一个任务要调用 40 多个工具）。从单对话框变多 Agent，再加上 Deep Thinking 先自言自语沙盘模拟，都算在应用公司的成本里。对明年的预测：总值肯定涨（股权结构变化后用户规模预计翻十倍以上），但占收入比会降，靠一系列优化（包括按峰谷时段调度调用）。
  • 合思的 token 占比低，因为打法相反：收入是固定的 10 元一单，只能拼命把单均消耗压下去；而且招投标 70% 是商务分、看最低价，不压 token 成本这生意就赚不起来。他的规律：场景越收敛、越固定，越能精简 token；场景越泛化、要自主规划试错，越烧钱。他还提出成本分账：生产环境按成本率和利润率考核（量产逻辑），Harness 实验室里的泛化试错应计入实验/研发成本——许丹青接话说 Anthropic 招股书正是这么拆的：计入收入成本率的只有生产环节的推理 token 消耗（约 15%），另有约 50% 的大头是研发下一代模型的算力，单独列示、不混在一起看。

七、利润什么时候流向应用层

  • 许丹青的剧本：模型公司像 5G 时代的移动、电信、联通，提供基础设施，原来卖流量、今天卖 token；5G 初期流量贵到刷个视频都要很多钱，等基础设施成熟、成本降下来，才长出抖音、拼多多这样的超级应用。今天同理——他举例说 Muse 上跑一个中等强度的 Personal Agent 成本约 30 美元/月。如果 token 降不下来，AI 应用爆不了，就会有 AI 泡沫，产业链没法继续。所以现在是一场赛跑：是开源模型先把推理成本打得足够低，还是闭源模型公司先建起平台与生态——这决定了独立 AI 应用公司能不能赚到钱。
  • 马春荃的「泛化不经济」：全行业对 AI 的预期是造一个完美的超人替代优秀人类；但人类文明几千年得出的办法恰恰是分工——任何一个人只在一个岗位上专注。我们日常要大量容忍人类的无能（90% 的同事达不到预期），却要求 AI 全能，「一个无能的人怎么造出一个全能的人呢」。泛化有三不经济：烧的 token 不经济、时间不经济、信息不经济。他主张把预期拉回合理化：让数字员工模仿一个被分工、被约束、被定义的专业的人，在岗位上拿结果，ROI 才算得出来；要拿结果就要短程高效推理，上下文贪多必慢——空间换时间、时间换空间，计算机的基本规律改不了。他自陈是悲观派：不认为大模型会有意识或超级智能。

八、SaaS 为什么跌：杀死软件的是 Vibe Coding，不是 Agent

  • 背景：美国 SaaS 整体估值从峰值约 20 倍市销率跌到约 3 倍；今年 2 月 Anthropic 的 Cowork 推出 11 个插件、Agent 直接帮人干活不用看 UI，被称作「SaaS 的地狱时刻」。
  • 马春荃的判断：SaaS 跌下来还不是 Agent 的冲击，而是 Vibe Coding 的冲击——他第一次用 Cursor 就开始做噩梦：客户上午提需求、下午自己就实现了一个够用的，为什么要等厂商？旧估值模型（年收入 6–8 倍、峰值 20 倍）赌的是把用户圈进来后 6 到 20 年走不了，现在这个模型塌了。但手搓软件有天花板：业务建模、体系设计、可扩展性取决于提需求那个人的思想框架、审美与经验，企业变革时就会發現软件不支持了。
  • 但他同时说 Agent 并没有摧毁软件，反而助长软件：Agent 不吃 IT 预算、吃人力资源预算；而且 Agent 最有效的交互对象不是人，是软件、MCP 和 API。所以 SaaS 正在两极分化：只有 UI 界面的死；沉淀了足够复杂度的活得更好——几任财务总监、CFO、CEO 把管理认知一层层堆进系统、写在代码里却没写成文档的非明文 know-how，极难被消解，Salesforce 这类按数据调用量收费的反而在涨。结论是：软件会走下历史舞台、变成底层设施——还存在，但不再是当红炸子鸡，也萃取不到新的 know-how，新的 know-how 会沉淀到 Agent 入口去；所以 SAP 们必须自己做 Agent，不做就完蛋。

九、被模型吞噬的路径图：什么不碰、什么死守

  • 财搭子经历的三波迭代：2025 年初 DeepSeek 推理能力（大好事，研报产品做起来、公司活下来）→ 模型公司卷 Deep Research（研报从主力收费功能变成边缘功能）→ Agent 能力这一波（以 OpenClaw「小龙虾」牵头的 Agent 浪潮，他们快速上线托管功能，付费转化再上一个台阶）。合思的最大分水岭是 2025 年春节 DeepSeek 发布：之前想用好模型只能用海外的，但数据不合规；国产模型又不可用，所以 2025 年之前他们从没正式发布过 AI 产品——DeepSeek 让 Agent「既合规又合格」，2025 年 4 月才发布 AI 产品。
  • 许丹青的边界原则：越靠近用户的重点做，越靠近模型内核的不碰。死守的是用户连接（账户授权、onboarding 十分钟内的体验、每次连接的差异）；Harness 与策略内核即使今天模型做得还不够好也不自己做，相信大模型会越做越好——策略的「个性化」做（每个人的策略不同），但策略内核（回测、因子拼接这类普适能力）不做，模型迭代后会比应用公司做得更好。贵的用户调最强的模型，一般的先用便宜模型跑一层，至少要知道当前最好模型的上限在哪。
  • 马春荃的壁垒论：直接去做服务、离客户更近，把活接过来对结果负责。比如智能审核的终局是把客户的共享财务中心承包下来：原来 1000 万的中心，800 万接走，客户算的是自己的人力账，不跟你算软件值多少钱，价格反而好谈，还顺带解决用工风险。为什么要承包而不是卖软件去改造客户？因为技术改造最大的难点是说服共享中心负责人——你的成功意味着他管的人从 100 个变 10 个，他会恐慌、会反抗；承包是「我改变不了你，我改变我自己」。第三条是要掌握客户不知道的 know-how：在客户的主营业务上没戏（那是人家的主业），但在它的成本项上，应用公司有足够空间沉淀专业化知识。
  • 模型会不会最终吃掉专业能力？两人的共识：财税这类公有、普世的专家数据会被模型训进去（厂商在花大价钱买专家标注）；但落到千行百业每家公司、每个人的特异性 know-how，对大模型来说是「脏数据」、是多样性数据，预训练的概率算法处理不了 100 个方向各 1% 的判断。许丹青举投资为例：你永远不会知道巴菲特和伯克希尔内部最后是怎么决定买苹果的——投资决策本质上是极致个性化的上下文加专业 know-how，有买有卖才成市场，没有共识答案，这正是应用的魅力所在。

十、与模型的分工：Memory、Harness、Benchmark

  • 财搭子的内部三关键词：Memory、Harness、Benchmark（Evaluation），其中 Memory 越来越重——这也是所有 Personal Agent 公司最看重的事。最高效的上下文采集方式是让用户做一次投资账户授权，比问卷问「你的投资目标是什么」准得多；使用过程中持续构建核心记忆与分层记忆。Harness 他们用的是 Codex 开源的那套（评估过 DeepSeek 的 Harness 后仍选 Codex），底层做模型路由与工具调度（自研工具加几百个现成 MCP）。Benchmark 是模型驱动的：从几十万条用户 query 里抽出 1000 条最高质量的轨迹做了标注——许丹青透露，曾有大模型公司想出约 100 万元买这 1000 条核心用户轨迹数据，他们没卖。任务管理上还要判断「这个任务对用户是否关键、该不该通知、以什么形式通知」，通知后的反馈再回流丰富 Memory。
  • 合思把模型层拆成两层：元思维模型（可复用的解题思路，如审核里大量用到的「证据链推导」式挑错；他认为人类思维模式归根到底就归纳、演绎、类比三种，AI 的推理动作同样可以抽象）与场景模型（某个场景下的数据、标签、Skill 封装）。拆开是为了生态合作：进入千行百业靠单个 Agent 干不完，必须让合作方保留其自主可控的知识、与合思形成「各有一半基因的孩子」式的共同 IP，元思维模型像模板一样复用出去，场景知识归对方构建。
  • 主持人补的参照：Cursor 之所以没被大模型吃掉，关键是它记录用户是否采纳代码补全、用交互数据训了自己的小模型——交互数据是静态公开数据之外的真金白银（她称 Cursor 以 600 亿美元卖给了 SpaceX）。问到应用公司要不要自己训模型：许丹青说后训练本质是商业算账——现在用户规模不够大，训模型成本反而更高；高质量 RL 后训练小几百万元人民币可以训出不错的版本，等规模到了、又需要压 token 成本时，做一版微调/RL 自有模型性价比更高。马春荃说合思去年坚决不做（大模型非粗暴发展期，绑定性投入会被淹没、不经济），今年反而开启了小尺寸私有化、硬件化产品：客户的全息运营数据不想扔出去、也买不起 B300，就用小模型选高延时可接受的场景（等 10–20 分钟没关系）跑通，既保数据安全又经济可行；而且通用大模型上 96 分和 90 分差别不显著，私有小模型能把分差拉开、让对手的 IT 团队短期内追不上，这就是门槛。

十一、暴论专场：know-how 的两种、与「重新投胎」

  • 马春荃的第二个暴论：软件厂商根本不掌握客户的 know-how。Know-how 分两种——how to do it（怎么做）和 how to think about it（怎么想）；多数软件厂商只承接了前者：你告诉我想怎么做，我给你记下来、做个按钮，把流程跑完；为什么要这么设计这个流程，往往不在软件厂商的视野内。主持人总结的防吞噬壁垒清单：用户个性化数据（静态数据加交互数据）、还没沉淀成明文的隐性知识与思考方式、线下交付、牌照，以及社交关系。
  • 社交算不算壁垒？许丹青认为算：所有人都认为 2C 的 Personal Agent 最可能在微信做成，核心是个人上下文大量沉淀在微信里、社交关系迁移成本极高，且社交关系本身能帮助构建上下文。
  • 马春荃的回应跳出了防守框架：所有行业都应该重新投胎，而不只是建壁垒。投胎和建壁垒的动作差异在于先杀掉自己——「你不是防着被它杀掉，你说算了，我被你吃掉好了，吃掉之后可能长出另外一朵花」。自杀是一瞬间的事，难的是跳下去之后心态完全不同。他说小公司反而有机会：老板说了算，阻力小；大公司难在地盘、内部政治与 quota。他举微软云转型为例：转型头一两年不给销售下 quota，先选 10–20 家全球头部企业打标杆、再扩到 100 家，然后才全面转型、砍掉一半销售——因为一旦设了销售目标，销售的惯性就会去卖好出业绩的旧产品。

十二、收费模式革命：从席位费到按结果付费

  • 合思的 AI 审核按单收费、一单 10 元（对私报销、差旅、招待、办公打车等单据），对标的是人力：普通公司一个财务一年约审 6000 张单，集约化的共享财务中心 8000–12000 张，人员成本全摊进去算，账很直观。他原来试过按软件包卖（20 万、30 万一年三档），demo、共创体验都很好，客户说很棒——就是不买单，半年只卖了三单，因为客户面对一个新东西算不清价值账。他在自己书里写过「马力」的类比：瓦特改良了蒸汽机，最难的却是卖出去，于是发明「马力」这个极不科学的单位——农场主养着马，听得懂三马力等于省多少钱。按单收费就是 AI 的马力。
  • FDE 要涨价：马春荃说今年才开始正式建 FDE 团队，此前自己麻痹大意、没给它定价，结果客户认知不足，临到交付才发现要重新梳理规则、还怪厂商。FDE 的活是组织变革、流程重塑、规则重建——企业内部沉淀的规则大量互相矛盾，人会遗忘、会选择性执法所以能兼容，一股脑丢给 AI 它就疯了；这件事必须卖贵，让老板重视。他区分 FDE 与客户成功：FDE 面向 know-how 萃取与整个产品解决方案的收敛交付，客户成功是「我已经有这个产品，如何让你用上」。他还提到一个认知鸿沟：销售总问「我们的标准产品边界在哪」，他很想回答「没有边界，你大脑的边界在哪」——软件时代的工程师和顾问被 PPT、宣传单页框住了，AI 时代一个对话窗口背后的思维模型与可泛化场景，是要用脑子想的。
  • 财搭子的收费是订阅加用量搭配：先订阅（68 元、128 元这类档位，给 C 端用户一个直观预期），超出订阅包再按「发币」的用量模式为复杂任务付费。
  • 结果怎么核定：财搭子主要靠 AI 审 AI——用户反馈（尤其是任务执行得特别好或特别差时的点赞与负反馈）进后台后，用更贵的模型在新 Harness 上重跑比对；量化标准包括数据时效、信息比率、边际增益、风险收益比；多个模型交叉判断（字幕转写提及 Astra、Claude、Gemini 等），全都判不了才上人工。合思是自动化加约 2% 的人工抽样平行审核，一致性率验证到 95% 以上，即可推定整体准确率在 90% 以上。合思传统业务仍按席位费，AI 板块按结果收费；马春荃预计真正的交叉点不在财务业务内部，而在 Harness 的元思维模型与更多行业结合之后——「对标软件和工具的生意一般，对标人的整个人力资源成本，才是好生意」。
  • 财搭子的退款机制值得单记：许丹青说它有三层作用——一是降低启用门槛，用户面对一个不知道会交付什么的 Agent，先用起来再说；二是激励高质量反馈，尤其负反馈（原来只推反馈入口用户懒得理，引入退款后，用户的不满意程度被量化，设计上或退积分、或免费重试），而 AI 迭代最缺的就是高质量反馈；三是团队对齐机制，让增长、前端、算法所有人都对「交付好结果」这一件事负责。机制上线后核心指标（任务完成率）没变，但团队效率与 Harness 的结果成功率明显提升。

十三、结果付费是终局吗

  • 许丹青：订阅加用量是过渡模式，未来大头应该是结果付费；投资领域早有先例——买基金付管理费、私募再拿超额分成，产品交付的就是结果。财搭子的终局因此是与持牌机构深度绑定：有了牌照才能给具体建议、参与交易环节、拿收入分成。
  • 马春荃：按结果付费仍停留在「类比阶段」。蒸汽机革命真正引来的不是替代磨坊主，而是火车——一种历史上不存在的可代表物，是能力跨越；电气革命开头是灯泡替代蜡烛，真正的爆发是冰箱、烤箱这些新增维度。AI 还没走到那一步：结果付费只是我们的想象力和创新还没到那个位置时的过渡，等「电视机时刻」出现，收费逻辑会被重新发明。

十四、入口之争：信任入口、消费品牌与工作入口

  • 许丹青：入口比过去更重要，但性质变了——过去争的是注意力/信息入口（查东西想到百度），今天 Agent 要往前一步替你把事做完，入口还承担了信任：你能不能相信它替你做的选择。同时他引 Manus 肖弘的看法：未来的 AI 应用更像一个个消费品牌，靠品位与设计吸引一类人（有人喜欢 Nike、有人喜欢阿迪），未必存在大而统一的入口。财搭子因此坚持做独立入口：投资场景足够特殊，需要独立的信任品牌承接；且稳定交付依赖连续上下文，持牌与账户授权壁垒能持续拿到用户账户授权，通用入口里嵌一个东西做不到端到端体验。他透露曾与 Kimi 谈过合作（他本人是 Kimi 的辅导员，与团队很熟）：Kimi 希望把财搭子像同花顺那样做成全透明的 Skill/MCP 接入，他拒绝了——财搭子的 Memory 记录的是用户「如何 think about 一个决策」的过程，全白盒等于交出壁垒与定价权；而且通用入口做账户导流容易，做端到端体验很难，最后放弃了这条路。
  • 马春荃：合思只做「工作入口」，不做流量入口，自称能力不允许、也没钱（互联网时代他就选择嵌入飞书、钉钉等既有系统）。他对流量入口的看法更哲学：流量入口本质是泛场景匹配，应该站在用户一侧过滤过载信息——比如现在很火的防诈骗 Agent，冒充你的身份陪骗子聊半小时，替主人过滤无效信息。但悖论在于：你每月只付它 200 块，它没有动力做得更好（「200 块钱你要什么自行车」）；一旦它有了超额利润，必然靠倒流、改变你的动线赚钱——入口会不会左右我们的思路与采购行为，大概率会，只是各家克制程度不同（微信、Google 相对克制）。他还补了平台经济学的一课：双边网络效应要求两边都不能是单一节点——买家只有一个、或卖家只有一个（比如只能买英伟达）时，平台就失效；2B 面对几个大厂、几个大客户，本就不是双边网络，平台没价值。
  • 未来形态（许丹青）：入口会分两层——第一层是新硬件或手机系统级入口（Apple Intelligence、华为小艺之类）承接分发，前台收敛；其后仍是多个 App、多个 Agent 为你服务的生态。若一个入口加一个 App 能做完所有事，「今天可能人类就会灭了」。

十五、融资：退钱、牌照与补贴之死

  • 许丹青解释了主持人提到的「退回融资」：2024 年 6 月创业时一级市场几乎没人看 AI 应用，首轮快速锁定百川智能（大模型公司要布局场景）等投资人，后续也有机构扶持场景企业。增长上他们一直谨慎，卡点在持牌与监管边界：2026 年 6 月后监管对 AI 投资明显收紧，「930 政策」后非持牌 KOL 不得再宣传、带货 AI 投资产品。产品侧几十万用户全靠口碑裂变、零投放，注册到付费的转化自称国内 AI 应用里最好，但用户们从模拟账户时期就催着「什么时候能接管我的账户」——没有牌照全都做不了。今年 7 月他想通：解决牌照既是壁垒、也是发展到这个阶段必须面对的问题。随后两个月与九方（港股上市的持牌机构）谈成战略控股合作——监管要求持牌资质的控股方必须是持牌机构——与老股东协商后老股东按比例退出，9 月 15 日完成股权变更，一个月内补齐持牌能力。他的结论：AI 应用创业融资不一定是为钱，可以为的是牌照这类外部必要资源。
  • 马春荃：如果是今天从 0 到 1 他会融资，但以合思现在的阶段不会。融资只有两个目的——续命，或者加杠杆；他既不需要续命，钱来了也暂时没有杠杆可加。融资节奏像种萝卜：天使期是挑种子（苗好不好看、能不能长出来不知道），第二轮进大棚、到田间（施肥灌溉，还是续命逻辑），等成果出现才谈得上加杠杆规模化。
  • 互联网式「讲大故事、烧钱买用户、数据飞轮」在 AI 时代还成立吗？两人都摇头。许丹青：纯市场竞争式补贴不成立——每个用户的边际成本不为零，补贴来的多是白嫖党，什么都没沉淀；只有场景已锁定、为产品效果而补贴（本质是花钱买数据）才可行，先想清楚要什么数据，再补贴、迭代、规模化、提价。马春荃：互联网卖的是确定性的信息与服务（打车、外卖、商品，总有一款能下单），来了就能转化、留存天然高；AI 卖的是「一个聪明的脑子」，用户发现脑子不聪明立刻流失——补贴换不来交易链条的沉淀。

十六、结尾判语

  • 许丹青：「当然是好生意。而且我觉得 27 年 AI 应用应该会有不一样的拐点」——市场会爆发，Personal Agent 已经有一些这样的趋势了。
  • 马春荃：「我不知道什么是爆发，但它肯定是个好生意。」它会显著拉开差距、增强差异化，而不是走向完全同质化；他更期待那个想象不到的「电视机时刻」——先上牌桌、先发明灯泡，剩下的东西，都可以由我们重新发明。

一句話

兩位一線創始人對帳 AI 應用的生意經：現在中美能賺到錢的 AI 應用，幾乎全是 2B/2P 裡「價值算得明白」的效率生意，2C 還沒跑出明確賺錢的，但兩人都判 2027 年是拐點。這一期最硬的共識有三條：token 帳單不會隨模型降價而下降（任務變複雜了）、ARR 是正在失效的虛榮指標、應用公司真正的壁壘是使用者個人化資料加未成文的 know-how——以及馬春荃那句暴論：與其建壁壘防著被大模型殺死，不如先殺掉自己、重新投胎。

一、兩家先自報家門

  • 財搭子（許丹青）：2024 年 6 月創立，為普通散戶做 AI 投資助手。團隊 12 人，自稱非常 AI 原生；收入規模幾百萬元人民幣，在盈虧平衡邊緣——不算人力成本是盈利的，加上人力還不夠。許丹青本人電腦出身，碩士期間用強化學習預測金融市場獲獎，後在通聯數據、羅伯投研做投研產品（主持人介紹其產品巔峰日活達百萬級）。
  • 合思（馬春荃）：2014 年成立（前身易快報），從費控報銷起家，延展到企業因公消費與財務收支管理，現做智慧財務與智慧商旅兩條線。團隊 400 人，營收三個多億，今年終於走出歷史虧損週期。馬春荃在用友十多年；主持人點出合思是老牌軟體廠商裡「革自己命最徹底的」——他曾拉一個 Harness 小組封閉開發，目標就是用 AI 幹掉合思自己。

二、使用者到底願意為什麼付錢

  • 財搭子最受歡迎的功能換過一茬：2025 年下半年到 2026 年初是研報（基於行業框架生成個股/行業研報，並做動態更新，追蹤邊際變化）；模型公司把 Deep Research 做成免費通用能力後，這個功能的轉化明顯下降。今年 3 月推出的託管功能成為新主力：使用者上傳資產截圖或做帳戶授權，系統全託管地跟進其帳戶相關資訊並主動推送，付費轉化率非常高，許丹青稱其在國內 2C 商業化裡算領先。他講的差異點：通用模型（豆包/GPT）是單次問答式的「泛而全」深度分析，而投資真正要的是邊際資訊的變化對你這個資產的當前影響，再疊加基於你投資畫像的個人化處理與推送——「更懂你」。
  • 合思客戶最願意買單的是能算清 ROI 的產品，典型是智慧審核：發票合規性、企業內部制度（消費標準、三單匹配）全都要審。案例：給一家 2000 家門店的上市服裝連鎖做「改衣費」單場景審核，省了 35 個人；該企業一年改衣費 6000 多萬元，至少再省 5% 的費用。馬春荃說，過去賣數位化工具，企業總懷疑「你真給我省錢了嗎」；AI 進去之後是肉眼可見的——「這 35 個人就沒這活了」。

三、為什麼不能把報銷制度丟給 ChatGPT 自己審

  • 馬春荃講了個段子：讓大模型比較清華和藍翔技校哪個好，它說清華好；操作者說「對不起，我是藍翔畢業的」，它立刻改口找出一堆藍翔好的理由。大模型有討好型人格，會順著使用者的態度走；而企業財務要的是一致性、準確性、沒有二義性——財務不能弄錯帳，有就是有、沒有就是沒有。更關鍵的是驗證：通用模型不提供準確率與一致性率的持續追蹤，合思的做法像流水線質檢，跑完要按抽樣率複核驗證。他順帶開炮：程式設計師是「掌握著最高科技、卻用最 low 的方式工作的一群人」——作坊式生產，每個人用 Codex 產出的 500 行程式碼品質都不一樣；AI 應該像工廠流水線一樣開機自動作業、自動驗證，而不是模仿程式設計師。他甚至跟豆包團隊當面說過：給員工儲值的模式是過渡產品，token 應該充給 Agent，不應該充給員工——就像鍋爐出現之後，煤是分配給鍋爐的，不是分配給鍋爐工的，鍋爐工效率高低不影響鍋爐吞吐。

四、誰在賺錢：中美兩張圖

  • 許丹青點名：Claude Code、Codex、Manus 算真正賺到錢的；他有朋友給海外商戶做直播智慧體（數位人直播），收入規模到了千萬美金級。共識判斷：現在能賺錢的中美應用都集中在偏 2B/2P、有明確場景、能算明白價值的效率類；2C 還沒有明確能賺錢的，但 2027 年 2C 會迎來真正賺錢的機會。
  • 馬春荃談中美分化：AI 之前中美軟體上市公司市值差 15 倍，AI 之後差了 37 倍——中國軟體公司在走下坡，美國的翻倍了。他在矽谷聽李飛飛的學生講數位人導購專案（試穿、問庫存尺碼），中國客戶問「這種工具在國內沒人買單，使用者覺得跟它對話有點傻，你怎麼賣出去的」；對方的回答很本質：你在舊金山的體育商店被店員冷落過 10 分鐘以上嗎？不是 AI 更先進，是美國的服務底座更低——替代一個崗位價值 10 萬美元，花 3 萬、5 萬、8 萬都划算。另一類賺錢的是美圖這種：原有龐大使用者基數，加 AI 多收錢，毛利 70%，海外增長很快；海外優質服務極貴、還要付 18% 小費，機械化一點的 AI 服務使用者已經覺得 OK。
  • 國內則是另一面：他上午剛拜訪的一家上市公司，不計代價做 Agent，單個客戶年費收幾百萬上千萬，很可能還是虧損的——他們不敢把 token 成本算進去，一算進去銷售就不敢賣了。

五、北極星指標：從 ARR 到任務完成率與續費

  • 財搭子每天面板上第一個蹦出來的指標是任務完成率：產品是 Personal Agent 架構，後台基於使用者投資畫像拆出要盯的任務，看每個任務的進展與是否到達可交付狀態；第二個是使用者回饋清單——App 內任意介面點右下角即可截圖回饋，後台有 Agent 管線判斷使用者對哪裡不滿，直接進多維表格。
  • 合思全公司對齊兩個指標：損益和續費率。新簽靠競爭招標、卷價格，不虧就算不錯，利潤全部來自老客戶續費，第二年不續費這個單子就是純虧。AI 之後續費要求更高，因為 AI 的收入形態變了：從一年發生一次的 license，變成每天發生的用量計費。合思的 AI 審核軟體本身不收費，按審核一張單 10 元收，前提是判別準確率大於 90% 才收費；收入增長靠擴場景（差旅單→招待費→應付付款→整個收支管理）和擴品類滲透，就像當年賣機票的把酒店、打車也託管過來。
  • ARR 為什麼沒人講了：許丹青說 ARR 本質是「樂觀情況下的現金外推」，首購或首訂乘以 12，對早期 AI 應用不 make sense，是虛榮指標；他引一組數據稱 AI 產品年留存率只有約 21%，遠低於優秀 SaaS（NDR 超 100%、續費率 70% 以上）。馬春荃補了 ARR 的來歷：它是資本市場為 SaaS 收入確定性設計的平衡工具，但銷售賣多年期合約會把它扭曲（提成先落袋，明年我走了誰管你）；而 AI 是時刻發生的用量，有高峰有低谷（雙十一簡訊量暴漲），電信營運商早就用「用量值」而不是 ARR 核定——AI 公司也應該回到用量口徑，月收入乘 12 頂多叫 forecast ARR，不科學。

六、token 帳：單價在降，帳單在漲

  • 財搭子的 token 成本約占總成本 30%（含人力），不含人力則占 70%–80%，且占比同比是上升的。許丹青拆解：底層是複雜模型路由，主力模型 DeepSeek 約占一半，性價比高；但模型單價並沒有傳說中的十倍下降，而帳單漲在三處——使用者變多、單使用者任務變多、單任務為追求完成率消耗變多（現在平均一個任務要呼叫 40 多個工具）。從單對話框變多 Agent，再加上 Deep Thinking 先自言自語沙盤模擬，都算在應用公司的成本裡。對明年的預測：總值肯定漲（股權結構變化後使用者規模預計翻十倍以上），但占收入比會降，靠一系列優化（包括按峰谷時段調度呼叫）。
  • 合思的 token 占比低，因為打法相反：收入是固定的 10 元一單，只能拚命把單均消耗壓下去；而且招投標 70% 是商務分、看最低價，不壓 token 成本這生意就賺不起來。他的規律：場景越收斂、越固定，越能精簡 token；場景越泛化、要自主規劃試錯，越燒錢。他還提出成本分帳：生產環境按成本率和利潤率考核（量產邏輯），Harness 實驗室裡的泛化試錯應計入實驗/研發成本——許丹青接話說 Anthropic 招股書正是這麼拆的：計入收入成本率的只有生產環節的推理 token 消耗（約 15%），另有約 50% 的大頭是研發下一代模型的算力，單獨列示、不混在一起看。

七、利潤什麼時候流向應用層

  • 許丹青的劇本：模型公司像 5G 時代的移動、電信、聯通，提供基礎設施，原來賣流量、今天賣 token；5G 初期流量貴到刷個影片都要很多錢，等基礎設施成熟、成本降下來，才長出抖音、拼多多這樣的超級應用。今天同理——他舉例說 Muse 上跑一個中等強度的 Personal Agent 成本約 30 美元/月。如果 token 降不下來，AI 應用爆不了，就會有 AI 泡沫，產業鏈沒法繼續。所以現在是一場賽跑：是開源模型先把推理成本打得足夠低，還是閉源模型公司先建起平台與生態——這決定了獨立 AI 應用公司能不能賺到錢。
  • 馬春荃的「泛化不經濟」：全行業對 AI 的預期是造一個完美的超人替代優秀人類；但人類文明幾千年得出的辦法恰恰是分工——任何一個人只在一個崗位上專注。我們日常要大量容忍人類的無能（90% 的同事達不到預期），卻要求 AI 全能，「一個無能的人怎麼造出一個全能的人呢」。泛化有三不經濟：燒的 token 不經濟、時間不經濟、資訊不經濟。他主張把預期拉回合理化：讓數位員工模仿一個被分工、被約束、被定義的專業的人，在崗位上拿結果，ROI 才算得出來；要拿結果就要短程高效推理，上下文貪多必慢——空間換時間、時間換空間，電腦的基本規律改不了。他自陳是悲觀派：不認為大模型會有意識或超級智慧。

八、SaaS 為什麼跌：殺死軟體的是 Vibe Coding，不是 Agent

  • 背景：美國 SaaS 整體估值從峰值約 20 倍市銷率跌到約 3 倍；今年 2 月 Anthropic 的 Cowork 推出 11 個外掛、Agent 直接幫人幹活不用看 UI，被稱作「SaaS 的地獄時刻」。
  • 馬春荃的判斷：SaaS 跌下來還不是 Agent 的衝擊，而是 Vibe Coding 的衝擊——他第一次用 Cursor 就開始做噩夢：客戶上午提需求、下午自己就實現了一個夠用的，為什麼要等廠商？舊估值模型（年收入 6–8 倍、峰值 20 倍）賭的是把使用者圈進來後 6 到 20 年走不了，現在這個模型塌了。但手搓軟體有天花板：業務建模、體系設計、可擴展性取決於提需求那個人的思想框架、審美與經驗，企業變革時就會發現軟體不支援了。
  • 但他同時說 Agent 並沒有摧毀軟體，反而助長軟體：Agent 不吃 IT 預算、吃人力資源預算；而且 Agent 最有效的互動對象不是人，是軟體、MCP 和 API。所以 SaaS 正在兩極分化：只有 UI 介面的死；沉澱了足夠複雜度的活得更好——幾任財務總監、CFO、CEO 把管理認知一層層堆進系統、寫在程式碼裡卻沒寫成文件的非明文 know-how，極難被消解，Salesforce 這類按資料呼叫量收費的反而在漲。結論是：軟體會走下歷史舞台、變成底層設施——還存在，但不再是當紅炸子雞，也萃取不到新的 know-how，新的 know-how 會沉澱到 Agent 入口去；所以 SAP 們必須自己做 Agent，不做就完蛋。

九、被模型吞噬的路徑圖：什麼不碰、什麼死守

  • 財搭子經歷的三波迭代：2025 年初 DeepSeek 推理能力（大好事，研報產品做起來、公司活下來）→ 模型公司卷 Deep Research（研報從主力收費功能變成邊緣功能）→ Agent 能力這一波（以 OpenClaw「小龍蝦」牽頭的 Agent 浪潮，他們快速上線託管功能，付費轉化再上一個台階）。合思的最大分水嶺是 2025 年春節 DeepSeek 發布：之前想用好模型只能用海外的，但資料不合規；國產模型又不可用，所以 2025 年之前他們從沒正式發布過 AI 產品——DeepSeek 讓 Agent「既合規又合格」，2025 年 4 月才發布 AI 產品。
  • 許丹青的邊界原則：越靠近使用者的重點做，越靠近模型內核的不碰。死守的是使用者連接（帳戶授權、onboarding 十分鐘內的體驗、每次連接的差異）；Harness 與策略內核即使今天模型做得還不夠好也不自己做，相信大模型會越做越好——策略的「個人化」做（每個人的策略不同），但策略內核（回測、因子拼接這類普適能力）不做，模型迭代後會比應用公司做得更好。貴的使用者調最強的模型，一般的先用便宜模型跑一層，至少要知道當前最好模型的上限在哪。
  • 馬春荃的壁壘論：直接去做服務、離客戶更近，把活接過來對結果負責。比如智慧審核的終局是把客戶的共享財務中心承包下來：原來 1000 萬的中心，800 萬接走，客戶算的是自己的人力帳，不跟你算軟體值多少錢，價格反而好談，還順帶解決用工風險。為什麼要承包而不是賣軟體去改造客戶？因為技術改造最大的難點是說服共享中心負責人——你的成功意味著他管的人從 100 個變 10 個，他會恐慌、會反抗；承包是「我改變不了你，我改變我自己」。第三條是要掌握客戶不知道的 know-how：在客戶的主營業務上沒戲（那是人家的主要業務），但在它的成本項上，應用公司有足夠空間沉澱專業化知識。
  • 模型會不會最終吃掉專業能力？兩人的共識：財稅這類公有、普世的專家資料會被模型訓進去（廠商在花大價錢買專家標註）；但落到千行百業每家公司、每個人的特異性 know-how，對大模型來說是「髒資料」、是多樣性資料，預訓練的機率演算法處理不了 100 個方向各 1% 的判斷。許丹青舉投資為例：你永遠不會知道巴菲特和伯克希爾內部最後是怎麼決定買蘋果的——投資決策本質上是極致個人化的上下文加專業 know-how，有買有賣才成市場，沒有共識答案，這正是應用的魅力所在。

十、與模型的分工：Memory、Harness、Benchmark

  • 財搭子的內部三關鍵詞：Memory、Harness、Benchmark（Evaluation），其中 Memory 越來越重——這也是所有 Personal Agent 公司最看重的事。最高效的上下文採集方式是讓使用者做一次投資帳戶授權，比問卷問「你的投資目標是什麼」準得多；使用過程中持續建構核心記憶與分層記憶。Harness 他們用的是 Codex 開源的那套（評估過 DeepSeek 的 Harness 後仍選 Codex），底層做模型路由與工具調度（自研工具加幾百個現成 MCP）。Benchmark 是模型驅動的：從幾十萬條使用者 query 裡抽出 1000 條最高品質的軌跡做了標註——許丹青透露，曾有大模型公司想出約 100 萬元買這 1000 條核心使用者軌跡資料，他們沒賣。任務管理上還要判斷「這個任務對使用者是否關鍵、該不該通知、以什麼形式通知」，通知後的回饋再回流豐富 Memory。
  • 合思把模型層拆成兩層：元思維模型（可複用的解題思路，如審核裡大量用到的「證據鏈推導」式挑錯；他認為人類思維模式歸根到底就歸納、演繹、類比三種，AI 的推理動作同樣可以抽象）與場景模型（某個場景下的資料、標籤、Skill 封裝）。拆開是為了生態合作：進入千行百業靠單個 Agent 幹不完，必須讓合作方保留其自主可控的知識、與合思形成「各有一半基因的孩子」式的共同 IP，元思維模型像模板一樣複用出去，場景知識歸對方建構。
  • 主持人補的參照：Cursor 之所以沒被大模型吃掉，關鍵是它記錄使用者是否採納程式碼補全、用互動資料訓了自己的小模型——互動資料是靜態公開資料之外的真金白銀（她稱 Cursor 以 600 億美元賣給了 SpaceX）。問到應用公司要不要自己訓模型：許丹青說後訓練本質是商業算帳——現在使用者規模不夠大，訓模型成本反而更高；高品質 RL 後訓練小幾百萬元人民幣可以訓出不錯的版本，等規模到了、又需要壓 token 成本時，做一版微調/RL 自有模型性價比更高。馬春荃說合思去年堅決不做（大模型非粗暴發展期，綁定性投入會被淹沒、不經濟），今年反而開啟了小尺寸私有化、硬體化產品：客戶的全息營運資料不想扔出去、也買不起 B300，就用小模型選高延時可接受的場景（等 10–20 分鐘沒關係）跑通，既保資料安全又經濟可行；而且通用大模型上 96 分和 90 分差別不顯著，私有小模型能把分差拉開、讓對手的 IT 團隊短期內追不上，這就是門檻。

十一、暴論專場：know-how 的兩種、與「重新投胎」

  • 馬春荃的第二個暴論：軟體廠商根本不掌握客戶的 know-how。Know-how 分兩種——how to do it（怎麼做）和 how to think about it（怎麼想）；多數軟體廠商只承接了前者：你告訴我想怎麼做，我給你記下來、做個按鈕，把流程跑完；為什麼要這麼設計這個流程，往往不在軟體廠商的視野內。主持人總結的防吞噬壁壘清單：使用者個人化資料（靜態資料加互動資料）、還沒沉澱成明文的隱性知識與思考方式、線下交付、牌照，以及社交關係。
  • 社交算不算壁壘？許丹青認為算：所有人都認為 2C 的 Personal Agent 最可能在微信做成，核心是個人上下文大量沉澱在微信裡、社交關係遷移成本極高，且社交關係本身能幫助建構上下文。
  • 馬春荃的回應跳出了防守框架：所有行業都應該重新投胎，而不只是建壁壘。投胎和建壁壘的動作差異在於先殺掉自己——「你不是防著被它殺掉，你說算了，我被你吃掉好了，吃掉之後可能長出另外一朵花」。自殺是一瞬間的事，難的是跳下去之後心態完全不同。他說小公司反而有機會：老闆說了算，阻力小；大公司難在地盤、內部政治與 quota。他舉微軟雲轉型為例：轉型頭一兩年不給銷售下 quota，先選 10–20 家全球頭部企業打標竿、再擴到 100 家，然後才全面轉型、砍掉一半銷售——因為一旦設了銷售目標，銷售的慣性就會去賣好出業績的舊產品。

十二、收費模式革命：從席位費到按結果付費

  • 合思的 AI 審核按單收費、一單 10 元（對私報銷、差旅、招待、辦公打車等單據），對標的是人力：普通公司一個財務一年約審 6000 張單，集約化的共享財務中心 8000–12000 張，人員成本全攤進去算，帳很直觀。他原來試過按軟體包賣（20 萬、30 萬一年三檔），demo、共創體驗都很好，客戶說很棒——就是不買單，半年只賣了三單，因為客戶面對一個新東西算不清價值帳。他在自己書裡寫過「馬力」的類比：瓦特改良了蒸汽機，最難的卻是賣出去，於是發明「馬力」這個極不科學的單位——農場主養著馬，聽得懂三馬力等於省多少錢。按單收費就是 AI 的馬力。
  • FDE 要漲價：馬春荃說今年才開始正式建 FDE 團隊，此前自己麻痺大意、沒給它定價，結果客戶認知不足，臨到交付才發現要重新梳理規則、還怪廠商。FDE 的活是組織變革、流程重塑、規則重建——企業內部沉澱的規則大量互相矛盾，人會遺忘、會選擇性執法所以能相容，一股腦丟給 AI 它就瘋了；這件事必須賣貴，讓老闆重視。他區分 FDE 與客戶成功：FDE 面向 know-how 萃取與整個產品解決方案的收斂交付，客戶成功是「我已經有這個產品，如何讓你用上」。他還提到一個認知鴻溝：銷售總問「我們的標準產品邊界在哪」，他很想回答「沒有邊界，你大腦的邊界在哪」——軟體時代的工程師和顧問被 PPT、宣傳單頁框住了，AI 時代一個對話窗口背後的思維模型與可泛化場景，是要用腦子想的。
  • 財搭子的收費是訂閱加用量搭配：先訂閱（68 元、128 元這類檔位，給 C 端使用者一個直觀預期），超出訂閱包再按「發幣」的用量模式為複雜任務付費。
  • 結果怎麼核定：財搭子主要靠 AI 審 AI——使用者回饋（尤其是任務執行得特別好或特別差時的點讚與負回饋）進後台後，用更貴的模型在新 Harness 上重跑比對；量化標準包括資料時效、資訊比率、邊際增益、風險收益比；多個模型交叉判斷（字幕轉寫提及 Astra、Claude、Gemini 等），全都判不了才上人工。合思是自動化加約 2% 的人工抽樣平行審核，一致性率驗證到 95% 以上，即可推定整體準確率在 90% 以上。合思傳統業務仍按席位費，AI 板塊按結果收費；馬春荃預計真正的交叉點不在財務業務內部，而在 Harness 的元思維模型與更多行業結合之後——「對標軟體和工具的生意一般，對標人的整個人力資源成本，才是好生意」。
  • 財搭子的退款機制值得單記：許丹青說它有三層作用——一是降低啟用門檻，使用者面對一個不知道會交付什麼的 Agent，先用起來再說；二是激勵高品質回饋，尤其負回饋（原來只推回饋入口使用者懶得理，引入退款後，使用者的不滿意程度被量化，設計上或退積分、或免費重試），而 AI 迭代最缺的就是高品質回饋；三是團隊對齊機制，讓增長、前端、演算法所有人都對「交付好結果」這一件事負責。機制上線後核心指標（任務完成率）沒變，但團隊效率與 Harness 的結果成功率明顯提升。

十三、結果付費是終局嗎

  • 許丹青：訂閱加用量是過渡模式，未來大頭應該是結果付費；投資領域早有先例——買基金付管理費、私募再拿超額分成，產品交付的就是結果。財搭子的終局因此是與持牌機構深度綁定：有了牌照才能給具體建議、參與交易環節、拿收入分成。
  • 馬春荃：按結果付費仍停留在「類比階段」。蒸汽機革命真正引來的不是替代磨坊主，而是火車——一種歷史上不存在的可代表物，是能力跨越；電氣革命開頭是燈泡替代蠟燭，真正的爆發是冰箱、烤箱這些新增維度。AI 還沒走到那一步：結果付費只是我們的想像力和創新還沒到那個位置時的過渡，等「電視機時刻」出現，收費邏輯會被重新發明。

十四、入口之爭：信任入口、消費品牌與工作入口

  • 許丹青：入口比過去更重要，但性質變了——過去爭的是注意力/資訊入口（查東西想到百度），今天 Agent 要往前一步替你把事做完，入口還承擔了信任：你能不能相信它替你做的選擇。同時他引 Manus 肖弘的看法：未來的 AI 應用更像一個個消費品牌，靠品位與設計吸引一類人（有人喜歡 Nike、有人喜歡阿迪），未必存在大而統一的入口。財搭子因此堅持做獨立入口：投資場景足夠特殊，需要獨立的信任品牌承接；且穩定交付依賴連續上下文，持牌與帳戶授權壁壘能持續拿到使用者帳戶授權，通用入口裡嵌一個東西做不到端到端體驗。他透露曾與 Kimi 談過合作（他本人是 Kimi 的輔導員，與團隊很熟）：Kimi 希望把財搭子像同花順那樣做成全透明的 Skill/MCP 接入，他拒絕了——財搭子的 Memory 記錄的是使用者「如何 think about 一個決策」的過程，全白盒等於交出壁壘與定價權；而且通用入口做帳戶導流容易，做端到端體驗很難，最後放棄了這條路。
  • 馬春荃：合思只做「工作入口」，不做流量入口，自稱能力不允許、也沒錢（網際網路時代他就選擇嵌入飛書、釘釘等既有系統）。他對流量入口的看法更哲學：流量入口本質是泛場景匹配，應該站在使用者一側過濾過載資訊——比如現在很火的防詐騙 Agent，冒充你的身分陪騙子聊半小時，替主人過濾無效資訊。但悖論在於：你每月只付它 200 塊，它沒有動力做得更好（「200 塊錢你要什麼自行車」）；一旦它有了超額利潤，必然靠倒流、改變你的動線賺錢——入口會不會左右我們的思路與採購行為，大概率會，只是各家克制程度不同（微信、Google 相對克制）。他還補了平台經濟學的一課：雙邊網路效應要求兩邊都不能是單一節點——買家只有一個、或賣家只有一個（比如只能買英偉達）時，平台就失效；2B 面對幾個大廠、幾個大客戶，本就不是雙邊網路，平台沒價值。
  • 未來形態（許丹青）：入口會分兩層——第一層是新硬體或手機系統級入口（Apple Intelligence、華為小藝之類）承接分發，前台收斂；其後仍是多個 App、多個 Agent 為你服務的生態。若一個入口加一個 App 能做完所有事，「今天可能人類就會滅了」。

十五、融資：退錢、牌照與補貼之死

  • 許丹青解釋了主持人提到的「退回融資」：2024 年 6 月創業時一級市場幾乎沒人看 AI 應用，首輪快速鎖定百川智慧（大模型公司要布局場景）等投資人，後續也有機構扶持場景企業。增長上他們一直謹慎，卡點在持牌與監管邊界：2026 年 6 月後監管對 AI 投資明顯收緊，「930 政策」後非持牌 KOL 不得再宣傳、帶貨 AI 投資產品。產品側幾十萬使用者全靠口碑裂變、零投放，註冊到付費的轉化自稱國內 AI 應用裡最好，但使用者們從模擬帳戶時期就催著「什麼時候能接管我的帳戶」——沒有牌照全都做不了。今年 7 月他想通：解決牌照既是壁壘、也是發展到這個階段必須面對的問題。隨後兩個月與九方（港股上市的持牌機構）談成戰略控股合作——監管要求持牌資質的控股方必須是持牌機構——與老股東協商後老股東按比例退出，9 月 15 日完成股權變更，一個月內補齊持牌能力。他的結論：AI 應用創業融資不一定是為錢，可以為的是牌照這類外部必要資源。
  • 馬春荃：如果是今天從 0 到 1 他會融資，但以合思現在的階段不會。融資只有兩個目的——續命，或者加槓桿；他既不需要續命，錢來了也暫時沒有槓桿可加。融資節奏像種蘿蔔：天使期是挑種子（苗好不好看、能不能長出來不知道），第二輪進大棚、到田間（施肥灌溉，還是續命邏輯），等成果出現才談得上加槓桿規模化。
  • 網際網路式「講大故事、燒錢買使用者、資料飛輪」在 AI 時代還成立嗎？兩人都搖頭。許丹青：純市場競爭式補貼不成立——每個使用者的邊際成本不為零，補貼來的多是白嫖黨，什麼都沒沉澱；只有場景已鎖定、為產品效果而補貼（本質是花錢買資料）才可行，先想清楚要什麼資料，再補貼、迭代、規模化、提價。馬春荃：網際網路賣的是確定性的資訊與服務（打車、外賣、商品，總有一款能下單），來了就能轉化、留存天然高；AI 賣的是「一個聰明的腦子」，使用者發現腦子不聰明立刻流失——補貼換不來交易鏈條的沉澱。

十六、結尾判語

  • 許丹青：「當然是好生意。而且我覺得 27 年 AI 應用應該會有不一樣的拐點」——市場會爆發，Personal Agent 已經有一些這樣的趨勢了。
  • 馬春荃：「我不知道什麼是爆發，但它肯定是個好生意。」它會顯著拉開差距、增強差異化，而不是走向完全同質化；他更期待那個想像不到的「電視機時刻」——先上牌桌、先發明燈泡，剩下的東西，都可以由我們重新發明。

Summary: YiLunAI — “Is AI Applications Actually a Good Business?” (Xu Danqing × Ma Chunquan)

  • Date: 2026-11-19 (published on YouTube; X post title: “Is AI Applications Actually a Good Business?”, YouTube title: “AI Applications Will Explode in 2027”)

  • Guests: Xu Danqing (founder of Caidazi), Ma Chunquan (founder of Hesi)

This summary was compiled in Chinese. Figures such as revenue and costs are as stated by the guests on the spot and have not been independently verified.

In one sentence

Two frontline founders compare notes on the business of AI applications: almost all AI applications currently making money in China and the US are efficiency businesses in 2B/2P where “the value can be calculated clearly”; 2C has not yet produced a clear money-maker, but both judge 2027 to be the inflection point. The three hardest points of consensus in this episode are: token bills will not fall as model prices fall (tasks have become more complex), ARR is a vanity metric that is becoming invalid, and an application company's real moat is users' personalised data plus unwritten know-how — plus Ma Chunquan's provocative claim: rather than building a moat to guard against being killed by large models, it is better to kill yourself first and be reincarnated.

1. The two companies introduce themselves

  • Caidazi (Xu Danqing): Founded in June 2024, it makes an AI investment assistant for ordinary retail investors. The team has 12 people and describes itself as highly AI-native; revenue is in the several million RMB range, on the edge of break-even — profitable if labour costs are excluded, not yet enough once labour is included. Xu Danqing himself has a computer science background, won an award during his master's for using reinforcement learning to predict financial markets, and later worked on investment-research products at Tonglian Data and Robo Research (the host noted that his product reached a peak of one million daily active users).
  • Hesi (Ma Chunquan): Founded in 2014 (formerly Yikuai Bao), it started with expense control and reimbursement, expanded into corporate business spending and financial income-and-expenditure management, and now runs two lines: intelligent finance and intelligent business travel. The team has 400 people and revenue of more than RMB 300 million; this year it finally emerged from a historical cycle of losses. Ma Chunquan spent more than a decade at Yonyou; the host noted that Hesi is the old software vendor that has “revolutionised itself most thoroughly” — he once pulled together a Harness team for closed-door development whose goal was to use AI to kill Hesi itself.

2. What are users actually willing to pay for?

  • Caidazi's most popular feature has changed once: from the second half of 2025 to early 2026 it was research reports (generating stock/industry research reports based on industry frameworks, with dynamic updates tracking marginal changes); after model companies turned Deep Research into a free general capability, conversion for this feature fell noticeably. The custodial feature launched in March this year became the new mainstay: users upload screenshots of their assets or authorise their accounts, and the system follows information related to their accounts in a fully custodial way and proactively pushes updates; the paid conversion rate is very high, and Xu Danqing said it is leading in domestic 2C commercialisation. The differentiation he described: general models (Doubao/GPT) provide one-off Q&A-style “broad and comprehensive” deep analysis, whereas what investing really needs is the impact of changes in marginal information on your specific assets right now, layered with personalised processing and pushes based on your investment profile — “it understands you better.”
  • What Hesi's customers are most willing to pay for is products where ROI can be calculated clearly, typically intelligent auditing: invoice compliance and internal corporate rules (spending standards, three-document matching) all have to be audited. Case: for a listed clothing chain with 2,000 stores, auditing the single scenario of “clothing alteration fees” saved 35 people; the company spends more than RMB 60 million a year on alteration fees, and at least another 5% of that cost was saved. Ma Chunquan said that in the past, when selling digital tools, companies always doubted “did you really save me money?”; after AI went in, it was visible to the naked eye — “those 35 people simply no longer have that job.”

3. Why you cannot just throw reimbursement rules to ChatGPT to audit by itself

  • Ma Chunquan told a joke: ask a large model whether Tsinghua or Lanxiang Technical School is better, and it says Tsinghua is better; the operator says “Sorry, I graduated from Lanxiang,” and it immediately changes its tune and finds a pile of reasons why Lanxiang is better. Large models have a people-pleasing personality and will follow the user's attitude; what corporate finance needs is consistency, accuracy and no ambiguity — finance cannot get the accounts wrong; something either exists or it does not. Even more critical is verification: general models do not provide continuous tracking of accuracy and consistency rates. Hesi's approach is like quality inspection on an assembly line — after a run, results are re-checked and verified according to a sampling rate. He also took a swipe in passing: programmers are “a group of people who master the highest technology but work in the lowest way” — workshop-style production, where the 500 lines of code each person produces with Codex are all of different quality; AI should operate automatically and verify automatically like a factory assembly line once switched on, rather than imitating programmers. He even told the Doubao team to their faces: the model of topping up employees is a transitional product; tokens should be topped up for Agents, not for employees — just as after boilers appeared, coal was allocated to boilers, not to boiler workers, and a boiler worker's efficiency does not affect the boiler's throughput.

4. Who is making money: two maps, China and the US

  • Xu Danqing named names: Claude Code, Codex and Manus count as truly making money; a friend of his builds live-streaming agents (digital-human live streaming) for overseas merchants, with revenue reaching the tens of millions of US dollars. The consensus judgement: applications making money now in both China and the US are concentrated in efficiency categories leaning towards 2B/2P, with clear scenarios and value that can be calculated; 2C does not yet have a clear money-maker, but 2027 will bring a real money-making opportunity for 2C.
  • Ma Chunquan on China-US divergence: before AI, the market capitalisation gap between Chinese and US listed software companies was 15 times; after AI it became 37 times — Chinese software companies are going downhill, while US ones have doubled. In Silicon Valley he heard a student of Fei-Fei Li talk about a digital-human shopping-guide project (try-ons, asking about stock and sizes); a Chinese customer asked, “Nobody in China would pay for a tool like this — users think talking to it is a bit silly — how did you sell it?” The answer was essential: have you ever been ignored by a shop assistant in a sports store in San Francisco for more than 10 minutes? It is not that AI is more advanced; it is that the US service baseline is lower — replacing a job worth US$100,000, spending 30,000, 50,000 or 80,000 is all worthwhile. Another category making money is companies like Meitu: an originally huge user base, charging more by adding AI, with a 70% gross margin and fast overseas growth; high-quality services overseas are extremely expensive and require an 18% tip on top, so users already find somewhat mechanised AI services OK.
  • Domestically it is the opposite: a listed company he had visited that morning was building Agents at any cost, charging individual customers several million to over ten million RMB a year in fees, and was very likely still losing money — they did not dare include token costs, because once they did, sales would not dare to sell.

5. North-star metrics: from ARR to task completion rate and renewal

  • The first metric that pops up on Caidazi's dashboard every day is the task completion rate: the product has a Personal Agent architecture, and the back end breaks down the tasks to monitor based on the user's investment profile, looking at each task's progress and whether it has reached a deliverable state; the second is the user feedback list — tapping the bottom-right corner on any screen in the App lets users screenshot and give feedback, and a back-end Agent pipeline judges what users are dissatisfied with and feeds it directly into a multidimensional table.
  • Hesi aligns the whole company on two metrics: profit and loss, and the renewal rate. New contracts are won through competitive bidding and price wars; not losing money is already decent, and all profit comes from renewals by existing customers — if a deal is not renewed in the second year, it is a pure loss. After AI, renewal requirements are higher, because the form of AI revenue has changed: from a licence that occurs once a year to usage-based billing that occurs every day. Hesi's AI auditing software itself is free; it charges RMB 10 per audited document, on the condition that the discrimination accuracy is greater than 90%; revenue growth comes from expanding scenarios (travel documents → entertainment expenses → accounts payable → the whole of income-and-expenditure management) and expanding category penetration, just as those who once sold air tickets also took over hotels and ride-hailing in custody.
  • Why does nobody talk about ARR any more? Xu Danqing said ARR is essentially “a cash extrapolation under optimistic assumptions” — first purchase or first subscription multiplied by 12 — which does not make sense for early AI applications and is a vanity metric; he cited data saying the annual retention rate for AI products is only about 21%, far below excellent SaaS (NDR above 100%, renewal rates above 70%). Ma Chunquan added ARR's origin: it was a balancing tool designed by capital markets for the certainty of SaaS revenue, but sales selling multi-year contracts distort it (the commission goes into their pocket first; if I leave next year, who cares about you?); AI, by contrast, is usage that happens moment by moment, with peaks and troughs (SMS volume surging on Double Eleven), and telecom operators have long used “usage value” rather than ARR for assessment — AI companies should also return to a usage basis, and monthly revenue multiplied by 12 can at most be called forecast ARR, which is not scientific.

6. The token bill: unit price falling, bill rising

  • Caidazi's token costs account for about 30% of total costs (including labour), or 70%–80% excluding labour, and the share is rising year on year. Xu Danqing broke it down: underneath is complex model routing, with the main model DeepSeek accounting for about half, offering good value for money; but model unit prices have not fallen tenfold as rumoured, and the bill is rising in three places — more users, more tasks per user, and more consumption per task in pursuit of completion rates (an average task now calls more than 40 tools). Moving from a single dialogue box to multiple Agents, plus Deep Thinking first talking to itself in a sandbox simulation, all count towards the application company's costs. His forecast for next year: the total will definitely rise (user scale is expected to increase more than tenfold after the change in equity structure), but the share of revenue will fall, through a series of optimisations (including scheduling calls by peak and off-peak periods).
  • Hesi's token share is low because its approach is the opposite: revenue is a fixed RMB 10 per document, so it can only desperately push down consumption per document; moreover, 70% of bidding is commercial score, based on the lowest price, so if token costs are not suppressed, this business cannot make money. His rule: the more converged and fixed the scenario, the more tokens can be streamlined; the more generalised the scenario, requiring autonomous planning and trial and error, the more money it burns. He also proposed splitting costs: the production environment is assessed by cost rate and profit rate (mass-production logic), while generalised trial and error in the Harness laboratory should be counted as experiment/R&D costs — Xu Danqing chimed in that Anthropic's prospectus splits it exactly this way: only inference token consumption in the production stage counts towards the revenue cost rate (about 15%), while another large chunk of about 50% is computing power for researching the next-generation model, listed separately and not mixed together.

7. When will profit flow to the application layer?

  • Xu Danqing's script: model companies are like China Mobile, China Telecom and China Unicom in the 5G era, providing infrastructure — they used to sell data traffic, today they sell tokens; in the early days of 5G, data was so expensive that watching a video cost a lot of money, and only when infrastructure matured and costs came down did super-apps like Douyin and Pinduoduo grow. The same is true today — he gave the example that running a medium-intensity Personal Agent on Muse costs about US$30/month. If tokens do not come down in price, AI applications cannot explode, and there will be an AI bubble, and the industry chain cannot continue. So it is now a race: whether open-source models first drive inference costs low enough, or closed-source model companies first build platforms and ecosystems — this determines whether independent AI application companies can make money.
  • Ma Chunquan's “generalisation is uneconomical”: the whole industry's expectation of AI is to create a perfect superhuman to replace excellent humans; but the method human civilisation arrived at over thousands of years is precisely division of labour — any one person focuses on only one job. We routinely tolerate a great deal of human incompetence (90% of colleagues fail to meet expectations), yet demand that AI be all-powerful — “how can an incompetent person create an all-powerful one?” Generalisation is uneconomical in three ways: the tokens it burns are uneconomical, time is uneconomical, and information is uneconomical. He argued for bringing expectations back to a rational level: let digital employees imitate a professional person who is divided, constrained and defined, and deliver results in a job role — only then can ROI be calculated; to deliver results requires short-range, efficient inference, and a context that grabs too much will inevitably be slow — trading space for time and time for space, the basic laws of computing cannot be changed. He described himself as a pessimist: he does not believe large models will have consciousness or superintelligence.

8. Why SaaS fell: what killed software is Vibe Coding, not Agents

  • Background: the overall valuation of US SaaS fell from a peak of about 20 times price-to-sales to about 3 times; in February this year Anthropic's Cowork launched 11 plugins, with Agents directly doing work for people without needing to look at a UI, which was called “SaaS's moment of hell.”
  • Ma Chunquan's judgement: SaaS has fallen not yet because of the impact of Agents, but because of the impact of Vibe Coding — the first time he used Cursor he started having nightmares: a customer raises a requirement in the morning and implements a good-enough version themselves in the afternoon — why wait for a vendor? The old valuation model (6–8 times annual revenue, 20 times at the peak) bet that once users were locked in they could not leave for 6 to 20 years; now that model has collapsed. But hand-built software has a ceiling: business modelling, system design and extensibility depend on the mental framework, taste and experience of the person raising the requirement, and when the enterprise changes, it will discover the software no longer supports it.
  • But he also said Agents have not destroyed software; instead they have boosted it: Agents do not eat the IT budget, they eat the human-resources budget; and an Agent's most effective interaction partner is not a person, but software, MCPs and APIs. So SaaS is polarising: those with only a UI interface die; those that have accumulated sufficient complexity live better — the non-explicit know-how layered into systems by successive finance directors, CFOs and CEOs, written in code but never written into documents, is extremely hard to dissolve, and companies like Salesforce that charge by data call volume are actually rising. The conclusion: software will step down from the historical stage and become underlying infrastructure — it will still exist, but will no longer be the hottest thing, nor will it extract new know-how; new know-how will accumulate at the Agent entry point; so the SAPs must build Agents themselves, or they are finished.

9. The map of being swallowed by models: what not to touch, what to hold fast

  • The three waves Caidazi went through: early 2025, DeepSeek's reasoning ability (a great thing — the research-report product got going and the company survived) → model companies competing on Deep Research (research reports went from a main paid feature to a marginal one) → the Agent-capability wave (the Agent wave led by OpenClaw's “little lobster”, during which they quickly launched the custodial feature and paid conversion rose another level). Hesi's biggest watershed was DeepSeek's release during Spring Festival 2025: before that, to use a good model they could only use overseas ones, but the data was non-compliant, and domestic models were unusable, so before 2025 they had never formally released an AI product — DeepSeek made Agents “both compliant and qualified”, and they only released an AI product in April 2025.
  • Xu Danqing's boundary principle: focus on what is closer to the user; do not touch what is closer to the model core. What they hold fast is the user connection (account authorisation, the experience within the first ten minutes of onboarding, the difference in each connection); even if models do not yet do Harness and strategy cores well enough today, they do not build them themselves, believing large models will get better and better — they do the “personalisation” of strategy (each person's strategy is different), but not the strategy core (universal capabilities like backtesting and factor assembly), because after model iteration it will be done better than by an application company. Expensive users are routed to the strongest model; ordinary ones first run a layer with a cheap model; at least you need to know where the ceiling of the currently best model is.
  • Ma Chunquan's moat theory: go directly into services, get closer to the customer, take the work over and be responsible for the result. For example, the endgame of intelligent auditing is to contract out the customer's shared finance centre: a centre that originally cost RMB 10 million is taken over for RMB 8 million; the customer calculates their own labour bill, not how much your software is worth, so price is actually easier to negotiate, and it also solves employment risk as a bonus. Why contract rather than sell software to transform the customer? Because the biggest difficulty in technical transformation is persuading the head of the shared centre — your success means the people he manages go from 100 to 10, and he will panic and resist; contracting is “I cannot change you, so I change myself.” The third point is to master know-how the customer does not know: there is no chance in the customer's main business (that is their core business), but in its cost items, an application company has enough room to accumulate specialised knowledge.
  • Will models eventually eat professional capabilities? The consensus of the two: public, universal expert data such as finance and tax will be trained into models (vendors are spending large sums buying expert annotations); but the idiosyncratic know-how of every company and every person across thousands of industries is, for large models, “dirty data”, diversity data, and the probabilistic algorithms of pre-training cannot handle judgements spread 1% in each of 100 directions. Xu Danqing gave investing as an example: you will never know how Buffett and Berkshire internally finally decided to buy Apple — investment decisions are essentially extremely personalised context plus professional know-how; only with buying and selling is there a market, and there is no consensus answer, which is precisely the charm of applications.

10. Division of labour with models: Memory, Harness, Benchmark

  • Caidazi's three internal keywords: Memory, Harness, Benchmark (Evaluation), with Memory becoming increasingly important — this is also what all Personal Agent companies value most. The most efficient way to collect context is to have the user authorise an investment account once, which is far more accurate than asking in a questionnaire “what are your investment goals?”; during use, core memory and layered memory are continuously built. For Harness they use the open-source set from Codex (after evaluating DeepSeek's Harness they still chose Codex), with model routing and tool scheduling underneath (in-house tools plus several hundred ready-made MCPs). Benchmark is model-driven: from several hundred thousand user queries they extracted 1,000 highest-quality trajectories and annotated them — Xu Danqing revealed that a large model company once offered about RMB 1 million to buy this data of 1,000 core user trajectories, and they did not sell. In task management they also have to judge “whether this task is critical for the user, whether to notify them, and in what form”, and feedback after notification flows back to enrich Memory.
  • Hesi splits the model layer into two layers: a meta-thinking model (reusable problem-solving approaches, such as the “evidence-chain deduction” style of fault-finding used extensively in auditing; he believes human thinking patterns ultimately come down to three — induction, deduction and analogy — and AI's reasoning actions can likewise be abstracted) and a scenario model (data, labels and Skill encapsulation for a given scenario). The split is for ecosystem cooperation: entering thousands of industries cannot be done by a single Agent alone; partners must be allowed to retain their autonomously controllable knowledge and form joint IP with Hesi like “a child with half its genes from each”, with the meta-thinking model reused like a template and scenario knowledge built by the partner.
  • A reference added by the host: the key to Cursor not being eaten by large models is that it records whether users accept code completions and trained its own small model on interaction data — interaction data is real gold beyond static public data (she said Cursor was sold to SpaceX for US$60 billion). Asked whether application companies should train their own models: Xu Danqing said post-training is essentially a commercial calculation — the current user scale is not large enough, and training a model would actually cost more; high-quality RL post-training for a few million RMB can train a decent version, and when scale arrives and token costs need to be suppressed, making a fine-tuned/RL in-house model offers better value. Ma Chunquan said Hesi resolutely did not do it last year (in a period when large models were not developing brutally, binding investments would be submerged and uneconomical), but this year it has instead launched small-size privatised, hardware-based products: customers do not want to throw their holographic operational data out, nor can they afford a B300, so they use small models in scenarios where high latency is acceptable (waiting 10–20 minutes is fine) to get things running, preserving data security while remaining economically feasible; moreover, on a general large model the difference between 96 points and 90 points is not significant, whereas a private small model can widen the score gap and make it impossible for a competitor's IT team to catch up in the short term — that is the threshold.

11. Provocative claims session: the two kinds of know-how, and “reincarnation”

  • Ma Chunquan's second provocative claim: software vendors simply do not master customers' know-how. Know-how comes in two kinds — how to do it and how to think about it; most software vendors only take on the former: you tell me how you want it done, I record it for you, make a button, and run the process through; why the process should be designed this way is often outside the software vendor's field of vision. The host summarised the anti-swallowing moat list: users' personalised data (static data plus interaction data), implicit knowledge and ways of thinking not yet accumulated into explicit text, offline delivery, licences, and social relationships.
  • Does social count as a moat? Xu Danqing thinks so: everyone believes a 2C Personal Agent is most likely to be made on WeChat, because a large amount of personal context accumulates in WeChat, the migration cost of social relationships is extremely high, and social relationships themselves can help build context.
  • Ma Chunquan's response stepped outside the defensive frame: all industries should be reincarnated, not just build moats. The difference in action between reincarnation and building a moat is to kill yourself first — “you are not guarding against being killed by it; you say, fine, let me be eaten by you; after being eaten, another flower may grow.” Suicide is a momentary thing; what is hard is that after jumping, your mindset is completely different. He said small companies actually have a chance: the boss decides, resistance is low; large companies struggle with turf, internal politics and quotas. He gave Microsoft's cloud transformation as an example: in the first year or two of transformation, sales were not given quotas; they first selected 10–20 top global enterprises as benchmarks, then expanded to 100, and only then transformed comprehensively and cut half the sales force — because once sales targets are set, sales inertia will push them to sell old products that easily deliver results.

12. The pricing-model revolution: from seat fees to paying for results

  • Hesi's AI auditing is charged per document, RMB 10 per document (documents such as private reimbursements, travel, entertainment, office ride-hailing), benchmarked against labour: an ordinary company's finance person audits about 6,000 documents a year, an intensive shared finance centre 8,000–12,000, and with personnel costs fully allocated, the bill is very intuitive. He originally tried selling it as a software package (three tiers of RMB 200,000 and RMB 300,000 a year); demos and co-creation experiences were all very good, customers said it was great — they just did not buy; only three deals were sold in half a year, because faced with something new, customers could not calculate the value. He wrote the “horsepower” analogy in his own book: Watt improved the steam engine, but the hardest part was selling it, so he invented “horsepower”, an extremely unscientific unit — a farmer who keeps horses understands how much money three horsepower saves. Charging per document is AI's horsepower.
  • FDE should raise its price: Ma Chunquan said they only started formally building an FDE team this year; before that he had been careless and had not priced it, so customers lacked awareness, and only at delivery did they discover rules needed to be reorganised, and blamed the vendor. FDE's work is organisational change, process reshaping and rule rebuilding — the rules accumulated inside enterprises contradict each other in large numbers; people forget and enforce selectively, so they can be compatible, but throw them all at AI and it goes mad; this must be sold expensively so bosses take it seriously. He distinguished FDE from customer success: FDE is oriented towards know-how extraction and convergent delivery of the whole product solution, while customer success is “I already have this product; how do I get you to use it.” He also mentioned a cognitive gap: sales always ask “where is the boundary of our standard product?”, and he really wants to answer “there is no boundary; where is the boundary of your brain?” — engineers and consultants of the software era were framed by PPTs and promotional leaflets; in the AI era, the thinking model and generalisable scenarios behind a single dialogue window have to be thought through with the brain.
  • Caidazi's pricing is a combination of subscription plus usage: first a subscription (tiers such as RMB 68 and RMB 128, giving C-end users an intuitive expectation), then beyond the subscription package, complex tasks are paid for in a usage model of “issuing coins”.
  • How are results verified? Caidazi mainly relies on AI auditing AI — after user feedback (especially likes and negative feedback when a task is executed particularly well or particularly badly) enters the back end, a more expensive model re-runs and compares it on a new Harness; quantitative criteria include data timeliness, information ratio, marginal gain and risk-return ratio; multiple models judge crosswise (the transcript mentioned Astra, Claude, Gemini, etc.), and only when none can judge is a human brought in. Hesi uses automation plus about 2% manual sampling in parallel auditing; once the consistency rate is verified above 95%, the overall accuracy can be presumed to be above 90%. Hesi's traditional business is still charged by seat fee, while the AI segment is charged by results; Ma Chunquan expected the real crossover point not to be inside the finance business, but after Harness's meta-thinking model combines with more industries — “business benchmarked against software and tools is average; business benchmarked against people's entire human-resource costs is the good business.”
  • Caidazi's refund mechanism deserves a separate note: Xu Danqing said it has three functions — first, lowering the activation threshold: faced with an Agent where users do not know what will be delivered, they can start using it first; second, incentivising high-quality feedback, especially negative feedback (originally, just pushing a feedback entry point, users were too lazy to bother; after introducing refunds, users' degree of dissatisfaction is quantified, and by design they either get points refunded or a free retry), and what AI iteration lacks most is high-quality feedback; third, a team-alignment mechanism, making everyone — growth, front end, algorithms — responsible for the single thing of “delivering good results.” After the mechanism went live, the core metric (task completion rate) did not change, but team efficiency and the Harness's result success rate improved noticeably.

13. Is paying for results the endgame?

  • Xu Danqing: subscription plus usage is a transitional model; in the future the bulk should be paying for results; investing already has precedents — buying a fund you pay a management fee, private equity takes an excess-profit share on top, and what the product delivers is the result. Caidazi's endgame is therefore to be deeply bound to licensed institutions: only with a licence can it give specific advice, participate in the transaction stage, and take a revenue share.
  • Ma Chunquan: paying for results is still at the “analogy stage”. What the steam-engine revolution really brought was not a replacement for the miller, but the train — a representable thing that did not exist in history, a leap in capability; the electrical revolution began with light bulbs replacing candles, but the real explosion was new dimensions such as refrigerators and ovens. AI has not yet reached that step: paying for results is only a transition while our imagination and innovation have not yet reached that position; when the “television moment” arrives, pricing logic will be reinvented.

14. The battle for the entry point: trust entry, consumer brands and work entry

  • Xu Danqing: the entry point is more important than in the past, but its nature has changed — in the past the contest was over attention/information entry points (thinking of Baidu to look something up); today an Agent has to go one step further and finish the job for you, and the entry point also bears trust: can you trust the choices it makes for you? He also cited the view of Manus's Xiao Hong: future AI applications will be more like consumer brands, attracting a type of person through taste and design (some people like Nike, some like Adidas), and there may not be a large, unified entry point. Caidazi therefore insists on building an independent entry point: the investment scenario is special enough to need an independent trust brand to carry it; and stable delivery depends on continuous context, and the moat of licences and account authorisation can continuously obtain users' account authorisation, which embedding something inside a general entry point cannot achieve as an end-to-end experience. He revealed he had talked with Kimi about cooperation (he himself is a counsellor for Kimi and knows the team well): Kimi wanted to make Caidazi into a fully transparent Skill/MCP integration like Tonghuashun; he refused — Caidazi's Memory records the process of how users “think about a decision”, and a fully white-box approach would mean handing over the moat and pricing power; moreover, a general entry point finds it easy to divert account traffic but hard to deliver an end-to-end experience, so in the end he abandoned that path.
  • Ma Chunquan: Hesi only builds a “work entry point”, not a traffic entry point, saying its capability does not allow it and it has no money (in the internet era he already chose to embed into existing systems such as Feishu and DingTalk). His view of traffic entry points is more philosophical: a traffic entry point is essentially general-scenario matching and should stand on the user's side to filter overloaded information — for example, the now-popular anti-fraud Agent, which impersonates your identity to chat with fraudsters for half an hour and filters invalid information for its owner. But the paradox is: you pay it only RMB 200 a month, so it has no motivation to do better (“what kind of bicycle do you want for 200 RMB?”); once it has excess profit, it will inevitably make money by diverting traffic and changing your path — will an entry point influence our thinking and purchasing behaviour? Most likely yes; it is just that each company restrains itself to a different degree (WeChat and Google are relatively restrained). He also added a lesson in platform economics: a two-sided network effect requires that neither side be a single node — when there is only one buyer, or only one seller (for example, you can only buy Nvidia), the platform fails; 2B facing a few large vendors and a few large customers is not a two-sided network in the first place, and a platform has no value.
  • The future form (Xu Danqing): entry points will be in two layers — the first layer is new hardware or mobile-phone system-level entry points (such as Apple Intelligence and Huawei's Xiaoyi) carrying distribution, with the front end converging; behind that there will still be an ecosystem of multiple Apps and multiple Agents serving you. If one entry point plus one App could do everything, “humanity might be wiped out today.”

15. Financing: returning money, licences, and the death of subsidies

  • Xu Danqing explained the “returning financing” mentioned by the host: when he started the company in June 2024, almost nobody in the primary market was looking at AI applications; the first round quickly locked in investors such as Baichuan Intelligence (a large-model company wanting to lay out scenarios), and later institutions also supported scenario companies. On growth they have always been cautious; the bottleneck is licences and regulatory boundaries: after June 2026 regulation of AI investing tightened noticeably, and after the “930 policy” non-licensed KOLs were no longer allowed to promote or sell AI investment products. On the product side, several hundred thousand users all came through word-of-mouth viral growth with zero advertising spend, and conversion from registration to payment is said to be the best among domestic AI applications, but users had been urging since the simulated-account period, “when can you take over my account?” — without a licence none of that can be done. In July this year he figured it out: solving the licence is both a moat and a problem that must be faced at this stage of development. Over the following two months he negotiated a strategic controlling-stake cooperation with Jiufang (a Hong Kong-listed licensed institution) — regulation requires the controlling party with licensed qualifications to be a licensed institution — and after consulting existing shareholders, they exited pro rata; the equity change was completed on 15 September, and licensed capability was completed within a month. His conclusion: financing for an AI application startup is not necessarily for money; it can be for externally necessary resources such as a licence.
  • Ma Chunquan: if he were starting from 0 to 1 today he would raise financing, but at Hesi's current stage he would not. Financing has only two purposes — extending life, or adding leverage; he neither needs to extend life, nor, if money came, would he have leverage to add for the time being. The rhythm of financing is like growing radishes: the angel stage is selecting seeds (whether the seedling looks good and whether it can grow is unknown), the second round goes into a greenhouse and then into the field (fertilising and irrigating, still a life-extension logic), and only when results appear can leverage and scale be discussed.
  • Does the internet-style “tell a big story, burn money to buy users, data flywheel” still hold in the AI era? Both shook their heads. Xu Danqing: purely market-competition subsidies do not hold — the marginal cost per user is not zero, and those who come for subsidies are mostly free-riders from whom nothing accumulates; only when the scenario is already locked in and subsidies are for product effectiveness (essentially spending money to buy data) is it feasible: first be clear about what data you want, then subsidise, iterate, scale and raise prices. Ma Chunquan: the internet sells deterministic information and services (ride-hailing, food delivery, goods — there is always something to order), and arrivals can convert, with naturally high retention; AI sells “a smart brain”, and when users discover the brain is not smart they churn immediately — subsidies cannot buy accumulation in the transaction chain.

16. Closing verdicts

  • Xu Danqing: “Of course it is a good business. And I think AI applications in '27 should have a different inflection point” — the market will explode, and Personal Agents already show some such trends.
  • Ma Chunquan: “I don't know what an explosion is, but it is definitely a good business.” It will significantly widen gaps and strengthen differentiation, rather than moving towards complete homogenisation; he looks forward more to that unimaginable “television moment” — first get a seat at the table, first invent the light bulb, and the rest can all be reinvented by us.
