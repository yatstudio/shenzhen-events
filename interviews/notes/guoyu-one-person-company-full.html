纪要｜《郭宇：用AI經營一人公司（全）》

视频：https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pDAmg04gXV4 ｜时长 2 小时 03 分｜与《AI做员工，开一人公司》（Prb_YPSdzH4，63 分钟）为同一场活动的完整版：前 63 分钟内容完全一致（回应质疑、协调者之辩、黄东旭「最终的软件」、Harness Engineering 与 Wanman 演示），本纪要只补记后 60 分钟新增的问答部分。完整论述见 Prb_YPSdzH4 纪要。 说明：机器转写，采样通读后半段，个别发言归属可能有偏差；数据为讲述者现场自述，未经核实。

后半程新增内容（现场问答）

  1. 信任与复核（与 diN 问答场同题，此处答得更细）：观众问——AI 产出的商业计划书、财务预测，你没有专业知识，怎么判断合理？他的做法分层：代码他能 review 就 review；操作系统、数据库之外的领域（如商业计划）他不假装懂——SOTA 模型会自己联网查证、交叉对比后生成漂亮的 PDF，合理性最终由专业机构裁定（银行放贷会审、律师会审）；隐私政策、用户条款这类文件他全交 AI 写、从没逐条看过。早年 ChatGPT 的幻觉问题已经大为改观。他重申自己的工作只有「调度」：让 agent 把所有行业的工作写成可执行的东西，他负责机会与方向。
  2. Wanman 的两个待解问题：一是隐私——接管模式理论上能拿到你全部数据，本地派（OpenClaw）强调数据不出本机；他的立场是能扩到云端就尽量在云端扩（性能与能力优先），后续会支持文件夹上传导入。二是很多人文件本身就乱，导入后 AI 也只能做到 30 分——数据整备是真实瓶颈。
  3. 企业场景预言：现在是混乱期。大公司不会想着「用 AI 接管员工」，他们更可能把 AI 装进员工的电脑里，记录每个人的工作习惯与流程，让系统自我进化、慢慢替代——美国公司干脆直接裁员；日本公司因终身雇佣传统，会用更「平衡」的方式（员工 6 点下班后 AI 接着干）。把 AI 当实习生用的公司，不会去做这种折衷。
  4. 交易员提问（与 diN 场同题）：做交易系统、想做涨跌幅提醒与定投 DCA——他答：不需要自己开发，开着券商网页保持登录，让 Computer Use 接管浏览器操作即可；这类需求的人很多。本地派的理由也成立：你电脑里有全部上下文，AI 了解你的处境。
  5. 怎么让身边人用起来：教不会的，靠内在动力。你每天用、越来越熟练，他无动于衷是他的事；焦虑可以转化成动力，但对他来说动力很简单——「我只在乎这件事好不好玩」。时代剧变对普通人反而是机会：一成不变的世界里阶层早就固化了，变化才让你能分一杯羹。另一个建议反直觉：别太执着于目标，去跑步、冲浪、游泳，放松下来、不去想它的时候，事情反而成了。
  6. 权力终局（即兴发挥）：AI 公司将是未来权力最大的公司；太空数据中心、月球基地这类事「毫无疑问是马斯克来做」——那是极致的权力与极致的安全保障，人类可能被「变成神」，革命必然伴随阵痛且越走越快。他说自己没法对这些发表太多看法，但这是他反复回到的话题。
  7. 现场任务演示：给 Wanman 一个目标（做百万粉丝 YouTuber），它排出完整计划与话题清单（没做满 60 个话题）、成本与收入测算（长视频广告与 Shorts 广告、10 万粉量级现金流约 25,000 美元量级）——结论仍是：全用 AI 压成本，否则 20–50 万粉之前都在亏钱。（与 diN 问答场演示为同一案例的不同场次版本。）
  8. 写作即遗产：他从小学写日记、饭否时代写起，大脑被 AI 占据后，仍坚持把生活与思考写下来发出去——「最终都会变成 AI 模型的一部分」，这是给世界留下的遗产；很多爱写的人未必发出来，这是性格差异。公司不报销、纯业余的人不必强迫自己为 AI 花钱。
  9. 最后一个问题（日本销售岗观众）：在日本销售导向的公司里如何自处——他的建议是往 AI 暂时替代不了的方向走：金牌销售靠的是多年积累的信任关系，这正是 AI 拿不走的；同时用 AI 提升自己在本职上的竞争力，双向准备。

New Content in the Second Half (Live Q&A)

  1. Trust and review (the same question as in the diN Q&A session, answered in more detail here): An audience member asked — for a business plan or financial forecast produced by AI, how do you judge whether it is reasonable when you have no specialist knowledge? His approach is layered: for code, he reviews it if he can; for fields beyond operating systems and databases (such as business plans), he does not pretend to understand — SOTA models will search online themselves, cross-check, and then generate a polished PDF, and reasonableness is ultimately decided by professional institutions (a bank will review it before lending, a lawyer will review it); documents such as privacy policies and terms of use he leaves entirely to AI to write and has never read item by item. The hallucination problem of the early ChatGPT has improved greatly. He reiterated that his only job is "orchestration": letting agents turn work from all industries into executable things, while he is responsible for opportunities and direction.
  2. Two unsolved problems for Wanman: First, privacy — the takeover mode can in theory obtain all your data, while the local camp (OpenClaw) emphasizes that data never leaves the local machine; his position is to expand to the cloud wherever possible (performance and capability first), and folder upload and import will be supported later. Second, many people's files are messy to begin with, and after import AI can only reach 30 points — data preparation is the real bottleneck.
  3. Prediction for enterprise scenarios: This is a period of chaos. Large companies will not think of "using AI to take over employees"; they are more likely to install AI into employees' computers, record each person's work habits and processes, let the system evolve itself and gradually replace them — American companies simply lay people off outright; Japanese companies, because of the lifetime employment tradition, will use a more "balanced" approach (after employees leave at 6 p.m., AI keeps working). Companies that treat AI as an intern will not make this kind of compromise.
  4. A trader's question (the same as in the diN session): Building a trading system, wanting price-change alerts and dollar-cost averaging (DCA) — he answered: there is no need to develop it yourself; just keep the broker's webpage open and logged in and let Computer Use take over the browser operations; many people have this kind of need. The local camp's reasoning also holds: your computer contains the full context, and AI understands your situation.
  5. How to get people around you to start using it: You cannot teach them; it depends on inner motivation. You use it every day and become more and more proficient; if he remains unmoved, that is his business; anxiety can be turned into motivation, but for him motivation is simple — "I only care whether this is fun." Dramatic change in the times is actually an opportunity for ordinary people: in an unchanging world, social strata would long have solidified, and only change lets you get a share. Another counterintuitive suggestion: do not cling too tightly to the goal; go running, surfing or swimming, and when you relax and stop thinking about it, things actually come together.
  6. The endgame of power (improvised remarks): AI companies will be the most powerful companies of the future; things such as space data centers and lunar bases "will undoubtedly be done by Musk" — that is ultimate power and ultimate security, and humans may be "turned into gods"; revolution inevitably comes with pain and moves faster and faster. He said he could not comment too much on these things, but this is a topic he returns to repeatedly.
  7. Live task demo: Give Wanman a goal (become a YouTuber with a million followers), and it lays out a complete plan and a list of topics (it did not fill all 60 topics), cost and revenue estimates (long-video ads and Shorts ads, cash flow at the 100,000-follower level on the order of about 25,000 US dollars) — the conclusion remains: use AI throughout to keep costs down, otherwise you will be losing money until you reach 200,000–500,000 followers. (This is a different session version of the same case as the demo in the diN Q&A session.)
  8. Writing as a legacy: He has kept a diary since primary school and has been writing since the Fanfou era; after his brain has been occupied by AI, he still insists on writing down his life and thoughts and posting them — "in the end it will all become part of AI models," and this is the legacy he leaves to the world; many people who love writing may not post what they write, which is a difference in personality. People whose companies do not reimburse them and who are purely amateurs do not need to force themselves to spend money on AI.
  9. The last question (an audience member in a sales role in Japan): How to position yourself in a sales-oriented company in Japan — his advice is to move in directions AI cannot yet replace: a top salesperson relies on trust relationships accumulated over many years, which is exactly what AI cannot take away; at the same time, use AI to improve your competitiveness in your own job, preparing in both directions.
