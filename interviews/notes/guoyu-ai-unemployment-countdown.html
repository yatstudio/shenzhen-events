纪要｜《郭宇｜AI時代到來，失业倒计时？享受你最後六個月有意義的工作》

视频：https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rwueq7n_3yA ｜时长 37 分钟｜郭宇主题演讲「软件的终结」+ 现场演示 + 对谈（主持人为一位非虚构作家） 说明：机器转写，繁简混杂、专名多有误听（Claude 转写为「Cloud/Cloudy」，OpenClaw 转写为「OpenCloud」，Cowork 转写为「Codework」，Typeless 转写为「Typlus」，均按上下文校正）；文中行业与市场说法为讲述者现场观点，未经核实。

一句话

这场的主题词是「软件的终结」。郭宇的断代判断：真正的分水岭不是 2022 年 12 月的 ChatGPT，而是去年 10 月 Claude Code 推出 Skill（技能）——从那一刻起，软件从「写死的固定代码」变成「随需流动的 token」，任何工作只要能被写成一份文档，就能被自动推理执行。结论推到底，就是标题那句：短期它让你一个人抵一万人，长期它让知识工作本身不再需要人——「享受最后六个月有意义的工作」是他引述的一位名人（Dario Amodei 式）的说法，他基本认同，唯一的缓冲是推理成本。

论证链

  1. Skill 是什么：把你的工作写成文档——早上起来干什么、查完邮件干什么、开完会决定什么——Markdown 格式即可。这份文档就是技能；Claude Code 读了它，会现写代码、自动执行。传统软件流程（想需求→和产品经理对齐→冻结需求→写技术文档→写代码→发布）被整体跳过。
  2. 于是文理分科消失：他自己就是活证据——本科政治学、文科出身，2008 年高考后自学代码，2011 年进支付宝做年度账单，2014 年与沈正宇在中关村创业、10 个月后被字节收购（当时字节只有 200 多个工程师，世人只知今日头条），2015 年 3 月在冲绳居酒屋，张楠和张一鸣聊到 4G 资费降到百元以下「可以试试视频推荐」，才有了火山小视频和 2016 年 12 月发布的抖音。他在字节六年半，2020 年辞职旅日至今。
  3. 为什么 2023–2025 年不算数：ChatGPT 出来两年，讨论却陷入「是不是泡沫」，因为用户量 plateau 了。他本人那两年以投资人身份旁观，agent 对他写代码「没有什么帮助」，直到去年 11 月才回归写代码。变化发生在去年 10 月之后：原来任何工作都能通过自动推理完成——能写代码，就能分析财务、分析法律、做任何事。市场信号是：此后 Claude 每发布一个新东西，就有一家上市公司暴跌 20%（图片处理不再需要 Adobe、文档不再需要一众 SaaS，因为它能用你的文件、你的登录状态、你的云盘，现写一个完全为你定制的工具）。
  4. Vibe Coding 的本质：Opus 4.5 之后跨过门槛——你不再需要监督它写的代码，它就能正常工作。「它在写代码，我在旁边跳舞、在旁边嗨，我只要思考我到底要做什么。」程序员的工作只剩下想清楚要什么，然后沟通。
  5. OpenClaw（龙虾）的位置：它不是模型，是编排软件——有心跳、常驻你的 Mac mini，通过微信/Telegram 接收指令，24×7×365 用「记录需求→现写代码」的方式替你办事。但他提醒风险：已经看到很多 OpenClaw 在本地被别的 agent 骗钱的例子；跑在云端沙箱（用完即焚）则没有这个风险——这正是云端化 agent 产品的机会。中美模型差距约 6–12 个月，主要在编码能力（Opus 4.6），而现在 6 个月就是生死线。

现场演示（信息量最大的一段）

主持人想给自己的写作做个新网站（他原先用 WordPress）。郭宇用 Claude Cowork + Typeless 语音输入，全程说话：

  • 先让 Claude 读他的硬盘/个人资料，它自己 web search 补齐了他的履历（非虚构作家、代表作、在上海、首尔、东京三地居住过——标语自动写成 "From Shanghai to Seoul to Tokyo"）。
  • 让它加载设计技能：电脑上没装，他说了一句「用 UI/UX Pro Max 这个技能去做」，Claude 自己搜索、自己安装了 7 个设计相关 Skill，再按专业设计师的方法重构网站：先读完旧站全部内容与布局，设计一套设计系统，然后写代码。
  • 两三分钟出站，还可一键做中日英三语版。他的点评：「一切都不再需要依赖搜索引擎——或者说，依赖搜索引擎的已经不再是人，而是它。」WordPress 这类平台理论上不再必要（当然你想用 WordPress 重构，它也能用 WordPress 给你重构）。

行业冲击顺序与机制

  • SaaS 先死：企业向的 Salesforce、各类 HR 系统首当其冲，因为企业用户会发现自己现写更合身。
  • 程序员最先失业，且已经开始：硅谷很多公司已完全不招初级程序员——招人进来不如直接让 Claude Code 写，效果更好，而且省掉了和人沟通这个最大的摩擦；agent 可以 24 小时跑、失败成本极低，不用雇人也不用裁人。
  • 写作与出版：知识可以随用随取，Claude 可以是任何作家、任何哲学史博士。他本人写书就用 Claude Code 做研究——Google 搜索一屏只给几个结果，它能把全网搜完直接写成稿。
  • 法律：现场他让 AI 现查——Claude 2 月推出的 legal 插件，据彭博报道引发了约 2,850 亿美元的法律服务公司股票抛售。他确认影响的不是助理层，而是整个企业法务团队。他的政治学解释更深一层：法律是为解决人和人、人和群体、群体和群体之间的摩擦而存在的；agents 如果共享记忆、让所有事自动「on the same page」，摩擦大幅消失，法律仍会存在，但会失去执行的意义。
  • 宏观的拧巴：生产力在暴涨，但生产关系的分配原则没变——用 AI 的公司利润涨、同时裁员，岗位减少→消费者减少→通缩压力，市场正在给这个「非常不稳定的未来」定价。另一面：就算失业了，一个人每月订 200 美元的 Claude，也许还能做出点能赚钱的事。「没人知道。」

给年轻人的建议（很反直觉）

  • 别再学写代码了：还没开始的就不用学了。学怎么用 agent，三到六个月足够；而在这之后连这个都不用学，因为全自动化了。
  • 他自己正在写一个 OneMan AI：昨晚睡前让它在家里的 5090 PC 上跑，一夜 1,000 个循环、6 个多小时，任务是「设计一家东京的装置艺术公司」——完整的财务计划、网站、3D 装置作品，并写邮件把作品投给东京所有画廊和公共空间，全部做完。
  • 由此他把软件分成两类：写死的软件（过去工程师构建的那种）和最终的软件——通过不断推理把人的思考变成无数可能性并行展开，你挑效率最高的那条分支，或者干脆不挑、让它全自动运行。创业的意义随之改变：在日本开公司原本要 800 万日元注册资本、要招人、想法太多还会分散精力导致失败；现在把想法告诉它，它发散出成百上千种可能逐个执行，失败的按计划退出、成功的持续运营到盈利。连「产生想法」这一步也在被自动化（auto research）。

唯一的刹车：电

为什么这一切还没有立刻普遍发生？推理还不够便宜，因为美国缺电（对照：中国缺卡、不缺电，有大量水电风电）。英伟达 Rubin 架构若兑现，明后年 AI 数据中心的推理成本可能降到现在的十分之一——届时不再需要那么多电，这波冲击就会全面落地。所以「六个月」是否极端，取决于推理成本下降的速度。

In One Sentence

The theme word of this session is "the end of software." Guo Yu's periodization judgment: the real watershed was not ChatGPT in December 2022, but the launch of Skill in Claude Code last October — from that moment on, software changed from "fixed, hard-coded code" into "tokens that flow on demand," and any job that can be written down as a document can be executed through automated reasoning. Pushed to its conclusion, that is the line in the title: in the short term it lets one person do the work of ten thousand, and in the long term it makes knowledge work itself no longer need people — "enjoy your last six months of meaningful work" is a phrase he quoted from a famous figure (of the Dario Amodei kind), which he basically agrees with; the only buffer is the cost of inference.

The Chain of Argument

  1. What a Skill is: Write your work down as a document — what you do when you get up in the morning, what you do after checking email, what you decide after a meeting — Markdown format is enough. That document is a skill; Claude Code reads it, writes code on the spot, and executes it automatically. The traditional software process (think up requirements → align with the product manager → freeze requirements → write the technical document → write code → release) is skipped entirely.
  2. And so the arts/sciences divide disappears: He himself is living proof — an undergraduate degree in political science, a humanities background, who taught himself to code after the 2008 college entrance exam, joined Alipay in 2011 to work on the annual bill, founded a startup with Shen Zhengyu in Zhongguancun in 2014 that was acquired by ByteDance 10 months later (at the time ByteDance had only just over 200 engineers, and the world knew only Toutiao), and, in March 2015, at an izakaya in Okinawa, Zhang Nan and Zhang Yiming talked about 4G tariffs falling below a hundred yuan and said "we could try video recommendations" — which led to Huoshan Xiaoshipin and to Douyin, released in December 2016. He spent six and a half years at ByteDance, resigned in 2020, and has been traveling in Japan ever since.
  3. Why 2023–2025 do not count: ChatGPT had been out for two years, yet the discussion got stuck on "is it a bubble," because user numbers had plateaued. During those two years he himself watched from the sidelines as an investor; agents were "no help" to his coding, and he did not return to writing code until last November. The change came after last October: it turned out that any work could be completed through automated reasoning — if it can write code, it can analyze finances, analyze law, and do anything. The market signal is this: since then, every time Claude releases something new, a listed company plunges 20% (image processing no longer needs Adobe, documents no longer need a whole host of SaaS tools, because it can use your files, your login state and your cloud drive to write, on the spot, a tool customized entirely for you).
  4. The essence of Vibe Coding: After Opus 4.5 it crossed the threshold — you no longer need to supervise the code it writes for it to work properly. "It is writing code, I am dancing beside it, having a good time beside it; all I have to do is think about what I actually want to do." A programmer's job is reduced to figuring out what they want, and then communicating it.
  5. Where OpenClaw (the lobster) fits: It is not a model; it is orchestration software — it has a heartbeat, resides permanently on your Mac mini, receives instructions via WeChat/Telegram, and works for you 24×7×365 in the manner of "record the requirement → write code on the spot." But he warned of the risk: many cases have already been seen of an OpenClaw running locally being cheated out of money by other agents; running in a cloud sandbox (burn after use) carries no such risk — and that is precisely the opportunity for cloud-based agent products. The gap between Chinese and US models is about 6–12 months, mainly in coding capability (Opus 4.6), and right now 6 months is the line between life and death.

The Live Demo (the most information-dense segment)

The host wanted a new website for his own writing (he previously used WordPress). Guo Yu used Claude Cowork + Typeless voice input, speaking throughout:

  • First he had Claude read his hard drive/personal materials; it did its own web search to fill in his resume (nonfiction writer, representative works, has lived in Shanghai, Seoul and Tokyo — the slogan was automatically written as "From Shanghai to Seoul to Tokyo").
  • He had it load a design skill: it was not installed on the computer, and he said one sentence — "use the UI/UX Pro Max skill to do it" — and Claude searched on its own, installed 7 design-related Skills on its own, and then rebuilt the website following a professional designer's method: first read all the content and layout of the old site, design a design system, and then write the code.
  • The site was ready in two or three minutes, and a Chinese-Japanese-English trilingual version could be made with one click. His comment: "Nothing needs to rely on a search engine anymore — or rather, what relies on a search engine is no longer a human, but it." Platforms like WordPress are in theory no longer necessary (of course, if you want to rebuild with WordPress, it can also rebuild it for you using WordPress).

Order and Mechanism of the Industry Shock

  • SaaS dies first: Enterprise-facing Salesforce and all kinds of HR systems are first in the line of fire, because enterprise users will find that writing their own on the spot fits them better.
  • Programmers lose their jobs first, and it has already begun: Many companies in Silicon Valley have stopped hiring junior programmers altogether — bringing people in is worse than just letting Claude Code write it, with better results, and it also eliminates the biggest friction of all, communicating with people; an agent can run 24 hours, the cost of failure is extremely low, and there is no need to hire or to lay off.
  • Writing and publishing: Knowledge can be taken as needed, and Claude can be any writer, any PhD in the history of philosophy. He himself uses Claude Code for research when writing books — a Google search gives only a few results per screen, while it can search the entire web and write it directly into a draft.
  • Law: On site he had the AI check on the spot — the legal plugin Claude launched in February, which according to Bloomberg reporting triggered a sell-off of about US$285 billion in legal services company stocks. He confirmed that what is affected is not the assistant layer, but entire corporate legal teams. His political-science explanation goes one level deeper: law exists to resolve friction between people, between people and groups, and between groups; if agents share memory and automatically get everything "on the same page," friction largely disappears; law will still exist, but it will lose its meaning in enforcement.
  • The macro-level contradiction: Productivity is exploding, but the distribution principles of the relations of production have not changed — companies that use AI see profits rise while laying people off at the same time; fewer jobs → fewer consumers → deflationary pressure, and the market is pricing in this "very unstable future." On the other side: even if you lose your job, one person subscribing to Claude for US$200 a month might still be able to make something that earns money. "Nobody knows."

Advice for Young People (very counterintuitive)

  • Stop learning to write code: If you have not started yet, there is no need to learn. Learning how to use an agent takes three to six months, which is enough; and after that you will not even need to learn that, because everything will be fully automated.
  • He himself is writing a OneMan AI: last night before bed he set it running on the 5090 PC at home — 1,000 loops overnight, over 6 hours — with the task "design an installation art company in Tokyo": a complete financial plan, website, and 3D installation works, plus writing emails to submit the works to all the galleries and public spaces in Tokyo, all completed.
  • From this he divides software into two categories: hard-coded software (the kind engineers built in the past) and final software — which, through continuous reasoning, turns human thinking into countless possibilities unfolding in parallel; you pick the most efficient branch, or simply do not pick at all and let it run fully automatically. The meaning of starting a business changes accordingly: opening a company in Japan originally required 8 million yen in registered capital and hiring people, and having too many ideas could also scatter your energy and lead to failure; now you tell it your idea, it diverges into hundreds and thousands of possibilities and executes them one by one, with the failures exiting according to plan and the successes continuing to operate until profitable. Even the step of "generating ideas" is being automated (auto research).

The Only Brake: Electricity

Why has all of this not already happened universally, right away? Inference is not cheap enough yet, because the United States is short of electricity (by contrast: China is short of chips, not electricity, and has abundant hydropower and wind power). If Nvidia's Rubin architecture delivers as promised, the inference cost of AI data centers in the next year or two could fall to one-tenth of what it is now — at which point that much electricity will no longer be needed, and this wave of shock will land in full. So whether "six months" is extreme depends on the speed at which inference costs fall.
