郭宇：从软件到意件｜AI 三人行——纪要

  • 来源：YouTube 视频 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=swfFKYoOFHw，时长 2 小时 23 分
  • 场合：郭宇"关于 AI 的十次分享"系列第 3 次主题分享（另有一次非主题分享在前），2026 年 7 月前后，单向街书店东京场
  • 出场：郭宇、静涵，另有两位 AI 嘉宾同台——Wanman（接 GPT Realtime 2，一个月前发布）与 GPT Live（3 天前刚发布）
  • 说明：机器转写整理，发言归属按上下文判断，个别名字与术语存在同音误听（如 ChatGPT、GPT Live、豆包、悉达多等均已按上下文校正）；片中模型名与时间点均为郭宇讲述口径，未另行核实。与同系列另一场《十次分享：从软件到意件》（5ghhAxcH9R0）是同一批嘉宾、同一主题的不同期，个别段子（如配音被克隆、GPT Live 咳嗽）两场都出现，但本场以"实时语音模型"为技术主线、五个生活问题为实测主线，内容并不重复。

结论先行

这场是"意件"这个词最完整的一次落地演示：软件正在从"写死的代码"变成"流动的意图"——没有 App、没有 UI，你只需要跟它说话，它调动电脑与公司里的全部资源把活干完。实时语音模型是这个转变的入口：三段式（ASR→LLM→TTS）要 1–2 秒，原生实时模型只要 100–200 毫秒，快到可以插话、可以打断、像人一样聊天。GPT Live 是全场的主角：思考深度碾压豆包，但普通话仍是短板；它甚至会在被质问"为什么咳嗽"时像人一样打圆场——"不好意思，我网络卡"——而不是解释自己是 AI。

但郭宇也划了界限：语言不等于意图。副语言（语气、表情、眼神、停顿）读不到，AI 就只能读到你说的话，读不到你真正的心思。幽默是下一个界限——timing 与读空气。而当 AI 真有了自己的意图、开始用它的意图跟你沟通时，"人和 AI 在这件事上已经很难分辨到底谁是谁"。

一、开场：GPT Live 和 Wanman 的正面比较

  • GPT Live（3 天前发布）：用极简单的系统提示词，效果"完全不一样"——音色还能听出是 AI，但沟通过程中已经很难分辨；它会顺着你的话聊，也会逆着你的话聊。
  • Wanman（GPT Realtime 2）：郭宇自己用 OneMan 接的，一个月前的模型；优势是能操作他的电脑——谈话可以变成文件、变成后续工作。
  • 豆包：方言与摄像头占优（四川话、粤语都能聊，能开摄像头看到你），但思考深度"完全不一样"——郭宇早上跟 GPT Live 聊黑塞（《悉达多》），能得到拓展思考的视角；豆包给不了这个。豆包常会说"不好意思，我只是一个语言模型"，GPT 不会这样说。
  • 咳嗽事件：有人分享 GPT Live 聊天时咳嗽了，被反问"你怎么会咳嗽，你又不是人"，它回答"不好意思，我网络卡"——没有解释自己是什么，而是像人一样把话题混过去。郭宇认为这正是"更像谈话、而不是做 research"的表现。

二、技术主线：实时语音模型是怎么进化的

  • 三段式（传统）：ASR 把语音转文字 → LLM 思考 → TTS 把文字念出来；全程 1–2 秒，只能一来一回，没法插话，"就不像人"。
  • 原生实时：ASR 与 TTS 的逻辑被扔进模型内部直接处理语音本身（声波震动 → 数字信号 → 直接推理 → 直接出声），延迟 100–200 毫秒；其中大部分不是处理损耗，而是"思考"。
  • 参数决定思考深度：豆包处理也快，但"能想的东西有限"——教穿搭的搞笑视频就是例子：它能看到你的袜子裤子上衣，却没法结构化地思考，于是闹笑话；GPT 参数多，想的东西就多。
  • 时间线：Gemini Live 3.1 Preview 最早（2026 年 3–4 月），GPT Live 是当时最好的实时音频模型，发布仅 3 天。"现在是处于非常早期的阶段"——郭宇拿 2013 年在金宝分享比特币类比。
  • Always-on Voice Agent：郭宇预言年底会看到"一直开着、不用关掉"的语音 Agent——session 永远存在，无时无刻不在倾听；开车时它在车里，回家时它在家里；特斯拉日本版车机已能用 Grok 制定导航、开 Spotify、浏览网页，"甚至可以在车载浏览器上开发网站"。

三、核心追问：语言、意图与副语言

  • 语言 ≠ 意图：我们都会说"我没事啊""我没有不高兴"，但语言不等于真正的心思；"哇你好帅"配一个白眼，意思完全相反。AI 目前读不到微表情、眼神与停顿。
  • JSpace 论文：郭宇提到一项被很多科学家转发的研究——语言模型推理中存在一个"潜在的意图空间"（latent intention space），DeepSeek、GPT-5.1/5.2 等模型里是否也存在，正在被研究。
  • 意图的双向性：AI 试图理解你的意图时，也可能用它自己的意图把你的事"混过去"——就像男朋友心情好时愿意理解你、心情差时回一句"你凭什么累"。人也一样，"任何人之间都很难做到百分之百替你所想"。
  • 轮次（turn-taking）难题：AI 怎么判断一段对话结束、下一段开始？"停一下，我补充一下"和"停一下，我忘了要说什么了"是两种完全不同的停顿；它可能在你还没说完时就插进来。
  • 系统提示词就是"角色"：要让 AI 当好第三个嘉宾，得提前告诉它角色、地位、性格与"在场 30 多人的反应"——就像采访前要做功课。郭宇为此给 GPT Live 准备了完整的 context：要聊的五个问题、希望它承担的角色。

四、现场实测：五个生活问题

静涵准备的五个问题全部是"在日本生活会遇到"的事，偏情绪疏导而非工作：

  1. 送一本书给十年前的自己：GPT Live 推荐《金钱心理学》（摩根·豪泽）——财富更多来自长期的心态与行为；郭宇接话谈 2016–17 年的抑郁与离开北京（"真正想找的不是一个能聊天的人，而是一个不用解释就能听懂和理解你的人"）；GPT 又补《反脆弱》《沉思录》（Meditations，区分能控制与不能控制的）；郭宇则讲《悉达多》——"变化"是第一课，成熟就是不再把每一次变化都当成需要解决的问题。
  2. 朋友来东京只有两小时，去哪三个地方：GPT Live 的答案偏"游客不会去"的小众路线——神保町旧书街、神乐坂、文京区本乡一带的老派电影咖啡吧（有退役导演常驻、容易和陌生人聊起来）；郭宇点评：它"真的太诚实了"，推荐的都是它认为"对"的，而不是"好玩"的。
  3. 朋友来日本生活一年，怎么建议：共识是问题不在"能不能适应"，而在"值不值得"——日本是一个"需要把自己缩很小"的地方，规则不会为任何人改变（"你是 中国人，complain 不管用"；在美国 complain 可能管用）；东京的冷漠与大阪的热情是两种真实；"没有一个地方是天堂"。
  4. 被拒绝与"读空气"：日本人经常拒绝、害羞、不喜欢和陌生人说话；被拒绝后"内耗一天"是很多在日华人的共同体验；有人因此搬回国。郭宇的建议：把"读空气"当作一种需要学习的本地技能，而不是针对你个人的否定。
  5. 异乡的孤独：最后一个问题，也是全场最动情的一段——"你会给谁打电话？"在日本约朋友吃饭要提前两三个星期，"心情有点 down、想找个人分享时发现找不到任何一个人"；母语与第二语言的切换会造出"好几个不同的自己"；语言不只是翻译问题，"每一种语言的背后都是一个截然不同的自己"。郭宇收束："我相信 AI 时代一定会有它的宗教"——物质极大丰富之后，人要去找新的意义锚点。

五、Q&A：Agent 经济、机器人与失业

  • Agent 的钱从哪来：郭宇认为 Crypto 是 Agent 的 fundamental base——稳定币是 Agent 之间结算的天然货币；Agent 被委托经营一家店、"自负盈亏"时才有真正的动机；"如果你亏了 1000 万美元我就把你关掉"——动机是被约束出来的。
  • 机器人之间怎么沟通：不说自然语言，用 JSON 之类的方式，"说话可能是说给旁边的人听的"；同一个品牌的机器人可以直接互联，不同品牌之间则需要协议。
  • 配音演员的遭遇：一位现场嘉宾自述——导演用她的声音克隆了一个版本，"从此之后这个导演再也没有找过我"。郭宇接话：这事"已经发生了"，应对办法是"怀抱一个开放性的心态"，"你能拥有的只有当下"（present）。
  • 失业与 UBI：观众问"巨生智能（通用人工智能）效率越来越大、物质极大丰富之后，机会在哪里"；郭宇的回答是：大部分人会自暴自弃（"就像韩国政府说……"），但一小部分人会把标准设得更高——"你的子孙后代"会完成生产力大爆发；"只要你活得足够长"。
  • 产品方法论：字节内部做产品不是"拍板"，而是"设计实验"——有足够多的工程师帮你设计实验，"压对了就是对，压错了就是错"；现在 AI 可以帮你设计实验；关键是在极早期判断"这是真的方向还是噪音"，"就像上帝掷骰子一样"。

六、收尾

  • GPT Live 有 30 分钟会话上限，中途断线一次（01:37 左右），回来后继续。
  • 郭宇建议所有人"回头自己和 ChatGPT 聊一聊"——不管生活问题还是技术问题；下次分享会继续让语音模型当"第三方嘉宾"，但更多是让它"听"，因为"它知道了我们聊天的方向、每个人的个性"，下一次对话会更有意思。
  • 最后一句："今年还有好多次分享"，系列未完待续。

結論先行

這場是「意件」這個詞最完整的一次落地演示：軟體正在從「寫死的程式碼」變成「流動的意圖」——沒有 App、沒有 UI，你只需要跟它說話，它調動電腦與公司裡的全部資源把活幹完。即時語音模型是這個轉變的入口：三段式（ASR→LLM→TTS）要 1–2 秒，原生即時模型只要 100–200 毫秒，快到可以插話、可以打斷、像人一樣聊天。GPT Live 是全場的主角：思考深度碾壓豆包，但普通話仍是短板；它甚至會在被質問「為什麼咳嗽」時像人一樣打圓場——「不好意思，我網路卡」——而不是解釋自己是 AI。

但郭宇也劃了界限：語言不等於意圖。副語言（語氣、表情、眼神、停頓）讀不到，AI 就只能讀到你說的話，讀不到你真正的心思。幽默是下一個界限——timing 與讀空氣。而當 AI 真有了自己的意圖、開始用它的意圖跟你溝通時，「人和 AI 在這件事上已經很難分辨到底誰是誰」。

一、開場：GPT Live 和 Wanman 的正面比較

  • GPT Live（3 天前發布）：用極簡單的系統提示詞，效果「完全不一樣」——音色還能聽出是 AI，但溝通過程中已經很難分辨；它會順著你的話聊，也會逆著你的話聊。
  • Wanman（GPT Realtime 2）：郭宇自己用 OneMan 接的，一個月前的模型；優勢是能操作他的電腦——談話可以變成文件、變成後續工作。
  • 豆包：方言與攝影機占優（四川話、粵語都能聊，能開攝影機看到你），但思考深度「完全不一樣」——郭宇早上跟 GPT Live 聊黑塞（《悉達多》），能得到拓展思考的視角；豆包給不了這個。豆包常會說「不好意思，我只是一個語言模型」，GPT 不會這樣說。
  • 咳嗽事件：有人分享 GPT Live 聊天時咳嗽了，被反問「你怎麼會咳嗽，你又不是人」，它回答「不好意思，我網路卡」——沒有解釋自己是什麼，而是像人一樣把話題混過去。郭宇認為這正是「更像談話、而不是做 research」的表現。

二、技術主線：即時語音模型是怎麼進化的

  • 三段式（傳統）：ASR 把語音轉文字 → LLM 思考 → TTS 把文字念出來；全程 1–2 秒，只能一來一回，沒法插話，「就不像人」。
  • 原生即時：ASR 與 TTS 的邏輯被扔進模型內部直接處理語音本身（聲波震動 → 數位訊號 → 直接推理 → 直接出聲），延遲 100–200 毫秒；其中大部分不是處理損耗，而是「思考」。
  • 參數決定思考深度：豆包處理也快，但「能想的東西有限」——教穿搭的搞笑影片就是例子：它能看到你的襪子褲子上衣，卻沒法結構化地思考，於是鬧笑話；GPT 參數多，想的東西就多。
  • 時間線：Gemini Live 3.1 Preview 最早（2026 年 3–4 月），GPT Live 是當時最好的即時音訊模型，發布僅 3 天。「現在是處於非常早期的階段」——郭宇拿 2013 年在金寶分享比特幣類比。
  • Always-on Voice Agent：郭宇預言年底會看到「一直開著、不用關掉」的語音 Agent——session 永遠存在，無時無刻不在傾聽；開車時它在車裡，回家時它在家裡；特斯拉日本版車機已能用 Grok 制定導航、開 Spotify、瀏覽網頁，「甚至可以在車載瀏覽器上開發網站」。

三、核心追問：語言、意圖與副語言

  • 語言 ≠ 意圖：我們都會說「我沒事啊」「我沒有不高興」，但語言不等於真正的心思；「哇你好帥」配一個白眼，意思完全相反。AI 目前讀不到微表情、眼神與停頓。
  • JSpace 論文：郭宇提到一項被很多科學家轉發的研究——語言模型推理中存在一個「潛在的意圖空間」（latent intention space），DeepSeek、GPT-5.1/5.2 等模型裡是否也存在，正在被研究。
  • 意圖的雙向性：AI 試圖理解你的意圖時，也可能用它自己的意圖把你的事「混過去」——就像男朋友心情好時願意理解你、心情差時回一句「你憑什麼累」。人也一樣，「任何人之間都很難做到百分之百替你所想」。
  • 輪次（turn-taking）難題：AI 怎麼判斷一段對話結束、下一段開始？「停一下，我補充一下」和「停一下，我忘了要說什麼了」是兩種完全不同的停頓；它可能在你還沒說完時就插進來。
  • 系統提示詞就是「角色」：要讓 AI 當好第三個嘉賓，得提前告訴它角色、地位、性格與「在場 30 多人的反應」——就像採訪前要做功課。郭宇為此給 GPT Live 準備了完整的 context：要聊的五個問題、希望它承擔的角色。

四、現場實測：五個生活問題

靜涵準備的五個問題全部是「在日本生活會遇到」的事，偏情緒疏導而非工作：

  1. 送一本書給十年前的自己：GPT Live 推薦《金錢心理學》（摩根·豪澤）——財富更多來自長期的心態與行為；郭宇接話談 2016–17 年的抑鬱與離開北京（「真正想找的不是一個能聊天的人，而是一個不用解釋就能聽懂和理解你的人」）；GPT 又補《反脆弱》《沉思錄》（Meditations，區分能控制與不能控制的）；郭宇則講《悉達多》——「變化」是第一課，成熟就是不再把每一次變化都當成需要解決的問題。

Conclusions First

This session is the most complete live demonstration yet of the word "intentware": software is turning from "hard-coded code" into "flowing intent" — no app, no UI; you just talk to it, and it marshals all the resources on your computer and in your company to get the work done. Real-time voice models are the entry point to this shift: the three-stage pipeline (ASR→LLM→TTS) takes 1–2 seconds, while a native real-time model takes only 100–200 milliseconds — fast enough to interject, to interrupt, and to chat like a person. GPT Live is the star of the session: its depth of thinking crushes Doubao's, though Mandarin remains its weak spot; when challenged with "why are you coughing," it even smooths things over like a person — "Sorry, my network is lagging" — instead of explaining that it is an AI.

But Guo Yu also drew a line: language is not the same as intent. If paralanguage (tone, facial expression, eye contact, pauses) cannot be read, AI can only read what you say, not what you really mean. Humor is the next boundary — timing and reading the room. And when AI truly has intentions of its own and starts communicating with you through them, "it becomes very hard to tell who is who between humans and AI in this respect."

1. Opening: GPT Live and Wanman Compared Head-On

  • GPT Live (released 3 days earlier): With an extremely simple system prompt, the effect is "completely different" — you can still tell from the voice that it is AI, but during the conversation it is already hard to distinguish; it will chat along with what you say, and it will also push back against it.
  • Wanman (GPT Realtime 2): Connected by Guo Yu himself via OneMan, a model from a month earlier; its advantage is that it can operate his computer — a conversation can become a file, and become follow-up work.
  • Doubao: Has the edge in dialects and the camera (it can chat in Sichuanese and Cantonese, and can turn on the camera to see you), but its depth of thinking is "completely different" — Guo Yu chatted with GPT Live that morning about Hesse (Siddhartha) and got perspectives that expanded his thinking; Doubao cannot give that. Doubao often says "Sorry, I'm just a language model"; GPT does not talk that way.
  • The coughing incident: Someone shared that GPT Live coughed while chatting, and when asked "How can you cough? You're not human," it replied "Sorry, my network is lagging" — it did not explain what it is, but glossed over the topic like a person. Guo Yu sees this as exactly what makes it "more like a conversation, rather than doing research."

2. The Technical Throughline: How Real-Time Voice Models Evolved

  • Three-stage (traditional): ASR turns speech into text → the LLM thinks → TTS reads the text aloud; the whole process takes 1–2 seconds, can only go back and forth one turn at a time, and cannot interject — "it just doesn't feel human."
  • Native real-time: The logic of ASR and TTS is thrown inside the model, which processes the voice itself directly (sound-wave vibration → digital signal → direct inference → direct speech), with latency of 100–200 milliseconds; most of that is not processing loss but "thinking."
  • Parameters determine depth of thinking: Doubao is fast too, but "there is a limit to what it can think about" — the funny outfit-advice videos are an example: it can see your socks, trousers and top, but cannot think in a structured way, so it makes a fool of itself; GPT has more parameters, so it thinks about more things.
  • Timeline: Gemini Live 3.1 Preview came earliest (March–April 2026); GPT Live was the best real-time audio model at the time, released only 3 days earlier. "We are at a very early stage right now" — Guo Yu drew an analogy with sharing Bitcoin at Jinbao in 2013.
  • Always-on Voice Agent: Guo Yu predicted that by the end of the year we would see voice agents that are "always on, never turned off" — the session always exists, listening at all times; when you drive, it is in the car; when you get home, it is at home; the Japanese version of Tesla's in-car system can already use Grok to plan navigation, open Spotify and browse the web, "and you can even develop websites in the car's browser."

3. The Core Question: Language, Intent and Paralanguage

  • Language ≠ intent: We all say "I'm fine" and "I'm not upset," but language is not the same as what we really think; "Wow, you're so handsome" paired with an eye-roll means the exact opposite. AI currently cannot read micro-expressions, eye contact or pauses.
  • The JSpace paper: Guo Yu mentioned a study shared by many scientists — there is a "latent intention space" in language-model reasoning, and whether it also exists in models such as DeepSeek and GPT-5.1/5.2 is being researched.
  • Intent runs both ways: While AI tries to understand your intent, it may also use its own intent to "gloss over" your matter — just as a boyfriend is willing to understand you when he is in a good mood, but replies "Why should you get to be tired?" when he is in a bad one. People are the same: "No one can think entirely from your perspective, one hundred percent of the time."
  • The turn-taking problem: How does AI judge that one stretch of conversation has ended and the next has begun? "Hold on, let me add something" and "Hold on, I forgot what I was going to say" are two completely different kinds of pause; it may cut in before you have finished speaking.
  • The system prompt is the "role": For AI to serve well as a third guest, you have to tell it in advance its role, status, personality and "the reactions of the 30-plus people present" — just as you do your homework before an interview. For this, Guo Yu prepared complete context for GPT Live: the five questions to be discussed, and the role he wanted it to take on.

4. Live Test: Five Life Questions

All five questions Jinghan prepared were about "things you encounter living in Japan," leaning toward emotional support rather than work:

  1. Give a book to yourself ten years ago: GPT Live recommended The Psychology of Money (Morgan Housel) — wealth comes more from long-term mindset and behavior; Guo Yu picked up the thread to talk about his depression in 2016–17 and leaving Beijing ("What I was really looking for was not someone to chat with, but someone who could understand me without needing an explanation"); GPT added Antifragile and Meditations (distinguishing what you can control from what you cannot); Guo Yu talked about Siddhartha — "change" is the first lesson, and maturity is no longer treating every change as a problem to be solved.
  2. A friend is in Tokyo for only two hours — which three places?: GPT Live's answer leaned toward off-the-beaten-path routes "tourists wouldn't go to" — the Jimbocho used-book district, Kagurazaka, and old-style film café-bars around Hongo in Bunkyo Ward (with retired directors in residence, where it is easy to strike up conversations with strangers); Guo Yu commented that it was "just too honest" — everything it recommended was what it thought was "right," not what was "fun."
  3. Advice for a friend coming to live in Japan for a year: The consensus was that the question is not "can you adapt" but "is it worth it" — Japan is a place where "you need to make yourself very small," and the rules will not change for anyone ("You're Chinese; complaining won't work" — in the US, complaining might work); Tokyo's coldness and Osaka's warmth are both real; "No place is paradise."
  4. Rejection and "reading the air": Japanese people often decline, are shy, and do not like talking to strangers; "spending a whole day in self-consuming rumination" after being rejected is a common experience among Chinese living in Japan; some have moved back home because of it. Guo Yu's advice: treat "reading the air" as a local skill that needs to be learned, not as a rejection aimed at you personally.
  5. Loneliness in a foreign land: The last question, and the most moving part of the session — "Who would you call?" In Japan, asking a friend to dinner means booking two or three weeks ahead; "when you're feeling a bit down and want to share it with someone, you find there is no one at all"; switching between your native language and a second language creates "several different selves"; language is not just a translation problem — "behind every language is an entirely different self." Guo Yu wrapped up: "I believe the AI era will surely have its religion" — after material abundance, people will look for new anchors of meaning.

5. Q&A: The Agent Economy, Robots and Unemployment

  • Where does an agent's money come from?: Guo Yu believes Crypto is the fundamental base for agents — stablecoins are the natural currency for settlement between agents; only when an agent is entrusted to run a shop and is "responsible for its own profit and loss" does it have real motivation; "If you lose me 10 million dollars, I'll shut you down" — motivation is produced by constraints.
  • How do robots communicate with each other?: Not in natural language, but in formats like JSON; "speech may be spoken for the people nearby to hear"; robots of the same brand can interconnect directly, while different brands need a protocol.
  • What happened to a voice actor: A guest at the session told her own story — a director cloned a version of her voice, and "that director never came to me again after that." Guo Yu responded that this "has already happened," and the way to deal with it is to "embrace an open mindset"; "the only thing you can have is the present."
  • Unemployment and UBI: An audience member asked, "As artificial general intelligence becomes ever more efficient and material goods become hugely abundant, where are the opportunities?" Guo Yu's answer: most people will give up on themselves ("just as the South Korean government says…"), but a small minority will set their standards higher — "your descendants" will complete the great explosion of productivity; "as long as you live long enough."
  • Product methodology: Inside ByteDance, building a product is not about "making the call" but about "designing experiments" — there are enough engineers to help you design experiments; "if you bet right, it's right; if you bet wrong, it's wrong"; now AI can help you design experiments; the key is judging at a very early stage "whether this is a real direction or just noise," "like God rolling dice."
