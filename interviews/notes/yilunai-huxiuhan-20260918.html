会议纪要：易论AI访谈胡修涵《被 AI 淘汰的人，才是 AI 最大的市场》

  • 时间：2026-09-18（YouTube 发布）
  • 主持：易论AI
  • 嘉宾：胡修涵（捏Ta 创始人；北大 → 阿里 P8 → 哥伦比亚大学机器人实验室 → Meta）
  • 时长：2 小时 03 分（YouTube 版 7403 秒）
  • 来源：YouTube 官方频道「易论AI」视频 D03SRcFtm7w，自带英文字幕（栏目提供），全文 3034 行

本纪要为中文整理。数字与判断均为嘉宾现场口径，未独立核实。时间戳指向字幕位置。

一句话

胡修涵的核心判断：指望 AI 像工业革命那样「消灭旧岗位、再造新岗位」是低估了 AI——按现有经济价值算，大批人会成为「无用之人」；但人的长期劳动价值不在生产端而在消费端：消费形成审美、审美最终变现，创作即消费。被 AI 淘汰的人不是包袱，而是下一个时代最大的市场；捏Ta 做的就是让他们在「第二世界」里塑造另一种人生。

核心要点

  1. 「无用之人」要拆开看：按现有分工的经济价值，他们确实无用（做的事不经济）；但长期劳动价值不为零——时尚买手就是例子：纯功利看完全多余，可消费投票赋予审美以意义，审美最终能变现，青山资本称之为「消费是新时代的生产」（[00:04:25]、[00:06:11]）。
  2. 新岗位≠好岗位：AI 短剧制作这类新职业更像被算法精确控制的流水线——生产资料与分发主动权全在系统手里，他称之为「佃农阶级」，和外卖骑手同构（[00:03:09]、[00:04:02]）。
  3. 创作即消费：看视频时发的一条弹幕，若成为世界模型的输入，消费行为就转化成了创作行为。未来人真正愿意花时间的，是创作过程本身（[00:00:26]）。
  4. 平台网络结构论：抖音、Facebook、Instagram 本质是双边结构（二部图：少数创作者辐射多数消费者），长期必然压制 UGC 利益；健康的社区是多边网状结构。扎克伯格早年在 Facebook 内部最担心的就是这个，提「有意义的社交互动」正源于此（[00:10:07]、[00:11:51]）。
  5. 代际反叛才致命：双边生态的问题一代人之内不致命，可怕的是代际更替时下一代创作者问「我为什么要看你们这帮精英老头」——生态的死法是新人不再进来（[00:12:48]）。
  6. 社区的定义是连接边的数量与深度：点赞最浅，评论转发次之，用别人的东西再创作更深，共同经历（如一起看直播）最深；只有发布、没有彼此交换，就不构成社区（[00:54:55]）。
  7. 冷启动纪律：绝不在早期花钱从别的平台挖成熟创作者——外部创作者占比一旦过高（他举 70% 为戒），自家原生创作者会被歧视、社区自身价值归零；要像入世前的保护期一样，先让原生创作者强到能出去竞争，再开闸（[01:01:39]、[01:03:19]）。
  8. Aha 时刻的物理约束：移动互联网的金标准是新用户 15 秒见到第一个惊喜，但 AI 生图要 40–50 秒——所以捏Ta 的 aha 只能存在于「生成过程之中」，这是这代 AI 产品对普通用户的根本限制（[01:05:53]、[01:07:03]）。
  9. 反对一句话生成（AIGC），主张 AIUGC：创作的本质是多轮校准，一句话生成假设 AI 能直接猜中你脑中所想，那是脑机接口才有的语境；降低操作门槛不等于降低参与门槛，用户一旦觉得自己只是 AI 的橡皮图章，就会离开（[01:19:30]、[01:24:31]）。
  10. 今年最大的信号与噪音：信号是「全民 Claude Code」——组织内部不用起 agent 这一波，会被甩下；噪音是「只谈现金流」——套壳 harness 做分发是最容易变现的路，但不是这个时代唯一能做的事（[01:50:32]）。

一、为什么他不信「工业革命叙事」

  • 主持人问：AI 淘汰的人会像工业革命后那样重新找到工作吗？胡修涵：这种期待本身就是低估 AI，「好比指望三体人善待地球人」。脑力劳动的效率提升太强、太全面，即使创造新岗位，也装不下被挤出的人（[00:00:12]、[00:02:42]）。
  • 出路在消费端完成闭环：消费 → 形成审美 → 审美被他人认可 → 变现。人在消费过程中的反馈、弹幕、投票，会成为对他人有价值的劳动，生产与消费的边界就此打破（[00:07:19]）。
  • 他自称的立场不是「反精英」：精英是客观存在，他信的是「连接」——连接精英与普通人，别走向生殖隔离式的分化（[00:09:35]）。

二、他这个人：三次压线过关

  • 北大：信息学竞赛关键一场，满分 320 他考 310（一道题 10 分之差）；当年 NOI 委员会定「宁缺毋滥」，50 人名额只取 49 人，后改为并列、一次拉进 10 人，他就这么被拉了进去（[00:16:15]）。
  • Meta：同批同学里最晚拿到实习 offer 的人（4 月才给，他主动要求面试官加了一轮）；实习结束却成了那届的 rock star，return offer 是同批最好的（约他人的 1.5–2 倍）（[00:17:55]）。
  • 他的解释：压线过关的人会因为珍惜机会或「知耻后勇」而超常发挥；人生常态不是明星前锋队，而是没有球星但后防扎实的队最后赢。投资人问他算不算幸运的人，他说：不相信自己幸运，就会变成不幸运的那个（[00:15:57]）。
  • 北大期间：成绩约前 30%，不焦虑；创办「网络社团」传播 Ruby on Rails（那代人的偶像是 DHH），给全校新生做了个 QQ 游戏大厅式的选宿舍工具，被团委老师相中。教训是技术太新没人接手维护——老师说要用 PHP 重写才能传给下一届（[00:20:04]、[00:23:26]）。
  • 哥伦比亚读机器人：毕业论文是「机器人熨衣服」——可变形物体至今仍是灵巧手的演示难题，当年手上几乎没有传感器，只能纯靠视觉（[00:28:14]）。

三、2022 年 7 月为什么出来做捏Ta

  • 触发点是 Disco Diffusion（2022 年 2 月，Colab 上跑个脚本、五分钟出一张艺术图）与 Stable Diffusion，ChatGPT 还没发布（11 月）。他当时在 Tezign（特赞）做 VP，回国已降薪 30%，创业再降 30%，职业生涯最高薪是工作第三年（[00:32:15]、[00:42:47]）。
  • 内容理论：一切内容都是积木，生产是「解构—重构」（即压缩—解压）。过去组织图片靠图层排版，扩散模型第一次让「用语言组织视觉」成为可能，重组速度提升两个数量级以上——改一句 prompt、立刻看市场反馈。他在特赞研究 Adobe 时把这件事抽象为「内容速度」：谁能让信息在人之间的时间速度更快，谁就是更好的内容组织（[00:36:40]、[00:41:15]）。
  • 为什么不等财务自由：他给人生排第一位的指标是「生命体验的丰富度」；沿着大厂路走下去，像在虚拟游戏里拿了高排名，代价是放弃大量真实世界的可能性（[00:44:29]）。

四、放弃的那条路：LoRA / ComfyUI 平台

  • 2023 年 4 月他们训了第一个广泛传播的 LoRA（黑神话悟空题材），AutoDL 上最常用的 ComfyUI 镜像与启动器就是他团队做的，一度站在成为国内 ComfyUI/LoRA 分享平台的位置上（[00:47:27]）。
  • 放弃的理由是第一性原理：把语言和一大堆小微调模型、工作流绑死，资产会重新变重，与这一代技术「解放、轻量化」的方向相反；他赌的是这些东西最终会被世界模型式的系统收编，而不是每个角色单独训一个模型（[00:48:40]）。
  • 顺带谈创始人注意力：资源多不是好事，资源多的团队容易变成「基金孵化器」——创始人怕单点出事，自然想多押四五个方向；单点创新要求创始人 80–90% 精力压在一处，用 20% 精力不可能赢一个同样全力以赴的对手（[00:45:54]）。
  • 对同行（Liblib、海艺、MaFei 等）的观察：活下来的都不是纯工具、都加了社区；差异在基因——商业化驱动的团队敢补贴、敢投流、长得快，他们是系统型团队，先问「积木问题解没解好」，更守纪律（[00:51:09]）。

五、捏Ta 的产品演化：从漫画到角色到世界

  • 第一版是最复杂的设计：一键生成漫画的小程序。做不成——技术上故事补全太难（四格漫画对故事的要求不低于编一个成语典故），语言模型当时也没有拆解结构化内容的 agent 能力（[01:08:43]）。
  • 转向「塑造角色」：门槛低得多，且角色之间天然长出关系（兄弟、姐妹、CP、正邪），两个朋友各捏一个角色就能玩「过家家」，社交连接自然形成。第二波尝试把角色放进现成小说剧情（冒险模式）曾被竞品模仿，但多模态延迟太大，用户开始抱怨「能不能别生图了、太费钱费时」，纯文本又和星野、猫箱没区别（[00:57:59]、[01:10:50]）。
  • 「庇护所效应」：捏Ta 的原住民是原创角色（OC）圈层——一批在别的平台被排斥的画手（有人头像上直接写 Anti-AI），越被反对、暗地里支持的人越多，他们无处可去，捏Ta 给了土壤。他刻意不迎合「反 AI 就不给 AI 功能」的捷径，而是先给这群人一个能交流的地方（[00:59:34]）。
  • 工具与社区的反馈差：工具只收到浅反馈（「图再好看点、字再大点」），那不是真实需求；真实需求只在社区讨论里冒出来——做婚礼请柬的人真正在意的是仪式感和宾客出席意愿，没人会跟一个工具聊这个（[00:53:30]）。
  • 强互动创作聚合不了 feed：要求用户做同款、二创，行为就按主题聚成社团，而不是信息流——像他大学时的社团（[00:57:29]）。
  • 成本账：DeepSeek V2（2024）把同级文本价格打下来数百倍（现场口径），但视频至今没便宜下来，因为全行业追 SOTA、不追普惠。他的临界点预测已接近：5–15 秒视频做到几分钱人民币、Seedance/海螺 H3 级质量，海螺新一代 H3 已经逼近这个点（[01:14:52]、[01:17:39]）。捏Ta 的工程优化不追生成质量本身，追的是协作网络——用户把自己的设定、角色打包成「元素」供他人调用，平台做资源型与交换型两种供给（[01:18:31]）。

六、第二世界：语境、四象限与可塑软件

  • 捏Ta 现在的定义：一个让用户塑造「另一种人生」的社区。年轻用户自己最常用的词是「世界」——「我和朋友的小世界」。数字原住民的手机里所有 App 就是他的世界（[01:26:44]、[01:27:42]）。
  • 世界的构成是语境（context）：环境、化身（avatar/角色）、关系三样。先把语境做厚、可治理，后面的体验才能反复做好——像空白记忆的 agent 进入第一个游戏就有了第一份记忆（[01:31:43]、[01:33:19]）。
  • Fork 机制：世界观必然分化，如宗教分教派、开源项目分叉，分化与汇流都是做大的方式；他纠正主持人「先做角色」的直觉——深度之后，场域比角色更重要，迪士尼乐园的本质不是 IP 周边，而是人结伴而来、以居民身份改变点什么（[01:34:15]、[01:30:17]）。
  • 内容四象限框架：横轴人 ↔ 世界，纵轴被动观看 ↔ 主动参与；长期所有内容向「世界 × 主动参与」迁移。AI 短剧是「看一个新世界」，AI 陪伴是「单角色互动」，都只是分区解（[01:47:58]、[01:50:21]）。
  • 可塑软件的自我批评：他们最近重度反思——给这一代 AI 预设单一模态、固定界面，违背技术本身的可塑性（DeepSeek Harness 就是可塑操作系统）；底层是命令行与语言模型（LUI），上层界面应由 AI 按场景现场生成。未来限制参与的不再是形式，而是语境的丰富程度（[01:44:24]、[01:46:47]）。
  • 孤独问题他换了个说法：不是孤独，是「更丰富体验的时间」问题——人活久了会无聊，要么延长时间、要么多开几局（[01:43:33]）。

七、公司光谱与不后悔

  • 公司光谱：最左端是亚马逊式用户驱动（customer obsession、被用户说服），最右端是 Google 式技术愿景驱动（最不听劝，但能招到最好的技术人），Uber、Facebook 体量感依次，Canva、Figma 居中。创业第一天就要想清楚自己落在光谱哪一格，否则会长成一家自己不想成为的公司（[01:53:58]、[01:58:12]）。
  • 主持人问：第一年本可以成为 Liblib，现在选会不会改？他答：人只能做一件事，我们天生不是商业化效率驱动整个团队的那种公司，不后悔。市面上横向比较只看活得好的，没看见两三年里大批速死名单——这很公平：不够好就是不够好，但也要尊重当时的技术条件（[01:56:51]、[01:58:32]）。
  • 为 Manus 说话：他不认为 Manus 是套壳——很多产品创新很强，商业化、投流、增长效率都有其所长，最后跑出来的产品质量不差（[01:52:48]）。
  • 组织信号：今年他们内部也在全员用 Claude Code 式的 agent 工作法；技术审美上他偏爱最新一代技术，但成本又贵到与普惠冲突——「两害相权，必须撕开一道口子」（[01:50:46]）。

八、最后一个问题：第二世界的世界观

  • 主持人问：当人被挤出（第一）世界，你们要造一个什么样的世界观？胡修涵：人不会觉得被挤出，他会主动选择在那个世界里建更有意义的东西——就像人类历史上为信仰众筹建圣家堂：无数人奋斗一生、没有经济回报，最终成了世界上最稀缺的东西，即人的精神本身。虚拟世界里同样可以建这样的「教堂」，它本质是表达「我存在」的纪念碑（[02:01:08]）。
  • 那你会自己建教堂，还是只提供土地？他笑答：还不知道，也许都会——但眼下打地基和施工队的责任已经够重，没时间想（[02:02:56]）。

核心要點

  1. 「無用之人」要拆開看：按現有分工的經濟價值，他們確實無用（做的事不經濟）；但長期勞動價值不為零——時尚買手就是例子：純功利看完全多餘，可消費投票賦予審美以意義，審美最終能變現，青山資本稱之為「消費是新時代的生產」（[00:04:25]、[00:06:11]）。
  2. 新崗位≠好崗位：AI 短劇製作這類新職業更像被演算法精確控制的流水線——生產資料與分發主動權全在系統手裡，他稱之為「佃農階級」，和外送騎手同構（[00:03:09]、[00:04:02]）。
  3. 創作即消費：看影片時發的一條彈幕，若成為世界模型的輸入，消費行為就轉化成了創作行為。未來人真正願意花時間的，是創作過程本身（[00:00:26]）。
  4. 平台網路結構論：抖音、Facebook、Instagram 本質是雙邊結構（二部圖：少數創作者輻射多數消費者），長期必然壓制 UGC 利益；健康的社群是多邊網狀結構。祖克柏早年在 Facebook 內部最擔心的就是這個，提「有意義的社交互動」正源於此（[00:10:07]、[00:11:51]）。
  5. 代際反叛才致命：雙邊生態的問題一代人之內不致命，可怕的是代際更替時下一代創作者問「我為什麼要看你們這幫精英老頭」——生態的死法是新人不再進來（[00:12:48]）。
  6. 社群的定義是連接邊的數量與深度：按讚最淺，留言轉發次之，用別人的東西再創作更深，共同經歷（如一起看直播）最深；只有發布、沒有彼此交換，就不構成社群（[00:54:55]）。
  7. 冷啟動紀律：絕不在早期花錢從別的平台挖成熟創作者——外部創作者占比一旦過高（他舉 70% 為戒），自家原生創作者會被歧視、社群自身價值歸零；要像入世前的保護期一樣，先讓原生創作者強到能出去競爭，再開閘（[01:01:39]、[01:03:19]）。
  8. Aha 時刻的物理約束：行動網際網路的金標準是新用戶 15 秒見到第一個驚喜，但 AI 生圖要 40–50 秒——所以捏Ta 的 aha 只能存在於「生成過程之中」，這是這代 AI 產品對普通用戶的根本限制（[01:05:53]、[01:07:03]）。
  9. 反對一句話生成（AIGC），主張 AIUGC：創作的本質是多輪校準，一句話生成假設 AI 能直接猜中你腦中所想，那是腦機介面才有的語境；降低操作門檻不等於降低參與門檻，用戶一旦覺得自己只是 AI 的橡皮圖章，就會離開（[01:19:30]、[01:24:31]）。
  10. 今年最大的訊號與噪音：訊號是「全民 Claude Code」——組織內部不用起 agent 這一波，會被甩下；噪音是「只談現金流」——套殼 harness 做分發是最容易變現的路，但不是這個時代唯一能做的事（[01:50:32]）。

一、為什麼他不信「工業革命敘事」

  • 主持人問：AI 淘汰的人會像工業革命後那樣重新找到工作嗎？胡修涵：這種期待本身就是低估 AI，「好比指望三體人善待地球人」。腦力勞動的效率提升太強、太全面，即使創造新崗位，也裝不下被擠出的人（[00:00:12]、[00:02:42]）。
  • 出路在消費端完成閉環：消費 → 形成審美 → 審美被他人認可 → 變現。人在消費過程中的回饋、彈幕、投票，會成為對他人有價值的勞動，生產與消費的邊界就此打破（[00:07:19]）。
  • 他自稱的立場不是「反精英」：精英是客觀存在，他信的是「連接」——連接精英與普通人，別走向生殖隔離式的分化（[00:09:35]）。

二、他這個人：三次壓線過關

  • 北大：資訊學競賽關鍵一場，滿分 320 他考 310（一道題 10 分之差）；當年 NOI 委員會定「寧缺毋濫」，50 人名額只取 49 人，後改為並列、一次拉進 10 人，他就這麼被拉了進去（[00:16:15]）。
  • Meta：同批同學裡最晚拿到實習 offer 的人（4 月才給，他主動要求面試官加了一輪）；實習結束卻成了那屆的 rock star，return offer 是同批最好的（約他人的 1.5–2 倍）（[00:17:55]）。
  • 他的解釋：壓線過關的人會因為珍惜機會或「知恥後勇」而超常發揮；人生常態不是明星前鋒隊，而是沒有球星但後防扎實的隊最後贏。投資人問他算不算幸運的人，他說：不相信自己幸運，就會變成不幸運的那個（[00:15:57]）。
  • 北大期間：成績約前 30%，不焦慮；創辦「網路社團」傳播 Ruby on Rails（那代人的偶像是 DHH），給全校新生做了個 QQ 遊戲大廳式的選宿舍工具，被團委老師相中。教訓是技術太新沒人接手維護——老師說要用 PHP 重寫才能傳給下一屆（[00:20:04]、[00:23:26]）。
  • 哥倫比亞讀機器人：畢業論文是「機器人熨衣服」——可變形物體至今仍是靈巧手的演示難題，當年手上幾乎沒有感測器，只能純靠視覺（[00:28:14]）。

三、2022 年 7 月為什麼出來做捏Ta

  • 觸發點是 Disco Diffusion（2022 年 2 月，Colab 上跑個腳本、五分鐘出一張藝術圖）與 Stable Diffusion，ChatGPT 還沒發布（11 月）。他當時在 Tezign（特贊）做 VP，回國已降薪 30%，創業再降 30%，職業生涯最高薪是工作第三年（[00:32:15]、[00:42:47]）。
  • 內容理論：一切內容都是積木，生產是「解構—重構」（即壓縮—解壓）。過去組織圖片靠圖層排版，擴散模型第一次讓「用語言組織視覺」成為可能，重組速度提升兩個數量級以上——改一句 prompt、立刻看市場回饋。他在特贊研究 Adobe 時把這件事抽象為「內容速度」：誰能讓資訊在人之間的時間速度更快，誰就是更好的內容組織（[00:36:40]、[00:41:15]）。
  • 為什麼不等財務自由：他給人生排第一位的指標是「生命體驗的豐富度」；沿著大廠路走下去，像在虛擬遊戲裡拿了高排名，代價是放棄大量真實世界的可能性（[00:44:29]）。

四、放棄的那條路：LoRA / ComfyUI 平台

  • 2023 年 4 月他們訓了第一個廣泛傳播的 LoRA（黑神話悟空題材），AutoDL 上最常用的 ComfyUI 映像與啟動器就是他團隊做的，一度站在成為國內 ComfyUI/LoRA 分享平台的位置上（[00:47:27]）。
  • 放棄的理由是第一性原理：把語言和一大堆小微調模型、工作流綁死，資產會重新變重，與這一代技術「解放、輕量化」的方向相反；他賭的是這些東西最終會被世界模型式的系統收編，而不是每個角色單獨訓一個模型（[00:48:40]）。
  • 順帶談創始人注意力：資源多不是好事，資源多的團隊容易變成「基金孵化器」——創始人怕單點出事，自然想多押四五個方向；單點創新要求創始人 80–90% 精力壓在一處，用 20% 精力不可能贏一個同樣全力以赴的對手（[00:45:54]）。
  • 對同行（Liblib、海藝、MaFei 等）的觀察：活下來的都不是純工具、都加了社群；差異在基因——商業化驅動的團隊敢補貼、敢投流、長得快，他們是系統型團隊，先問「積木問題解沒解好」，更守紀律（[00:51:09]）。

五、捏Ta 的產品演化：從漫畫到角色到世界

  • 第一版是最複雜的設計：一鍵生成漫畫的小程式。做不成——技術上故事補全太難（四格漫畫對故事的要求不低於編一個成語典故），語言模型當時也沒有拆解結構化內容的 agent 能力（[01:08:43]）。
  • 轉向「塑造角色」：門檻低得多，且角色之間天然長出關係（兄弟、姐妹、CP、正邪），兩個朋友各捏一個角色就能玩「扮家家酒」，社交連接自然形成。第二波嘗試把角色放進現成小說劇情（冒險模式）曾被競品模仿，但多模態延遲太大，用戶開始抱怨「能不能別生圖了、太費錢費時」，純文字又和星野、貓箱沒區別（[00:57:59]、[01:10:50]）。
  • 「庇護所效應」：捏Ta 的原住民是原創角色（OC）圈層——一批在別的平台被排斥的畫手（有人頭像上直接寫 Anti-AI），越被反對、暗地裡支持的人越多，他們無處可去，捏Ta 給了土壤。他刻意不迎合「反 AI 就不給 AI 功能」的捷徑，而是先給這群人一個能交流的地方（[00:59:34]）。
  • 工具與社群的回饋差：工具只收到淺回饋（「圖再好看點、字再大點」），那不是真實需求；真實需求只在社群討論裡冒出來——做婚禮請柬的人真正在意的是儀式感和賓客出席意願，沒人會跟一個工具聊這個（[00:53:30]）。
  • 強互動創作聚合不了 feed：要求用戶做同款、二創，行為就按主題聚成社團，而不是資訊流——像他大學時的社團（[00:57:29]）。
  • 成本帳：DeepSeek V2（2024）把同級文字價格打下來數百倍（現場口徑），但影片至今沒便宜下來，因為全行業追 SOTA、不追普惠。他的臨界點預測已接近：5–15 秒影片做到幾分錢人民幣、Seedance/海螺 H3 級品質，海螺新一代 H3 已經逼近這個點（[01:14:52]、[01:17:39]）。捏Ta 的工程優化不追生成品質本身，追的是協作網路——用戶把自己的設定、角色打包成「元素」供他人調用，平台做資源型與交換型兩種供給（[01:18:31]）。

六、第二世界：語境、四象限與可塑軟體

  • 捏Ta 現在的定義：一個讓用戶塑造「另一種人生」的社群。年輕用戶自己最常用的詞是「世界」——「我和朋友的小世界」。數位原住民的手機裡所有 App 就是他的世界（[01:26:44]、[01:27:42]）。
  • 世界的構成是語境（context）：環境、化身（avatar/角色）、關係三樣。先把語境做厚、可治理，後面的體驗才能反覆做好——像空白記憶的 agent 進入第一個遊戲就有了第一份記憶（[01:31:43]、[01:33:19]）。
  • Fork 機制：世界觀必然分化，如宗教分教派、開源專案分叉，分化與匯流都是做大的方式；他糾正主持人「先做角色」的直覺——深度之後，場域比角色更重要，迪士尼樂園的本質不是 IP 周邊，而是人結伴而來、以居民身分改變點什麼（[01:34:15]、[01:30:17]）。
  • 內容四象限框架：橫軸人 ↔ 世界，縱軸被動觀看 ↔ 主動參與；長期所有內容向「世界 × 主動參與」遷移。AI 短劇是「看一個新世界」，AI 陪伴是「單角色互動」，都只是分區解（[01:47:58]、[01:50:21]）。
  • 可塑軟體的自我批評：他們最近重度反思——給這一代 AI 預設單一模態、固定介面，違背技術本身的可塑性（DeepSeek Harness 就是可塑作業系統）；底層是命令列與語言模型（LUI），上層介面應由 AI 按場景現場生成。未來限制參與的不再是形式，而是語境的豐富程度（[01:44:24]、[01:46:47]）。
  • 孤獨問題他換了個說法：不是孤獨，是「更豐富體驗的時間」問題——人活久了會無聊，要麼延長時間、要麼多開幾局（[01:43:33]）。

七、公司光譜與不後悔

  • 公司光譜：最左端是亞馬遜式用戶驅動（customer obsession、被用戶說服），最右端是 Google 式技術願景驅動（最不聽勸，但能招到最好的技術人），Uber、Facebook 體量感依次，Canva、Figma 居中。創業第一天就要想清楚自己落在光譜哪一格，否則會長成一家自己不想成為的公司（[01:53:58]、[01:58:12]）。
  • 主持人問：第一年本可以成為 Liblib，現在選會不會改？他答：人只能做一件事，我們天生不是商業化效率驅動整個團隊的那種公司，不後悔。市面上橫向比較只看活得好的，沒看見兩三年裡大批速死名單——這很公平：不夠好就是不夠好，但也要尊重當時的技術條件（[01:56:51]、[01:58:32]）。
  • 為 Manus 說話：他不認為 Manus 是套殼——很多產品創新很強，商業化、投流、增長效率都有其所長，最後跑出來的產品品質不差（[01:52:48]）。
  • 組織訊號：今年他們內部也在全員用 Claude Code 式的 agent 工作法；技術審美上他偏愛最新一代技術，但成本又貴到與普惠衝突——「兩害相權，必須撕開一道口子」（[01:50:46]）。

八、最後一個問題：第二世界的世界觀

  • 主持人問：當人被擠出（第一）世界，你們要造一個什麼樣的世界觀？胡修涵：人不會覺得被擠出，他會主動選擇在那個世界裡建更有意義的東西——就像人類歷史上為信仰眾籌建聖家堂：無數人奮鬥一生、沒有經濟回報，最終成了世界上最稀缺的東西，即人的精神本身。虛擬世界裡同樣可以建這樣的「教堂」，它本質是表達「我存在」的紀念碑（[02:01:08]）。
  • 那你會自己建教堂，還是只提供土地？他笑答：還不知道，也許都會——但眼下打地基和施工隊的責任已經夠重，沒時間想（[02:02:56]）。

Meeting Notes: YiLunAI Interviews Hu Xiuhan — "The People AI Displaces Are AI's Biggest Market"

  • Date: 2026-09-18 (published on YouTube)
  • Host: YiLunAI
  • Guest: Hu Xiuhan (founder of Neta; Peking University → Alibaba P8 → Columbia University robotics lab → Meta)
  • Duration: 2 h 03 min (YouTube version: 7,403 seconds)
  • Source: Video D03SRcFtm7w on the official YouTube channel "YiLunAI," with English subtitles provided by the program; full transcript of 3,034 lines

These notes were compiled in Chinese. All figures and judgments are as stated by the guest on the spot and have not been independently verified. Timestamps point to positions in the subtitles.

In One Sentence

Hu Xiuhan's core judgment: expecting AI to work like the Industrial Revolution — "destroy old jobs, then create new ones" — underestimates AI. Measured by current economic value, large numbers of people will become "useless people"; but the long-term value of human labor lies not on the production side but on the consumption side: consumption forms taste, taste eventually monetizes, and creation is consumption. The people displaced by AI are not a burden — they are the biggest market of the next era; what Neta does is let them shape an alternative life in a "second world."

Key Points

  1. "Useless people" needs unpacking: by the economic value of the current division of labor, they are indeed useless (what they do is uneconomical); but their long-term labor value is not zero — fashion buyers are the example: purely utilitarian, they look completely superfluous, yet consumption votes give taste meaning, and taste can ultimately be monetized. Qingshan Capital calls this "consumption is the production of the new era" ([00:04:25], [00:06:11]).
  2. New jobs ≠ good jobs: new occupations like AI short-drama production are more like assembly lines precisely controlled by algorithms — the means of production and the initiative in distribution are entirely in the system's hands. He calls this the "tenant-farmer class," structurally identical to food-delivery riders ([00:03:09], [00:04:02]).
  3. Creation is consumption: if a bullet comment you post while watching a video becomes input to a world model, the act of consumption has turned into an act of creation. What people will truly want to spend time on in the future is the creative process itself ([00:00:26]).
  4. A theory of platform network structure: Douyin, Facebook and Instagram are essentially two-sided structures (a bipartite graph: a few creators radiating to many consumers), which in the long run inevitably suppress the interests of UGC; a healthy community is a multi-sided mesh structure. This is what Zuckerberg worried about most inside Facebook in the early years, and his call for "meaningful social interactions" came from exactly this ([00:10:07], [00:11:51]).
  5. Generational rebellion is what kills: the problems of a two-sided ecosystem are not fatal within one generation; the scary part is generational turnover, when the next generation of creators asks "why should I watch you elite old-timers?" — an ecosystem dies when newcomers stop coming in ([00:12:48]).
  6. A community is defined by the number and depth of its connecting edges: likes are the shallowest, comments and reposts next, remixing other people's work is deeper, and shared experiences (such as watching a livestream together) are the deepest; if there is only publishing and no exchange between people, it is not a community ([00:54:55]).
  7. Cold-start discipline: never spend money early on poaching mature creators from other platforms — once external creators make up too high a share (he cites 70% as the danger line), your own native creators get discriminated against and the community's own value goes to zero; like a protection period before opening up, first let native creators grow strong enough to compete outside, then open the gates ([01:01:39], [01:03:19]).
  8. The physical constraint on the aha moment: the gold standard of the mobile internet is that a new user sees the first delight within 15 seconds, but AI image generation takes 40–50 seconds — so Neta's aha can only exist "within the generation process itself," which is this generation of AI products' fundamental limitation for ordinary users ([01:05:53], [01:07:03]).
  9. Against one-sentence generation (AIGC); for AIUGC: the essence of creation is multi-round calibration. One-sentence generation assumes AI can directly guess what is in your head — a context only a brain-computer interface could provide. Lowering the operational barrier is not the same as lowering the participation barrier; once users feel they are merely AI's rubber stamp, they leave ([01:19:30], [01:24:31]).
  10. The year's biggest signal and noise: the signal is "Claude Code for everyone" — organizations that don't get on this wave of agents internally will be left behind; the noise is "only talking about cash flow" — wrapping a harness and doing distribution is the easiest path to monetization, but it is not the only thing worth doing in this era ([01:50:32]).

I. Why He Doesn't Believe the "Industrial Revolution Narrative"

  • The host asked: will the people AI displaces find work again, as after the Industrial Revolution? Hu Xiuhan: that expectation itself underestimates AI — "it's like expecting the Trisolarians to treat Earthlings kindly." The efficiency gains in mental labor are too strong and too comprehensive; even if new jobs are created, they cannot hold all the people squeezed out ([00:00:12], [00:02:42]).
  • The way out is to close the loop on the consumption side: consumption → taste is formed → taste is recognized by others → monetization. People's feedback, bullet comments and votes during consumption become labor that is valuable to others, and the boundary between production and consumption dissolves ([00:07:19]).
  • His self-described stance is not "anti-elite": elites objectively exist; what he believes in is "connection" — connecting elites and ordinary people, rather than drifting toward a reproductive-isolation-style split ([00:09:35]).

II. The Man Himself: Three Times Squeaking Through

  • Peking University: in a key informatics competition, he scored 310 out of 320 (a single 10-point problem made the difference); that year the NOI committee insisted on "quality over quantity," taking only 49 of 50 slots, then switched to ties and pulled in 10 more at once — that is how he got in ([00:16:15]).
  • Meta: he was the last in his cohort to receive an internship offer (it only came in April, after he proactively asked the interviewer to add another round); yet by the end of the internship he had become that cohort's rock star, and his return offer was the best of the group (about 1.5–2× the others') ([00:17:55]).
  • His explanation: people who squeak through cherish the opportunity, or rise out of shame, and overperform; the norm in life is not a team of star forwards, but the team with no stars and a solid defense that wins in the end. When an investor asked whether he counts as a lucky person, he said: if you don't believe you are lucky, you become the unlucky one ([00:15:57]).
  • At Peking University: his grades were around the top 30%, and he wasn't anxious; he founded an "online society" to spread Ruby on Rails (that generation's idol was DHH), and built a QQ-game-hall-style dormitory-selection tool for all incoming freshmen, which caught the eye of a Youth League teacher. The lesson was that the technology was too new for anyone to take over maintenance — the teacher said it would have to be rewritten in PHP to be passed to the next class ([00:20:04], [00:23:26]).
  • Robotics at Columbia: his thesis was "a robot ironing clothes" — deformable objects remain a demonstration challenge for dexterous hands to this day; back then he had almost no sensors at hand and had to rely purely on vision ([00:28:14]).

III. Why He Left in July 2022 to Build Neta

  • The trigger was Disco Diffusion (February 2022 — run a script on Colab, get an art image in five minutes) and Stable Diffusion; ChatGPT had not yet been released (November). He was then a VP at Tezign, having already taken a 30% pay cut to return to China; starting the company meant another 30% cut, and the highest salary of his career was in his third year of work ([00:32:15], [00:42:47]).
  • His content theory: all content is building blocks, and production is "deconstruction–reconstruction" (i.e., compression–decompression). Images used to be organized by layer layout; diffusion models for the first time made it possible to "organize vision with language," speeding up recombination by more than two orders of magnitude — change one line of a prompt and immediately see the market's feedback. While studying Adobe at Tezign, he abstracted this into "content velocity": whoever makes information move faster between people is the better organizer of content ([00:36:40], [00:41:15]).
  • Why not wait for financial freedom: the metric he ranks first in life is "richness of lived experience"; continuing down the big-company path is like holding a high rank in a virtual game, at the cost of giving up a great many possibilities in the real world ([00:44:29]).

IV. The Road Not Taken: A LoRA / ComfyUI Platform

  • In April 2023 they trained the first widely circulated LoRA (on a Black Myth: Wukong theme); the most commonly used ComfyUI image and launcher on AutoDL were built by his team, and for a time they stood on the verge of becoming China's ComfyUI/LoRA sharing platform ([00:47:27]).
  • The reason for giving it up was first principles: tying language to a pile of small fine-tuned models and workflows would make assets heavy again, running against this generation of technology's direction of "liberation and lightness"; his bet was that these things would ultimately be absorbed into world-model-style systems, rather than training a separate model for every character ([00:48:40]).
  • On founders' attention, in passing: having more resources is not a good thing — resource-rich teams easily turn into "fund incubators," because founders fear a single-point failure and naturally want to bet on four or five directions; single-point innovation requires the founder to put 80–90% of their energy in one place, and you cannot beat an equally all-in competitor with 20% of your energy ([00:45:54]).
  • His observations on peers (Liblib, Haiyi, MaFei, etc.): the survivors are none of them pure tools — all added a community; the difference is in the genes — commercially driven teams dare to subsidize and buy traffic and grow fast, while his is a systems team that first asks "have we solved the building-block problem properly" and keeps stricter discipline ([00:51:09]).

V. Neta's Product Evolution: From Comics to Characters to Worlds

  • The first version was the most complex design: a mini-program for one-click comic generation. It didn't work — technically, completing a story was too hard (a four-panel comic demands no less of a story than coining an idiom), and language models at the time had no agent capability for breaking down structured content ([01:08:43]).
  • The pivot to "shaping characters": the barrier is far lower, and relationships grow naturally between characters (siblings, couples, good vs. evil); two friends each shaping a character can play "house" together, and social connection forms naturally. A second wave of attempts put characters into ready-made novel plots (adventure mode) and was copied by competitors, but multimodal latency was too high — users began complaining "can you stop generating images, it's too expensive and slow," while pure text was no different from Xingye or Maoxiang ([00:57:59], [01:10:50]).
  • The "sanctuary effect": Neta's natives are the original-character (OC) community — a group of artists rejected on other platforms (some wrote "Anti-AI" right on their avatars); the more they were opposed, the more quiet supporters they had; they had nowhere to go, and Neta gave them soil. He deliberately did not take the shortcut of catering to "if you're anti-AI we won't give you AI features," but first gave these people a place to communicate ([00:59:34]).
  • The feedback gap between tools and communities: tools only receive shallow feedback ("make the image prettier, the text bigger"), which is not real demand; real demand only surfaces in community discussion — people making wedding invitations truly care about the sense of ceremony and guests' willingness to attend, and nobody talks to a tool about that ([00:53:30]).
  • Strongly interactive creation cannot aggregate into a feed: asking users to make the same thing or remix turns behavior into theme-based clubs rather than an information feed — like the societies from his university days ([00:57:29]).
  • The cost ledger: DeepSeek V2 (2024) cut the price of comparable text models by several hundred times (as stated on the spot), but video has not gotten cheaper, because the whole industry chases SOTA rather than affordability. His predicted tipping point is close: 5–15-second video at a few Chinese cents, at Seedance / Hailuo H3-level quality — Hailuo's new-generation H3 is already approaching that point ([01:14:52], [01:17:39]). Neta's engineering optimization does not chase generation quality itself but the collaboration network — users package their settings and characters into "elements" for others to invoke, and the platform provides both resource-type and exchange-type supply ([01:18:31]).

VI. The Second World: Context, the Four Quadrants, and Malleable Software

  • Neta's current definition: a community that lets users shape "an alternative life." The word young users themselves use most is "world" — "the little world of me and my friends." For digital natives, all the apps on their phone are their world ([01:26:44], [01:27:42]).
  • A world is composed of context: environment, avatar/character, and relationships. Make the context thick and governable first, and the experiences on top can be done well again and again — like a blank-memory agent entering its first game and gaining its first memory ([01:31:43], [01:33:19]).
  • The fork mechanism: worldviews inevitably diverge, as religions split into sects and open-source projects fork; both divergence and convergence are ways to grow. He corrected the host's "characters first" intuition — beyond a certain depth, the field matters more than the character; the essence of Disneyland is not IP merchandise, but people arriving together and changing something as residents ([01:34:15], [01:30:17]).
  • The four-quadrant framework for content: the horizontal axis runs from person ↔ world, the vertical from passive viewing ↔ active participation; in the long run, all content migrates toward "world × active participation." AI short dramas are "watching a new world," AI companionship is "single-character interaction" — both are only partial solutions ([01:47:58], [01:50:21]).
  • Self-criticism on malleable software: they have recently reflected deeply — presetting a single modality and a fixed interface for this generation of AI violates the technology's inherent malleability (DeepSeek Harness is a malleable operating system); underneath are the command line and language models (LUI), and the upper interface should be generated by AI on the spot, per scenario. In the future, what limits participation will no longer be form, but the richness of context ([01:44:24], [01:46:47]).
  • He reframed the loneliness problem: it is not loneliness, but a question of "time for richer experiences" — people who live long get bored; either extend the time, or start a few more games ([01:43:33]).

VII. The Company Spectrum, and No Regrets

  • The company spectrum: at the far left is the Amazon-style user-driven company (customer obsession, persuaded by users); at the far right is the Google-style technology-vision-driven company (least willing to listen, but able to recruit the best technical people); Uber and Facebook follow in scale, with Canva and Figma in the middle. From day one of a startup you must be clear about which cell of the spectrum you fall into, or you will grow into a company you never wanted to be ([01:53:58], [01:58:12]).
  • The host asked: in the first year you could have become Liblib — would you choose differently now? He answered: a person can only do one thing; we are innately not the kind of company whose whole team is driven by commercialization efficiency, and he has no regrets. Horizontal comparisons in the market only look at those living well and don't see the long list of quick deaths over two or three years — that is fair: not good enough is not good enough, but one should also respect the technical conditions of the time ([01:56:51], [01:58:32]).
  • Speaking up for Manus: he does not consider Manus a mere wrapper — many of its product innovations are strong, and its commercialization, traffic buying and growth efficiency have their strengths; the product that ultimately emerged is of decent quality ([01:52:48]).
  • An organizational signal: this year his team, too, is all working in the Claude Code-style agent way; aesthetically he favors the newest generation of technology, but its cost is so high it conflicts with affordability — "between two harms, you have to tear open a gap" ([01:50:46]).

VIII. The Last Question: The Worldview of the Second World

  • The host asked: when people are squeezed out of the (first) world, what kind of worldview do you want to build? Hu Xiuhan: people won't feel squeezed out; they will actively choose to build more meaningful things in that world — just as, in human history, people built the Sagrada Família through faith-driven crowdfunding: countless people striving their whole lives with no economic return, ultimately creating the scarcest thing in the world, the human spirit itself. The same kind of "cathedral" can be built in a virtual world; it is essentially a monument expressing "I exist" ([02:01:08]).
  • So will you build the cathedral yourself, or only provide the land? He laughed: he doesn't know yet, maybe both — but right now the responsibility for the foundation and the construction crew is heavy enough that there's no time to think about it ([02:02:56]).
