一句话

Manus 的诞生路径是：砍掉做了七个月的 AI 浏览器，转身押注「模型解锁的不是编程能力、而是 agent 能力」，用 400 道「实习生任务」当评测集，靠过程透明和 session replay 解决信任问题，再靠「面向六个月后的模型做产品」吃到模型升级的全部红利。

一、张涛其人

张涛是 Manus 联合创始人、首席产品官，1986 年生，是公司里年纪最大的合伙人。十几年职业生涯里只在腾讯和字节各干过一年，其余时间全在创业；产品从 C 端做到 B 端、从国内做到海外，自称「典型的古典产品经理」。他自嘲：2009 年同一批入行做产品的人，回头看只有他还在 AI 赛道一线——因为一直没有大成，所以一直还在坚持干。

二、从 Monica 到砍掉 AI 浏览器

  • Monica（2023）：ChatGPT 刚出来时，用户写完邮件文案要在标签页间来回复制粘贴，他们做了活在侧边栏里的浏览器插件 Monica——框选一段文字直接改写或翻译，不用离开页面。同类功能 Google 过了一年半才加进 Gmail。Monica 一年增长很快，但 2024 年撞到天花板：世界上很多人一辈子不知道浏览器能装插件，市场就这么大。
  • AI 浏览器（2024 年 3–10 月）：组新团队做了七个月的 AI 原生浏览器，成熟度自称远超后来的 Arc Dia 和 Perplexity Comet，发布网站和视频全部做好——最后没发。原因很扎心：用户拿到 AI 浏览器的第一个问题永远是「我在 Chrome 里用的那个功能去哪找」，用户永远拿旧体验的基准线对标你；三十人的团队去跟苹果、谷歌对齐二十年的浏览器基础，几乎不可能；一旦发出去，团队要被拖进去三到六个月。CEO Red 最后拍板砍掉。张涛的总结是：创业里钱是最不值钱的，最浪费不起的是时间。回头看，这是最重要的决定。

三、Manus 的立项：解锁的是 agent，不是编程

  • 浏览器砍掉后，五个人在小办公室里找方向。他们天天用 Cursor，但张涛用 Cursor 干的是报销、把 MP4 转 MP3、写小说这类杂事。2024 年 11 月 Anthropic 发布 Sonnet 3.5 V2，Cursor 和 Windsurf 的能力集体飞跃。全世界都在做 Cursor 的复制品，他们看到的是另一件事：这个模型解锁的不是编程能力，是 agent 能力——两个标志是长程规划（模型知道一个任务不该在一轮对话里完成）和工具调用。判断是：「这个世界不需要更多工程师，这个能力是给普通人的。」
  • 2024 年 12 月立项，团队慌得不行，怕被抢先。2025 年 3 月 5 日发布后发现，全世界只有他们在做这个。
  • 评测集的来历：张涛在朋友圈发了一条——「如果你有一个本科水平的实习生，你会交代什么任务给他做」。一百多个朋友交来四百多个任务，去掉买奶茶这类需要具身智能的，剩三百多条，成了 Manus 最早的评测集。1 月 5 日调通第一版，此后每天这套题都给他惊喜，agent 用完全超出想象的方式完成任务。他的原话（转述）：「智能跳出屏幕拍在你脸上的那种感觉，你无法抗拒，你知道这就是未来。」

四、信任问题：把透明做成功能

  • 冲击太强反而带来问题：直接交付结果，用户觉得是假的。发布第一周，很多人以为他们发的是假演示（fake demo）。
  • 解法是两个后来成为全行业标配的设计：一是把 agent 的工作过程一步步展示出来——它先怎么想、调用什么工具、访问哪个网页、写了什么笔记；二是 session replay——把任务分享出去，别人不用邀请码、不用充值，就能完整回看一遍执行过程。这两个设计完全是面向传播做的：很多人不信 agent，但看到它像人一样一步步工作，就开始信了。

五、发布视频：创始人自己拍的

2 月 28 日 CEO 看官网觉得有点素，问要不要加个视频。张涛打给北京几家视频公司，工期都是两周起。被逼无奈自己拍：索尼 A7C2 机身，镜头是从真格的投资人那借的，Peak 当演员，在共享办公园区找了两个背景干净的地方，3 月 1 日拍完，张涛自己用剪映剪了两天。唯一的开销是导出 4K 要会员，花十几美元买了一个月。那个「创始人坐沙发、背后两棵绿植」的视频后来被无数公司模仿。张涛的观点：真正重要的从来不是视频而是产品本身，但早期创业的营销必须创始人自己上，别人讲不出那个故事。

六、四个关键判断

  1. 理解模型能力的边界：永远面向六个月后的模型做产品。 首席科学家 Peak 做了十几年 NLP，能从模型表现里感知前沿实验室在往哪走。2025 年 2 月中旬 Sonnet 3.7 发布那晚，张涛发了条朋友圈，四个字：「信仰充值」——整个系统一行代码没改，只是把模型接口从 3.5 V2 换成 3.7，剩下两三成没解决的难题全部解了。这就是 less structure, more intelligence：当年这是异端，共识是人教 AI 做事、写死流程；他们坚持把每一步决策交给模型。模型每升一级，你就白得一级红利。
  2. 为什么不做 Chatbot。 Chatbot 的产品上限被用户的提问能力限制住了——用户不会提问题，产品价值就出不来。另一个反共识点：免费补贴是最大的陷阱，补贴吸来的是免费用户，给你一堆无效反馈、拖垮注意力；大厂尤其明显，一个总监做到一二十万免费日活就成了 KPI，没人敢转收费。Manus 上付费的第一个月 ARR 就冲到四千多万、接近五千万美元；核心用户一个月在平台上花四五千美元。「真智能，就有人付费。」
  3. 坚持 General。 发布时投资人都劝：你老演示稀奇古怪的长尾任务，能不能专注做个网站生成器。没听。回头看这是最重要的战略判断：品牌心智一辈子只有一次机会，因为一开始就叫出了 general agent，后来推什么子功能都顺理成章。他们内部有一张图：横轴付费金额、纵轴满意度，最右上角的用户是老板——公司里杂事最多的人，CEO 今天想这个明天想那个，只有什么都能干的 agent 能让他最爽。
  4. 理解用户：用户是普通人，不是工程师。 所以做了云沙箱——所有任务在云端跑，手机上布置完任务就可以揣兜里，不用开着电脑。一个有意思的数据：美国是最大收入来源，但旧金山和西雅图都不在用户前十，前十是洛杉矶、迈阿密、芝加哥这些城市——那不是天天切换工具的科技人群。张涛说：别老想着功能对功能的竞争，想清楚你到底服务谁。

七、邀请码的真相：不是饥饿营销，是烧不动

很多人以为邀请码是饥饿营销，其实是账上没钱：当时公司账上只有八九百万美元，有邀请码控制着，高峰一天还要烧五十万美元；没有邀请码，3 月 10 号就能关门。当时连付费系统都没做，是火了之后才紧急加上去的。更深层是算力问题：一个 agent 用户的 token 消耗是 Chatbot 的两百到一千倍；上线第三天就需要 800M 的 TPM，打电话给云厂商说要 500M，人家以为他在吹牛砍价。最后从一家没什么客户的厂商那捡漏，撑过了头一个月。等到五月取消邀请码，云厂商的储备也上来了——后来的 agent 创业者再没遇到过这个问题。

八、给创业者的三条建议

  1. 全球化视野。 去年六月他去法国参加 VivaTech，出发前收到爱丽舍宫邮件说马克龙请他吃饭，第一反应是诈骗。晚宴上马克龙坐他右手边，黄仁勋坐对面。他听欧洲创业者和投资人聊天觉得不对劲：整个欧洲是防御心态，build in Europe、build for Europe。他想，如果当初只做中国市场，他不会坐在那个位置。大语言模型移除了语言这个最大障碍；他不准公司里说「国外」这个词——世界不是国内加国外的二元对立。Manus 最大收入来自美国，最大用户地区是巴西。
  2. 多实操，少读自媒体。 他面试 AI 产品经理的观感特别差：最夸张的手机上没装那个应用，次夸张的打开之后任务列表只有两三条，明显是面试前突击的。「AI 冲击行业四年了，还有这种事。」自媒体的问题是没有行业经验、靠流量思维刺激情绪；Manus 二十个月里被「颠覆」了无数次，业务一直增长。真正的产品感觉全在细节里，要自己上手。
  3. 观察生活。 技术过剩的时代，最缺的是发现和定义问题的能力，这个能力来自对生活的好奇心。

九、名字的来源，与现场问答

  • Manus 来自 MIT 的校训 Mens et Manus（拉丁语：头脑和手）。所有大模型厂商都在做头脑；2024 年底那么强的头脑为什么产生不了那么大的影响？因为它只有大脑没有手。Manus 就是大模型的手，让大脑的思考作用到真实世界。这句话同样适用于创业本身：所有思考都需要实践，而不是停留在 PPT 推演上。
  • 现场问答里，有人问 Codex 和 Claude Code 的竞争，张涛说真实用户不用那些东西，旧金山满地的科技人群不是他们的用户；有人问通用 agent 还有没有机会，真格的 Tina 说：先跳进去开始做，别老想机会存不存在。
  • 频道主的收尾总结：七个月做完的浏览器说砍就砍是战略定力的教科书；一个 agent 用户两百到一千倍的 token 消耗，逼着整个云行业重估算力储备；less structure, more intelligence 从异端变成共识；四百道实习生考题成了最早的评测集。最后一句是张涛在北大的收尾——所有的大机会都在未知里，不在确定性里。

  • Monica（2023）：ChatGPT 剛出來時，使用者寫完郵件文案要在分頁間來回複製貼上，他們做了活在側邊欄裡的瀏覽器外掛 Monica——框選一段文字直接改寫或翻譯，不用離開頁面。同類功能 Google 過了一年半才加進 Gmail。Monica 一年增長很快，但 2024 年撞到天花板：世界上很多人一輩子不知道瀏覽器能裝外掛，市場就這麼大。

四、信任問題：把透明做成功能

  • 衝擊太強反而帶來問題：直接交付結果，使用者覺得是假的。發布第一週，很多人以為他們發的是假演示（fake demo）。

六、四個關鍵判斷

  1. 理解模型能力的邊界：永遠面向六個月後的模型做產品。 首席科學家 Peak 做了十幾年 NLP，能從模型表現裡感知前沿實驗室在往哪走。2025 年 2 月中旬 Sonnet 3.7 發布那晚，張濤發了條朋友圈，四個字：「信仰儲值」——整個系統一行程式碼沒改，只是把模型介面從 3.5 V2 換成 3.7，剩下兩三成沒解決的難題全部解了。這就是 less structure, more intelligence：當年這是異端，共識是人教 AI 做事、寫死流程；他們堅持把每一步決策交給模型。模型每升一級，你就白得一級紅利。
  2. 為什麼不做 Chatbot。 Chatbot 的產品上限被使用者的提問能力限制住了——使用者不會提問題，產品價值就出不來。另一個反共識點：免費補貼是最大的陷阱，補貼吸來的是免費使用者，給你一堆無效回饋、拖垮注意力；大廠尤其明顯，一個總監做到一二十萬免費日活就成了 KPI，沒人敢轉收費。Manus 上付費的第一個月 ARR 就衝到四千多萬、接近五千萬美元；核心使用者一個月在平台上花四五千美元。「真智能，就有人付費。」
  3. 堅持 General。 發布時投資人都勸：你老演示稀奇古怪的長尾任務，能不能專注做個網站產生器。沒聽。回頭看這是最重要的戰略判斷：品牌心智一輩子只有一次機會，因為一開始就叫出了 general agent，後來推什麼子功能都順理成章。他們內部有一張圖：橫軸付費金額、縱軸滿意度，最右上角的使用者是老闆——公司裡雜事最多的人，CEO 今天想這個明天想那個，只有什麼都能幹的 agent 能讓他最爽。
  4. 理解使用者：使用者是普通人，不是工程師。 所以做了雲端沙箱——所有任務在雲端跑，手機上布置完任務就可以揣兜裡，不用開著電腦。一個有意思的數據：美國是最大收入來源，但舊金山和西雅圖都不在使用者前十，前十是洛杉磯、邁阿密、芝加哥這些城市——那不是天天切換工具的科技人群。張濤說：別老想著功能對功能的競爭，想清楚你到底服務誰。

七、邀請碼的真相：不是飢餓行銷，是燒不動

很多人以為邀請碼是飢餓行銷，其實是帳上沒錢：當時公司帳上只有八九百萬美元，有邀請碼控制著，高峰一天還要燒五十萬美元；沒有邀請碼，3 月 10 號就能關門。當時連付費系統都沒做，是火了之後才緊急加上去的。更深層是算力問題：一個 agent 使用者的 token 消耗是 Chatbot 的兩百到一千倍；上線第三天就需要 800M 的 TPM，打電話給雲廠商說要 500M，人家以為他在吹牛砍價。最後從一家沒什麼客戶的廠商那撿漏，撐過了頭一個月。等到五月取消邀請碼，雲廠商的儲備也上來了——後來的 agent 創業者再沒遇到過這個問題。

八、給創業者的三條建議

  1. 全球化視野。 去年六月他去法國參加 VivaTech，出發前收到愛麗舍宮郵件說馬克龍請他吃飯，第一反應是詐騙。晚宴上馬克龍坐他右手邊，黃仁勳坐對面。他聽歐洲創業者和投資人聊天覺得不對勁：整個歐洲是防禦心態，build in Europe、build for Europe。他想，如果當初只做中國市場，他不會坐在那個位置。大語言模型移除了語言這個最大障礙；他不准公司裡說「國外」這個詞——世界不是國內加國外的二元對立。Manus 最大收入來自美國，最大使用者地區是巴西。
  2. 多實操，少讀自媒體。 他面試 AI 產品經理的觀感特別差：最誇張的手機上沒裝那個應用，次誇張的打開之後任務列表只有兩三條，明顯是面試前突擊的。「AI 衝擊行業四年了，還有這種事。」自媒體的問題是沒有行業經驗、靠流量思維刺激情緒；Manus 二十個月裡被「顛覆」了無數次，業務一直增長。真正的產品感覺全在細節裡，要自己上手。
  3. 觀察生活。 技術過剩的時代，最缺的是發現和定義問題的能力，這個能力來自對生活的好奇心。

九、名字的來源，與現場問答

  • Manus 來自 MIT 的校訓 Mens et Manus（拉丁語：頭腦和手）。所有大模型廠商都在做頭腦；2024 年底那麼強的頭腦為什麼產生不了那麼大的影響？因為它只有大腦沒有手。Manus 就是大模型的手，讓大腦的思考作用到真實世界。這句話同樣適用於創業本身：所有思考都需要實踐，而不是停留在 PPT 推演上。
  • 現場問答裡，有人問 Codex 和 Claude Code 的競爭，張濤說真實使用者不用那些東西，舊金山滿地的科技人群不是他們的使用者；有人問通用 agent 還有沒有機會，真格的 Tina 說：先跳進去開始做，別老想機會存不存在。
  • 頻道主的收尾總結：七個月做完的瀏覽器說砍就砍是戰略定力的教科書；一個 agent 使用者兩百到一千倍的 token 消耗，逼著整個雲行業重估算力儲備；less structure, more intelligence 從異端變成共識；四百道實習生考題成了最早的評測集。最後一句是張濤在北大的收尾——所有的大機會都在未知裡，不在確定性裡。

In One Sentence

Manus was born along this path: they cut the AI browser they had spent seven months building, bet instead that "what models unlocked was not coding ability but agent ability," used 400 "intern tasks" as their evaluation set, solved the trust problem through process transparency and session replay, and then captured the full dividend of model upgrades by "building products for the models of six months from now."

I. Who Zhang Tao Is

Zhang Tao is a Manus co-founder and chief product officer, born in 1986, and the oldest partner in the company. In a career of more than a decade, he worked only one year each at Tencent and ByteDance, and spent all the rest of the time founding startups; his products went from consumer-facing to business-facing, and from China to overseas, and he calls himself "a typical classical product manager." He jokes about himself: of the cohort who entered product work with him in 2009, looking back, he is the only one still on the front line of the AI race — because he never achieved a big success, he has kept at it.

II. From Monica to Cutting the AI Browser

  • Monica (2023): When ChatGPT had just come out, users who had finished writing email copy had to copy and paste back and forth between tabs. They built Monica, a browser extension that lived in the sidebar — select a passage of text and rewrite or translate it directly, without leaving the page. Google did not add a similar feature to Gmail until a year and a half later. Monica grew very fast in its first year, but hit a ceiling in 2024: many people in the world never in their lives learn that browsers can have extensions installed; the market is only that big.
  • AI browser (March–October 2024): They formed a new team and spent seven months building an AI-native browser, whose maturity they claimed far exceeded the later Arc Dia and Perplexity Comet. The launch website and video were all ready — and in the end they did not launch it. The reason was painful: the first question users always ask when they get an AI browser is "where do I find that feature I used in Chrome?" Users always benchmark you against the baseline of the old experience; for a thirty-person team to catch up with twenty years of browser foundations at Apple and Google is almost impossible; and once launched, the team would be dragged into it for three to six months. CEO Red made the final call to cut it. Zhang Tao's summary was: in startups, money is the least valuable thing; what you can least afford to waste is time. Looking back, this was the most important decision.

III. The Founding of Manus: What Was Unlocked Was Agents, Not Coding

  • After the browser was cut, five people searched for a direction in a small office. They used Cursor every day, but what Zhang Tao used Cursor for was chores such as expense claims, converting MP4 to MP3, and writing fiction. In November 2024 Anthropic released Sonnet 3.5 V2, and the capabilities of Cursor and Windsurf leapt forward collectively. The whole world was building replicas of Cursor; what they saw was something else: what this model unlocked was not coding ability, but agent ability — the two signs being long-horizon planning (the model knows a task should not be completed within a single round of conversation) and tool calling. Their judgment was: "The world does not need more engineers; this capability is for ordinary people."
  • The project was established in December 2024, and the team was in a panic, afraid of being preempted. After launching on March 5, 2025, they discovered that they were the only ones in the whole world doing this.
  • Where the evaluation set came from: Zhang Tao posted in his WeChat Moments — "If you had an intern at undergraduate level, what tasks would you assign them to do?" More than a hundred friends submitted more than four hundred tasks; after removing those requiring embodied intelligence, such as buying milk tea, just over three hundred remained, and they became Manus's earliest evaluation set. The first version was made to work on January 5, and from then on this set of tasks surprised him every day, as the agent completed tasks in ways completely beyond imagination. In his own words (as retold): "That feeling of intelligence jumping out of the screen and hitting you in the face — you cannot resist it; you know this is the future."

IV. The Trust Problem: Making Transparency a Feature

  • Too strong an impact created a problem instead: when results were delivered directly, users thought they were fake. In the first week after launch, many people thought what they had released was a fake demo.
  • The solution was two designs that later became industry standards: first, showing the agent's work process step by step — how it thinks first, what tools it calls, which web pages it visits, what notes it writes; and second, session replay — share a task out, and others can watch the entire execution process again in full, without an invite code and without paying. Both designs were made entirely for propagation: many people did not believe in agents, but when they saw it working step by step like a human, they began to believe.

V. The Launch Video: Filmed by the Founder Himself

On February 28, the CEO looked at the official website and felt it was a bit plain, and asked whether they should add a video. Zhang Tao called several video companies in Beijing; all quoted timelines starting at two weeks. Forced by necessity, he filmed it himself: a Sony A7C2 body, a lens borrowed from an investor at ZhenFund, Peak as the actor, two clean-background spots found in a coworking park, filming finished on March 1, and Zhang Tao edited it himself in Jianying over two days. The only expense was that exporting in 4K required a membership, which cost a dozen or so dollars for a month. That video — "the founder sitting on a sofa, with two green plants behind him" — was later imitated by countless companies. Zhang Tao's view: what truly matters is never the video but the product itself, but marketing in early startups must be done by the founders themselves; no one else can tell that story.

VI. Four Key Judgments

  1. Understanding the boundaries of model capability: always build products for the models of six months from now. Chief Scientist Peak had worked in NLP for more than a decade and could sense from model performance where the frontier labs were heading. On the night in mid-February 2025 when Sonnet 3.7 was released, Zhang Tao posted four words in his Moments: "Faith recharged" — not a single line of code in the entire system was changed; they only switched the model interface from 3.5 V2 to 3.7, and the remaining twenty to thirty percent of unsolved problems were all solved. This is less structure, more intelligence: at the time this was heresy; the consensus was that humans teach AI to do things and hard-code the workflows; they insisted on handing every step of decision-making to the model. Each time the model levels up, you get a level of dividend for free.
  2. Why not build a Chatbot. A Chatbot's product ceiling is limited by users' ability to ask questions — if users cannot ask questions, the product's value cannot come out. Another counter-consensus point: free subsidies are the biggest trap. Subsidies attract free users, who give you a pile of invalid feedback and drag down your attention; this is especially obvious at large companies, where once a director reaches one or two hundred thousand free daily active users it becomes a KPI, and no one dares to switch to charging. In Manus's first month of charging, ARR surged to more than forty million, close to fifty million US dollars; core users spent four to five thousand dollars a month on the platform. "With real intelligence, people will pay."
  3. Insisting on General. At launch, investors all advised: you keep demonstrating odd, long-tail tasks; why not focus on building a website generator? They did not listen. Looking back, this was the most important strategic judgment: brand mindshare gets only one chance in a lifetime. Because they called out "general agent" from the very beginning, every sub-feature they launched later fell naturally into place. Internally they have a chart: the horizontal axis is amount paid, the vertical axis is satisfaction, and the users in the top-right corner are bosses — the people in a company with the most chores, with the CEO wanting one thing today and another tomorrow; only an agent that can do everything can make them happiest.
  4. Understanding users: users are ordinary people, not engineers. So they built a cloud sandbox — all tasks run in the cloud; once you have assigned a task on your phone you can put it in your pocket, without keeping a computer on. One interesting data point: the United States is the largest source of revenue, but neither San Francisco nor Seattle is in the top ten user cities; the top ten are cities such as Los Angeles, Miami, and Chicago — not tech crowds who switch tools every day. Zhang Tao said: do not always think about feature-against-feature competition; think clearly about whom you actually serve.

VII. The Truth About Invite Codes: Not Hunger Marketing, But Unable to Afford the Burn

Many people thought invite codes were hunger marketing; in fact the company simply had no money in its account: at the time the company had only eight or nine million US dollars in its account, and even with invite codes controlling volume, at peak it still burned five hundred thousand dollars a day; without invite codes, it could have shut down by March 10. At the time they had not even built a payment system; it was urgently added only after they became popular. At a deeper level it was a compute problem: one agent user's token consumption is two hundred to one thousand times that of a Chatbot; by the third day after launch they needed 800M TPM, and when they called cloud vendors asking for 500M, the vendors thought he was bluffing to bargain. In the end they picked up capacity from a vendor with few customers and got through the first month. By the time invite codes were removed in May, the cloud vendors' reserves had also come up — later agent founders never encountered this problem again.

VIII. Three Pieces of Advice for Founders

  1. A global perspective. In June last year he went to France to attend VivaTech, and before departure he received an email from the Élysée Palace saying Macron was inviting him to dinner; his first reaction was that it was a scam. At the dinner, Macron sat on his right and Jensen Huang sat across from him. Listening to European founders and investors talk, he felt something was off: all of Europe has a defensive mindset — build in Europe, build for Europe. He thought: if he had only done the Chinese market at the start, he would not be sitting in that seat. Large language models removed language, the biggest barrier; he does not allow the word "abroad" to be used in the company — the world is not a binary opposition of domestic plus foreign. Manus's largest revenue comes from the United States, and its largest user region is Brazil.
  2. Do more hands-on work, read less self-media. His impression from interviewing AI product managers was especially poor: in the most extreme case, the candidate did not have the app installed on their phone; in the next most extreme, after opening it the task list had only two or three items, clearly crammed in before the interview. "AI has been impacting the industry for four years, and this still happens." The problem with self-media is that it has no industry experience and relies on traffic thinking to stir emotions; in twenty months Manus was "disrupted" countless times, while its business kept growing. Real product sense is all in the details; you have to get hands-on yourself.
  3. Observe life. In an era of excess technology, what is scarcest is the ability to discover and define problems, and that ability comes from curiosity about life.

IX. The Origin of the Name, and the On-Site Q&A

  • Manus comes from MIT's motto Mens et Manus (Latin: mind and hand). All the large-model vendors are building the mind; why could such a strong mind at the end of 2024 not produce an equally large impact? Because it had only a brain and no hands. Manus is the hand of the large model, letting the brain's thinking act on the real world. The same sentence applies to founding a company itself: all thinking requires practice, rather than staying in PPT deduction.
  • In the on-site Q&A, someone asked about competition from Codex and Claude Code. Zhang Tao said real users do not use those things, and the tech crowds all over San Francisco are not their users; someone asked whether there is still an opportunity for general agents, and Tina from ZhenFund said: jump in and start building first; do not keep thinking about whether the opportunity exists.
  • The channel host's closing summary: cutting a browser that took seven months to build, just like that, is a textbook case of strategic resolve; one agent user consuming two hundred to one thousand times the tokens forced the entire cloud industry to re-estimate compute reserves; less structure, more intelligence went from heresy to consensus; and the four hundred intern exam questions became the earliest evaluation set. The final line was Zhang Tao's closing at Peking University — all the big opportunities lie in the unknown, not in certainty.
